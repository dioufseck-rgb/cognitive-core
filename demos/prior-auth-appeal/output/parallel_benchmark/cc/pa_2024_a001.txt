Dear Ms. Torres,

This letter serves as the determination for your appeal regarding the prior authorization denial for the Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) requested by Dr. James Kim. Your initial request was denied on November 1, 2024, with the stated reasons that you had completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy (less than the plan's minimum 6 weeks, or 4 weeks under the Myelopathy Pathway Exception), only one pharmacotherapy agent was trialed, and therefore, you did not meet the conservative treatment requirements under HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2.

Upon review of your appeal, the submitted clinical record, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), the California Regulatory Framework, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, HealthFirst Health Plan has determined to **OVERTURN** the prior authorization denial. The requested procedure for ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is approved.

**Comprehensive Justification for Overturn:**

The decision to overturn the denial is based on the application of the established hierarchy of authority: California Regulatory Framework > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Health Plan Criteria. The HealthFirst plan's conservative treatment requirements, specifically those related to physical therapy and pharmacotherapy duration, are overridden by controlling California law and prevailing clinical evidence for your specific condition.

**1. Overriding California Regulatory Provisions:**

Your clinical presentation of progressive cervical myelopathy C5-C6 with myelomalacia and objective neurological deficits (bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, positive Babinski, gait ataxia) triggers several critical California regulatory provisions that supersede HealthFirst's internal criteria:

*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3):** This provision explicitly states that a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where "[t]he condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Your MRI report dated August 15, 2024, documents "Severe central cord compression C5-C6" and "T2 hyperintensity within cord (myelomalacia)." Myelomalacia is objective evidence of existing spinal cord injury and structural neurological compromise (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2). Therefore, HealthFirst's requirement for a minimum duration of physical therapy (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a) and Exception 2.2(A)) is legally unenforceable in your case.

*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1):** This provision further prohibits conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy when "[t]he member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition." Your treating physician, Dr. James Kim, explicitly documented on October 15, 2024, that "PT is contraindicated in this patient. Myelomalacia is present on initial MRI. Continuing PT risks irreversible cord injury." This physician declaration, supported by clinical findings, provides an additional basis for overriding the plan's physical therapy requirement.

*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 II.1 and II.2:** This guidance mandates that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI be treated as an urgent condition due to the potential for irreversible harm (DMHC APL 22-014 II.1). For such urgent conditions, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization" when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents urgency and surgical indication under generally accepted guidelines (DMHC APL 22-014 II.2). Your case meets these criteria, overriding HealthFirst's conservative treatment requirements (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a) and 2.2(b)) for both physical therapy and pharmacotherapy duration.

**2. Overriding Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:**

Generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, as referenced in HealthFirst's own policy (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 5) and detailed in the Clinical Reference Summary, contradict the plan's conservative treatment requirements for your specific condition:

*   **Myelomalacia as a Strong Surgical Indication:** The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is considered by major spine societies (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) to be a "strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2). It signifies existing spinal cord injury and increased risk of permanent deficit with delayed treatment.

*   **Limited Efficacy of Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** For moderate to severe cervical myelopathy, physical therapy does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement, and delaying surgery to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by evidence and may result in neurological deterioration (AO Spine Guidelines, NASS Guidelines, Cochrane Review, Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1). The 6-week (or 4-week) PT requirement in many payer policies is derived from radiculopathy evidence, not myelopathy (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.2).

*   **Surgical Urgency and Tier 1 Indication:** Your clinical presentation, including confirmed cervical myelopathy, myelomalacia on MRI, and objective neurological deficits, meets the "TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION" for ACDF (CPT 22551) according to AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.1, 3.2). For this presentation, the clinical standard does not require completion of a minimum course of physical therapy as a prerequisite to surgery.

**Conclusion:**

Based on the direct overrides provided by California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3), and DMHC APL 22-014 II.1 and II.2, as well as the strong contradiction from generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, HealthFirst's conservative treatment requirements under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 (including the Myelopathy Pathway Exception 2.2(A)) are not applicable to your case. The denial based on these unmet requirements is therefore invalid.

The requested Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is approved.
