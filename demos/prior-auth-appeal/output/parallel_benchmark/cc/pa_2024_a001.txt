Dear Ms. Torres and Dr. Kim,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its clinical and regulatory review of the first-level appeal submitted on November 15, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). 

After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California statutory mandates, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has determined that the initial denial issued on November 1, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, must be OVERTURNED. The requested procedure is medically necessary and is hereby authorized.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND HISTORY
Ms. Diana Torres is a 61-year-old female with a documented history of progressive cervical myelopathy at C5-C6 with myelomalacia. 
- On August 10, 2024, Ms. Torres presented with a 4-month history of bilateral hand clumsiness and gait instability. 
- A neurological examination on August 10, 2024, revealed significant upper motor neuron signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia in both upper and lower extremities, bilateral positive Babinski signs, and documented gait ataxia.
- A cervical spine MRI performed on August 15, 2024, demonstrated severe central cord compression at C5-C6 and T2 hyperintensity within the spinal cord, confirming active myelomalacia (spinal cord injury/damage).
- Ms. Torres attempted physical therapy at Valley PT from September 1, 2024, to September 14, 2024 (completing 6 sessions over 2 weeks). This therapy was discontinued by written order of Dr. Kim because it caused a severe exacerbation of her neurological symptoms.
- Ms. Torres also trialed a 2-week course of oral methylprednisolone, which provided only temporary, mild improvement with no sustained benefit.
- In a formal declaration dated October 15, 2024, Dr. Kim documented that further physical therapy is strictly contraindicated due to the presence of myelomalacia, and that continuing conservative therapy risks irreversible spinal cord injury.

II. PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)
The initial denial on November 1, 2024, was based on Clinical Coverage Criteria Document CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2, which requires:
1. A minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy (reduced to 4 weeks under the Section 2.2(A) Myelopathy Pathway Exception).
2. A trial of at least two pharmacotherapy agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
3. One interventional treatment (waived under the Myelopathy Pathway).

Ms. Torres completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy and trialed only one pharmacotherapy agent (methylprednisolone) for 2 weeks. Under a literal, mechanical reading of the plan's internal criteria, these requirements were not met. However, as detailed below, California law and clinical standards prohibit the application of these conservative treatment requirements to Ms. Torres's clinical presentation.

III. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY OVERRIDE AND LEGAL UNENFORCEABILITY
Because Ms. Torres is a California resident enrolled in a plan subject to California jurisdiction, state regulatory mandates supersede any conflicting internal plan criteria.

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a) (Physical Therapy Prerequisite Prohibitions):
Under CIC § 10169.5(a), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy under any of the following circumstances:
- § 10169.5(a)(1): The treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition. Dr. Kim's written declaration of October 15, 2024, explicitly states that PT is contraindicated and poses a risk of irreversible cord injury.
- § 10169.5(a)(3): The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Ms. Torres's MRI dated August 15, 2024, provides objective evidence of severe central cord compression and myelomalacia (structural neurological compromise).
Consequently, under California law, HealthFirst's internal requirement for a minimum duration of physical therapy is legally unenforceable and cannot be applied to this case.

2. Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:
APL 22-014 addresses prior authorization for time-sensitive neurological conditions. It states:
- Cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated.
- Plans shall not require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents urgency.
Dr. Kim's clinical documentation and formal declaration satisfy all elements of APL 22-014, rendering the plan's conservative treatment requirements (both physical therapy and pharmacotherapy) inapplicable.

IV. GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS OF MEDICAL PRACTICE
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, the standard for medical necessity is governed by generally accepted standards of medical practice, which override restrictive internal plan criteria.

According to the AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine Guidelines (2023 Update), the North American Spine Society (NASS) Clinical Guidelines (2020), and the AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):
1. Moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI is classified as a Tier 1 "Strong Indication" for surgical decompression.
2. There is no high-quality clinical evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy or extended conservative management prior to surgical intervention in patients with established myelomalacia and upper motor neuron signs.
3. Delaying surgical decompression in patients with myelomalacia to complete arbitrary conservative treatment trials is clinically contraindicated, as it is associated with a high risk of irreversible neurological deterioration and poorer long-term postoperative outcomes.

V. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS
In resolving conflicts between the plan's internal clinical criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) and governing authorities, HealthFirst applies the following hierarchy of standards:
1. California Statutory and Regulatory Law (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014) — Controlling Authority.
2. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards of Medical Practice (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine Guidelines) — Secondary Authority.
3. Plan Internal Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) — Tertiary Authority.

While Ms. Torres did not meet the conservative treatment durations outlined in CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, California law (CIC § 10169.5) and DMHC regulations (APL 22-014) legally prohibit the plan from enforcing these requirements due to her documented structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) and the treating physician's written contraindication. Furthermore, generally accepted clinical standards establish that immediate surgical decompression is the medically necessary and appropriate standard of care for her presentation.
