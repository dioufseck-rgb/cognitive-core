**SUBJECT: APPEAL DETERMINATION – PRIOR AUTHORIZATION DENIAL FOR ACDF C5-C6**

Dear Ms. Torres,

This letter serves as the determination of your appeal regarding the prior authorization denial for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551), requested by Dr. James Kim. Your initial request was denied by HealthFirst on November 1, 2024, citing that conservative treatment requirements under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 were not met, specifically regarding the duration of physical therapy (2 weeks completed vs. 6 weeks required) and the number of pharmacotherapy agents trialed (one agent vs. two required).

After a comprehensive review of your clinical record, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), relevant California regulatory provisions, and generally accepted clinical guidelines, we have determined that the prior authorization denial must be **OVERTURNED**.

**Clinical Background:**
Your clinical record indicates a diagnosis of progressive cervical myelopathy C5-C6 with myelomalacia, presenting with a 4-month history of bilateral hand clumsiness and gait instability. A neurological exam on August 10, 2024, documented bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, positive Babinski signs bilaterally, and gait ataxia (Clinical Record). An MRI on August 15, 2024, revealed severe central cord compression at C5-C6 with T2 hyperintensity within the cord, consistent with myelomalacia (Clinical Record, Imaging). You underwent 2 weeks (6 sessions) of physical therapy from September 1 to September 14, 2024, which was discontinued by your treating physician, Dr. James Kim, due to symptom exacerbation. Dr. Kim explicitly documented that physical therapy was contraindicated and that myelomalacia was present on the initial MRI, posing a risk of irreversible cord injury (Clinical Record, Physician Declaration).

**Analysis of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2, requires a minimum of 6 weeks of physical therapy and a trial of at least two pharmacotherapy agents. While the Myelopathy Pathway Exception 2.2(A) reduces the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks, your record indicates only 2 weeks of physical therapy and one pharmacotherapy agent (Methylprednisolone taper) were completed (Clinical Record, Conservative Treatment). Therefore, based solely on HealthFirst's internal criteria, the conservative treatment requirements were not met (Investigate Plan Criteria Application, Finding).

**Analysis of California Regulatory Requirements (Controlling Provisions):**
As a California-regulated health plan, HealthFirst is bound by California law, which supersedes internal plan criteria when conflicts arise. Several provisions are directly applicable to your case:

1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3) – Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This statute explicitly prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where:
    *   Your treating physician, Dr. James Kim, documented in writing that physical therapy was contraindicated and caused symptom exacerbation, risking irreversible cord injury (Clinical Record, Physician Declaration). This satisfies CIC § 10169.5(a)(1).
    *   Your condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia on MRI) that physical therapy cannot address (Clinical Record, Imaging). This satisfies CIC § 10169.5(a)(3).
    *   CIC § 10169.5(c) further states that a plan's minimum physical therapy requirement shall not be applied mechanically without reference to neurological urgency under subdivision (a)(3) (Regulatory Document, Part III).

2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 II.1, II.2, and II.3 – Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions:** This guidance mandates that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm and SHALL be treated as urgent (Regulatory Document, Part II, II.1). For such conditions, plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents urgency (Regulatory Document, Part II, II.2). Dr. Kim's declaration confirms the urgency and the presence of myelomalacia (Clinical Record, Physician Declaration). Furthermore, DMHC APL 22-014 II.3 states that plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice, and where a conflict exists, the clinical standard governs (Regulatory Document, Part II, II.3).

**Analysis of Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:**
Generally accepted clinical standards, as referenced in the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines, NASS Guidelines, and AO Spine Guidelines, strongly support surgical intervention for your condition:

1.  **Myelomalacia as a Strong Surgical Indication:** The presence of myelomalacia (T2 signal change within the spinal cord on MRI) indicates existing spinal cord injury, a higher risk of permanent deficit with continued compression, and reduced likelihood of recovery if surgery is delayed. It is considered a strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials (Clinical Evidence Document, Section 1.2, 3.1 TIER 1(c)).

2.  **Limited Efficacy of Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** For moderate-severe cervical myelopathy, physical therapy does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement, and delaying surgery to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by evidence and may result in neurological deterioration (Clinical Evidence Document, Section 2.1, 2.3). The 6-week PT requirement in many payer policies derives from radiculopathy evidence, not myelopathy, and applying such requirements to myelopathy patients is a category error (Clinical Evidence Document, Section 2.2).

3.  **Meeting Tier 1 Surgical Criteria:** Your presentation of confirmed cervical myelopathy, myelomalacia on MRI, and objective neurological deficits (Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, gait disturbance) meets the TIER 1 strong indication for ACDF. The clinical standard does NOT require completion of a minimum course of physical therapy as a prerequisite for surgery in this setting (Clinical Evidence Document, Section 3.1, 3.2).

**Resolution of Conflicts:**
There is a direct conflict between HealthFirst's internal conservative treatment requirements and both California regulatory law and generally accepted clinical standards. In accordance with the established hierarchy (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria), the California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3), along with DMHC APL 22-014 II.2 and II.3, override HealthFirst's criteria regarding the physical therapy requirement. Furthermore, generally accepted clinical standards support urgent surgical intervention for cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and do not endorse delaying surgery for extended conservative treatment (Verify Cross-Source Conflicts, Violations).

**Determination:**
Based on the foregoing analysis, the prior authorization denial for ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) for Ms. Diana Torres is **OVERTURNED**. The requested procedure is deemed medically necessary and authorized.
