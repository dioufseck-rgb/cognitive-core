Dear Ms. Torres,

This letter serves as the determination for your appeal regarding the prior authorization denial for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551), requested by Dr. James Kim, MD, to be performed at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center.

**Disposition: OVERTURN**

HealthFirst Health Plan has overturned the initial denial dated 2024-11-01. Authorization for the requested ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is hereby approved.

**Summary of Initial Denial:**
The initial denial for your requested procedure was based on the following reasons, citing HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2:
1.  **Physical Therapy Requirement Not Met:** Completion of only 2 weeks of physical therapy, falling short of the standard pathway's minimum 6 weeks.
2.  **Pharmacotherapy Requirement Not Met:** Only one agent trialed, failing to meet the requirement for at least two agents.

**Summary of Appeal Basis:**
Your appeal asserted that:
1.  You have severe myelomalacia on MRI, and California law prohibits the physical therapy requirement when structural neurological compromise is present.
2.  Your treating physician declared physical therapy contraindicated due to documented cord signal change.
3.  A regulatory override under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) makes the plan's criteria irrelevant.

**Review Findings and Rationale for Overturn:**
Upon review of your clinical record, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical evidence, we have determined that the initial denial was inconsistent with applicable state law and clinical standards for your condition. While your case did not meet all conservative treatment requirements as outlined in CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, these requirements are superseded by California law and clinical evidence in your specific circumstances.

**1. Clinical Presentation:**
Your clinical record confirms a diagnosis of progressive cervical myelopathy C5-C6 with myelomalacia, supported by objective findings:
*   **Symptoms:** 4-month history of bilateral hand clumsiness and gait instability (clinical_record).
*   **Neurological Exam:** Bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, positive Babinski bilateral, and gait ataxia (clinical_record).
*   **Imaging:** MRI dated 2024-08-15 demonstrates severe central cord compression at C5-C6 with T2 hyperintensity within the cord (myelomalacia), indicating existing spinal cord injury (clinical_record).

**2. Regulatory Override of Physical Therapy Requirement:**
The plan's denial based on insufficient physical therapy is overridden by California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5, which states that a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where:
*   **(a)(1) Physician Contraindication:** Your treating physician, Dr. James Kim, documented on 2024-10-15 that physical therapy was contraindicated and was causing symptom exacerbation, risking irreversible cord injury (clinical_record). You completed 2 weeks of PT, which was discontinued per physician order (clinical_record).
*   **(a)(3) Structural Neurological Compromise:** Your condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia on MRI) that physical therapy cannot address (clinical_record, CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)).
*   **(c) Mechanical Application of Criteria:** CIC § 10169.5(c) explicitly states that a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to neurological urgency under subdivision (a)(3).

Therefore, the plan's physical therapy requirement (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a), even with the myelopathy exception reducing it to 4 weeks) is legally unenforceable in your case.

**3. Regulatory and Clinical Override of Pharmacotherapy Requirement:**
The plan's denial based on the pharmacotherapy requirement (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b) requiring two agents for 4 weeks each) is also overridden by California regulatory guidance and generally accepted clinical standards:
*   **Urgent Condition:** DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 II.1 states that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated, and plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent (regulatory_document).
*   **Waiver of Conservative Treatment:** DMHC APL 22-014 II.2 specifies that for conditions where delay of treatment risks irreversible harm (as in your case with myelomalacia), plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization. The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements when the treating physician documents urgency (regulatory_document). Dr. Kim has documented that surgery is urgently indicated (clinical_record).
*   **Clinical Standard Governs:** DMHC APL 22-014 II.3 mandates that plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. Where a conflict exists, the clinical standard governs (regulatory_document).
*   **Limited Efficacy and Risk of Delay:** Generally accepted clinical guidelines indicate that conservative treatment, including pharmacotherapy, has limited efficacy for established cervical myelopathy, particularly in moderate-severe cases (clinical_evidence_document, Section 1.1, 2.1). Myelomalacia is a strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials, as delaying surgery is associated with decreased likelihood of neurological recovery (clinical_evidence_document, Section 1.2, 2.3, 3.1). Requiring a full trial of two pharmacotherapy agents for 4 weeks each in the presence of myelomalacia and progressive myelopathy would constitute an inappropriate delay and is not supported by evidence (clinical_evidence_document, Section 2.2, 3.2).

Therefore, the plan's pharmacotherapy requirement, which remains in full under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(A), is more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for your urgent condition and is overridden by DMHC APL 22-014 and clinical evidence.

**4. Medical Necessity under Clinical Standards:**
Your condition meets Tier 1 criteria for strong surgical indication per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), specifically due to moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression, myelomalacia on MRI, and progressive neurological deficits (clinical_evidence_document, Section 3.1, 3.2).

**Conclusion:**
Based on the comprehensive review, the initial denial for ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED. The plan's conservative treatment requirements, specifically for physical therapy and pharmacotherapy, are superseded by California regulatory mandates (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014) and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice for your urgent condition of cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia.
