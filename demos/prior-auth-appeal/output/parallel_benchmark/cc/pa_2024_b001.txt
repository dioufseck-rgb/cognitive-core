The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the appeal submitted on November 10, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial of a two-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 and C6-C7. 

After a comprehensive review of the clinical documentation, the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria, California regulatory standards, and generally accepted clinical guidelines, we have made the following determination:

DISPOSITION: PARTIAL OVERTURN
1. C5-C6 Level (CPT 22551): OVERTURNED (APPROVED). The prior authorization denial for the C5-C6 level is reversed, and this level is approved as medically necessary.
2. C6-C7 Level (CPT 22552 / second level): UPHELD (DENIED). The prior authorization denial for the C6-C7 level is confirmed, and this level remains denied as not medically necessary.

A detailed explanation of this decision, including specific clinical, regulatory, and guideline citations, is provided below.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CLINICAL SUMMARY & FINDINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Ms. Linda Park is a 45-year-old female with a history of right-sided C6 cervical radiculopathy. 
- Neurological Examination (dated September 5, 2024): Documents right C6 dermatomal numbness and tingling, a diminished right bicep reflex, and right grip strength of 4/5. There are no documented C7 neurological findings, no bilateral findings, and no upper motor neuron signs.
- Cervical Spine MRI (dated September 1, 2024): 
  * C5-C6: Demonstrates a large disc herniation with moderate-to-severe nerve root compression and moderate foraminal stenosis, which directly correlates with the patient's right C6 radicular symptoms.
  * C6-C7: Demonstrates a mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing, with no cord contact and no identifiable nerve root compression.
- Conservative Treatment: The clinical record documents completion of:
  * Physical Therapy: 8 weeks (24 sessions) across two providers, directed at the cervical spine, with persistent C6 radicular symptoms.
  * Pharmacotherapy: Gabapentin 600mg TID for 4 months (partial relief) and Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks (minimal benefit).
  * Interventional Treatment: A right C6 epidural steroid injection on October 1, 2024, which provided 50% temporary relief of right C6 symptoms, with symptoms fully returning by week 4.
- Surgeon's Recommendation: Dr. James Wilson recommended a two-level ACDF to address the C5-C6 pathology and to prophylactically fuse the C6-C7 level to prevent adjacent segment disease, citing a 25% 5-year incidence of adjacent segment disease after single-level ACDF.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DETAILED ANALYSIS BY SURGICAL LEVEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

I. C5-C6 LEVEL — OVERTURNED (APPROVED)

A. Plan Criteria Compliance (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)
The clinical record fully satisfies all medical necessity criteria outlined in HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria Document CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2:
1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1(a)): The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy, supported by a neurological exam showing right C6 dermatomal numbness, diminished right bicep reflex, and 4/5 grip strength, and an MRI within 6 months demonstrating structural pathology (large C5-C6 disc herniation with moderate-severe nerve root compression) consistent with these symptoms.
2. Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2):
   - Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): The member completed 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy (exceeding the 6-week minimum) with documented persistent symptoms.
   - Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): The member completed trials of two covered agents for at least 4 weeks each (Gabapentin for 4 months and Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks).
   - Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): The member underwent a right C6 epidural steroid injection on October 1, 2024 (meeting the minimum of 1 injection).
3. Imaging Requirement (Section 2.3): The MRI dated September 1, 2024, demonstrates structural pathology at C5-C6 consistent with the C6 radicular symptoms.
4. Physician Documentation (Section 2.4): Dr. Wilson provided the necessary clinical rationale and documentation of functional limitations.

B. California Regulatory Standards
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(a), "medically necessary" services include those that a physician, exercising prudent clinical judgment, would provide to treat an illness or its symptoms in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice. The C5-C6 ACDF is clinically appropriate, effective, and not primarily for convenience, fully satisfying the statutory definition of medical necessity.

C. Clinical Evidence Base
Generally accepted clinical standards, including the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Clinical Guidelines (2020), classify cervical radiculopathy with persistent symptoms and correlating MRI findings after an adequate conservative trial as a Tier 2 "Reasonable Indication" for surgical decompression and fusion. 

Therefore, the prior authorization denial for the C5-C6 level is OVERTURNED.

---

II. C6-C7 LEVEL — UPHELD (DENIED)

A. Plan Criteria Non-Compliance (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)
The clinical record fails to satisfy the medical necessity criteria for the C6-C7 level under CC-SPINE-2024:
1. Diagnosis and Imaging Correlation (Section 2.1 & 2.3): There is no documented diagnosis of C7 radiculopathy or myelopathy. The neurological exam dated September 5, 2024, explicitly notes "No C7 findings documented."
2. Imaging Pathology (Section 2.3 & Exclusion 3.3): Section 2.3 requires that imaging demonstrate structural pathology consistent with the proposed surgical level. The MRI shows only a "mild disc bulge" and "minimal foraminal narrowing" at C6-C7, with "no nerve root compression identifiable." Under Section 3.3, authorization must be denied when imaging does not demonstrate pathology at the proposed surgical level consistent with the clinical presentation.
3. Prophylactic Fusion: Dr. Wilson's rationale for fusing C6-C7 is prophylactic stabilization to prevent adjacent segment disease. The plan's criteria do not provide coverage for prophylactic fusion of asymptomatic levels with minimal degenerative changes.

B. California Regulatory Standards
Under CHSC § 1374.32(a)(2), a service must be "clinically appropriate, in terms of type, frequency, extent, site, and duration" to be deemed medically necessary. Fusing an asymptomatic spinal level (C6-C7) with no correlating clinical symptoms or significant imaging pathology is not clinically appropriate and does not meet the statutory standard of medical necessity.

C. Clinical Evidence Base
According to the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023) and NASS Guidelines (2020), surgical intervention is not indicated for imaging findings without correlating symptoms (Tier 3: Surgery Not Indicated). While adjacent segment disease is a recognized long-term risk of fusion, clinical evidence does not support prophylactic fusion of an adjacent level that lacks significant structural pathology, cord/nerve compression, or correlating clinical symptoms. Doing so unnecessarily increases surgical morbidity, blood loss, and adjacent segment stress at the next level (C7-T1) without proven clinical benefit.

Therefore, the prior authorization denial for the C6-C7 level is UPHELD.

PROCEDURAL DEFECT IN INITIAL DENIAL NOTICE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pursuant to California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a)(2), a health plan's denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the denial. The initial denial notice dated October 28, 2024, failed to cite the specific plan document or sections (HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024) used to make the determination. Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied is procedurally defective. While this defect does not alter the clinical medical necessity determination for the C6-C7 level, HealthFirst acknowledges this administrative error and has corrected it in this appeal determination by providing full citations to CC-SPINE-2024.
