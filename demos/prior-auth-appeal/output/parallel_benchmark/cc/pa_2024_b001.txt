I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION & BACKGROUND
Ms. Linda Park is a 45-year-old female with a presenting diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy at C5-C6 with adjacent C6-C7 degenerative disease. Clinical records demonstrate a history of right C6 radicular symptoms, including right C6 dermatomal numbness and tingling, a diminished right bicep reflex, and right grip strength of 4/5 documented on neurological examination dated September 5, 2024.

An MRI of the cervical spine dated September 1, 2024, revealed a large disc herniation at C5-C6 causing moderate-to-severe nerve root compression and moderate foraminal stenosis, which directly correlates with the member's right C6 radicular symptoms. At the C6-C7 level, the MRI demonstrated a mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing, with no cord contact and no identifiable nerve root compression. No C7 dermatomal symptoms, C7 reflex changes, or other clinical findings attributable to the C6-C7 level have been documented.

Conservative treatments completed include:
1. Physical Therapy: 8 weeks (24 sessions) across two providers, with persistent C6 radicular symptoms.
2. Pharmacotherapy: Gabapentin 600mg TID for 4 months (providing partial relief) and Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks (providing minimal benefit).
3. Interventional Treatment: A right C6 epidural steroid injection on October 1, 2024, which provided 50% temporary relief of right C6 symptoms that fully returned by week 4.

The requesting surgeon, Dr. James Wilson, MD, recommended a two-level ACDF to address the C5-C6 pathology and to prophylactically stabilize the C6-C7 level to prevent adjacent segment disease, noting a 25% 5-year incidence of adjacent segment disease after single-level ACDF.

II. PROCEDURAL REVIEW & REGULATORY COMPLIANCE
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a)(2), a health plan's denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reason for the denial. The initial denial notice issued on October 28, 2024, failed to cite the specific sections of the plan's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) utilized to make the determination. Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied is procedurally defective.

While this procedural defect is noted for the record, this appeal review conducts a de novo substantive evaluation of the clinical record against both the plan's clinical coverage criteria and generally accepted standards of medical practice, as mandated by California regulatory standards.

III. CLINICAL ANALYSIS: C5-C6 LEVEL (OVERTURNED / APPROVED)
The request for ACDF at the C5-C6 level satisfies all clinical coverage criteria under HealthFirst Health Plan Clinical Coverage Criteria Document CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2:

1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1): The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with correlating right C6 dermatomal numbness, tingling, diminished bicep reflex, and 4/5 grip strength. The MRI dated September 1, 2024 (within 6 months of the request) demonstrates structural pathology (large C5-C6 disc herniation with moderate-to-severe nerve root compression) consistent with these symptoms.
2. Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2):
   - Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): The member completed 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy (exceeding the 6-week minimum requirement) directed at the cervical condition.
   - Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): The member completed trials of two qualifying agents for at least 4 weeks each (Gabapentin for 4 months and Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks).
   - Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): The member underwent a right C6 epidural steroid injection on October 1, 2024.
3. Imaging Requirement (Section 2.3): The MRI dated September 1, 2024, demonstrates structural pathology at C5-C6 consistent with the proposed surgical level and symptoms.
4. Physician Documentation (Section 2.4): Dr. Wilson provided the required clinical rationale, functional limitations, and risk discussion.

Furthermore, under California's Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard (CHSC § 1374.32(a)), a service is medically necessary if it is in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice and clinically appropriate. Generally accepted clinical standards, as outlined in the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines for Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update) and NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), classify ACDF for radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after an adequate conservative trial (6-12 weeks) as a Tier 2 "Reasonable Indication."

Because the C5-C6 level meets all plan criteria and generally accepted clinical standards, the prior denial for the C5-C6 level is OVERTURNED, and this level is APPROVED.

IV. CLINICAL ANALYSIS: C6-C7 LEVEL (UPHELD / DENIED)
The request for ACDF at the C6-C7 level does not meet the plan's clinical coverage criteria or generally accepted standards of medical practice:

1. Imaging and Symptom Mismatch (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.3 & Section 3.3): Section 2.3 requires that current imaging demonstrate structural pathology consistent with the member's symptoms and the proposed surgical level. Section 3.3 mandates denial when imaging does not demonstrate pathology at the proposed surgical level consistent with the clinical presentation. The MRI dated September 1, 2024, shows only a "mild disc bulge" and "minimal foraminal narrowing" at C6-C7, with "no cord contact" and "no nerve root compression identifiable." There are no documented C7 radicular symptoms (such as pain, numbness, or weakness in a C7 distribution) or correlating neurological deficits on exam.
2. Prophylactic Fusion and Medical Necessity (CHSC § 1374.32(a)): Dr. Wilson's clinical rationale for fusing the C6-C7 level is prophylactic stabilization to prevent adjacent segment disease. Under California's statutory definition of medical necessity (CHSC § 1374.32(a)), health care services must be clinically appropriate and "not primarily for the convenience of the patient, treating physician, or other provider." Generally accepted clinical standards, including the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) and NASS Guidelines (2020), do not support prophylactic fusion of an asymptomatic adjacent level with minimal degenerative changes. Fusing an asymptomatic level solely to prevent potential future adjacent segment disease does not meet the threshold of medical necessity. Under Section 3.1 (Tier 3) of the Clinical Reference Summary, surgical intervention is "not indicated" for imaging findings without correlating symptoms.

Because there is no correlating clinical pathology or symptomatology at C6-C7, and prophylactic fusion does not meet the standard of medical necessity, the denial of the C6-C7 level is UPHELD.
