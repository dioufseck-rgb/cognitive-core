HealthFirst Health Plan has completed a comprehensive review of your appeal, including all submitted clinical documentation, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria, relevant California regulatory provisions, and generally accepted clinical guidelines.\n\nDisposition: UPHOLD DENIAL\n\nBased on this thorough review, HealthFirst Health Plan upholds the initial denial of prior authorization for the requested ACDF C5-C6 procedure. The requested service does not meet HealthFirst's medical necessity criteria or generally accepted standards of medical practice for surgical intervention at this time.\n\nBackground of Request and Initial Denial:\nOn October 15, 2024, a prior authorization request for ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) was submitted for Ms. Stephanie Oduya. The request was denied following an independent clinical review by Dr. Maria Rivera, a board-certified neurosurgeon, who determined that imaging showed mild-moderate disc herniation with no cord compression and no neurological deficit on examination, and that the conservative treatment pathway had not been completed. The initial denial stated the request did not meet medical necessity criteria for surgical intervention at this time (Denial Info, Case Record).\n\nSummary of Appeal Basis:\nDr. Patrick Walsh, the treating surgeon, submitted an appeal on your behalf, disputing Dr. Rivera's interpretation of the clinical findings. Dr. Walsh emphasized his 25 years of spine surgery experience, his direct examination of the patient, and asserted that you have significant cervical pathology requiring immediate surgery to prevent permanent disability. He stated that his clinical judgment should take precedence over a remote reviewer (Appeal Basis, Clinical Record: Treating Surgeon Statement).\n\nDetailed Review and Rationale for Upholding Denial:\n\n1.  Clinical Presentation and Diagnosis:\n    *   Your presenting diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy C5-C6, right-sided (Clinical Record: Presenting Diagnosis).\n    *   The MRI from September 10, 2024, shows C5-C6 mild to moderate disc herniation with right C6 nerve root contact without measurable compression. Crucially, the MRI explicitly states \"No cord contact. No cord compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia\" (Clinical Record: Imaging).\n    *   Your neurological examination on September 15, 2024, revealed right shoulder and neck pain (6/10) and mild right C6 dermatomal tingling. However, there was \"No weakness. No UMN signs. Functional assessment: working full-time, no activity limitations\" (Clinical Record: Neurological Exam).\n    *   This clinical picture is consistent with cervical radiculopathy, which involves compression of an individual nerve root, typically presenting with unilateral or dermatomal symptoms. It does not meet the diagnostic criteria for cervical myelopathy, which would involve spinal cord compression, bilateral symptoms, upper motor neuron signs (e.g., hyperreflexia, Hoffman sign), or myelomalacia on MRI (Clinical Evidence Document: Section 1.1, 1.2; Classify Clinical Presentation). The absence of myelomalacia and cord compression is a critical distinction, as myelopathy often warrants more urgent surgical consideration (Clinical Evidence Document: Section 1.2).\n\n2.  HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):\n    *   HealthFirst's criteria for cervical spinal surgery (CPT 22551) require that ALL criteria in Section 2 be satisfied (Plan Criteria Document: Section 2).\n    *   Diagnosis Requirement (2.1): While you have a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (2.1a), the imaging and neurological exam do not demonstrate an \"objective neurological deficit\" as required for cervical disc herniation (2.1c) that would necessitate immediate surgical intervention (Clinical Record: Imaging, Neurological Exam; Plan Criteria Document: Section 2.1).\n    *   Conservative Treatment Requirement (2.2): This requirement mandates a documented course of conservative treatment, including:\n        *   Physical Therapy (2.2a): A minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. Your record indicates only 4 weeks of physical therapy (12 sessions) with mild improvement in pain and no neurological deficit to track (Clinical Record: Conservative Treatment; Plan Criteria Document: Section 2.2a). This does not meet the 6-week minimum.\n        *   Pharmacotherapy (2.2b): A trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. Your record shows a trial of only one agent, Naproxen 500mg BID for 8 weeks, which provided moderate pain relief (Clinical Record: Conservative Treatment; Plan Criteria Document: Section 2.2b). This does not meet the requirement for two agents.\n        *   Interventional Treatment (2.2c): One interventional treatment (e.g., epidural steroid injection). Your record indicates no interventional treatments were performed (Clinical Record: Conservative Treatment; Plan Criteria Document: Section 2.2c).\n    *   The Myelopathy Pathway Exception (2.2(A)) does not apply, as your primary diagnosis is radiculopathy, not myelopathy (Classify Clinical Presentation; Plan Criteria Document: Section 2.2(A) Note).\n    *   Therefore, the conservative treatment requirements outlined in Section 2.2 of HealthFirst's criteria have not been met (Investigate Plan Criteria Application).\n\n3.  Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:\n    *   Major spine societies, including AANS/CNS, NASS, and AO Spine International, provide guidelines for ACDF indications (Clinical Evidence Document: Section 3.1).\n    *   Your clinical presentation, characterized by mild to moderate disc herniation, nerve root contact without measurable compression, mild dermatomal tingling, and no objective weakness or upper motor neuron signs, falls under TIER 2 (Reasonable Indication) or TIER 3 (Surgery Not Indicated) criteria for ACDF (CPT 22551) (Clinical Evidence Document: Section 3.1).\n    *   Specifically, Tier 2 includes \"Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx\" or \"Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care.\" Tier 3 includes \"Mild radiculopathy not yet trialed on conservative treatment\" (Clinical Evidence Document: Section 3.1). Given the incomplete conservative trial, your condition aligns more closely with Tier 3 at this stage.\n    *   Your current presentation does not meet TIER 1 (Strong Indication) criteria, which would require moderate to severe myelopathy, progressive neurological deficit, myelomalacia on MRI, or acute severe myelopathy (Clinical Evidence Document: Section 3.1). As noted, your MRI shows no myelomalacia or cord compression, and your neurological exam shows no weakness or UMN signs.\n    *   The clinical evidence base emphasizes that conservative treatment is typically effective for cervical radiculopathy (70-80% resolution) and that the 6-week PT requirement in many payer policies derives from radiculopathy evidence (Clinical Evidence Document: Section 1.1, 2.2). Your 4 weeks of PT and single pharmacotherapy agent are insufficient to meet these generally accepted standards for a full conservative trial for radiculopathy (Investigate Clinical Evidence Standard).\n\n4.  California Regulatory Compliance:\n    *   The initial denial notice was procedurally adequate, specifying the reason code (MED-003) and clinical reasons for denial, including imaging findings and incomplete conservative treatment, and provided notice of IMR rights (Denial Info; Regulatory Document: CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2); Investigate Regulatory Compliance).\n    *   Regarding Dr. Walsh's appeal points: While HealthFirst respects the experience and clinical judgment of treating physicians, medical necessity determinations must be based on objective clinical findings and established criteria. Dr. Walsh's forceful declaration and experience do not override the objective MRI findings (no cord compression, no myelomalacia) or the neurological exam (no weakness, no UMN signs, full functional capacity) (Case Record: Treating Surgeon Statement; Case Record: Failure Mode Description, Correct Approach).\n    *   The conditions for waiving physical therapy requirements under CIC § 10169.5 do not apply in this case. There is no documentation that physical therapy is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit you (a)(1), nor that you have reached a functional plateau as defined by the clinical standard (a)(2) (e.g., less than 10% improvement over 3 sessions, or therapist documentation of maximum benefit, especially given only 4 weeks of PT) (Clinical Record: Conservative Treatment; Regulatory Document: CIC § 10169.5; Clinical Evidence Document: Section 5). Most importantly, your condition does not involve \"objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address\" (a)(3), as there is no cord compression or myelomalacia (Clinical Record: Imaging; Regulatory Document: CIC § 10169.5).\n    *   Your condition does not meet the criteria for an urgent condition under DMHC APL 22-014, as there is no objective evidence of cord compression with myelomalacia or progressive pathology on serial imaging (Clinical Record: Imaging; Regulatory Document: DMHC APL 22-014, II.1, II.2).\n\nConclusion:\nThe objective clinical evidence, including your MRI and neurological examination, consistently indicates cervical radiculopathy without signs of spinal cord compression or myelomalacia. Your conservative treatment course, consisting of 4 weeks of physical therapy and one pharmacotherapy agent, does not meet HealthFirst's established medical necessity criteria or generally accepted clinical guidelines for surgical intervention at this time. The regulatory framework supports the application of these criteria. Therefore, the prior authorization denial for ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is upheld.\n\nIndependent Medical Review (IMR) Rights Notice:\nYou have the right to request an Independent Medical Review (IMR) of this decision.
