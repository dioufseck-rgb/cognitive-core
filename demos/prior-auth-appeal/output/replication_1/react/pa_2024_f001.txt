**I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

This appeal concerns the denial of prior authorization for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C6-C7 for member Dorothy James. The initial denial was based on the administrative reason that physical therapy (PT) records from a second provider were not received, thus failing to verify the claimed 8 weeks of treatment and concluding the PT requirement was not met.

Upon review of the submitted appeal, which included the previously missing records from Mountain Physical Therapy, it is confirmed that the member completed a total of 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy across two providers (Valley Physical Therapy and Mountain Physical Therapy). The documentation further confirms that the member reached a functional plateau, with no improvement in neurological symptoms, at the conclusion of this therapy.

Applying the hierarchy of authority (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), the denial for insufficient physical therapy is not sustainable. California state law (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)) explicitly prohibits plans from denying a surgical procedure if the member has completed a course of physical therapy and reached a functional plateau. Furthermore, the member has met all other conservative treatment requirements of the HealthFirst plan criteria.

Therefore, the initial denial is **OVERTURNED**.

---

**II. REASON FOR DENIAL (INITIAL)**

The initial denial, dated 2024-11-02, cited reason code MED-003, stating: "Physical therapy requirement not met. Plan received PT records from Valley Physical Therapy documenting 4 weeks of treatment. Member claims 8 total weeks but second provider (Mountain PT) records not received by plan. Cannot verify claimed treatment without complete records."

**III. APPEAL BASIS**

The appeal submitted the missing records from Mountain Physical Therapy, confirming an additional 4 weeks of treatment, bringing the total to 8 weeks of PT. The appeal asserted that 8 weeks of PT were completed across two providers and that the records were submitted with the initial request but may not have been received by the plan.

**V. APPEAL REVIEW AND RATIONALE**

This review applies the three-source hierarchy: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.

**A. Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024) Application:**

1.  **Diagnosis (Section 2.1):** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy C6-C7 (bilateral) with objective neurological deficit (bilateral tricep weakness, diminished reflex, finger tingling) and imaging demonstrating structural pathology (C6-C7 large disc herniation, bilateral C7 nerve root compression, bilateral foraminal stenosis). This meets plan criterion 2.1(a) or 2.1(c).
2.  **Conservative Treatment (Section 2.2):**
    *   **Physical Therapy (2.2(a)):** The plan requires a "minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy with documented functional outcomes." The member completed 8 weeks of PT, which exceeds this minimum. The documentation from both PT providers clearly indicates that the member reached a functional plateau, with no further improvement, particularly in neurological symptoms. This explicitly satisfies the plan's implicit expectation for "functional outcomes."
    *   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2(b)):** The plan requires "trial of at least two... for minimum 4 weeks each." The member completed a 3-month trial of Pregabalin and a 2-month trial of Naproxen, meeting this requirement.
    *   **Interventional Treatment (2.2(c)):** The plan requires "one of the following interventional treatments." The member received a bilateral C7 epidural steroid injection, meeting this requirement.

**Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** Based on the submitted records, the member has fully met all conservative treatment requirements as outlined in HealthFirst's own clinical coverage criteria for standard cervical spine procedures. The administrative reason for the initial denial (missing records) has been resolved.

**B. California Regulatory Framework Application:**

1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2) — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This state law explicitly states: "A health insurer or disability insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
    *   **Application:** The member completed 8 weeks of physical therapy, and the physical therapists' notes explicitly document a "functional plateau" and "no improvement in neurological symptoms." This directly invokes and is covered by CIC § 10169.5(a)(2). As per this state law, the plan is prohibited from denying based on physical therapy requirements once a functional plateau has been reached.

2.  **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) — Standard of Review for IMR:** This code stipulates that the IMR standard (which guides appeal review) "does NOT require that the service be consistent with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the service meets the standard in subdivision (a), the reviewer SHALL recommend approval regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive."
    *   **Application:** This reinforces that even if there were perceived discrepancies with plan criteria, the overriding standard is generally accepted medical practice, which the member's condition and proposed treatment align with (as detailed below).

3.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 — Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions (Section II.3):** This guidance states: "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for conditions identified in this guidance."
    *   **Application:** While the primary diagnosis is listed as "radiculopathy," the member's presentation of *bilateral* neurological deficits (weakness, diminished reflexes) and MRI findings of "mild cord contact" are highly concerning and suggestive of a condition approaching myelopathy, even if not fully meeting all plan criteria for "primary myelopathy." This APL emphasizes that overly restrictive plan criteria are superseded by generally accepted standards, especially for neurological conditions where delay risks harm.

**C. Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) Application:**

1.  **Distinction between Radiculopathy and Myelopathy (Section 1.1):** The clinical standards highlight that myelopathy and radiculopathy require different treatment algorithms. While the member's primary diagnosis is radiculopathy, the *bilateral* nature of symptoms (weakness, diminished reflexes) coupled with "mild cord contact" on MRI suggests a more severe presentation, potentially with spinal cord involvement, which makes conservative treatment less effective.
2.  **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy/Severe Neurological Deficits (Section 2.1, 2.2):** Clinical guidelines (AO Spine 2021, AANS/CNS 2023) state there is "no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy." They caution that delaying surgery may result in neurological deterioration. While the patient doesn't have confirmed myelomalacia, the *persistent bilateral neurological deficits* despite conservative care, including 8 weeks of PT to a plateau, align with a condition where further PT is unlikely to provide benefit and surgical intervention is indicated.
3.  **Surgical Indications (AANS/CNS 2023, Section 3.1):** The member's presentation of persistent bilateral neurological deficits after extensive and failed conservative treatment (8 weeks PT to plateau, two medications, one injection) meets Tier 2 criteria for surgical indication for radiculopathy ("Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care"). Given the bilateral nature and cord contact, the urgency is elevated.

**D. Conflict Resolution (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria):**

The plan's initial denial was based on an administrative issue (missing PT records) which has been resolved by the appeal. With the complete documentation, the member has met and exceeded the plan's 6-week PT requirement. More importantly, the documentation clearly shows that the member reached a functional plateau after 8 weeks of PT.

California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2) definitively states that a plan "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau." This state law directly overrides any attempt by the plan to deny on the basis of PT requirements, regardless of whether HealthFirst's criteria were interpreted to require more PT or to deny based on lack of "completion" beyond the plateau.

Furthermore, the clinical evidence supports surgical intervention given the persistent bilateral neurological deficits unresponsive to conservative care, and the clinical standards emphasize that prolonged PT is not indicated in such cases, especially when there are signs of potential cord involvement.

**VI. DISPOSITION**

**OVERTURN**

**VII. WARRANT**

The initial denial was predicated on incomplete physical therapy (PT) documentation. The appeal successfully resolved this administrative issue by providing the missing records, confirming that the member, Dorothy James, completed a total of 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy across two providers. This duration exceeds HealthFirst's plan criterion for a "minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy" (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a)).

Crucially, the clinical record clearly documents that the member reached a "functional plateau" during physical therapy, with "no improvement in neurological symptoms" at discharge from the second PT provider (Clinical record: physical_therapy_provider_2, outcome_note). Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2), a health plan "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau." This state law takes precedence over plan criteria and mandates approval, as further PT cannot be required as a basis for denial.

Moreover, the member has fully met the plan's criteria for pharmacotherapy (Pregabalin 3 months, Naproxen 2 months) and interventional treatment (bilateral C7 epidural injection) (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(b) and 2.2(c)). The member's persistent bilateral neurological deficits (bilateral C7 weakness 4/5, diminished bilateral tricep reflex, bilateral finger tingling) and MRI findings of "mild cord contact without compression", despite comprehensive conservative treatment to a documented plateau, align with generally accepted standards of medical practice for surgical intervention (AANS/CNS 2023, Section 3.1; AO Spine 2021, Section 2.1). Clinical guidelines advise against delaying surgery for conditions with persistent neurological compromise that has failed conservative care, especially when PT has reached a functional plateau (AANS/CNS 2023, Section 2.2; AO Spine 2021, Section 2.1).

Therefore, the requested ACDF C6-C7 is medically necessary and should be authorized, as the member has fulfilled all applicable requirements under HealthFirst's criteria and, where those criteria might conflict, state law and generally accepted clinical standards dictate approval.
