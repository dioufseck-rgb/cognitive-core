**APPEAL DETERMINATION: OVERTURNED**

**WARRANT:**
The denial for the requested ACDF C4-C5 for Helen Brooks is hereby **OVERTURNED**. The requested procedure is medically necessary and meets the criteria for approval based on a comprehensive review applying the hierarchy of State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria. The plan's initial denial incorrectly applied its own criteria and failed to comply with California state regulations and generally accepted clinical standards for cervical myelopathy.

**ANALYSIS:**

**1. Primary Clinical Presentation: Cervical Myelopathy**
The clinical record clearly indicates that Helen Brooks presents with cervical myelopathy. Key findings include:
*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Early cervical myelopathy.
*   **Neurological Exam:** Bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia in upper extremities, and subtle balance impairment on tandem gait (2024-10-05). These are objective Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) signs characteristic of cervical myelopathy.
*   **Imaging:** MRI demonstrates C4-C5 disc herniation with cord contact (compression). While the significance of T2 signal change (myelomalacia) is disputed by the plan's radiologist, a second independent radiologist (from the treating hospital) confirmed "early myelomalacia" with reproducible T2 hyperintensity representing "early cord signal change, not artifact," consistent with early myelopathy. Crucially, the plan's own criteria for "Cervical myelopathy" (Section 2.1(b)) require "spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms," which are demonstrably present (cord compression + UMN signs).

**2. Application of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
The initial denial incorrectly asserted that the "Myelopathy exception does not apply."
*   **Diagnosis (Section 2.1(b)):** The member meets the plan's definition of cervical myelopathy due to spinal cord compression and confirmed myelopathic symptoms (UMN signs and gait disturbance). **MET.**
*   **Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2(A)):** This exception modifies conservative treatment for confirmed myelopathy when it is the primary diagnosis.
    *   **Primary Diagnosis:** Given the UMN signs, gait impairment, and explicit documentation by the treating physician, myelopathy is clearly the primary diagnosis.
    *   **Physical Therapy:** The plan's exception reduces PT to 4 weeks minimum. The member completed 8 weeks of PT. **MET.**
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** The member completed a trial of Gabapentin (3 months) and Cyclobenzaprine (6 weeks), meeting the requirement for two agents for at least 4 weeks each. **MET.**
    *   **Interventional Treatment:** The exception waives this requirement if the treating physician documents risk of neurological deterioration. The treating physician explicitly documented, "did not refer for epidural due to myelopathy concern — epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated in cord compression." This constitutes valid documentation of risk. **MET (with waiver).**
*   **Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The plan's initial denial was based on a flawed interpretation of the myelopathy exception, stating it "does not apply." However, all conditions for the myelopathy exception, including physician documentation of risk for interventional procedures, were met according to the plan's own policy.

**3. Application of California Regulatory Requirements:**
California state law and regulations provide further basis for overturning the denial:
*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Prerequisite):** This statute prohibits plans from conditioning approval of surgery on PT completion where:
    *   (a)(2) "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." The member completed 8 weeks of PT with "No improvement in UMN signs," indicating a functional plateau for the myelopathic symptoms that PT cannot address.
    *   (a)(3) "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Helen Brooks has documented cord compression on MRI and objective UMN signs, which constitutes structural neurological compromise. The presence of myelomalacia, even if disputed by the plan's radiologist, reinforces this.
    *   **Conclusion:** CIC § 10169.5 overrides any plan requirement for further PT, as the member has reached a functional plateau and has structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address.
*   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This All-Plan Letter specifically addresses prior authorization for myelopathy.
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** States that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm. While the plan's radiologist disputed myelomalacia, another radiologist confirmed it, and objective cord compression is undisputed.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** Mandates that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia), the treating physician documents conservative failure, and surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. All these conditions are met.
    *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** Requires that plans "shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice."
    *   **Conclusion:** DMHC APL 22-014 directly supports waiving the plan's standard conservative treatment requirements for this patient's myelopathy, particularly the interventional procedure component, which is not clinically indicated or beneficial for myelopathy with cord compression.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This section confirms that services meeting "generally accepted standards of medical practice" SHALL be approved regardless of more restrictive plan internal criteria.

**4. Application of Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
Generally accepted clinical guidelines strongly support surgical intervention for Helen Brooks:
*   **Distinction between Myelopathy and Radiculopathy (Section 1.1):** The guidelines emphasize that myelopathy requires fundamentally different treatment algorithms, and radiculopathy-derived conservative treatment requirements are not applicable to myelopathy.
*   **Myelomalacia Significance (Section 1.2):** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) indicates existing cord injury and is a strong predictor of permanent deficit with delayed surgery, warranting surgical urgency. Two of the three radiological opinions support its presence, as does the treating physician.
*   **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy (Section 2.1 & 2.2):** Clinical guidelines (AO Spine 2021) state there is "no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery... is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." Interventional procedures like epidural steroid injections are not indicated for myelopathy and may be contraindicated in cord compression.
*   **Risk of Delay (Section 2.3):** Delay in surgical treatment for moderate-severe myelopathy is associated with decreased neurological recovery.
*   **Surgical Indications (Section 3.1):** Helen Brooks meets TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION criteria for ACDF, including:
    *   (a) Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI.
    *   (c) Myelomalacia on MRI (as confirmed by the second radiologist and treating physician).
*   **Conclusion:** Clinical standards overwhelmingly indicate that surgical intervention is strongly recommended for Helen Brooks' condition, and further mandatory conservative treatment, especially interventional procedures, is not supported by evidence and may be detrimental.

**5. Conflict Resolution:**
The plan's denial, predicated on a contested radiological interpretation and failure to apply its own myelopathy exception, is superseded by both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards. State law (CIC §10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) explicitly waives conservative treatment requirements for myelopathy with structural neurological compromise. Clinical standards confirm the urgency and surgical indication for Helen Brooks' presentation and strongly advise against delaying surgery for further conservative care.

**Conclusion:**
Helen Brooks clearly presents with cervical myelopathy with objective UMN signs and cord compression. She has fulfilled or is exempt from all conservative treatment requirements based on the plan's own myelopathy exception (which was inappropriately denied), state law, and generally accepted clinical standards. Surgical intervention is medically necessary and strongly indicated.
