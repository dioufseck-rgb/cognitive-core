**DISPOSITION: OVERTURN**

**Decision Rationale:**

Upon comprehensive review of the member's clinical record, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, the denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C4-C5) for Helen Brooks is hereby **OVERTURNED**. The requested service is determined to be medically necessary.

The denial was based on the assertion that the myelopathy exception did not apply and that an interventional procedure, required by the standard conservative pathway, was not completed. This determination is flawed for the following reasons, applying the hierarchy of authority: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.

**1. Clinical Presentation Summary:**
Helen Brooks, a 49-year-old female, presents with objective clinical signs of cervical myelopathy, including bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia in the upper extremities, and subtle balance impairment on tandem gait (Clinical Record: Neurological Exam, 2024-10-05). Imaging (MRI) from 2024-10-01 shows C4-C5 disc herniation with cord contact. A second radiologist's opinion (2024-10-15) confirmed C4-C5 cord compression with *early myelomalacia*, noting T2 hyperintensity reproducible on multiple sequences, consistent with early cord signal change. The treating physician has documented myelopathy on clinical grounds and has attempted conservative treatment including 8 weeks of physical therapy (24 sessions) with no improvement in UMN signs, and a trial of two pharmacotherapy agents (Gabapentin for 3 months, Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks) with partial to minimal benefit. Interventional procedures were not pursued due to the treating physician's documented concern regarding epidural steroid injections being relatively contraindicated in the presence of cord compression and myelopathy.

**2. Analysis Against HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

*   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** **MET.** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, satisfying criterion 2.1(b) ("spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms [gait disturbance, bilateral upper extremity weakness, hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign]"). The neurological exam and imaging confirm this.
*   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:** The plan's denial stated the myelopathy exception did not apply due to the plan radiologist's interpretation of the T2 signal, and that an interventional procedure was not completed. This constitutes a misapplication of HealthFirst's own criteria:
    *   **Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A):** This exception applies "For members with confirmed cervical myelopathy (criterion 2.1(b))." As established, Ms. Brooks *does* meet 2.1(b). The plan's denial incorrectly disputes the application of the myelopathy exception based on the *significance* of T2 signal change, but the plan's policy for myelopathy only requires the symptoms and cord compression, which are clearly present.
    *   **Interventional Treatment Waiver:** Exception 2.2(A) states the "Interventional treatment requirement (2.2(c)) is WAIVED if treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses risk of neurological deterioration." The treating physician explicitly documented that epidural steroid injections were not performed "due to myelopathy concern — epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated in cord compression" (Clinical Record: Conservative Treatment). This documentation *fully satisfies* the waiver condition for the interventional procedure requirement under the plan's own myelopathy exception.
    *   **PT and Pharmacotherapy:** The member completed 8 weeks of PT (exceeding the reduced 4-week minimum for myelopathy) and a trial of two pharmacotherapy agents (meeting the criteria).
    *   **Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The plan incorrectly applied its own myelopathy exception (2.2(A)). The criteria for the exception are met, and the waiver for the interventional procedure is clearly applicable based on the treating physician's documentation. Therefore, the denial is inconsistent with HealthFirst's internal policy when properly applied.

**3. Analysis Against California Regulatory Requirements (State Law):**

California state law provides further grounds for overturning the denial, as these provisions supersede plan criteria.

*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):**
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** This APL states, "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent..." While the plan's radiologist disputed the significance of myelomalacia, the second radiologist confirmed "early myelomalacia" and the clinical picture strongly supports an urgent myelopathy.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** The APL mandates that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment... when: (a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia...), AND (b) Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed... AND (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines..."
        *   (a) The MRI shows cord compression, and a second radiologist confirms early myelomalacia.
        *   (b) The treating physician documented 8 weeks of PT with "No improvement in UMN signs" and only partial/minimal benefit from pharmacotherapy, demonstrating failure for the myelopathic symptoms.
        *   (c) As detailed below, surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines.
        *   The APL specifically notes, "The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements..."
    *   **Conclusion:** The plan's requirement for an interventional procedure is in direct conflict with DMHC APL 22-014, which explicitly allows for waiver of conservative treatment requirements in urgent myelopathy cases, especially with myelomalacia and documented conservative failure.
*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):**
    *   (a)(3) states that a plan shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Ms. Brooks' cervical myelopathy with cord compression and UMN signs represents significant structural neurological compromise. While PT was completed, the principle that conservative measures cannot address the underlying structural cord compression and myelomalacia reinforces the urgency of surgical intervention and renders further non-surgical requirements medically inappropriate.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This provision clarifies that "The IMR standard does NOT require that the service be consistent with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the service meets the standard in subdivision (a), the reviewer SHALL recommend approval regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." This confirms that even if the plan's criteria were deemed valid, compliance with generally accepted medical standards would mandate approval.

**4. Analysis Against Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

Generally accepted clinical guidelines strongly support surgical intervention for this member and contradict the plan's denial reasoning.

*   **Distinction between Myelopathy and Radiculopathy (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.1):** Myelopathy and radiculopathy have fundamentally different treatment algorithms. The evidence for conservative treatment, including specific durations for PT or interventional procedures, is primarily for radiculopathy. Applying these radiculopathy-derived requirements to myelopathy patients is "NOT supported by clinical evidence." Ms. Brooks clearly has myelopathy.
*   **Significance of Myelomalacia (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2):** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) indicates existing cord injury, higher risk of permanent deficit, reduced recovery, and *surgical urgency*. It is a "strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials." The plan's internal radiologist dismissing this finding contradicts leading clinical guidelines.
*   **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2):**
    *   **PT for Myelopathy (2.1):** For moderate-severe myelopathy, "physical therapy does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement." "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery... is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration" (AO Spine Guidelines, 2021). Ms. Brooks' lack of improvement in UMN signs after 8 weeks of PT confirms this.
    *   **Conservative Treatment Duration (2.2):** "No randomized controlled trials support a defined minimum duration of conservative treatment before surgery [for myelopathy]."
    *   **Risk of Delay (2.3):** Delaying surgery for myelopathy, especially with myelomalacia, is associated with decreased neurological recovery and worse outcomes.
*   **Surgical Indications for ACDF (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.1):** Ms. Brooks meets **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION (surgery recommended)** criteria per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023):
    *   (a) Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI.
    *   (b) Progressive neurological deficit despite conservative treatment (no improvement in UMN signs).
    *   (c) Myelomalacia on MRI (confirmed by second radiologist).
    *   (d) Acute severe myelopathy with risk of permanent deficit.

**5. Conclusion based on Hierarchy of Authority:**

The plan's denial for ACDF at C4-C5 for Ms. Brooks is demonstrably flawed.
1.  **Plan Criteria Misapplication:** HealthFirst's denial directly misapplies its own myelopathy exception (2.2(A)). Ms. Brooks meets the criteria for confirmed myelopathy, and her treating physician's documentation explicitly satisfies the waiver for interventional treatment due to contraindication in cord compression.
2.  **State Law Override:** Even if the plan's criteria were applied as intended, California regulatory framework (DMHC APL 22-014 and CIC § 10169.5) explicitly overrides requirements for a full course of conservative treatment, including interventional procedures, in cases of urgent cervical myelopathy, particularly when myelomalacia is present and conservative care has failed to address neurological deficits.
3.  **Clinical Standard Override:** Generally accepted clinical standards from major spine societies (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) overwhelmingly support immediate surgical intervention for patients with Ms. Brooks' presentation (myelopathy, UMN signs, cord compression, and myelomalacia) and explicitly caution against delaying surgery for extended conservative trials, which lack evidence for efficacy in this condition. The plan's denial is inconsistent with these standards.

Therefore, pursuant to California law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, the requested ACDF C4-C5 is medically necessary for Helen Brooks.

The prior authorization denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C4-C5, one level) for Helen Brooks (PA-2024-C003) is **OVERTURNED**. Authorization for the requested procedure is hereby approved.
