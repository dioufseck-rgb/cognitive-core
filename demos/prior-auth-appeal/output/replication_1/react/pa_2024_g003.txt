**Appeal Reviewer:** [Your Name/Title, Expert Health Plan Appeals Reviewer]

### 1. Case Summary

The member, Bernard Achebe, is a 61-year-old male with a diagnosis of mild cervical myelopathy at C5-C6. Prior authorization for ACDF C5-C6 was denied by HealthFirst on 2024-11-05, citing incomplete conservative treatment, specifically 3 of 6 weeks of physical therapy, one pharmacotherapy agent instead of two, and no interventional procedure. The treating neurologist, Dr. Kwame Osei, MD, appeals this denial, declaring surgical urgency due to severe and progressive cervical cord compression, contraindication to further delay, and failure of conservative treatment to arrest neurological progression, despite the absence of myelomalacia on the MRI.

### 2. Clinical Presentation and Diagnosis

The member's clinical record supports a diagnosis of cervical myelopathy:
*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Mild cervical myelopathy C5-C6.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-10-01):** Confirms moderate central cord compression at C5-C6. Notably, there is no T2 signal change or myelomalacia present.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-10-05):** Reveals objective upper motor neuron signs consistent with myelopathy, including bilateral Hoffman signs and bilateral mild hyperreflexia. Hand grip strength is 4+/5, tandem gait is intact, and ADLs are fully intact. The mJOA score is 15/18, consistent with mild myelopathy.
*   **Treating Neurologist's Assessment:** Dr. Osei explicitly declares the need for immediate surgical intervention, stating the compression is "severe and progressive" and that "further delay is medically contraindicated" due to the risk of "irreversible neurological deterioration."

### 3. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)

1.  **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** **MET.** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (2.1(b)) supported by MRI and neurological exam findings.
2.  **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:** The denial cited non-completion of this section.
    *   **(a) Physical therapy:** Plan requires 6 weeks (standard pathway). Member completed 3 weeks. If the myelopathy exception (2.2(A)) applied, 4 weeks would be required. Member completed 3 weeks. **NOT MET by plan criteria.**
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** Plan requires two agents for 4+ weeks. Member had one agent (Baclofen) for 6 weeks. **NOT MET by plan criteria.**
    *   **(c) Interventional treatments:** Plan requires one. Member had none. **NOT MET by plan criteria.**
    *   **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY:**
        *   PT requirement is reduced to 4 weeks (member had 3). **Still NOT MET.**
        *   Interventional treatment is waived if physician documents risk. Dr. Osei did not explicitly document risk for interventional procedures, though he did document urgency and risk of delay. **NOT MET by exception's specific wording.**
        *   Pharmacotherapy requirement remains in full. **Still NOT MET.**

Based solely on HealthFirst's stated criteria, the denial aligns with the plan's internal guidelines for conservative treatment, even considering the myelopathy exception.

### 4. Application of California Regulatory Framework (State Law)

**A. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:**
This state law *directly addresses and overrides* the plan's physical therapy requirement in specific circumstances:
*   **(a)(1):** "The member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition."
    *   **Analysis:** Dr. Osei explicitly halted PT due to myelopathy concern and declared that "further delay is medically contraindicated" and risks "irreversible neurological deterioration." This strongly supports that further PT is unlikely to benefit the patient's neurological condition and/or is contraindicated.
*   **(a)(3):** "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
    *   **Analysis:** The MRI confirms "moderate central cord compression," and the neurological exam shows objective upper motor neuron signs (bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia). These constitute objective structural neurological compromise (spinal cord compression) that physical therapy cannot resolve.
*   **Conclusion on PT:** Pursuant to CIC § 10169.5, HealthFirst **cannot** condition approval of this surgical procedure on the completion of further physical therapy. The plan's denial based on incomplete physical therapy is superseded by state law.

**B. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 — Prior Authorization for Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions:**
This guidance emphasizes that plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for conditions with potential for irreversible harm, such as cervical myelopathy.
*   **II.2 — CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT EXCEPTIONS:** While this section often relies on imaging evidence of *progressive pathology* (e.g., myelomalacia, serial changes), the treating neurologist *has declared* the compression to be "severe and progressive" and conservative treatment to have failed to arrest neurological progression (as per the appeal basis). The APL generally supports waiving full conservative treatment requirements when delay risks irreversible harm and surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines.

**C. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) — Standard of Review for IMR:**
This provision states that services meeting "generally accepted standards of medical practice" SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. This sets the precedent that clinical standards can override plan criteria.

### 5. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)

1.  **Diagnosis Distinction:** The clinical references clearly distinguish cervical myelopathy from radiculopathy, noting they require fundamentally different treatment algorithms. The plan's 6-week (or even 4-week) PT requirement is primarily derived from radiculopathy evidence and is explicitly **not supported by clinical evidence for myelopathy**.
2.  **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration" (AO Spine 2021). The member's myelopathy, though mild by mJOA score, involves objective cord compression and UMN signs, which the guidelines place at a higher risk level where PT is not expected to be effective.
    *   **Risk of Delay:** Clinical standards emphasize that delaying surgery for moderate-severe myelopathy is associated with decreased likelihood of neurological recovery. Dr. Osei's declaration of urgency aligns with this.
3.  **Surgical Indications (AANS/CNS 2023):**
    *   The member's presentation of "mild myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI" and "progressive neurological deficit despite conservative treatment" (as documented by the treating neurologist in the appeal basis) meets TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION criteria for surgery.
    *   Even if considered "mild myelopathy," the presence of objective UMN signs and cord compression, coupled with the treating physician's assessment of progression and urgency, indicates that surgical intervention is a medically appropriate and generally accepted standard of care.
    *   The clinical standard does *not* require a defined minimum duration of conservative treatment (including pharmacotherapy or interventional procedures) before surgery for established cervical myelopathy, particularly when progression is noted and previous conservative efforts have not arrested the condition.

### 6. Conflict Resolution and Rationale

The HealthFirst denial is based on strict adherence to its conservative treatment criteria, including specific durations for physical therapy and multiple pharmacotherapy agents. This is in direct conflict with both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice for cervical myelopathy.

*   **Physical Therapy (PT):** California Insurance Code § 10169.5 **directly prohibits** the plan from requiring further physical therapy in this case. The treating physician documented medical contraindication to further delay and PT's unlikeliness to benefit given the neurological progression, and there is objective structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. Furthermore, clinical guidelines explicitly state that mandatory PT is not supported by evidence for myelopathy and may be harmful.
*   **Pharmacotherapy and Interventional Procedures:** While the plan's myelopathy exception retained the pharmacotherapy requirement and only conditionally waived interventional procedures, the overarching clinical standards for myelopathy dictate that conservative treatment has limited efficacy, and delaying definitive surgical treatment for an arbitrary duration of conservative measures (including medications or injections) is not supported by evidence and carries a risk of neurological deterioration. The treating physician's declaration of surgical urgency and failure of conservative treatment to arrest progression, coupled with the patient's objective myelopathic signs and cord compression, aligns with the clinical standard that surgery is indicated.

Applying the hierarchy of authority (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):
1.  **State Law (CIC § 10169.5)** unequivocally overrides the plan's physical therapy requirement.
2.  **Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine, NASS)** overwhelmingly indicate that the plan's conservative treatment requirements for myelopathy (including PT duration, number of medications, and interventional procedures) are not evidence-based, are a "category error" when applied to myelopathy, and may pose a risk to the patient by delaying necessary surgery.
3.  Therefore, the plan's internal criteria, which are more restrictive than both state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice for this condition, cannot be upheld.

### 7. Disposition

**OVERTURN**

### 8. Warrant

The denial for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) for member Bernard Achebe (PA-2024-G003) is **OVERTURNED**.

This decision is warranted based on the following:

1.  **California State Law Override of Physical Therapy Requirement:** The denial's primary reason regarding incomplete physical therapy directly conflicts with California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3). The treating neurologist explicitly documented that further delay and conservative treatment are medically contraindicated due to the progressive nature of the myelopathy and risk of irreversible neurological deterioration. Additionally, objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (moderate central cord compression, bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia) is present, which physical therapy cannot address. Therefore, the plan cannot legally condition approval on further physical therapy.
2.  **Inconsistency with Generally Accepted Clinical Standards for Myelopathy:** The member has confirmed cervical myelopathy with objective signs and moderate cord compression. Major clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) do not support mandatory or extended courses of physical therapy or other conservative treatments as prerequisites for surgical intervention in cases of established myelopathy, particularly when a treating physician notes progression and urgency. These guidelines highlight that delaying surgery for such conservative trials is not evidence-based and may lead to irreversible neurological deficits. The plan's conservative treatment requirements, therefore, are more restrictive than, and directly contradicted by, generally accepted standards of medical practice.
3.  **Medical Necessity under Clinical Guidelines:** The member's condition meets accepted criteria for surgical intervention for cervical myelopathy (AANS/CNS 2023 TIER 1 strong indication, specifically "Progressive neurological deficit despite conservative treatment"). Despite the myelopathy being classified as "mild" by mJOA score, the presence of objective cord compression and upper motor neuron signs, coupled with the treating neurologist's clinical assessment of progression and urgency, makes surgical decompression medically necessary without further delay.

The HealthFirst denial, based on its internal conservative treatment criteria, is superseded by both California state law and prevailing clinical standards of medical practice for cervical myelopathy.
