**APPEAL DETERMINATION LETTER**

**Case ID:** PA-2024-A002
**Member:** Marcus Webb (M-223344)
**Procedure:** Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C6-C7, one level (CPT 22551)
**Date of Initial Denial:** 2024-11-05
**Reviewer:** Expert Appeals Reviewer
**Date of Appeal Determination:** 2024-11-15

**Dear Mr. Webb, HealthFirst Plan 77441, and Dr. Chen,**

This letter serves as the appeal determination for the prior authorization request for an ACDF C6-C7 for Mr. Marcus Webb. We have thoroughly reviewed the provided clinical documentation, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), relevant California state laws, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.

**Summary of Initial Denial:**
The initial denial (MED-003) stated that Mr. Webb had completed 4 weeks of physical therapy, falling short of the standard pathway's 6-week minimum. The denial further asserted that the myelopathy exception was not applicable because the treating physician's most recent note listed "cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy" as the primary diagnosis, thereby disqualifying the member from the myelopathy exception pathway.

**Summary of Appeal Basis:**
The appeal contends that myelomalacia documented on MRI mandates a regulatory override, regardless of diagnosis labeling. It further argues that the treating physician used "cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy" as a billing code, but clinical notes consistently document cervical myelopathy. Finally, APL 22-014 is cited as mandating waiver of conservative treatment when myelomalacia is present.

**Clinical Record Findings:**
Our review of the clinical record reveals the following key information:
*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** While the ICD-10 billing code (M47.812) on some referrals was "cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy," the treating physician's narrative notes consistently document "Cervical spondylotic myelopathy C6-C7." The physician explicitly states this billing code is an administrative artifact.
*   **Initial Presentation (2024-07-01):** Clinical notes describe bilateral hand weakness, coordination problems, and presence of Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) signs.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-07-01):** Documented findings include bilateral grip weakness (3/5), positive bilateral Hoffman sign, bilateral upper extremity hyperreflexia, and impaired tandem gait. These are classic signs of cervical myelopathy.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-07-10):** Demonstrated C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate cord compression and, crucially, T2 signal change within the cord consistent with early myelomalacia.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy (PT):** 4 weeks completed (12 sessions). The treating physician halted PT after myelomalacia was confirmed on MRI, documenting that further PT was contraindicated.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Gabapentin 300mg TID for 3 months (partial pain relief, no improvement in weakness) and Naproxen 500mg BID for 2 months (inadequate). This meets HealthFirst's requirement for two pharmacotherapy agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
    *   **Interventional:** One cervical epidural on 2024-09-15, which resulted in minimal improvement and no change in motor deficits. This meets HealthFirst's requirement for one interventional procedure.

**Analysis of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**

1.  **Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1):** Mr. Webb has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (2.1(b)) supported by clinical presentation and MRI showing cord compression. He also meets 2.1(c) cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit. This criterion is met.
2.  **Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2):**
    *   **Standard Pathway:** Requires 6 weeks of PT. Mr. Webb completed 4 weeks.
    *   **Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2(A)):** Reduces PT to 4 weeks minimum and waives interventional treatment if risk is documented. The plan's denial incorrectly stated this exception did not apply because myelopathy was not the "primary diagnosis," relying on a billing code (M47.812) rather than the comprehensive clinical picture.

**Analysis of California Regulatory Requirements:**

1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This state law explicitly states that a health insurer cannot condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where:
    *   **(a)(1):** The treating physician has documented that physical therapy is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition. In this case, the treating physician halted PT and documented it as contraindicated after myelomalacia was confirmed.
    *   **(a)(3):** The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. The presence of moderate cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes such objective evidence.
    *   **Conclusion:** Under CIC § 10169.5, HealthFirst is legally prohibited from requiring further physical therapy.

2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This regulatory guidance specifically addresses conditions like Mr. Webb's:
    *   **II.1:** "Cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent." Mr. Webb's MRI confirms this condition.
    *   **II.2:** Plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia), the physician documents conservative treatment failure (PT halted, contraindicated), and surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. APL 22-014 specifically identifies myelomalacia as a finding that supports waiver of standard conservative treatment.
    *   **II.3:** Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. Where a conflict exists, the clinical standard governs.
    *   **Conclusion:** DMHC APL 22-014 directly overrides HealthFirst's 6-week PT requirement and its restrictive interpretation of "primary diagnosis" for the myelopathy exception in cases with myelomalacia.

3.  **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) (Standard of Review for IMR):** This statute clarifies that if a service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, it SHALL be approved regardless of the plan's more restrictive internal criteria.

**Analysis of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

1.  **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines emphasize that myelopathy and radiculopathy are distinct conditions requiring different treatment algorithms. The clinical presentation of Mr. Webb (bilateral weakness, UMN signs, gait disturbance) unequivocally points to myelopathy.
2.  **Significance of Myelomalacia:** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change on MRI) is a critical finding, indicating existing spinal cord injury, higher risk of permanent deficit, reduced recovery, and surgical urgency. It is considered a strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials.
3.  **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** Clinical guidelines from AO Spine (2021) explicitly state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." The 6-week PT requirement is primarily derived from radiculopathy evidence and is a category error when applied to myelopathy.
4.  **Surgical Indication:** Per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), Mr. Webb's presentation (confirmed cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression, myelomalacia on MRI, and objective neurological deficits) meets TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION for surgery. The clinical standard does not require a minimum course of physical therapy as a prerequisite for surgery in this setting.

**Resolution of Conflicts (Hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):**

The plan's denial was based on its internal criteria (6-week PT, restrictive "primary diagnosis" for myelopathy exception). However, this denial directly conflicts with both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.

*   **State law (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014)** explicitly waives or overrides the plan's requirement for a minimum duration of physical therapy when myelomalacia is present and the physician deems further PT contraindicated due to structural neurological compromise. The plan's reliance on a billing code rather than comprehensive clinical documentation to deny the myelopathy exception is contrary to regulatory intent.
*   **Clinical standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine guidelines)** definitively indicate that Mr. Webb's condition (moderate-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and cord compression) is a strong surgical indication, and delaying surgery for extended conservative therapy, particularly PT, is not supported by evidence and may worsen neurological outcomes. The plan's 6-week PT requirement for this condition is medically inappropriate and not evidence-based.

Given that both state law and generally accepted clinical standards supersede HealthFirst's internal criteria, and both strongly support the requested surgery and override the conservative treatment requirements cited in the denial, the denial must be overturned.

**Disposition:**
**OVERTURNED.**

**Warrant:**
The prior authorization for CPT 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) for Marcus Webb is hereby OVERTURNED. The initial denial was based on a misapplication of HealthFirst's internal clinical criteria, which are superseded by California state law and generally accepted standards of medical practice for this patient's condition.

1.  **Undisputed Myelopathy Diagnosis:** The clinical record overwhelmingly supports a diagnosis of cervical myelopathy with objective neurological deficits (bilateral weakness, positive Hoffman sign, hyperreflexia, gait impairment) and MRI evidence of moderate cord compression with myelomalacia. The treating physician's note clarifies that the "radiculopathy" billing code was an administrative artifact and not reflective of the primary clinical diagnosis. The plan's denial incorrectly prioritized a billing code over clear clinical documentation for the "primary diagnosis" requirement of its myelopathy exception.
2.  **State Law Mandates Waiver of PT:**
    *   Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3)**, the plan cannot require completion of physical therapy because the treating physician documented it as contraindicated after myelomalacia was confirmed, and the condition involves objective structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia and cord compression) that PT cannot address.
    *   **DMHC APL 22-014 (II.1, II.2, II.3)** mandates that plans treat myelopathy with myelomalacia as an urgent condition and SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment, explicitly identifying myelomalacia as a finding that supports waiver of standard conservative requirements. Furthermore, it states that internal plan criteria more restrictive than generally accepted standards SHALL NOT apply.
3.  **Clinical Standards Support Surgery and Override PT Requirement:**
    *   **AANS/CNS (2023), NASS (2020), and AO Spine (2021) guidelines** establish that moderate to severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia is a TIER 1 strong surgical indication. These guidelines explicitly state there is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy for such patients and that delaying surgery may result in neurological deterioration. Myelomalacia is a strong predictor of poor outcome if surgery is delayed and a contraindication to extended conservative trials.
    4.  **Conservative Treatment Adequacy:** Even applying the plan's myelopathy exception, the member completed 4 weeks of PT (meeting the reduced minimum), two pharmacotherapy trials, and one interventional procedure, satisfying the modified conservative treatment requirements for myelopathy.

Therefore, based on the three-source hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), the denial of prior authorization for CPT 22551 is reversed. The requested ACDF C6-C7 is medically necessary and approved.
