**Disposition: OVERTURN**

**Warrant:**

Upon review of the submitted prior authorization appeal, clinical record, HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria, relevant California state regulations, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial denial for CPT code 22551 for Marcus Webb is hereby OVERTURNED. The requested service is medically necessary and should be approved.

The denial was based on two primary reasons: (1) failure to complete the standard 6-week physical therapy (PT) requirement, and (2) the determination that the member did not qualify for the myelopathy exception because "cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy" was listed as the primary diagnosis. This appeal finds that both reasons for denial are unsubstantiated based on the clinical evidence, plan criteria, and higher authorities of California state law and generally accepted clinical standards.

**1. Primary Clinical Presentation: Cervical Myelopathy with Myelomalacia**

The clinical record unequivocally demonstrates that Mr. Webb's primary condition is Cervical Spondylotic Myelopathy (CSM), not solely radiculopathy. Key findings supporting this include:
*   **Symptoms:** Bilateral hand weakness and coordination problems, impaired tandem gait.
*   **Neurological Exam:** Bilateral grip weakness 3/5, positive bilateral Hoffman sign, bilateral upper extremity hyperreflexia (classic upper motor neuron signs).
*   **Imaging (MRI 07/10/2024):** C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate cord compression and critically, **T2 signal change within the cord consistent with early myelomalacia.**

The diagnosis note explicitly clarifies a "BILLING DISCREPANCY," stating that while ICD-10 code M47.812 (radiculopathy) was used for billing/referrals, the treating physician's narrative notes consistently describe cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia. The plan's reliance on a billing code over objective clinical presentation and imaging findings to determine the primary diagnosis is inappropriate and inconsistent with accepted medical practice.

**2. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

*   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** Mr. Webb meets criterion 2.1(b) for "Cervical myelopathy," given spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms and objective neurological signs. The MRI further supports this.
*   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT - Myelopathy Exception:** The plan failed to correctly apply its own "EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY." This exception reduces the physical therapy requirement to a "4 weeks minimum" and waives interventional treatment if the physician documents risk.
    *   Mr. Webb completed 4 weeks of physical therapy.
    *   His treating physician explicitly halted PT after myelomalacia was confirmed and documented that further PT was contraindicated.
    *   Since Mr. Webb has confirmed cervical myelopathy (as established above, despite the billing code discrepancy), he **does qualify** for this exception. Under this exception, his 4 weeks of PT *meet* the reduced requirement, and the interventional waiver is applicable.
    *   The plan's denial reasoning, which stated Mr. Webb "does not qualify for the myelopathy exception" due to a billing code, is an erroneous application of its own criteria, as the clinical evidence overwhelmingly confirms myelopathy as the primary diagnosis.

**3. Overriding California State Regulatory Requirements:**

Even if the plan's criteria were interpreted as denying the exception, California state law mandates approval:

*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute explicitly prohibits conditioning approval of surgery on PT completion when:
    *   **(a)(1):** The treating physician documented PT as contraindicated or unlikely to benefit. In this case, the physician halted PT after myelomalacia confirmation and stated further PT was contraindicated.
    *   **(a)(3):** The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. Myelomalacia with cord compression on MRI precisely fits this description.
    *   Therefore, the plan's 6-week PT requirement, even under the standard pathway, cannot be applied to Mr. Webb's case.
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This regulatory guidance specifically addresses cases like Mr. Webb's.
    *   **II.1:** It classifies "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI" as a condition with "potential for irreversible harm if untreated" which "Plans SHALL treat... as urgent."
    *   **II.2:** It mandates that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when objective imaging (like myelomalacia) demonstrates progressive pathology, the physician documents conservative failure, and surgery is indicated by generally accepted guidelines. The APL specifically identifies myelomalacia as a finding supporting the waiver of standard conservative treatment.
    *   **II.3:** It states that plans "shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice." The plan's insistence on a rigid PT duration for myelopathy with myelomalacia conflicts with this directive.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (Standard of Review for IMR):** This statute specifies that if a service is "medically necessary" according to generally accepted standards of medical practice, it "SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." The evidence clearly shows Mr. Webb's condition and the requested surgery meet generally accepted standards.

**4. Conflict with Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:**

*   **AANS/CNS (2023), NASS (2020), AO Spine (2021) Guidelines:** These authoritative clinical guidelines strongly support surgical intervention for patients with moderate-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI. Mr. Webb's presentation meets TIER 1 criteria for strong surgical indication.
*   **Myelomalacia Significance:** Myelomalacia on MRI signifies existing spinal cord injury and is a strong predictor of permanent deficit if surgery is delayed. It is considered a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2).
*   **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** Clinical evidence explicitly states that for moderate-severe myelopathy, physical therapy does not produce meaningful functional improvement, and there is "no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention" (AO Spine Guidelines, Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1). Delaying surgery in such cases "is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1). The 6-week PT requirement is derived from radiculopathy evidence, not myelopathy.

**Conclusion:**

The HealthFirst denial of prior authorization for ACDF C6-C7 is inconsistent with HealthFirst's own clinical coverage criteria when correctly applied, and is directly contradicted by multiple California state laws and generally accepted clinical standards for managing cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia. The risk of neurological deterioration from delaying surgery in this specific presentation is well-documented in the clinical literature and explicitly recognized by California regulators.

Therefore, the requested ACDF C6-C7 (CPT 22551) for Marcus Webb is medically necessary and is approved.
