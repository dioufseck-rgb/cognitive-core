## APPEAL DETERMINATION: Prior Authorization for ACDF C6-C7

**Case ID:** PA-2024-B002
**Member:** Thomas Reed (M-667788)
**Procedure:** ACDF C6-C7, one level (CPT 22551)
**Requested By:** Dr. Karen Liu, MD
**Original Denial Date:** 2024-10-20
**Review Level:** First-Level Appeal

**Summary of Original Denial Reason:**
The initial denial (MED-003) stated the member did not meet criteria for ACDF, citing imaging showing C6-C7 disc herniation with mild cord contact but no measurable compression, no myelopathy findings on exam, and "PT not yet completed to functional plateau."

**Appeal Basis:**
The appeal asserts that the member completed 7 weeks of Physical Therapy (PT) with documented failure to improve, a functional plateau was reached per the PT discharge summary, and the surgeon believes ACDF is indicated for persistent radiculopathy.

---

### REVIEW AND ANALYSIS

The appeal has been reviewed in accordance with the established three-source hierarchy: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria**.

**1. Primary Clinical Presentation:**
The clinical record and neurological exam consistently describe symptoms consistent with **Cervical Radiculopathy C6-C7**. Key findings include right C7 dermatomal numbness, diminished right tricep reflex, and 7/10 pain at rest. Crucially, there are "No bilateral findings" and "No upper motor neuron signs," and imaging shows "No cord signal change. No myelomalacia." This explicitly rules out myelopathy as the primary diagnosis, which is an important distinction for applying both plan criteria and clinical guidelines.

**2. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

*   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** The member has confirmed Cervical Radiculopathy C6-C7, persistent after conservative treatment, meeting criterion 2.1(a). The denial's statement regarding "no measurable compression" and "no myelopathy findings" is consistent with a radiculopathy diagnosis but does not negate the indication for radiculopathy if other criteria are met.
*   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT (Standard Pathway):** Since myelopathy is not the primary diagnosis, the standard conservative treatment pathway applies (not the Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A)).
    *   **(a) Physical therapy:** The plan requires a "minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy with documented functional outcomes." The member completed 7 weeks (21 sessions). The PT discharge summary explicitly states, "functional plateau reached – patient has not improved over final 3 sessions." HealthFirst's own definition of functional plateau within the plan criteria states "<10% improvement over 3 sessions OR therapist documents maximum benefit reached." The clinical record demonstrates that the member has met this definition. Therefore, the plan's assertion in the denial that "PT not yet completed to functional plateau" is directly contradicted by the clinical record and the plan's own criteria.
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** The plan requires a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The member completed trials of Pregabalin (4 months) and Meloxicam (3 months), meeting this criterion.
    *   **(c) Interventional treatment:** The plan requires at least one interventional treatment. The member received a Right C7 epidural, meeting this criterion.
*   **2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:** MRI dated 2024-08-01 demonstrates C6-C7 disc herniation and mild right C7 foraminal stenosis, consistent with symptoms and the proposed surgical level. This criterion is met.
*   **2.4 PHYSICIAN DOCUMENTATION:** The appeal basis confirms the surgeon's belief in the indication, implying that requisite documentation is provided.

**Plan Criteria Conclusion:** The member meets all applicable HealthFirst plan criteria for ACDF. The primary basis for the original denial – that PT was "not yet completed to functional plateau" – is inaccurate given the clinical record and HealthFirst's own definition of functional plateau.

**3. Application of California Regulatory Requirements (State Law):**

*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This state law directly addresses the PT requirement.
    *   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)** states that a health insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
    *   **CIC § 10169.5(b)** defines "functional plateau" as "documented failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement after a course of therapy, as determined by the treating physical therapist or physician."
    *   **CIC § 10169.5(c)** prohibits the mechanical application of minimum PT durations without reference to whether a functional plateau has been achieved.
    *   **Application:** The clinical record clearly documents that the member completed 7 weeks of PT and reached a functional plateau. Therefore, under California law, HealthFirst cannot deny the procedure based on the duration or "completion" of physical therapy. The plan's denial directly violates this state statute.

*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014:** This APL primarily concerns myelopathy, which is not the primary diagnosis in this case. Therefore, the specific waivers for conservative treatment for myelopathy are not directly applicable. However, it generally reinforces that plans shall not apply internal criteria more restrictive than generally accepted standards.

**State Law Conclusion:** State law (CIC § 10169.5) explicitly overrides the plan's ability to deny based on the PT requirement once a functional plateau is documented. This provides a strong legal basis for overturning the denial.

**4. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

*   **Diagnosis:** Clinical standards (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.1) clearly differentiate radiculopathy from myelopathy. This case aligns with radiculopathy.
*   **Conservative Treatment for Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines for radiculopathy indicate that physical therapy can be effective and that surgery is generally considered after 6-12 weeks of failed conservative treatment where symptoms persist.
*   **Functional Plateau:** The clinical standard for functional plateau (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 5) is consistent with the member's documented PT outcome: "Less than 10% improvement in validated functional outcome measure... over 3 consecutive therapy sessions, OR Therapist documentation that patient has reached maximum therapeutic benefit." The member's PT discharge summary meets this definition.
*   **Surgical Indications:** Per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update), Section 3.1, TIER 2, surgery is a "Reasonable Indication" for "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care." The member's 7 weeks of PT, 4+ months of pharmacotherapy, and an epidural with persistent 7/10 pain and neurological deficits satisfy these clinical indications.

**Clinical Standard Conclusion:** The clinical standards support surgical intervention for persistent radiculopathy after a documented course of conservative therapy that has reached a functional plateau, as is the case for this member.

---

### CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND FINAL DISPOSITION

Applying the three-source hierarchy (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):

1.  **Plan Criteria:** The member meets all HealthFirst plan criteria, as the stated reason for denial ("PT not yet completed to functional plateau") is factually incorrect based on the provided clinical record and the plan's own definition of functional plateau.
2.  **State Law (CIC § 10169.5):** This California law explicitly prohibits conditioning approval on further PT once a functional plateau is documented. The plan's denial directly contravenes this state mandate. As State Law is the highest authority, this alone necessitates overturning the denial.
3.  **Clinical Standard:** Generally accepted clinical guidelines also support ACDF for persistent radiculopathy after an adequate trial of conservative treatment that has reached a functional plateau.

The plan's denial is based on a misinterpretation of the clinical record regarding the completion of physical therapy and fails to adhere to California state law regarding functional plateau.

**DISPOSITION: OVERTURNED**

**WARRANT:**
The denial for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) for member Thomas Reed is **OVERTURNED**.

The initial denial was based on the premise that "PT not yet completed to functional plateau." However, the clinical record clearly demonstrates that the member completed 7 weeks of physical therapy, and the PT discharge summary explicitly states that a functional plateau was reached, with no improvement over the final three sessions and persistent radicular symptoms. This documentation satisfies HealthFirst's own definition of a functional plateau within its Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a).

Furthermore, California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2) states that a health insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." Subdivision (c) of the same code section explicitly prohibits the mechanical application of minimum PT durations without considering whether a functional plateau has been achieved. The plan's denial, therefore, directly conflicts with and is superseded by California state law.

In addition to meeting state law and the plan's own criteria, the member's condition of persistent cervical radiculopathy, unresponsive to 7 weeks of PT (to plateau), 4 months of neuropathic agents, 3 months of NSAIDs, and a cervical epidural injection, meets the generally accepted clinical standards for surgical intervention as outlined by AANS/CNS (2023 Update), NASS (2020), and AO Spine (2021) guidelines (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.1, TIER 2(b) and (c)).

Therefore, based on the documented clinical evidence, HealthFirst's own medical necessity criteria, and the governing California state law, the requested ACDF C6-C7 is medically necessary and should be approved.
