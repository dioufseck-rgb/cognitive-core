**SUBJECT: Appeal Determination for ACDF C5-C6 (Prophylactic)**

Dear Mr. Flores,

This letter serves as the final determination regarding the appeal of your prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6. Your request was previously denied by HealthFirst on 2024-10-25, citing a lack of neurological deficit and that surgical intervention was not indicated for axial pain only, per plan criteria CC-SPINE-2024.

We have conducted a comprehensive review of your clinical record, the initial denial, your appeal basis, HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), and applicable California state laws and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice. Our review follows the mandated hierarchy: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.

---

**SUMMARY OF CLINICAL INFORMATION:**

Mr. Raymond Flores, a 54-year-old construction worker, presents with severe cervical stenosis at C5-C6. An MRI dated 2024-09-15 confirms severe central canal stenosis with cord contact, noting a canal diameter of 6mm (significantly narrower than the normal >13mm). The MRI report explicitly states, "Single trauma could cause acute cord injury given severely reduced canal diameter."

A neurological examination on 2024-09-20 was completely normal, documenting no signs of radiculopathy, myelopathy, or upper motor neuron (UMN) signs. Mr. Flores's only symptom is axial neck pain, currently rated 5/10, which showed modest improvement (from 7/10 to 5/10) after an 8-week course of physical therapy, 3 months of Naproxen, 6 weeks of Cyclobenzaprine, and a C5-C6 epidural steroid injection providing 60% pain relief for 6 weeks.

Dr. Karen Davis, your treating spine surgeon, has requested prophylactic decompression, emphasizing the critically narrow canal diameter, the high risk of acute spinal cord injury from minor trauma given your occupation operating heavy equipment, and the need to prevent catastrophic injury.

---

**HEALTHFIRST'S INITIAL DENIAL REASONING:**

The initial denial by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, on 2024-10-25 (Reason Code MED-003) stated: "Cervical stenosis documented but no neurological deficit on examination. Member has axial neck pain only. No radiculopathy, no myelopathy documented. Surgical intervention not indicated for axial pain without neurological deficit under CC-SPINE-2024."

This denial correctly reflects HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria CC-SPINE-2024, specifically Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement, which mandates symptomatic radiculopathy or myelopathy, or objective neurological deficit) and Exclusion 3.1 (denying surgery solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy). Based on these criteria, your current lack of neurological deficits and primary complaint of axial pain would lead to a denial. The plan's myelopathy exception (Section 2.2(A)) was not applicable, as you do not have a confirmed diagnosis of current cervical myelopathy.

---

**REVIEW AGAINST APPLICABLE STANDARDS (Hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):**

**1. California Regulatory Framework:**
*   **CHSC § 1374.31(b) (Denial Notice Adequacy):** The denial notice was procedurally adequate, specifying the clinical criteria and reason for denial.
*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Prerequisite):** While your conservative treatment was extensive and met the plan's requirements, this law allows for waivers of PT under certain conditions. It is not directly pertinent to the plan's denial reason, which was based on the lack of neurological deficit, not insufficient PT.
*   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This All-Plan Letter addresses time-sensitive neurological conditions, particularly myelopathy with myelomalacia. While your condition involves cord contact and high risk, the absence of *current myelopathy* or *myelomalacia* on your MRI means this APL's specific guidance on urgent *existing* myelopathy does not directly compel an overturn based on *current* neurological compromise. However, the broader intent of preventing irreversible harm is relevant to the "prevention" aspect of medical necessity.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard of Review):** This is the most critical state law for this appeal. It mandates that the standard for medical necessity during an appeal is "generally accepted standards of medical practice" and specifically states that services meeting this standard "SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." The law further clarifies that "Medically necessary" health care services include those provided "for the purpose of **preventing**, evaluating, diagnosing, or treating an illness, injury, disease, or its symptoms." This legal definition explicitly includes prophylactic (preventative) care.

**2. Clinical Standard of Medical Practice:**
*   **AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine Guidelines:** The primary clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine International 2021) extensively detail surgical indications for *symptomatic* cervical radiculopathy and myelopathy. These guidelines typically classify axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy, or imaging findings without correlating symptoms, as situations where surgery is *not indicated* (Tier 3). They do not explicitly list "prophylactic decompression for severe asymptomatic stenosis with cord contact in a high-risk occupation" as a Tier 1 or Tier 2 indication for *treating existing conditions*.
*   **Prevention as a Standard of Care:** While not explicitly codified in the guidelines for *treating symptomatic myelopathy*, the severe anatomical compromise (6mm canal diameter, cord contact), the radiologist's warning about acute cord injury from minor trauma, and your high-risk occupation (construction worker operating heavy equipment) create a situation where a prudent spine surgeon would consider intervention to prevent a catastrophic and irreversible spinal cord injury. This falls under the "prevention" aspect of generally accepted medical practice. To defer surgery until an acute traumatic event causes symptomatic myelopathy, with its inherent risk of permanent neurological deficit, would be inconsistent with sound preventative medical judgment in such an extreme anatomical predisposition.

---

**CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND DETERMINATION:**

HealthFirst's denial, while consistent with its internal criteria, is overly restrictive and conflicts with California state law regarding the definition of medical necessity. The plan's criteria require the presence of *current neurological symptoms or deficits* to approve cervical spine surgery, effectively precluding coverage for critical preventative interventions.

California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32(a) broadly defines "medically necessary" services to include those for the purpose of "preventing" illness or injury. In your case, the objective imaging evidence (6mm canal, cord contact) and the professional judgment of both the radiologist and treating surgeon clearly identify a severe structural risk that places you at exceptionally high probability of acute, catastrophic spinal cord injury with even minor trauma, particularly given your occupation. The requested prophylactic decompression is therefore a medically necessary intervention aimed at preventing such a severe and potentially irreversible injury.

While the clinical guidelines for *treating symptomatic* myelopathy or radiculopathy do not explicitly address this specific prophylactic scenario, the severity of the asymptomatic anatomical risk, combined with the clear risk of trauma, constitutes a situation where surgical prevention aligns with the prudent clinical judgment encompassed by "generally accepted standards of medical practice" for preventing severe harm.

Therefore, applying the hierarchy of authority, California state law's broader definition of medical necessity, which includes prevention, overrides HealthFirst's more restrictive internal criteria.

---

**DISPOSITION:**

**OVERTURN**

**WARRANT:**
The prior authorization denial for ACDF C5-C6 for Mr. Raymond Flores is OVERTURNED. While HealthFirst's denial was consistent with its internal clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Sections 2.1 and 3.1) which require objective neurological deficits or symptomatic radiculopathy/myelopathy for surgical approval, these criteria are more restrictive than California law. California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32(b) mandates that health plans approve services that meet "generally accepted standards of medical practice," regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. Crucially, CHSC § 1374.32(a) defines "medically necessary" to include services for the express purpose of "preventing" an illness, injury, or disease.

In this case, the member's clinical record demonstrates severe cervical stenosis with cord contact and a critically narrowed spinal canal (6mm), as documented by MRI. Both the radiologist and the treating surgeon explicitly articulate an extremely high risk of acute, catastrophic spinal cord injury (myelopathy) from even minor trauma. This risk is amplified by the member's occupation as a heavy equipment operator. The requested ACDF, while prophylactic and not addressing existing neurological deficits, is a reasonable and medically appropriate intervention to prevent a high-probability, severe, and irreversible spinal cord injury. This preventative measure falls squarely within the definition of "medically necessary" under California law and aligns with prudent clinical judgment for risk mitigation in such severe anatomical circumstances, thereby meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice for prevention.
