**I. BACKGROUND**

Mr. Raymond Flores, a 54-year-old construction worker, requested prior authorization for an anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 for severe cervical stenosis with cord contact, indicated as prophylactic decompression. The initial denial by HealthFirst was based on the absence of documented neurological deficits (radiculopathy or myelopathy) and the presence of only axial neck pain, citing HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024. The appeal argues that prophylactic surgery is indicated due to severe stenosis with cord contact and high trauma risk associated with his occupation.

**II. CLINICAL SUMMARY**

*   **Diagnosis:** Severe cervical stenosis C5-C6, prophylactic decompression requested.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-15):** C5-C6 severe central canal stenosis. Cord contact without compression. No T2 signal change (no myelomalacia). Canal diameter 6mm (normal >13mm). Radiologist note: "Single trauma could cause acute cord injury given severely reduced canal diameter."
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-09-20):** Completely normal. No radiculopathy, no myelopathy signs, no UMN signs. Axial neck pain only (5/10).
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   Physical Therapy: 8 weeks, 24 sessions. Modest pain improvement (7/10 to 5/10). No neurological symptoms addressed.
    *   Pharmacotherapy: Naproxen (3 months, moderate relief), Cyclobenzaprine (6 weeks, sleep improvement).
    *   Interventional: C5-C6 epidural (60% pain relief for 6 weeks).
*   **Surgeon's Rationale (Dr. Karen Davis):** Patient has critically narrow canal diameter. Significant cervical trauma (elevated risk due to heavy equipment operation in construction) would likely cause acute spinal cord injury. Prophylactic decompression is indicated to prevent catastrophic injury.

**III. DENIAL REASON (HealthFirst)**

"Cervical stenosis documented but no neurological deficit on examination. Member has axial neck pain only. No radiculopathy, no myelopathy documented. Surgical intervention not indicated for axial pain without neurological deficit under CC-SPINE-2024." (Reason code: MED-003)

**IV. REVIEW AND ANALYSIS**

The appeal determination will follow the hierarchy of authority: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.

**A. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)**

1.  **Section 2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** This section requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (2.1a), cervical myelopathy (2.1b), or cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit (2.1c).
    *   Based on the clinical record, Mr. Flores currently presents with *no objective neurological deficits* of radiculopathy or myelopathy. The denial correctly notes this.
    *   The request is for *prophylactic* surgery due to the *risk* of myelopathy, which is not explicitly listed as a diagnosis criterion for approval.
2.  **Section 2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:** The member completed 8 weeks of PT, trialed two pharmacotherapy agents, and underwent one interventional procedure.
    *   These conservative treatment requirements *have been met* under the standard pathway (6 weeks PT minimum, 2 pharmacotherapy agents, 1 interventional). The denial was not based on unmet conservative treatment.
    *   The "Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A)" was not applied, as the plan deemed the member did not have "confirmed cervical myelopathy."
3.  **Section 3.1 Exclusions:** "Surgery is requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy."
    *   The denial reason directly aligns with this exclusion, stating "Surgical intervention not indicated for axial pain without neurological deficit."

*Conclusion on Plan Criteria:* The denial is consistent with a literal and restrictive interpretation of HealthFirst's own criteria, specifically Sections 2.1 and 3.1, which require *current symptomatic* neurological deficits.

**B. Application of California Regulatory Framework**

1.  **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This state law provision states that a plan shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
    *   The clinical record clearly documents "Severe cervical stenosis C5-C6... Cord contact... Canal diameter 6mm (normal >13mm)." This constitutes "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise."
    *   Physical therapy, while attempted for axial pain, cannot address this severe structural compromise or mitigate the risk of acute spinal cord injury.
    *   Although the denial was not *directly* for failure of conservative treatment, the underlying rationale (denial due to lack of current neurological *symptoms* despite severe structural issues) is challenged by this law. This law emphasizes that severe structural compromise, independent of symptoms, can bypass mechanical PT requirements.

2.  **DMHC APL 22-014 (II.3) & CHSC § 1374.32(b) — Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** These provisions mandate that plans "SHALL NOT apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice" and that services meeting "generally accepted standards... SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive."
    *   The plan's criteria, by requiring *current symptoms* of myelopathy or radiculopathy and excluding surgery *solely* for axial pain without these, are arguably more restrictive than a prudent clinical judgment standard for a patient with severe structural pathology and a documented high risk of catastrophic neurological injury.
    *   The APL also identifies myelopathy with "potential for irreversible harm" as urgent conditions that may warrant exceptions to conservative treatment. While Mr. Flores does not have *active* myelopathy with myelomalacia, the *risk* of acute myelopathy with irreversible harm is precisely the concern raised by the surgeon.

*Conclusion on State Law:* California state law provides a strong basis to question the plan's strict adherence to its criteria in this specific case, particularly concerning structural compromise and the overriding authority of generally accepted clinical standards.

**C. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)**

1.  **Severity of Stenosis and Cord Contact:** The MRI reveals a critically narrow canal diameter of 6mm (normal >13mm) with cord contact. This represents severe structural pathology.
2.  **Prophylactic Surgery:** While standard guidelines primarily address *symptomatic* conditions, the surgeon's rationale is critical: "This patient has critically narrow canal diameter. Any significant cervical trauma would likely cause acute spinal cord injury. Given his occupation operating heavy equipment, the risk of such trauma is elevated. Prophylactic decompression is indicated to prevent catastrophic injury."
    *   The AANS/CNS Tier 1 strong indication includes "Acute severe myelopathy with risk of permanent deficit." While Mr. Flores does not *currently* have acute myelopathy, the surgeon is asserting that the *risk* of developing acute severe myelopathy with permanent deficit due to minor trauma in his high-risk occupation is extremely high and *imminent*.
    *   From a clinical perspective, allowing a patient with such severe, unstable structural compromise and high occupational risk to suffer a potentially catastrophic and irreversible spinal cord injury before intervention is inconsistent with "prudent clinical judgment" and generally accepted standards of preventive care in spine surgery. The absence of myelomalacia currently means there is no existing cord damage, making *prophylactic* intervention even more impactful.
    *   The clinical guidelines state that for moderate-severe myelopathy, conservative treatment often does not yield meaningful improvement and delaying surgery may result in deterioration. While not active myelopathy, the *risk* of it is high due to the structural compromise that PT cannot address.

*Conclusion on Clinical Standard:* While prophylactic surgery for asymptomatic severe stenosis isn't explicitly codified in the same manner as symptomatic myelopathy, the confluence of critical stenosis (6mm canal), documented cord contact, and a high-risk occupation, combined with the surgeon's professional judgment regarding imminent catastrophic risk, falls within the scope of preventing "Acute severe myelopathy with risk of permanent deficit," a Tier 1 indication. Waiting for an inevitable injury in this scenario is not consistent with prudent clinical practice.

**V. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND DISPOSITION**

The plan's denial, based on a literal interpretation of its criteria requiring *current symptomatic* neurological deficits, conflicts with:

1.  **California State Law (CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)):** This law prohibits conditioning approval on PT when there is "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Mr. Flores has severe cervical stenosis with cord contact (6mm canal), which is a clear structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot resolve.
2.  **California State Law (DMHC APL 22-014 II.3 & CHSC § 1374.32(b)):** These provisions establish that plan criteria cannot be more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. The plan's criteria, by rigidly requiring *symptoms* and excluding prophylactic intervention for severe, critically unstable structural compromise, are more restrictive than prudent clinical judgment would dictate for a high-risk patient.
3.  **Clinical Standard (Interpretation):** The surgeon's rationale for prophylactic decompression to prevent "acute severe myelopathy with risk of permanent deficit" (a Tier 1 indication) in a patient with severe stenosis and cord contact in a high-risk occupation aligns with generally accepted standards to prevent catastrophic neurological injury.

The hierarchy of authority dictates that California State Law and generally accepted Clinical Standards override more restrictive Plan Criteria. The plan's denial fails to account for the severity of the objective structural pathology, the documented high risk of acute neurological injury, and the prudent clinical judgment of the treating surgeon to prevent irreversible harm.

**DISPOSITION:** OVERTURN

**WARRANT:**

The denial is OVERTURNED because, while consistent with a restrictive reading of the plan's criteria, it directly conflicts with California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.

1.  **State Law (CIC § 10169.5(a)(3))**: The member's MRI demonstrates "severe central canal stenosis" and "cord contact" with a critically reduced canal diameter of 6mm. This constitutes "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." While physical therapy was completed for axial pain, it cannot mitigate the risk of acute spinal cord injury posed by the severe structural defect. The plan's implicit requirement for *symptoms* to justify intervention, despite severe structural compromise, is superseded by this law.
2.  **State Law (DMHC APL 22-014 II.3 and CHSC § 1374.32(b))**: HealthFirst's criteria, which require *current symptomatic* myelopathy or radiculopathy and deny surgery solely for axial pain, are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for managing severe, critical structural spinal pathology.
3.  **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine Guidelines)**: The combination of critically severe cervical stenosis (6mm canal, cord contact), the documented high risk of acute spinal cord injury from minor trauma, and the member's occupation (construction worker operating heavy equipment) warrants prophylactic surgical intervention. The treating surgeon's professional judgment that prophylactic decompression is "indicated to prevent catastrophic injury" aligns with the intent of clinical guidelines to prevent "Acute severe myelopathy with risk of permanent deficit" (AANS/CNS Tier 1) and constitutes prudent clinical judgment. To delay intervention until a catastrophic injury occurs would contravene the principles of preventing irreversible neurological harm in a high-risk individual.

Therefore, HealthFirst is directed to approve the prior authorization for CPT code 22551 for Mr. Raymond Flores.
