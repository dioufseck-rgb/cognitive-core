**I. BACKGROUND**

Catherine Mills, a 53-year-old member under HealthFirst PPO Gold, requested prior authorization for a three-level anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C3-C4, C4-C5, and C5-C6. The request was submitted by Dr. James Torres, MD (Neurosurgery) to be performed at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center.

The initial authorization request was denied by HealthFirst on 2024-11-25 (Reason Code MED-003). The denial stated, "Three-level ACDF not approved. Review of imaging and clinical record supports surgical intervention at one level (C4-C5) only. C3-C4 and C5-C6 do not demonstrate pathology of sufficient severity to warrant surgical intervention at this time."

The appeal basis provided by the treating surgeon includes: "All three levels are symptomatic," "Treating surgeon has documented reasoning for three-level fusion," and "Conservative treatment completed for all levels."

---

**II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND DIAGNOSIS**

The member's presenting diagnosis is multi-level cervical spondylosis with myeloradiculopathy.

**Key Clinical Findings:**
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-10-01):**
    *   C3-C4: Mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing.
    *   C4-C5: Large disc herniation, severe cord compression, T2 signal change (myelomalacia).
    *   C5-C6: Moderate disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression, foraminal stenosis.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-10-05):**
    *   Myelopathy findings: bilateral hyperreflexia, bilateral Hoffman sign, wide-based gait.
    *   C5 level: bilateral deltoid weakness (4/5).
    *   C6 level: left bicep weakness (4-/5), bicep reflex diminished left.
    *   C4 level: Unclear, neck pain with C3-C4 palpation but no specific radicular pattern.
*   **Conservative Treatment History:**
    *   Physical Therapy: 9 weeks (27 sessions). Myelopathic symptoms stable but not improved; C5-C6 radicular symptoms persist; C3-C4 contribution unclear.
    *   Pharmacotherapy: Baclofen 10mg TID (3 months – minimal spasticity relief), Pregabalin 300mg BID (4 months – partial pain relief).
    *   Interventional: Cervical epidural (2024-10-20 – modest improvement in radicular symptoms, no change in myelopathy signs).

**Primary Clinical Presentation Determination:**
Based on the objective imaging (severe cord compression, myelomalacia at C4-C5) and neurological exam findings (bilateral hyperreflexia, Hoffman sign, wide-based gait, bilateral C5 weakness), the primary clinical presentation is **moderate to severe cervical myelopathy**. The presence of myelomalacia (T2 signal change) at C4-C5 indicates existing spinal cord injury and is a critical finding for treatment algorithms. The C5-C6 level also demonstrates radiculopathy with persistent symptoms, which occurs in the context of the overall myelopathic presentation.

**III. REGULATORY AND CLINICAL STANDARDS REVIEW**

This review applies the hierarchy of authority: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.**

**A. California Regulatory Requirements:**

1.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):**
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent..." The member's C4-C5 pathology (severe cord compression, myelomalacia) clearly falls under this classification, indicating an urgent condition.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** For such urgent conditions, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment... when (a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia)... AND (b) Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression... AND (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines..." The presence of myelomalacia at C4-C5 specifically supports the waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements.
    *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice... Where a conflict exists..., the clinical standard governs." This provision dictates that the plan's denial reasoning must align with generally accepted clinical standards, especially for myelopathy.

2.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):**
    *   **(a)(3):** "A health insurer... shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Myelomalacia at C4-C5 represents objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (spinal cord injury) that physical therapy cannot reverse or address functionally. This provision directly overrides any mandatory PT duration requirement for the myelopathic component of the member's condition.

3.  **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):**
    *   "The IMR standard does NOT require that the service be consistent with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the service meets the standard in subdivision (a) [generally accepted standards of medical practice], the reviewer SHALL recommend approval regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." This reinforces that if the requested surgery (including specific levels) is medically necessary under clinical standards, it must be approved, even if the plan's internal criteria are more stringent.

**B. Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

1.  **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy (Section 1.1):** Myelopathy involves spinal cord compression with UMN signs and typically requires different treatment algorithms than radiculopathy. Conservative treatment has limited efficacy for established myelopathy.
2.  **Myelomalacia Significance (Section 1.2):** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) indicates existing spinal cord injury, a higher risk of permanent deficit, and reduced recovery. It is a strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials, signifying surgical urgency.
3.  **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy (Section 2.1, 2.2, 2.3):**
    *   For moderate-severe myelopathy, physical therapy does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement (Cochrane 2021).
    *   "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery... may result in neurological deterioration" (AO Spine 2021).
    *   No defined minimum duration for conservative treatment for myelopathy is established by evidence. Applying radiculopathy-derived PT requirements is a category error.
4.  **Surgical Indications (Section 3.1, 3.2):**
    *   **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION (surgery recommended):** (a) Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI; (c) Myelomalacia on MRI; (d) Acute severe myelopathy with risk of permanent deficit.
    *   **TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION (surgery reasonable option):** (b) Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx; (c) Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care.

**C. HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

1.  **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** The member meets criteria 2.1(b) for cervical myelopathy (spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms) and 2.1(a) for cervical radiculopathy at C5-C6.
2.  **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:** The member completed 9 weeks of PT (exceeding 6 weeks standard or 4 weeks myelopathy exception), 2 pharmacotherapy agents for 3-4 months (exceeding 4 weeks each), and 1 interventional treatment. The conservative treatment requirements (even under the myelopathy exception, 2.2(A)) are met or exceeded.
3.  **2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:** MRI within 6 months is present.

**IV. ANALYSIS OF DENIAL REASON & APPEAL BASIS**

The plan's denial stated, "Review of imaging and clinical record supports surgical intervention at one level (C4-C5) only. C3-C4 and C5-C6 do not demonstrate pathology of sufficient severity to warrant surgical intervention at this time." The denial explicitly *approved* C4-C5, indicating medical necessity at that level. The dispute is over C3-C4 and C5-C6.

**Application of Hierarchy:**

**1. Level C4-C5 (Approved by Plan):**
*   **Clinical Standard:** The member has severe cord compression and myelomalacia at C4-C5, with bilateral myelopathic signs (hyperreflexia, Hoffman, gait disturbance, bilateral C5 weakness). This meets TIER 1 strong surgical indication per AANS/CNS (2023). Myelomalacia mandates urgency and contraindicates extended conservative trials (AO Spine 2021).
*   **State Law:** DMHC APL 22-014 mandates urgent treatment for myelopathy with myelomalacia. CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) waives PT for such structural neurological compromise.
*   **Plan Criteria:** Meets 2.1(b) for myelopathy, and conservative treatment was completed beyond its requirements (9 weeks PT).
*   **Conclusion:** The approval for C4-C5 is strongly supported by all sources.

**2. Level C5-C6 (Denied by Plan - "not of sufficient severity"):**
*   **Clinical Record:** Moderate disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression, foraminal stenosis, with left C6 weakness (4-/5) and diminished reflex. The PT summary notes "C5-C6 radicular symptoms persist." This occurs in the context of an overall multi-level myelopathy.
*   **Clinical Standard:** Surgical intervention for radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment (which was completed for 9 weeks) is a TIER 2 reasonable indication per AANS/CNS (2023). Given the severe myelopathy at C4-C5, a multi-level decompression that addresses adjacent symptomatic levels like C5-C6 is often considered the standard of care to achieve comprehensive neurological decompression and prevent adjacent segment degeneration. The denial's assertion of "insufficient severity" for C5-C6, when considering persistent symptoms and its contribution to the multi-level pathology, is inconsistent with clinical standards which support intervention for radiculopathy that has failed conservative care.
*   **State Law:** DMHC APL 22-014 and CHSC § 1374.32(b) mandate that clinical standards govern over more restrictive plan criteria. The plan's "severity" threshold for C5-C6 appears more restrictive than generally accepted standards for symptomatic radiculopathy failing conservative management, especially when adjacent to severe myelopathy.
*   **Plan Criteria:** The member meets 2.1(a) for cervical radiculopathy and has completed conservative treatment (2.2). Imaging (2.3) demonstrates pathology consistent with symptoms. The plan's denial based purely on a subjective assessment of "sufficient severity" at C5-C6, without explicitly stating which criteria are unmet, is in conflict with the robust clinical evidence for this level.
*   **Conclusion:** The denial for C5-C6 is **NOT supported** by clinical standards or state law. The plan's decision regarding severity for this level is more restrictive than generally accepted medical practice.

**3. Level C3-C4 (Denied by Plan - "not of sufficient severity"):**
*   **Clinical Record:** MRI shows only "mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing." Neurological exam notes "C4 level: unclear — neck pain with C3-C4 palpation but no specific radicular pattern." The PT summary states "C3-C4 contribution unclear."
*   **Clinical Standard:** There is no objective evidence of radiculopathy or myelopathy at C3-C4 that would meet TIER 1 or TIER 2 surgical indications per AANS/CNS (2023). Surgical intervention for "mild disc bulge" without correlating neurological deficit or persistent radicular symptoms is not indicated by generally accepted standards.
*   **State Law:** No state law mandates approval without underlying medical necessity supported by clinical standards.
*   **Plan Criteria:** The imaging (2.3) and diagnosis (2.1) criteria would likely not be met for this level given the lack of correlating symptoms and minimal pathology.
*   **Conclusion:** The denial for C3-C4 is **supported** by the clinical record, clinical standards, and plan criteria.

**V. DISPOSITION**

**PARTIAL OVERTURN.**

The denial for the three-level ACDF is **OVERTURNED for C5-C6** and **UPHELD for C3-C4**. The C4-C5 level was already approved by the plan.

**WARRANT:**

1.  **Overturn for C5-C6 (ACDF):** The member presents with multi-level cervical spondylosis with significant myelopathy at C4-C5 (severe cord compression, myelomalacia, UMN signs, bilateral C5 weakness) and symptomatic radiculopathy at C5-C6 (moderate disc herniation, nerve root compression, left C6 weakness, diminished reflex). All required conservative treatment, including 9 weeks of physical therapy and pharmacotherapy, was completed with persistence of C5-C6 radicular symptoms, meeting HealthFirst's own conservative treatment requirements (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2). Per AANS/CNS (2023) guidelines, surgical intervention for radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after an adequate conservative trial is a TIER 2 reasonable indication. The plan's denial for C5-C6 based on "insufficient severity" is more restrictive than generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, especially given the concurrent severe myelopathy at an adjacent level. California law (CHSC § 1374.32(b) and DMHC APL 22-014) mandates that generally accepted clinical standards, not more restrictive plan criteria, shall govern in such circumstances. Therefore, the requested ACDF at C5-C6 is medically necessary.

2.  **Uphold for C3-C4 (ACDF):** The clinical record for C3-C4 indicates only a "mild disc bulge" and "minimal foraminal narrowing" on MRI, with no specific radicular pattern or objective neurological deficit identified on exam ("C3-C4 contribution unclear"). This pathology does not meet the established TIER 1 or TIER 2 surgical indications for myelopathy or radiculopathy per AANS/CNS (2023) guidelines. The lack of correlating symptoms and objective findings at C3-C4 supports the plan's determination that pathology is not of sufficient severity to warrant surgical intervention at this time.

---

**REQUIRED ACTION:**
HealthFirst Health Plan is directed to approve the prior authorization for a two-level ACDF at C4-C5 and C5-C6 for Catherine Mills. The denial for C3-C4 is upheld.
