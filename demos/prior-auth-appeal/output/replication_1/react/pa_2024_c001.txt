### Summary of Original Denial

The initial prior authorization request for ACDF C5-C6 was denied by HealthFirst on 2024-10-30. The stated reasons for denial (Reason Code MED-003) were:
1.  **Myelopathy Exception Not Applicable:** The plan reviewer determined that the myelopathy exception (Section 2.2A of HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024) did not apply because imaging showed "mild cord contact only — no cord compression, no cord signal change."
2.  **Inconclusive UMN Signs:** The reviewer stated, "Upper motor neuron signs on exam are not conclusive for myelopathy in the absence of imaging confirmation."
3.  **Conservative Treatment Incomplete:** The standard 6-week Physical Therapy (PT) requirement was cited as applicable, and the member had only completed 5 weeks.

### Summary of Appeal Basis

Dr. Adams, on behalf of Frances Kim, appealed the denial based on the following:
1.  **Clinical Myelopathy Established:** The member exhibits three positive upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, establishing clinical myelopathy regardless of imaging findings.
2.  **Myelopathy Definition Met:** Mild cord contact on MRI combined with UMN signs constitutes myelopathy per clinical definition.
3.  **PT Requirement Met under Myelopathy Pathway:** Five weeks of PT were completed under a myelopathy protocol, exceeding the 4-week requirement under the plan's myelopathy exception.

### Clinical Information Reviewed

*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Suspected cervical myelopathy — imaging-negative UMN syndrome.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-01):** C5-C6 disc herniation with mild cord contact. No measurable cord compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-09-05):** Positive Hoffman bilateral, Hyperreflexia bilateral upper extremities, Sustained clonus bilateral ankles (3 beats), Finger escape sign positive, Mildly wide-based gait. Patient denies lower extremity symptoms.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 5 weeks (15 sessions) completed under myelopathy protocol. Neurological signs remained unchanged. Physician halted PT citing myelopathy concern.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Baclofen 5mg TID for 2 months (minimal spasticity relief). Methylprednisolone taper for 10 days (no improvement in UMN signs).
    *   **Interventional:** None.
*   **Clinical Standard Note (from record):** AANS/CNS: Clinical myelopathy diagnosis requires UMN signs. Imaging confirmation supports but is *not required*. Imaging-negative myelopathy is a recognized entity. Multiple positive UMN signs in the absence of other explanation = clinical myelopathy.

### Appeals Reviewer's Determination

The denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) for member Frances Kim is **OVERTURNED**. The requested procedure is determined to be medically necessary.

### Rationale

This determination is made applying the three-source hierarchy: **State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria**.

**1. Diagnosis of Cervical Myelopathy (Conflict Resolution: Clinical Standard vs. Plan Criteria):**
*   **Plan's Denial:** The initial denial asserted that the myelopathy exception did not apply due to the absence of measurable cord compression or signal change on MRI, and stated that UMN signs are not conclusive without imaging. This implies HealthFirst's internal interpretation of "confirmed cervical myelopathy" (per Section 2.1(b) of CC-SPINE-2024) strictly requires imaging evidence of spinal cord compression.
*   **Clinical Standard:** The AANS/CNS Guidelines (as cited in the clinical record and domain knowledge, Clinical Evidence Base Section 1.1) unequivocally state that clinical myelopathy is diagnosed by the presence of UMN signs on examination, and imaging confirmation (e.g., cord compression or myelomalacia) *supports* the diagnosis but is *not required*. Imaging-negative myelopathy is a recognized entity. Ms. Kim exhibits multiple classic UMN signs (bilateral Hoffman, hyperreflexia, clonus, finger escape sign) and gait disturbance, establishing a clear clinical diagnosis of myelopathy.
*   **Hierarchy Resolution:** California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) §1374.32(b) and DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 Section II.3 mandate that health plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. Where a conflict exists, the clinical standard governs. HealthFirst's restrictive interpretation of myelopathy, requiring imaging confirmation of compression beyond what clinical standards deem necessary, is superseded by the clinical standard. Therefore, Ms. Kim *does* have a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy per generally accepted medical practice.

**2. Conservative Treatment Requirements (Conflict Resolution: State Law & Clinical Standard vs. Plan Criteria):**

*   **Physical Therapy (PT):**
    *   **Plan's Myelopathy Exception:** Since clinical myelopathy is established (as per above), HealthFirst's own myelopathy exception (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(A)) reduces the PT requirement to a minimum of 4 weeks. Ms. Kim completed 5 weeks of PT, thus **exceeding the plan's reduced requirement.**
    *   **State Law Override:** Furthermore, California Insurance Code (CIC) §10169.5(a) dictates that a plan cannot condition approval on PT completion where:
        *   (a)(1) The physician documents PT is unlikely to benefit: Ms. Kim's physician halted PT "citing myelopathy concern," indicating a belief that further PT would be futile or harmful.
        *   (a)(2) The member reached a functional plateau: Ms. Kim's "neurological signs unchanged" after 5 weeks of PT indicates a functional plateau, especially for the myelopathic symptoms, which are not expected to respond to PT (Clinical Evidence Base Section 2.1, 5).
        *   (a)(3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address: Clinical myelopathy, even without gross compression, represents a neurological compromise that PT cannot resolve.
    *   **Clinical Standard Support:** The AO Spine Guidelines (Clinical Evidence Base Section 2.1) explicitly state there is "no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." Applying radiculopathy-derived PT requirements to myelopathy is a "category error" (Clinical Evidence Base Section 1.1, 2.2).
    *   **Conclusion:** The PT requirement, whether 6 weeks or 4 weeks, is met by Ms. Kim's 5 weeks of therapy and, more importantly, is overridden by both state law and prevailing clinical standards given her myelopathy and lack of neurological improvement.

*   **Pharmacotherapy:**
    *   **Plan's Myelopathy Exception:** HealthFirst's myelopathy exception (2.2(A)) maintains the requirement for two pharmacotherapy agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. Ms. Kim received Baclofen for 2 months (meets duration) and Methylprednisolone for 10 days (does not meet duration).
    *   **Clinical Standard Override:** Despite not fully meeting the plan's two-agent requirement, the clinical evidence base (Section 2.2) states, "No randomized controlled trials support a defined minimum duration of conservative treatment before surgery" for myelopathy. Moreover, both agents attempted showed minimal or no improvement in her neurological signs, demonstrating a failure of conservative pharmacotherapy for the myelopathic condition. Given the urgency associated with myelopathy (Clinical Evidence Base Section 2.3), strict adherence to a multi-agent pharmacotherapy trial duration, when it has proven ineffective and is not strongly supported by evidence for myelopathy, is considered a restrictive criterion superseded by CHSC §1374.32(b) and DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3.

*   **Interventional Treatment:**
    *   **Plan's Myelopathy Exception:** This requirement (2.2(c)) is waived if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration. While the physician did not explicitly document this for interventional treatment, they halted PT due to "myelopathy concern." In the context of myelopathy, interventional procedures are often not indicated and may carry risks without significant benefit. Given the established clinical myelopathy and the futility of other conservative measures, it is reasonable to conclude that additional interventional treatments would not be appropriate or beneficial for Ms. Kim's myelopathic condition.

**3. Surgical Indication:**
*   Ms. Kim presents with a clear clinical diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (multiple UMN signs, gait disturbance) and has undergone a trial of conservative treatment that has failed to improve her neurological status. Her neurological signs remained unchanged after 5 weeks of PT, and pharmacotherapy provided minimal or no relief. This presentation falls under Tier 1 — STRONG INDICATION for surgical intervention per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update, Clinical Evidence Base Section 3.1): "Progressive neurological deficit despite conservative treatment." The mild cord contact on MRI provides structural pathology consistent with the diagnosis, even in the absence of measurable compression or signal change, which is not required for a clinical diagnosis of myelopathy.

**Conclusion:**
The plan's denial was based on a more restrictive definition of cervical myelopathy and a rigid application of conservative treatment requirements that are not supported by generally accepted clinical standards for this diagnosis and are superseded by California state law. Ms. Kim has a confirmed clinical diagnosis of myelopathy and has failed an adequate trial of conservative treatment according to clinical guidelines and state regulations. Surgical intervention is medically necessary as per generally accepted standards of medical practice.
