This letter is to inform you of the decision reached regarding the appeal for prior authorization of an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 for Ms. Frances Kim.

We have conducted a comprehensive review of the submitted appeal, the member's clinical record, HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), applicable California state laws, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice. Our review is conducted in accordance with the established hierarchy of authority: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.**

**Summary of Clinical Information Reviewed:**

*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Suspected cervical myelopathy – imaging-negative UMN syndrome.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-01):** C5-C6 disc herniation with "mild cord contact." Explicitly noted "No measurable cord compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia. NOTE: Cord contact is present but not quantifiable as compression on standard sequences."
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-09-05):** Significant positive findings consistent with Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) dysfunction: Positive Hoffman bilateral, Hyperreflexia bilateral upper extremities, Sustained clonus at bilateral ankles (3 beats), Finger escape sign positive, and a mildly wide-based gait. Patient denies lower extremity symptoms.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 5 weeks (15 sessions) completed "under myelopathy protocol." Physician halted PT due to myelopathy concern, noting neurological signs were unchanged.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Baclofen 5mg TID for 2 months (minimal spasticity relief), Methylprednisolone taper for 10 days (no improvement in UMN signs).
    *   **Interventional Treatment:** None documented.

**Plan's Initial Denial Rationale:**

The initial denial (MED-003) stated that the myelopathy exception did not apply because imaging showed "mild cord contact only — no cord compression, no cord signal change," and that UMN signs were "not conclusive for myelopathy in the absence of imaging confirmation." Consequently, the standard 6-week physical therapy requirement was applied, which the member had not met (completed 5 weeks).

**Appeal Basis:**

The appeal argued that clinical myelopathy is established by the presence of three positive UMN signs regardless of imaging findings, that "mild cord contact" on MRI combined with UMN signs constitutes myelopathy per clinical definition, and that 5 weeks of PT was completed under the myelopathy pathway (which requires 4 weeks).

---

**Appeal Determination and Rationale:**

Upon thorough review, we have determined that the denial should be **OVERTURNED**. Our decision is based on the following analysis:

**1. Diagnosis of Cervical Myelopathy:**

*   **HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(b)):** Requires "spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms." The initial denial asserted that "no cord compression, no cord signal change" and that UMN signs were "not conclusive for myelopathy in the absence of imaging confirmation."
*   **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines, as noted in Clinical Record and Clinical Reference Summary):** Explicitly states, "Clinical myelopathy diagnosis requires UMN signs on examination. Imaging confirmation supports but is not required for diagnosis. Imaging-negative myelopathy is a recognized entity where clinical signs precede MRI changes. Multiple positive UMN signs in the absence of other explanation = clinical myelopathy." The patient presents with unequivocal UMN signs (bilateral Hoffman, hyperreflexia, clonus, finger escape, wide-based gait). The MRI findings of "mild cord contact" at C5-C6, while not "measurable compression," represent structural pathology consistent with the clinical picture of early or imaging-negative myelopathy.
*   **Conflict Resolution:** Under CHSC § 1374.32(b) and DMHC APL 22-014 II.3, HealthFirst's internal criteria cannot be more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. The plan's requirement for definitive imaging-confirmed compression to diagnose myelopathy, when robust clinical UMN signs are present and imaging shows disc pathology with cord contact, conflicts with established clinical guidelines that recognize imaging-negative myelopathy. The clinical findings are sufficient to establish a primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy.

**2. Conservative Treatment Requirement (Physical Therapy):**

*   **HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a) and Exception 2.2(A)):** Standard pathway requires 6 weeks of PT. The myelopathy exception reduces this to a 4-week minimum. The initial denial denied based on the standard 6-week requirement, implying the myelopathy exception was not applied.
*   **Application of Plan Criteria:** If the diagnosis of myelopathy is established (as determined above), the myelopathy exception (2.2(A)) would apply, reducing the PT requirement to a 4-week minimum. The member completed 5 weeks of PT, which exceeds this 4-week minimum. Therefore, even by the plan's own myelopathy exception, the PT requirement was met.
*   **California State Law (CIC § 10169.5(a)):** This law explicitly states that a health insurer shall not condition approval of surgery on completion of physical therapy where:
    *   **(1) Physician documents PT contraindicated or unlikely to benefit:** The treating physician halted PT citing "myelopathy concern" and documented "neurological signs unchanged," indicating PT was unlikely to benefit the primary myelopathic symptoms.
    *   **(2) Member completed PT and reached a functional plateau:** The clinical record confirms "neurological signs unchanged" after 5 weeks (15 sessions). This meets the clinical standard definition of a functional plateau for neurological symptoms, which PT is not expected to address in myelopathy.
    *   **(3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address:** The presence of multiple, confirmed UMN signs and "mild cord contact" on MRI constitutes objective evidence of neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot resolve.
    *   **Conflict Resolution:** CIC § 10169.5 overrides the plan's arbitrary minimum PT duration, as the conditions for waiver have been clearly met.
*   **Clinical Standard (AO Spine 2021, NASS 2020, AANS/CNS 2023):** "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients... is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." The guidelines further emphasize that applying radiculopathy-derived PT requirements to myelopathy patients is a "category error."

**Conclusion:**

The plan's denial was based on an overly restrictive interpretation of the myelopathy diagnosis that contradicts generally accepted clinical standards recognizing imaging-negative myelopathy, and an erroneous application of conservative treatment requirements. The member's robust clinical presentation of myelopathy, combined with structural pathology on MRI, satisfies the diagnostic criteria for surgery under generally accepted guidelines. Furthermore, the completed 5 weeks of physical therapy meets the plan's own myelopathy exception (4 weeks minimum) and, more importantly, the requirement for additional PT is waived under California state law due to functional plateau and the nature of the neurological compromise. Delaying surgery for further conservative treatment is not supported by current medical evidence for established myelopathy.

**Disposition:** **OVERTURNED**
