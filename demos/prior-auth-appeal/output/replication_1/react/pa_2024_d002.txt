**APPEAL DETERMINATION: PA-2024-D002**

**Member:** Michael Green, M-445577
**Procedure:** Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C5-C6, one level (CPT 22551)
**Date of Denial:** 2024-11-08
**Disposition:** OVERTURNED

**WARRANT:**

The denial for Michael Green's requested ACDF at C5-C6 is **OVERTURNED** based on multiple grounds, including significant procedural defects in the initial denial notice, the member's satisfaction of the health plan's own criteria, and alignment with California state law and generally accepted clinical standards.

**1. Procedural Defects of the Initial Denial:**

The initial denial notice from HealthFirst PPO Gold was procedurally defective, rendering it invalid under California law.
*   The denial notice referenced an attached criteria document (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024) but failed to provide this document to the member and provider. This directly violates **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a)(2)**, which requires the denial notice to include "the specific criteria applied."
*   Furthermore, **CHSC § 1374.31(b)** explicitly states, "A denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is DEFECTIVE and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."
*   The denial notice also failed to include notice of the member's Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights, which is mandated by **CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2)**.

These procedural deficiencies alone are sufficient grounds to overturn the denial, as they prevent the member and provider from adequately understanding and appealing the decision.

**2. Application of HealthFirst's Own Plan Criteria:**

Upon review of the provided plan criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024) against Michael Green's clinical record, the member **meets** all applicable medical necessity criteria for cervical radiculopathy.

*   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy C5-C6 with functional impairment, supported by MRI findings of a large disc herniation and moderate left C6 nerve root compression. The neurological exam findings (left C6 dermatomal numbness, diminished bicep reflex, bicep weakness 4/5 left) are consistent with radiculopathy. (CRITERIA MET)
    *   *Note:* It is critical to distinguish radiculopathy from myelopathy. The clinical record explicitly states "No cord contact" and presents no upper motor neuron signs (e.g., hyperreflexia, Hoffman's sign, gait disturbance), confirming radiculopathy, not myelopathy. Therefore, the myelopathy exception (2.2(A)) does not apply, nor are the specific urgent myelopathy provisions in DMHC APL 22-014 relevant to this case.

*   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **(a) Physical therapy:** The plan requires a "minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy." Michael Green completed 7 weeks of PT, with notes documenting a functional plateau at week 6. This exceeds the minimum requirement and indicates the maximum benefit from PT has been reached. (CRITERIA MET)
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** The plan requires a trial of "at least two... for minimum 4 weeks each." The member underwent a trial of Gabapentin (4 months, partial relief) and Naproxen (3 months, inadequate relief). Both agents were used for durations exceeding the minimum. (CRITERIA MET)
    *   **(c) Interventional treatments:** The plan requires "one of the following." The member received a Left C6 epidural steroid injection, which provided only 40% relief and returned to baseline within 3 weeks, indicating failure of this intervention to provide lasting relief. (CRITERIA MET)

*   **2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:** Current MRI (2024-09-20) demonstrates C5-C6 disc herniation with C6 nerve root compression, consistent with the member's symptoms and the proposed surgical level. (CRITERIA MET)

Given that the member's clinical situation meets all of HealthFirst's own criteria for surgical authorization, the initial denial based on "Member does not meet criteria" appears to be an incorrect application of the plan's policy.

**3. Compliance with California State Law:**

Even if HealthFirst had correctly applied its criteria, California state law would support overturning this denial.
*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2)** explicitly states that a health insurer cannot condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." Michael Green completed 7 weeks of PT and documented a plateau at week 6 with persistent radicular symptoms. This statutory protection overrides any plan-specific mechanical application of PT duration if the member has already plateaued.
*   While DMHC APL 22-014 specifically addresses myelopathy, its general principle in **II.3** that "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice" remains relevant.

**4. Adherence to Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:**

The requested ACDF for Michael Green is consistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice for cervical radiculopathy.
*   **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) and NASS (2020)** classify radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment and significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care as a **TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION** for surgical intervention.
*   Michael Green has undergone 7 weeks of PT, multi-agent pharmacotherapy for 3-4 months, and an epidural injection, all failing to provide adequate or lasting relief from persistent radicular symptoms including weakness and numbness. This course of failed conservative treatment, coupled with objective neurological deficit and imaging findings, aligns with clinical guidelines supporting surgical decompression.
*   The clinical evidence base clearly distinguishes myelopathy from radiculopathy, noting that while conservative treatment is often effective for radiculopathy, prolonged trials are not necessary if symptoms persist and functional impairment is significant after an adequate attempt.

**CONCLUSION:**

The initial denial was procedurally deficient, invalidating the decision under California law. Furthermore, a thorough review of Michael Green's clinical record demonstrates that all of HealthFirst's own medical necessity criteria for cervical radiculopathy have been met. The denial also conflicts with California state law regarding physical therapy requirements and generally accepted clinical standards for surgical intervention for persistent cervical radiculopathy.

Therefore, the prior authorization denial is **OVERTURNED**. HealthFirst PPO Gold is instructed to approve the requested ACDF C5-C6 for Michael Green.
