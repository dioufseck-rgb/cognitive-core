Dear HealthFirst Appeals Committee,

This letter serves as the official determination for the prior authorization appeal concerning Mr. Robert Chang's request for an anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6. This appeal was reviewed applying the three-source hierarchy: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.

**I. Case Summary:**

Mr. Robert Chang, a 38-year-old male, presented to the Emergency Room on 2024-11-10 with acute onset bilateral arm weakness and difficulty walking, progressing over 72 hours. Neurological examination revealed significant deficits, including bilateral arm weakness 2/5, bilateral leg weakness 3/5, hyperreflexia, bilateral Babinski signs, and urinary urgency, consistent with an acute incomplete spinal cord syndrome. An emergent MRI of the cervical spine performed on the same day showed severe C5-C6 central cord compression with extensive T2 hyperintensity throughout the cord (myelomalacia), indicating acute cord injury.

The treating neurosurgeon, Dr. Elena Marchetti, requested emergency authorization for ACDF C5-C6, declaring the patient's condition an "acute cervical myelopathy with rapidly progressive deficits – surgical emergency." Dr. Marchetti explicitly documented that physical therapy (PT) was "absolutely contraindicated and would be dangerous," further stating that "initiating PT would risk converting incomplete cord syndrome to complete cord syndrome" due to the extensive myelomalacia. Standard acute spinal cord injury protocol, including IV methylprednisolone, was administered in the ER. No PT or interventional procedures were initiated due to the acute, emergent nature and contraindication.

HealthFirst denied the initial prior authorization request on 2024-11-18, citing "MED-003: Physical therapy not completed: 0 weeks documented. No pharmacotherapy documented. No interventional procedure documented. Member has not initiated conservative treatment. Request denied pending completion of required conservative treatment under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2." This denial was issued by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD.

The appeal argues that the member presented with acute myelopathy, PT was never started because it was deemed to cause immediate harm, MRI showed severe cord compression with extensive myelomalacia, and this is an emergency surgical situation where California Insurance Code (CIC) 10169.5(a)(3) applies from day one.

**II. Review Findings:**

My review meticulously examined the clinical record, HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 criteria, applicable California state laws, and generally accepted clinical standards.

1.  **Primary Clinical Presentation:**
    The clinical record unequivocally establishes a diagnosis of **acute, severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia**. This is critically important as myelopathy requires a fundamentally different treatment algorithm than radiculopathy. The symptoms (bilateral weakness, gait disturbance, hyperreflexia, Babinski, urinary urgency) and imaging (severe cord compression, extensive T2 myelomalacia) are classic for this diagnosis. The "CLINICAL REFERENCE SUMMARY" explicitly states that applying radiculopathy-derived conservative treatment requirements to myelopathy patients is "NOT supported by clinical evidence."

2.  **HealthFirst's Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
    *   **Section 2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** Mr. Chang clearly meets criterion 2.1(b) for confirmed cervical myelopathy.
    *   **Section 2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:** The initial denial cited a lack of conservative treatment under Section 2.2.
    *   **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY:** This exception applies as myelopathy is the primary diagnosis. Under this exception, the PT requirement is reduced to 4 weeks, and interventional treatment is waived if the physician documents risk. Pharmacotherapy remains in full (two agents for 4+ weeks each).
        *   The initial denial *did not specifically refer to or apply* the myelopathy exception 2.2(A) in its reasoning, stating only "Section 2.2." This suggests the plan reviewer either overlooked the specific myelopathy exception or deemed the acute intervention (24h IV methylprednisolone) insufficient to meet even the reduced pharmacotherapy requirement. However, the appeal basis directly challenges the applicability of *any* conservative treatment requirement in this urgent scenario.

3.  **California Regulatory Requirements:**
    *   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This state law explicitly limits a plan's ability to condition surgical approval on physical therapy.
        *   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1):** Prohibits requiring PT where "The member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition." Dr. Marchetti's clear documentation that PT is "absolutely contraindicated and would be dangerous" and would "risk converting incomplete cord syndrome to complete cord syndrome" directly triggers this provision.
        *   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3):** Prohibits requiring PT where "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The MRI findings of "Severe C5-C6 central cord compression" and "extensive T2 hyperintensity throughout cord (extensive myelomalacia)" unequivocally constitute such objective evidence. Myelomalacia specifically indicates existing cord injury that PT cannot address.
        *   **CIC § 10169.5(c):** States that a plan's minimum PT requirement "shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether... the condition involves neurological urgency." The plan's denial mechanically applied a conservative treatment requirement without addressing the documented contraindications and neurological urgency.
        *   **Conclusion:** CIC § 10169.5 directly overrides HealthFirst's conservative treatment requirements for PT in this specific case.
    *   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 — Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions:** This guidance specifically addresses prior authorization for time-sensitive neurological conditions.
        *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** States that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent." Mr. Chang's presentation perfectly aligns with this classification.
        *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** Mandates that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when "objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia)" and surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines. The APL specifically identifies "myelomalacia on MRI" as a finding that "may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements even where a full course has not been completed, when the treating physician documents urgency." This provision directly applies to Mr. Chang's case.
        *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** Reaffirms that "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice."
        *   **Conclusion:** DMHC APL 22-014 directly mandates the waiver of conservative treatment requirements for Mr. Chang's condition.
    *   **CHSC § 1374.31(b) — Denial Notice Adequacy:** While the denial cited "CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2," its failure to acknowledge or specifically address the documented acute myelopathy, myelomalacia, and the treating physician's contraindication to PT, or the applicability of the plan's own myelopathy exception (2.2A), renders the specific clinical reasoning inadequate.

4.  **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
    *   **Myelomalacia Significance:** The "CLINICAL REFERENCE SUMMARY" highlights myelomalacia (T2 signal change) as indicating "existing spinal cord injury/damage," "surgical urgency," and a "contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials."
    *   **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** For "moderate-severe myelopathy," physical therapy "does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement." The AO Spine Guidelines (2021) explicitly state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." Mr. Chang's symptoms (2/5 arm weakness, 3/5 leg weakness) are indicative of moderate to severe myelopathy.
    *   **Risk of Delay:** Clinical guidelines consistently emphasize that "Each month of delay in surgical treatment for moderate-severe myelopathy is associated with decreased likelihood of neurological recovery," and myelomalacia is a "strongest predictor of incomplete neurological recovery postoperatively."
    *   **Surgical Indications:** Per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), Mr. Chang's condition meets multiple "TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION" criteria for surgery: (a) Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression, (c) Myelomalacia on MRI, and (d) Acute severe myelopathy with risk of permanent deficit.
    *   **Conclusion:** Clinical standards overwhelmingly support immediate surgical intervention and contraindicate a trial of conservative therapy, particularly PT, given the acute, severe myelopathy with myelomalacia and progressive neurological deficits.

**III. Conflict Resolution and Disposition:**

The plan's denial was based on its internal conservative treatment criteria (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2). However, both California state laws and generally accepted clinical standards directly contradict the application of these requirements to Mr. Chang's acute, severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia.

Applying the three-source hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria):

1.  **State Law Precedence:** CIC § 10169.5 (a)(1) and (a)(3) explicitly prohibit conditioning surgery on PT when contraindicated by the physician or when objective neurological compromise (like myelomalacia) exists. DMHC APL 22-014 mandates waiving conservative treatment requirements for urgent myelopathy with myelomalacia. These state laws directly override HealthFirst's conservative treatment requirements.
2.  **Clinical Standard Precedence:** Leading clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) deem moderate-to-severe myelopathy with myelomalacia a strong surgical indication, advise against delaying surgery for PT, and explicitly state that conservative treatment, especially PT, is not supported by evidence for such presentations and can be harmful. This directly contradicts the plan's criteria.
3.  **Plan Criteria:** While HealthFirst has a myelopathy exception (2.2A), it still requires a 4-week PT minimum and pharmacotherapy, which is more restrictive than what state law and clinical standards permit for this urgent, severe presentation. The plan's criteria, as applied in the denial, are therefore superseded.

Based on the compelling evidence from the clinical record and the clear guidance provided by California state law and generally accepted clinical standards, the denial of prior authorization for ACDF C5-C6 for Mr. Robert Chang is inconsistent with the required hierarchy of authority.

**Disposition:** **OVERTURNED**

**IV. Warrant for Disposition:**

The denial is OVERTURNED because the member presents with an acute, severe cervical myelopathy with rapidly progressive neurological deficits and extensive myelomalacia, which is a surgical emergency. The treating neurosurgeon has documented that physical therapy (PT) is absolutely contraindicated and dangerous, posing an immediate risk of permanent complete paralysis. This situation directly triggers exceptions under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3), which prohibit a health plan from conditioning surgical approval on PT completion where a physician documents contraindication or objective neurological compromise that PT cannot address. Furthermore, DMHC All-Plan Letter 22-014 explicitly mandates that plans shall not require a full course of conservative treatment for such time-sensitive neurological conditions with myelomalacia where surgical intervention is indicated by generally accepted clinical guidelines. The AANS/CNS (2023), NASS (2020), and AO Spine (2021) guidelines unanimously classify this presentation as a TIER 1 strong surgical indication, with no high-quality evidence supporting mandatory PT and strong evidence against delaying surgery, particularly in the presence of myelomalacia. The plan's internal criteria, as cited in the denial, are more restrictive than both state law and clinical standards and are therefore superseded.
