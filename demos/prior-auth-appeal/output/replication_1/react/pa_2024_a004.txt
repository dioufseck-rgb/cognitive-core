**Member:** Robert Chang (M-445566), Age 38
**Procedure Requested:** ACDF C5-C6, one level (CPT 22551)
**Requested By:** Dr. Elena Marchetti, MD (Neurosurgery)
**Date of Denial:** 2024-11-18
**Reviewer:** Dr. Patricia Huang, MD (initial level)
**Appeal Basis:** Acute myelopathy, PT contraindicated, severe cord compression with extensive myelomalacia, emergency surgical situation (CIC 10169.5(a)(3) applies).

**DISPOSITION: OVERTURN DENIAL**

---

**WARRANT:**

The denial for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) is hereby **OVERTURNED**. The initial denial was based on the failure to complete conservative treatment requirements as outlined in HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. However, a comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's own criteria, California state law, and generally accepted clinical standards reveals that the denial is inconsistent with both regulatory requirements and best medical practice for the member's urgent clinical presentation.

**1. Clinical Presentation and Diagnosis:**
The member, Robert Chang, presents with **acute cervical myelopathy** (spinal cord compression) with rapidly progressive neurological deficits. The clinical record documents acute onset bilateral arm weakness (2/5), bilateral leg weakness (3/5), hyperreflexia, bilateral Babinski signs, and urinary urgency, indicating an incomplete spinal cord syndrome. Emergency MRI findings on 2024-11-10 demonstrate "Severe C5-C6 central cord compression" and "Extensive T2 hyperintensity throughout cord (extensive myelomalacia)." These findings unequivocally confirm a diagnosis of acute, moderate-severe cervical myelopathy.

**2. Application of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
*   **Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement):** The member clearly meets criterion 2.1(b) for confirmed cervical myelopathy, supported by MRI and neurological exam. **MET.**
*   **Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement):** Current imaging (MRI 2024-11-10) demonstrates severe C5-C6 central cord compression and myelomalacia consistent with symptoms. **MET.**
*   **Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement) and Exception 2.2(A) — Myelopathy Pathway:**
    *   The initial denial cited failure to complete the standard conservative treatment (6 weeks PT, 2 pharmacotherapy agents for 4+ weeks, 1 interventional procedure) from Section 2.2.
    *   However, the plan's own criteria include **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY**, which applies when myelopathy is the primary diagnosis (as it is here). This exception reduces the PT requirement to 4 weeks minimum and waives interventional treatment if the physician documents risk.
    *   The initial reviewer **failed to apply the plan's own myelopathy exception (2.2A)**, which is a significant error in applying HealthFirst's internal policy. The denial reason explicitly refers to "required conservative treatment under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2" without acknowledging or addressing the applicable exception.

**3. Application of California Regulatory Requirements (State Law Hierarchy):**
Even if the plan's myelopathy exception were correctly applied, California state law takes precedence and overrides the plan's remaining conservative treatment requirements in this case.

*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This law explicitly prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of surgery on the completion of physical therapy where:
    *   **(a)(1):** The treating physician documents PT is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit. Dr. Marchetti clearly documented that "PT is absolutely contraindicated and would be dangerous. Initiating PT would risk converting incomplete cord syndrome to complete cord syndrome." This satisfies CIC § 10169.5(a)(1).
    *   **(a)(3):** The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. The MRI's finding of "Severe C5-C6 central cord compression" and "Extensive T2 hyperintensity throughout cord (extensive myelomalacia)" constitutes objective evidence of structural neurological compromise. This satisfies CIC § 10169.5(a)(3).
    *   Therefore, under California law, the plan **cannot require** completion of physical therapy for this member.

*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This guidance explicitly addresses cases like the member's:
    *   **II.1 (Myelopathy Classification):** States that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent." The member's condition falls precisely within this classification.
    *   **II.2 (Conservative Treatment Exceptions):** Stipulates that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when... (a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia)... (b) Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression... (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines."
        *   The MRI showing myelomalacia satisfies (a).
        *   Dr. Marchetti's documentation of the acute, rapidly progressive nature and the contraindication to PT due to risk satisfies (b) and the general urgency.
        *   As discussed below, surgical intervention is strongly indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines, satisfying (c).
    *   Crucially, APL 22-014 specifically states: "The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements even where a full course has not been completed, when the treating physician documents urgency." This directly applies to Mr. Chang's case and mandates a waiver of the conservative treatment requirements.

**4. Application of Clinical Standards (Generally Accepted Medical Practice):**
Leading clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) consistently reinforce the urgency and primary surgical indication for acute cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia:

*   **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines emphasize that myelopathy and radiculopathy are fundamentally different conditions requiring distinct treatment algorithms. The evidence base for conservative treatment (including prolonged PT) is primarily for radiculopathy, not myelopathy. Applying radiculopathy-derived conservative treatment requirements to myelopathy is a "category error" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.1).
*   **Myelomalacia Significance:** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change on MRI) is a strong indicator of existing spinal cord injury, increased risk of permanent neurological deficit with delayed treatment, and is considered a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2). It necessitates surgical urgency.
*   **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** For moderate to severe myelopathy, physical therapy "does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement" (Cochrane Review, 2021 cited in Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1). The AO Spine Guidelines (2021) explicitly state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." This directly contradicts the plan's denial reason.
*   **Surgical Indication:** Mr. Chang's presentation (acute, severe myelopathy, objective cord compression, extensive myelomalacia, rapidly progressive deficits) meets multiple **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION (surgery recommended)** criteria under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.1).
*   **Pharmacotherapy:** While the plan's myelopathy exception retains the pharmacotherapy requirement (two agents for 4+ weeks each), this is not supported by clinical guidelines for acute, severe myelopathy with myelomalacia where surgical urgency is paramount. The 24-hour IV methylprednisolone administered in the ER is consistent with acute spinal cord injury protocol, but a prolonged *conservative* pharmacotherapy trial would be inappropriate and dangerous given the rapidly progressing neurological deficits and myelomalacia.

**5. Hierarchy of Authority and Conflict Resolution:**
The hierarchy of authority is State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.

*   The plan's denial was based on its own criteria (Section 2.2) but failed to properly apply its internal myelopathy exception (2.2A).
*   Even if the plan's exception was applied, the remaining PT requirement (4 weeks) is directly prohibited by **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) & (3)** due to physician documentation of contraindication and objective structural neurological compromise.
*   Furthermore, **DMHC APL 22-014** and **generally accepted clinical standards** override *any* requirement for a full course of conservative treatment, including pharmacotherapy, for acute, severe myelopathy with myelomalacia where surgery is indicated. The clinical evidence strongly indicates that delaying surgery to complete such requirements is medically inappropriate and harmful.
*   Per **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**, the service must be approved if it meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, regardless of more restrictive plan criteria. The requested ACDF for this member's condition meets these standards.

**Conclusion:**
The member's acute cervical myelopathy with severe cord compression and extensive myelomalacia is a time-sensitive, surgically urgent condition. The plan's denial, based on a failure to complete conservative treatment requirements, is directly contradicted by California state law (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine), which prioritize immediate surgical intervention in such cases and explicitly advise against delaying surgery for conservative trials, including physical therapy. The initial reviewer's failure to apply the plan's own myelopathy exception further undermines the denial.

---

**ACTION:**

The prior authorization denial for CPT 22551 for Robert Chang is hereby **OVERTURNED**. Authorization for the Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 is approved as medically necessary.
