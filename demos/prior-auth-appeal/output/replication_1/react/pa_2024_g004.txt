**I. BACKGROUND**

Stephanie Oduya, a 44-year-old member of HealthFirst PPO Gold, sought prior authorization for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6. The request was initially denied by HealthFirst on 2024-10-15 (reason code MED-003), citing "mild-moderate disc herniation with no cord compression and no neurological deficit on examination" and that the "conservative treatment pathway not completed." The denial was based on an independent clinical review by Dr. Rivera (Neurosurgery).

Dr. Walsh, the treating spine surgeon, appealed the denial on grounds that Dr. Rivera's interpretation was inadequate, his own 25 years of experience should take precedence, and the patient risks permanent disability without immediate surgery.

---

**II. CLINICAL RECORD SUMMARY**

*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Cervical radiculopathy C5-C6, right-sided.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-10):** C5-C6 mild to moderate disc herniation. Right C6 nerve root *contact without measurable compression*. **No cord contact. No cord compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia.** Mild right foraminal narrowing.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-09-15):** Right shoulder pain and neck pain 6/10. RIGHT C6 dermatomal tingling (mild). Bicep reflex 2+ bilateral, symmetric. Grip strength 5/5 bilateral. **No weakness. No UMN signs.** Functional assessment: working full-time, no activity limitations.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 4 weeks, 12 sessions. "Mild improvement in pain. No neurological deficit to track. Dr. Walsh elected to halt PT."
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Naproxen 500mg BID for 8 weeks, with "Moderate pain relief." (One agent)
    *   **Interventional:** None.
*   **Treating Surgeon Statement (Dr. Walsh, 2024-10-18):** Disputes Dr. Rivera's review, asserts "significant cervical pathology" and immediate surgical need based on his clinical judgment and experience, and warns of permanent disability.

**III. REVIEW ANALYSIS**

This review applies the hierarchy of authority: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.

**A. Clinical Presentation: Radiculopathy vs. Myelopathy**
The clinical record clearly defines the patient's condition as **cervical radiculopathy**.
*   The presenting diagnosis is "Cervical radiculopathy C5-C6, right-sided."
*   MRI shows nerve root *contact* but explicitly states **"No cord contact. No cord compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia."**
*   The neurological exam reveals right C6 dermatomal tingling, but **"No weakness. No UMN signs."**
*   Per **Clinical Standard Knowledge** and **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), Section 1.1**, these findings are characteristic of radiculopathy, not myelopathy. The absence of cord compression, myelomalacia, and upper motor neuron signs (UMN) definitively rules out cervical myelopathy as the primary diagnosis or a significant finding requiring urgent intervention. Dr. Rivera's initial denial accurately notes the absence of cord compression and neurological deficit.

**B. HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Effective: January 1, 2024)**
The plan's criteria for CPT 22551 requires satisfying all criteria in Section 2.
*   **2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** Member has cervical radiculopathy (2.1(a)) consistent with symptoms.
*   **2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:** This section is key to the denial.
    *   **(a) Physical therapy:** Requires a minimum of 6 weeks. Member completed only 4 weeks. **FAILED.**
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** Requires a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. Member completed only one agent (Naproxen) for 8 weeks. **FAILED.**
    *   **(c) Interventional treatment:** Requires one interventional treatment. Member completed none. **FAILED.**
*   **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY:** This exception reduces PT to 4 weeks and waives interventional treatment for confirmed cervical myelopathy (criterion 2.1(b)) where myelopathy is the *primary diagnosis*. As established above, the member does not have cervical myelopathy. Therefore, this exception **does not apply**.

Based on HealthFirst's plan criteria, the request fails to meet the conservative treatment requirements.

**C. California Regulatory Framework**

*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute outlines conditions under which PT completion cannot be required.
    *   **(a)(1) Physician documents PT contraindicated or unlikely to benefit:** Dr. Walsh elected to halt PT and requested surgery but did not provide specific documentation that PT was *contraindicated* or *unlikely to benefit* given the *nature and severity of radiculopathy*. His letter states she needs surgery *now*, not why PT is ineffective for her radiculopathy.
    *   **(a)(2) Member completed PT and reached functional plateau:** The record states "Mild improvement in pain. No neurological deficit to track." This does not meet the functional plateau definition from **Clinical Standard Knowledge, Section 5** (less than 10% improvement over 3 sessions or therapist documentation of maximum benefit). Mild pain improvement was noted, and "no neurological deficit to track" is due to the absence of objective neurological deficits, not a plateau in their recovery.
    *   **(a)(3) Objective evidence of structural neurological compromise:** MRI explicitly shows "No cord compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia." This exception **does not apply**.
*   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This All-Plan Letter specifically addresses myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia, identifying them as conditions risking irreversible harm. The patient's clinical presentation (radiculopathy, no cord compression, no myelomalacia) means this APL is **not applicable** to this case.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This provision states that if a service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice," it shall be approved regardless of more restrictive plan criteria. This requires evaluating the clinical standard for radiculopathy.

**D. Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)**

*   **Radiculopathy Treatment:** Per **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), Section 3.1**, surgery for radiculopathy falls under TIER 2 ("Reasonable Indication") and is typically indicated "with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx." The **Clinical Reference Summary, Section 4 Table** confirms that for radiculopathy, PT duration is typically 6-12 weeks, surgery indication is *after PT trial*, urgency is *elective*, and risk of PT delay is *low*.
*   The patient has not completed a 6-12 week conservative trial. The treating surgeon's assertion of urgency and risk of "permanent disability" is not supported by the objective clinical findings for *radiculopathy*, which lacks cord compression or myelomalacia.

**E. Conflict Resolution & Hierarchy Application**

1.  **State Law:** CIC 10169.5's exceptions for PT completion are not met. DMHC APL 22-014 regarding urgency for myelopathy is not applicable.
2.  **Clinical Standard:** For radiculopathy, generally accepted standards recommend a conservative treatment trial of 6-12 weeks *before* surgical intervention is considered a reasonable option. The patient has not completed this. There is no clinical consensus supporting immediate surgical intervention for mild-moderate radiculopathy without objective neurological deficits or signs of myelopathy/cord damage.
3.  **Plan Criteria:** HealthFirst's plan criteria align with the clinical standard by requiring conservative treatment (6 weeks PT, 2 pharmacotherapy agents, 1 interventional). The patient has not met these criteria.

The clinical record demonstrates radiculopathy, which generally responds well to conservative treatment. Neither state law nor the clinical standard supports bypassing the conservative treatment pathway for this specific presentation. The treating surgeon's strong qualitative appeal, while highlighting his experience, does not provide objective clinical evidence to override the generally accepted guidelines for managing cervical radiculopathy, particularly given the absence of myelopathy or objective neurological deficits.

---

**IV. DISPOSITION**

The denial is **UPHELD**.

---

**V. WARRANT**

The denial for CPT code 22551 for member Stephanie Oduya is UPHELD because the clinical record confirms a diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy, not cervical myelopathy. For cervical radiculopathy, both HealthFirst's plan criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2) and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023, Section 3.1 TIER 2(b); Clinical Reference Summary Section 4 Table) require a more extensive and comprehensive conservative treatment pathway than what has been completed.

Specifically:
1.  **Plan Criteria Not Met:** The member completed only 4 weeks of physical therapy, whereas 6 weeks are required (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a)). She received only one pharmacotherapy agent (Naproxen), whereas two are required (Section 2.2(b)). No interventional treatment was performed, though one is required (Section 2.2(c)).
2.  **Myelopathy Exception Not Applicable:** The plan's myelopathy exception (Section 2.2(A)) is not applicable as the clinical record, including MRI findings ("No cord contact. No cord compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia.") and neurological exam ("No weakness. No UMN signs."), clearly rules out cervical myelopathy (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023, Section 1.1).
3.  **State Law Exceptions Not Met:** The conditions under which California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 permits waiver of physical therapy requirements are not met. There is no physician documentation of physical therapy being contraindicated or unlikely to benefit given the nature of her *radiculopathy* (CIC § 10169.5(a)(1)), nor clear documentation that a functional plateau was reached per the established definition (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2); Clinical Reference Summary Section 5). Critically, there is no objective evidence of structural neurological compromise such as myelomalacia or cord compression (CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)), rendering DMHC APL 22-014 irrelevant to this case.
4.  **Clinical Standard Supports Conservative Trial:** Generally accepted clinical guidelines indicate surgery for radiculopathy is a reasonable option only "after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023, Section 3.1 TIER 2(b)). The patient has not completed this duration of conservative management. The risk of delaying surgery for radiculopathy is considered low (Clinical Reference Summary Section 4 Table), in contrast to myelopathy cases.

Therefore, both HealthFirst's plan criteria and generally accepted clinical standards, supported by California state law, require a more complete conservative treatment trial prior to surgical authorization for this member's presentation of cervical radiculopathy.
