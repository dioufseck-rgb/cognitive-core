**WARRANT FOR DISPOSITION:**

The request for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) for member Stephanie Oduya is upheld due to insufficient completion of conservative treatment as required by both HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria and generally accepted clinical standards for cervical radiculopathy.

This determination is based on a comprehensive review of the submitted clinical record, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), relevant California regulatory frameworks (CIC, CHSC, DMHC APL), and established clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine), applying the hierarchy: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.

**1. Clinical Presentation Analysis (Radiculopathy vs. Myelopathy):**
The member's presenting diagnosis is "Cervical radiculopathy C5-C6, right-sided." Objective findings support this:
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-10):** Shows C5-C6 mild to moderate disc herniation, right C6 nerve root contact *without measurable compression*, *no cord contact, no cord compression, no T2 signal change, and no myelomalacia*. Mild right foraminal narrowing. (Clinical record)
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-09-15):** Findings include right shoulder and neck pain (6/10), *mild right C6 dermatomal tingling*, but crucially, *no weakness, no Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) signs*, and *no activity limitations* (working full-time). (Clinical record)

These findings are consistent with cervical radiculopathy, which involves compression of an individual nerve root. They **do not** meet the criteria for cervical myelopathy, which would require evidence of spinal cord compression, UMN signs, bilateral symptoms, or myelomalacia (AANS/CNS 2023, Section 1.1). This distinction is critical, as myelopathy and radiculopathy have different treatment algorithms and urgencies.

**2. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
For cervical radiculopathy, HealthFirst's criteria (Section 2.2) require the completion of conservative treatment, including:
*   **(a) Physical Therapy:** Minimum 6 weeks. The member completed 4 weeks of PT. **(Not Met)**
*   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** Trial of at least two agents for minimum 4 weeks each. The member received Naproxen for 8 weeks. Only one agent is documented. **(Not Met)**
*   **(c) Interventional Treatment:** Minimum one procedure. The member had no interventional treatments. **(Not Met)**

The "Myelopathy Exception" (Section 2.2(A)) is not applicable as the member's primary diagnosis is radiculopathy, not myelopathy.

Therefore, the request fails to meet HealthFirst's conservative treatment requirements for surgical authorization for cervical radiculopathy (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 3.2).

**3. Application of California Regulatory Requirements:**

*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute outlines conditions under which a plan cannot require PT completion.
    *   **(a)(1) PT contraindicated or unlikely to benefit:** Dr. Walsh *elected to halt* PT, but did not document it as *contraindicated* or *unlikely to benefit given the nature and severity of the radicular condition*. The PT summary even noted "mild improvement in pain."
    *   **(a)(2) Functional plateau reached:** While PT was halted after 4 weeks, the clinical record does not definitively document that the member reached a "functional plateau" by therapist documentation after a sufficient course of therapy for radiculopathy. The member is working full-time with no activity limitations.
    *   **(a)(3) Objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address:** The MRI explicitly shows "no cord compression" and "no T2 signal change/myelomalacia." This exception, which specifically refers to myelomalacia with cord compression in the domain knowledge, does not apply here.
    *   **Conclusion:** None of the exceptions under CIC § 10169.5 are met to waive the plan's 6-week PT requirement for this radiculopathy presentation.

*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This guidance pertains to "Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions" and "MYELOPATHY CLASSIFICATION" with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia. As established, the member does not have myelopathy, cord compression, or myelomalacia. **(Not Applicable)**

*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard - Generally Accepted Standards):** This statute mandates that services meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice shall be approved regardless of more restrictive plan criteria. This leads to the assessment of clinical guidelines. The initial denial notice was procedurally adequate, citing specific reasons and criteria (CHSC § 1374.31(b)).

**4. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine Guidelines):**

*   **Radiculopathy Treatment:** For cervical radiculopathy, generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020) indicate that conservative treatment is typically effective (70-80% resolution) and is the primary initial management.
*   **Conservative Treatment Duration:** The clinical standard summary (Section 4) indicates that for radiculopathy, physical therapy has "Strong" evidence and a typical minimum duration of "6-12 weeks." Surgery is indicated "After PT trial," and the urgency is "Elective" with a "Low" risk of PT delay.
*   **Surgical Indications:** Per AANS/CNS Guidelines (2023, Section 3.1), the member's presentation does not meet TIER 1 ("Strong Indication") criteria, which are for moderate to severe myelopathy, progressive neurological deficit, myelomalacia, or acute severe myelopathy. For radiculopathy, surgery is considered a "Reasonable Indication" (TIER 2) after "persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx." The member has not completed a 6-12 week conservative trial.
*   **Treating Surgeon's Statement:** While Dr. Walsh's experience is noted, his assertions of "significant cervical pathology," "requires surgery now," and "permanent disability" are **not supported by the objective clinical findings** (no cord compression, no myelomalacia, no weakness, mild tingling, working full-time with no activity limitations). The clinical standards for radiculopathy do not support immediate surgical intervention in this non-urgent presentation.

**5. Hierarchy Resolution:**
There is no conflict among the sources in this case.
*   **State Law:** The California statutes and APL do not provide a basis to override the conservative treatment requirements for *this specific radiculopathy case*.
*   **Clinical Standard:** Generally accepted clinical guidelines for cervical radiculopathy support the need for a more extensive course of conservative treatment (6-12 weeks of PT, and other modalities) before surgical intervention is considered medically necessary.
*   **Plan Criteria:** HealthFirst's plan criteria for conservative treatment for radiculopathy align with the general principles of the clinical standards.

The initial denial correctly identified that the conservative treatment pathway was not completed and that the objective findings did not support immediate surgical intervention. The member's symptoms are mild, and there are no signs of neurological urgency or progressive deficit that would justify bypassing a full conservative trial for radiculopathy.

**Conclusion:**
Based on the objective clinical evidence and established guidelines, the member has not met the medical necessity criteria for an ACDF at this time due to incomplete conservative treatment. Further conservative management, including a full course of physical therapy (6-12 weeks for radiculopathy), a trial of a second pharmacotherapy agent, and consideration of an interventional procedure, is indicated prior to surgical authorization.

---

**DISPOSITION:** The prior authorization denial for CPT 22551 is **UPHELD**.

**Recommendation:** The member should continue with conservative management as outlined in HealthFirst's policy and generally accepted clinical guidelines for cervical radiculopathy. A new prior authorization request may be submitted once a complete and failed conservative treatment pathway has been documented.
