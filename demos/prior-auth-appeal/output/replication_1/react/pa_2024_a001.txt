## HEALTHFIRST HEALTH PLAN — PRIOR AUTHORIZATION APPEAL DETERMINATION

**Case ID:** PA-2024-A001
**Member:** Diana Torres (ID: M-112233)
**Plan:** HealthFirst PPO Gold — Group Plan 77441
**State:** California
**Procedure:** ACDF C5-C6, one level (CPT 22551)
**Requested By:** Dr. James Kim, MD — Neurosurgery
**Denial Date:** 2024-11-01
**Denial Reason:** Member has completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy. Standard pathway requires minimum 6 weeks. Pharmacotherapy requirement not met: only one agent trialed. Member does not meet conservative treatment requirements under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2.
**Review Level:** Initial Denial Appeal

---

**REVIEWER FINDINGS:**

1.  **Member's Clinical Presentation:**
    *   The member, Diana Torres, presents with a confirmed diagnosis of **Progressive Cervical Myelopathy C5-C6 with Myelomalacia**.
    *   Clinical symptoms include a 4-month history of bilateral hand clumsiness, gait instability, and objective upper motor neuron signs (bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, positive Babinski, gait ataxia) documented on neurological exam.
    *   MRI dated 2024-08-15 demonstrates severe central cord compression at C5-C6 with **T2 hyperintensity within the cord (myelomalacia)**.
    *   Conservative treatment consisted of 2 weeks (6 sessions) of physical therapy (PT), which was discontinued by the treating physician due to symptom exacerbation, with documentation that PT was contraindicated. One pharmacotherapy agent (Methylprednisolone taper) was trialed with only temporary mild improvement. No interventional procedures were performed.

2.  **HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) Application:**
    *   **Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement):** The member clearly meets criterion 2.1(b) for Cervical Myelopathy. **MET.**
    *   **Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement):** The plan's initial denial cited the "standard pathway" requiring 6 weeks of PT and 2 pharmacotherapy agents, neither of which were met.
        *   The plan's own policy, **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY**, reduces the PT requirement to a minimum of 4 weeks and waives interventional treatment if the treating physician documents risk. Even under this myelopathy exception, the member only completed 2 weeks of PT, and only one pharmacotherapy agent was trialed.
        *   The denial's explicit reference to "Standard pathway requires minimum 6 weeks" rather than the myelopathy exception's 4 weeks indicates an inconsistency in the application of the plan's own criteria, especially given the clear myelopathy diagnosis.

3.  **California Regulatory Framework Overrides:**
    *   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This state law directly addresses the plan's PT requirement.
        *   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1):** Prohibits conditioning approval on PT where "The member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition." Dr. Kim's letter and the clinical record clearly document that PT was discontinued due to symptom exacerbation and was declared contraindicated, with documented risk of irreversible cord injury. **MET.**
        *   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3):** Prohibits conditioning approval on PT where "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The MRI finding of myelomalacia (T2 hyperintensity within the cord) at C5-C6 is definitive objective evidence of structural neurological compromise. **MET.**
        *   Therefore, under state law, the plan is prohibited from denying based on incomplete physical therapy.
    *   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This regulatory guidance provides specific directives for cases like the member's.
        *   **II.1 (Myelopathy Classification):** The DMHC states that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent." This member's condition perfectly matches this classification.
        *   **II.2 (Conservative Treatment Exceptions):** The APL mandates that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment... when: (a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia...), AND (b) Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression, AND (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines..." All these conditions are **MET** in this case (myelomalacia, PT failure/contraindication, and surgical indication as detailed below). The APL specifically identifies myelomalacia as a finding supporting waiver of standard conservative treatment.
        *   **II.3 (Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard):** The APL clarifies that plans "shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice."
    *   **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) (Standard of Review for IMR):** This section emphasizes that the IMR standard does not require consistency with plan criteria and that services meeting generally accepted standards SHALL be approved regardless of the plan's more restrictive internal criteria.

4.  **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
    *   **Diagnosis & Urgency:** The clinical record describes moderate to severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia. The AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) classify this presentation as a **TIER 1 (STRONG INDICATION)** for surgery (Section 3.1).
    *   **Myelomalacia Significance:** Myelomalacia indicates existing spinal cord injury, is a "strong predictor of permanent deficit if surgery delayed," and is considered a "strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2, 2.3).
    *   **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** The AO Spine Guidelines (2021) explicitly state, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1). The plan's PT requirements (6 or 4 weeks) for this myelopathic presentation are not supported by the clinical evidence and represent a "category error" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.1, 2.2).
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** While the plan requires two pharmacotherapy agents, the clinical urgency associated with myelomalacia and progressive myelopathy, as recognized by clinical guidelines and DMHC APL 22-014, overrides the strict adherence to this requirement when surgery is strongly indicated.

**DISPOSITION:**

**OVERTURN**

---

**WARRANT:**

The denial for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level) for member Diana Torres is **OVERTURNED**.

The member has a clear and urgent diagnosis of **Progressive Cervical Myelopathy with Myelomalacia**. This condition is recognized by generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021) as a strong indication for surgical intervention, and delaying surgery to complete a mandatory course of conservative treatment, particularly physical therapy, is not supported by evidence and may lead to irreversible neurological deterioration (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2, 2.1, 2.3, 3.1).

Furthermore, the plan's denial based on unmet conservative treatment requirements (specifically physical therapy duration and number of pharmacotherapy agents) is directly contradicted and overridden by California state law:

1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3)** explicitly prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy when the treating physician documents PT as contraindicated (as Dr. Kim did due to symptom exacerbation and risk of irreversible cord injury) or when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (as demonstrated by the myelomalacia on MRI).
2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014** mandates that plans shall not require completion of a full course of conservative treatment for myelopathy with myelomalacia when objective imaging shows progressive pathology, the physician documents conservative treatment failure, and surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. The APL specifically identifies myelomalacia as a finding that supports the waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements.

Therefore, the plan's internal criteria for conservative treatment are superseded by both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of care for this patient's specific presentation. The service is medically necessary and meets the standard for approval, despite not meeting the plan's more restrictive criteria.
