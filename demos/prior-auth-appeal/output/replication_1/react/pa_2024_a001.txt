**I. BACKGROUND & INITIAL DENIAL REASON:**

The member, Diana Torres, is a 61-year-old female presenting with progressive cervical myelopathy at C5-C6 with documented myelomalacia. Her treating neurosurgeon, Dr. James Kim, requested prior authorization for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6.

The initial denial by HealthFirst Utilization Management (Dr. Alan Foster, MD) cited reason code MED-003, stating that the member had completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy (standard pathway requires minimum 6 weeks), only one pharmacotherapy agent had been trialed (standard requires two), and therefore, the member did not meet conservative treatment requirements under HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2.

The appeal basis provided by the treating physician asserts that severe myelomalacia on MRI, coupled with the treating physician's declaration that PT is contraindicated due to documented cord signal change, prohibits the physical therapy requirement under California law, specifically citing CIC 10169.5(a)(3). The appeal argues for a regulatory override.

---

**II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION & RECORD REVIEW:**

*   **Diagnosis:** Progressive cervical myelopathy C5-C6 with myelomalacia.
*   **Symptoms:** 4-month history of bilateral hand clumsiness and gait instability. Symptoms worsened during physical therapy.
*   **Neurological Exam:** Bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia bilateral upper and lower extremities, positive Babinski bilateral, gait ataxia. These are definitive upper motor neuron signs, confirming cervical myelopathy.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-08-15):** Severe central cord compression C5-C6 with T2 hyperintensity within the cord (myelomalacia).
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy (PT):** Completed 2 weeks (6 sessions), discontinued per Dr. Kim's order due to symptom exacerbation and documented contraindication.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Methylprednisolone taper for 2 weeks, resulting in temporary mild improvement. Only one agent trialed.
    *   **Interventional:** None.
*   **Treating Physician's Declaration (Dr. Kim, 2024-10-15):** Explicitly states PT is contraindicated due to myelomalacia and risk of irreversible cord injury, citing CIC 10169.5(a)(3), and that surgery is urgently indicated.

---

**III. APPLICATION OF PLAN CRITERIA (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**

HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 policy (Section 2.2) requires a minimum of 6 weeks of physical therapy, a trial of at least two pharmacotherapy agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each, and one interventional treatment.

The policy includes an **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY**, which reduces the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks minimum and waives the interventional treatment if the treating physician documents risk of neurological deterioration. The pharmacotherapy requirement remains in full.

*   **Diagnosis (2.1):** The member clearly meets the criteria for confirmed cervical myelopathy (2.1(b)). Myelopathy is the primary diagnosis, making the myelopathy exception applicable.
*   **Conservative Treatment (2.2):**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** Even with the myelopathy exception, the member completed only 2 weeks, falling short of the plan's 4-week minimum.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** The member only trialed one agent (Methylprednisolone), falling short of the plan's requirement for two agents.
    *   **Interventional:** No interventional treatment was performed. While Dr. Kim documented PT as contraindicated due to risk of cord injury, his letter did not explicitly waive interventional treatment under the plan's specific language.

Based purely on a strict reading of HealthFirst's internal criteria, even with the myelopathy exception, the conservative treatment requirements were not fully met. However, the appeal raises specific regulatory and clinical overrides that must be considered under the established hierarchy.

---

**IV. APPLICATION OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY REQUIREMENTS:**

**A. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:**
This state law, effective January 1, 2023, directly addresses physical therapy requirements. It states that a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where:

*   **(a)(1) The member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member:** Dr. Kim's letter (2024-10-15) explicitly states, "PT is contraindicated in this patient. Myelomalacia is present on initial MRI. Continuing PT risks irreversible cord injury." This directly satisfies subdivision (a)(1).
*   **(a)(3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address:** The MRI finding of "T2 hyperintensity within cord (myelomalacia)" is definitive objective evidence of existing spinal cord injury and structural neurological compromise. Physical therapy is ineffective at resolving myelomalacia and cord compression. This directly satisfies subdivision (a)(3).
*   **(c):** "A plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether... the condition involves neurological urgency under subdivision (a)(3)." HealthFirst's denial mechanically applied its PT duration requirement despite the documented neurological compromise and contraindication.

**Conclusion:** CIC § 10169.5 unequivocally overrides HealthFirst's physical therapy requirement in this case.

**B. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 — Prior Authorization for Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions (Myelopathy):**
This guidance, issued in September 2022, specifically addresses myelopathy cases:

*   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** The DMHC identifies "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI" as a condition with "potential for irreversible harm if untreated" and mandates that plans "SHALL treat such cases as urgent." Diana Torres's case precisely matches this description.
*   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** This section states that for conditions where delay risks irreversible harm, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when:
    *   **(a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia):** The MRI finding of myelomalacia fulfills this criterion.
    *   **(b) Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression:** Dr. Kim documented PT was discontinued due to symptom exacerbation and contraindicated, and pharmacotherapy provided only temporary mild benefit. This meets the intent of this criterion in the context of myelopathy with myelomalacia.
    *   **(c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines:** As detailed below, surgery is strongly indicated per clinical guidelines.
    *   The APL specifically notes that "The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements even where a full course has not been completed, when the treating physician documents urgency." Dr. Kim documented urgency (risk of irreversible cord injury).
*   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for conditions identified in this guidance. Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs."

**Conclusion:** DMHC APL 22-014 mandates a waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements, including both physical therapy and pharmacotherapy duration, for this member's condition.

---

**V. APPLICATION OF CLINICAL STANDARD (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

*   **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** The clinical guidelines emphasize the critical distinction between myelopathy and radiculopathy, noting that conservative treatment evidence is primarily derived from radiculopathy studies and does not apply to myelopathy. Diana Torres's presentation (bilateral symptoms, UMN signs, gait disturbance) is definitively myelopathy.
*   **Myelomalacia Significance:** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change on MRI) is identified as indicating "existing spinal cord injury/damage," "higher risk of permanent deficit," and "surgical urgency." It is considered a strong indication for surgery and a "contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials." (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2).
*   **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** For moderate-severe myelopathy (as in this case), physical therapy "does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement." "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." (AO Spine Guidelines 2021, Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1). The plan's 6-week (or even 4-week) PT requirement has no evidentiary basis for myelopathy and is a "category error." (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.2).
*   **Surgical Indications:** The member meets TIER 1 strong surgical indications: "Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI" and "Myelomalacia on MRI (cord signal change)." (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023, Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.1). For this presentation, the clinical standard "does NOT require completion of a minimum course of physical therapy as a prerequisite to surgery." (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.2).
*   **Risk of Delay:** Delay in surgical treatment for moderate-severe myelopathy with myelomalacia is associated with decreased likelihood of neurological recovery and worse outcomes. (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.3).

**Conclusion:** The clinical standard strongly supports the surgical intervention for Diana Torres's severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and actively advises against mandatory prolonged conservative treatment, especially physical therapy, as it is ineffective and carries a risk of neurological deterioration. The plan's conservative treatment requirements are contrary to generally accepted standards of medical practice for this condition.

---

**VI. CONFLICT RESOLUTION (HIERARCHY: STATE LAW > CLINICAL STANDARD > PLAN CRITERIA):**

The HealthFirst denial was based on its internal plan criteria (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2) regarding conservative treatment duration. However, both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards directly conflict with and override these criteria for this specific clinical scenario:

1.  **State Law Override (CIC § 10169.5):** California law prohibits conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy when the treating physician documents contraindication (which Dr. Kim did) or when objective structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) is present. This directly invalidates the plan's PT requirement.
2.  **State Law Override (DMHC APL 22-014):** California regulatory guidance mandates the waiver of *full conservative treatment* requirements for myelopathy with myelomalacia due to the urgency and risk of irreversible harm, specifically identifying myelomalacia as a supporting finding for waiver. This directly invalidates both the plan's PT and pharmacotherapy duration requirements.
3.  **Clinical Standard Override:** Leading spine society guidelines (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) clearly establish myelopathy with myelomalacia as a strong surgical indication where conservative treatment, particularly prolonged PT, is ineffective, potentially harmful, and not supported by evidence. The plan's criteria are more restrictive and inconsistent with these standards.

Therefore, the plan's denial based on unmet conservative treatment requirements cannot stand, as it is superseded by both state law and prevailing clinical standards of care.

---

**VII. DISPOSITION:**

The denial for CPT code 22551 for Diana Torres is **OVERTURNED**.

---

**VIII. WARRANT:**

The denial for ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is overturned because the plan's conservative treatment requirements, specifically those related to physical therapy and pharmacotherapy duration, are not applicable and are legally overridden by California state law and are inconsistent with generally accepted clinical standards for the member's diagnosis of progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia.

1.  **California Law Prohibits PT Requirement:** Pursuant to California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3), HealthFirst is prohibited from conditioning approval on physical therapy. The treating physician, Dr. Kim, documented that PT was contraindicated due to the risk of irreversible cord injury exacerbated by myelomalacia, and the MRI objectively demonstrates severe central cord compression with T2 hyperintensity (myelomalacia), which represents structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address.
2.  **DMHC Mandates Waiver of Conservative Treatment:** DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 mandates that plans shall not require a full course of conservative treatment for cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI, as this constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm. The presence of myelomalacia specifically supports the waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements. Therefore, the plan's denial for insufficient physical therapy and pharmacotherapy is contrary to state regulatory guidance.
3.  **Clinical Standard Supports Immediate Surgery:** Generally accepted standards of medical practice from AANS/CNS (2023), NASS (2020), and AO Spine (2021) unequivocally classify moderate to severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia as a Tier 1 strong surgical indication. These guidelines explicitly state that there is no high-quality evidence supporting mandatory physical therapy prior to surgery for this condition and that delaying surgery may result in neurological deterioration. The plan's criteria are more restrictive than, and in direct conflict with, these generally accepted standards of care.
4.  **Application of Hierarchy:** Given the conflict between HealthFirst's internal criteria and both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards, the higher authorities (state law and clinical standards) govern, mandating approval of the requested procedure.
