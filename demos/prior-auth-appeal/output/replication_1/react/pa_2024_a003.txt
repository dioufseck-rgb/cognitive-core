**Subject: Appeal Decision for Prior Authorization Request for ACDF C4-C5**

This letter communicates the determination of your appeal regarding the denial of prior authorization for an anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5. We have carefully reviewed the medical record, the original denial, your appeal basis, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), relevant California state laws, and generally accepted clinical standards.

---

**1. Summary of Original Denial:**

The initial request for CPT 22551 (ACDF C4-C5) was denied on 2024-10-15 by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, under reason code MED-003. The denial stated that the "Member has not completed required conservative treatment. Physical therapy: 5 weeks completed, 6 required. No cervical epidural steroid injection on record. Member does not meet Section 2.2 requirements."

---

**2. Summary of Appeal Basis:**

The appeal asserts that:
*   The member has a permanent pacemaker, rendering a cervical epidural steroid injection (CESI) medically contraindicated.
*   The treating physician documented this interventional contraindication in the medical chart.
*   DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, specifically its myelopathy provisions, applies to this case due to documented myelomalacia.

---

**3. Clinical and Regulatory Findings:**

Our review identifies the following critical information from the submitted clinical record and applicable regulatory and clinical guidance:

*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** The member's primary presenting diagnosis is "Progressive cervical myelopathy C4-C5 with myelomalacia." This is supported by a neurological exam demonstrating bilateral lower extremity spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor bilateral hands, and a positive Lhermitte sign \— all indicative of upper motor neuron involvement consistent with myelopathy.
*   **Imaging Findings:** MRI performed on 2024-08-20 (with pacemaker-compatible protocol) confirms "C4-C5 severe central cord compression" and, importantly, "T2 signal change (myelomalacia) confirmed. Progressive cord injury." Myelomalacia signifies existing spinal cord damage.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy (PT):** 5 weeks of supervised PT (15 sessions) were completed. Therapy was stopped due to pacemaker precautions limiting certain modalities and the physician's determination that further PT was not addressing the neurological progression.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** A trial of Baclofen 10mg TID for 3 months provided partial spasticity relief, and Methylprednisolone taper (twice for 10 days each) provided only temporary improvement. This meets the plan's pharmacotherapy requirement.
    *   **Interventional Treatment:** No cervical epidural steroid injection was performed because it is explicitly "CONTRAINDICATED" due to the member's permanent pacemaker. This contraindication was documented by the treating physician and confirmed by an anesthesiology consultation (Dr. Rivera, 2024-09-10).

---

**4. Application of Hierarchy (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):**

**A. HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
The original denial cited Section 2.2, which outlines standard conservative treatment requirements (6 weeks PT, 1 interventional procedure). However, the denial failed to apply **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY**, which is explicitly applicable to this member:
*   **Primary Diagnosis:** The member has a confirmed primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (criterion 2.1(b)) with strong clinical and imaging evidence.
*   **Physical Therapy:** The myelopathy exception reduces the PT requirement to "4 weeks minimum." The member completed 5 weeks of PT, thereby **meeting** this modified requirement.
*   **Interventional Treatment:** The myelopathy exception states the interventional treatment requirement (2.2(c)) is "WAIVED if treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses risk of neurological deterioration." The treating physician clearly documented a medical contraindication to cervical epidural steroid injection due to the member's permanent pacemaker. This condition is **met**, and the requirement for an interventional procedure is waived.

Therefore, even under HealthFirst's own criteria, the member meets the requirements for surgical authorization through the Myelopathy Exception pathway, which was not appropriately applied during the initial review.

**B. California State Law and Regulations:**

*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (September 2022) — Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions:**
    *   Section II.1 mandates that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent." The member's MRI clearly shows severe central cord compression and myelomalacia.
    *   Section II.2 states that for conditions where delay of treatment risks irreversible harm, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when:
        *   Objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia and progressive cord injury confirmed).
        *   Treating physician documents conservative treatment failure (PT stopped as not addressing neurological progression).
        *   Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines (as confirmed below).
    *   The APL specifically notes that "the presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements."
    *   Section II.3 dictates that plans "shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice."
    *   **Conclusion:** DMHC APL 22-014 directly overrides any plan criteria that would require further conservative treatment for this member's condition.

*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Effective January 1, 2023) — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:**
    *   This statute prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where:
        *   "(1) The member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition." (Physician documented PT not addressing neurological progression).
        *   "(3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." (Myelomalacia, severe cord compression, and progressive neurological deficit constitute clear objective evidence of structural neurological compromise).
    *   **Conclusion:** CIC § 10169.5 explicitly mandates waiver of any specific PT duration requirement for this member's condition.

*   **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) — Standard of Review for IMR:**
    *   While this is an internal appeal, the IMR standard (which plans must adhere to) clarifies that services meeting "generally accepted standards of medical practice" SHALL be approved regardless of the plan's more restrictive internal criteria. This reinforces the need to prioritize clinical standards and state regulations over plan criteria when conflicts arise.

**C. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:**

*   **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines for Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update), NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guideline (2021):**
    *   These guidelines uniformly distinguish myelopathy from radiculopathy, emphasizing that they have fundamentally different treatment algorithms. Applying radiculopathy-derived conservative treatment requirements (like a 6-week PT minimum) to myelopathy is a "category error not supported by clinical evidence."
    *   For moderate-severe myelopathy with objective cord compression, these guidelines establish surgery as a **TIER 1 STRONG INDICATION**. The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is considered a particularly strong indicator for surgical intervention and a **contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials**.
    *   AO Spine Guidelines (2021) specifically state, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
    *   The member's symptoms (spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor, Lhermitte sign) and imaging (severe cord compression, myelomalacia) clearly define a moderate-severe myelopathy meeting Tier 1 surgical indication.
    *   **Conclusion:** The clinical standard overwhelmingly supports surgical intervention and does not require further conservative treatment for this member's condition, especially given the presence of myelomalacia and progressive neurological symptoms.

---

**5. Disposition:**

Based on the comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's own Clinical Coverage Criteria, applicable California state law and regulations, and generally accepted clinical standards, the denial of prior authorization for ACDF C4-C5 is hereby **OVERTURNED**.

---

**6. Warrant for Decision:**

The denial was primarily based on unfulfilled conservative treatment requirements, specifically the duration of physical therapy and the absence of an interventional procedure. This determination is incorrect and not supported by the available evidence for the following reasons:

1.  **Failure to Apply Plan's Own Myelopathy Exception:** The member's primary diagnosis is progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia. HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A)) includes a specific "Myelopathy Pathway" that reduces the PT requirement to a minimum of 4 weeks (member completed 5 weeks) and waives the interventional treatment requirement when physician-documented medical contraindications exist (member's pacemaker contraindicates CESI, which was documented). The initial denial failed to correctly apply this exception, which the member clearly meets.

2.  **Violation of California State Law (DMHC APL 22-014 and CIC § 10169.5):**
    *   DMHC APL 22-014 explicitly directs health plans to treat cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia as an urgent condition and prohibits requiring completion of a full course of conservative treatment when objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia) and surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines.
    *   CIC § 10169.5 prohibits conditioning surgery on PT completion where objective evidence of structural neurological compromise exists (severe cord compression, myelomalacia) that PT cannot address, or where the physician documents PT as unlikely to benefit. Both conditions are present in this case.

3.  **Inconsistency with Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:** Major spine societies (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) recognize moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia as a strong (Tier 1) indication for surgery. These guidelines explicitly state that there is no high-quality evidence to support mandatory prolonged physical therapy before surgery for this condition, and that delaying surgical decompression when myelomalacia is present can lead to irreversible neurological deterioration. The application of radiculopathy-derived PT requirements to myelopathy is contrary to established clinical practice.

In conclusion, the requested ACDF C4-C5 is medically necessary under HealthFirst's own myelopathy exception, is mandated for approval by California state law, and is consistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice for progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia.
