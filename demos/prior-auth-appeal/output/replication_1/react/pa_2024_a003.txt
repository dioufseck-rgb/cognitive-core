## APPEAL DETERMINATION: PA-2024-A003

**Member:** Patricia Osei (M-334455)
**Plan:** HealthFirst PPO Gold — Group Plan 77441
**Procedure:** Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C4-C5 (CPT 22551)
**Requested By:** Dr. Michael Torres, MD (Neurosurgery)
**Facility:** Cedars-Sinai Medical Center
**Date of Denial:** 2024-10-15
**Denial Reason:** "Member has not completed required conservative treatment. Physical therapy: 5 weeks completed, 6 required. No cervical epidural steroid injection on record. Member does not meet Section 2.2 requirements." (Initial Level Reviewer: Dr. Alan Foster, MD)
**Date of Appeal Review:** 2024-10-22

**Disposition:** **OVERTURN**

**Warrant:**

Upon review of the submitted appeal, the member's clinical record, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory framework, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial denial for ACDF C4-C5 is hereby **OVERTURNED**. The denial was based on an incorrect application of HealthFirst's own criteria, directly conflicts with California state law, and is inconsistent with generally accepted clinical standards for the management of cervical myelopathy.

The following specific points support this determination:

1.  **Failure to Apply Plan's Own Myelopathy Exception:**
    *   The member's primary diagnosis is "Progressive cervical myelopathy C4-C5 with myelomalacia," as confirmed by MRI and neurological exam findings (bilateral lower extremity spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor, Lhermitte sign positive). This explicitly meets HealthFirst's Section 2.1(b) for Cervical Myelopathy.
    *   HealthFirst's own Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A) \u2013 MYELOPATHY PATHWAY) provides an exception for confirmed myelopathy cases. Under this exception:
        *   The physical therapy (PT) requirement is reduced to a "4 weeks minimum." The member completed 5 weeks of PT, which exceeds this reduced minimum.
        *   The interventional treatment requirement (Section 2.2(c)) is "WAIVED if treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses risk of neurological deterioration." The clinical record clearly documents that a cervical epidural steroid injection (CESI) is **CONTRAINDICATED** due to the member's permanent pacemaker, and this was confirmed by anesthesiology consultation.
    *   Therefore, the member **fully meets** HealthFirst's modified conservative treatment requirements under its own myelopathy exception. The initial denial's mechanical application of the standard (6-week PT, mandatory interventional procedure) demonstrates a clear error in applying the plan's *own* policy.

2.  **Violation of California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 \u2013 Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:**
    *   CIC § 10169.5(a) prohibits a health insurer from conditioning surgical approval on PT completion where specific conditions are met. In this case:
        *   **(a)(1):** The treating physician documented that "further PT was not addressing neurological progression," indicating PT was unlikely to benefit the member's progressive neurological symptoms.
        *   **(a)(2):** The member completed 5 weeks of PT, and the physician's assessment that it was not addressing neurological progression indicates a functional plateau regarding the neurological deficits.
        *   **(a)(3):** The condition involves "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address." The MRI findings of "C4-C5 severe central cord compression" and "T2 signal change (myelomalacia) confirmed. Progressive cord injury" unequivocally meet this criterion. Myelomalacia signifies existing spinal cord injury.
    *   Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(c) explicitly states that a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy "shall not be applied mechanically" under such circumstances. The denial's sole reliance on "5 weeks completed, 6 required" is a direct violation of this state law.

3.  **Violation of DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 \u2013 Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions:**
    *   DMHC APL 22-014, Section II.1, mandates that cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI be treated as an urgent condition due to potential for irreversible harm.
    *   Section II.2 explicitly states that for such conditions, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when:
        *   **(a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia):** The MRI confirming myelomalacia and progressive cord injury fulfills this.
        *   **(b) Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression:** The physician's determination that PT was "not addressing neurological progression" and pharmacotherapy only provided "partial spasticity relief" satisfies this requirement.
        *   **(c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines:** As detailed below, surgery is strongly indicated.
    *   The APL specifically identifies the "presence of myelomalacia on MRI" as a finding that "may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements." The denial's insistence on standard conservative measures directly contradicts this regulatory guidance.

4.  **Inconsistency with Generally Accepted Clinical Standards of Medical Practice:**
    *   The AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), and AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guideline (2021) consistently identify moderate to severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia as a **TIER 1 strong indication for surgical intervention.**
    *   These guidelines explicitly state that there is **no high-quality evidence** supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgery for moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Requiring extended PT in such cases is considered a "category error" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.1) and may lead to neurological deterioration due to treatment delay (AO Spine 2021, Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1).
    *   Myelomalacia signifies existing cord injury and is a strong predictor of permanent deficit if surgery is delayed, making surgical urgency paramount (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2, 2.3).
    *   CHSC § 1374.32(b) clarifies that the Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard requires approval if the service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice," regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. The requested ACDF for this presentation clearly meets these generally accepted standards.

**Conclusion:**

The denial for ACDF C4-C5 is inconsistent with HealthFirst's own myelopathy exception, is in direct conflict with California state law (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014), and contradicts generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice for the management of progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia. The criteria for approval have been met.

Therefore, the prior authorization for CPT 22551 (ACDF C4-C5, one level) for Patricia Osei (M-334455) is **APPROVED**.
