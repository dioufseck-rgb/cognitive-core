**Summary of Request and Denial:**
Mr. Steven Clark, a 46-year-old member, requested prior authorization for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 for cervical radiculopathy. The initial denial was issued due to conflicting documentation regarding the duration of physical therapy (PT), making it uncertain if the plan's required PT duration was met.

**Appeal Basis:**
The appeal clarified that the discrepancy in PT records was due to an incomplete initial submission for Coastal PT. Full records for Coastal PT (6 weeks) are now provided, demonstrating a total of 12 weeks of supervised physical therapy, with documented functional plateaus from both providers.

**Clinical Record Review:**
*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Cervical radiculopathy C5-C6 with functional impairment.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-08-15):** C5-C6 large disc herniation, severe right C6 nerve root compression, foraminal stenosis. (Note: No myelomalacia or clear myelopathic signs were documented in the submitted clinical record for this patient.)
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-08-20):** Right C6 dermatomal numbness, bicep weakness 4/5 right, bicep reflex absent right, positive Spurling right. These findings are consistent with C6 radiculopathy.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy (PT):**
        *   Riverside Physical Therapy: 6 weeks (18 sessions), 2024-04-01 to 2024-05-10. Documented functional plateau, symptoms persist.
        *   Coastal Physical Therapy: 6 weeks (18 sessions), 2024-06-01 to 2024-07-12. Complete records now submitted. Documented functional plateau, no improvement in neurological deficit, maximum therapeutic benefit reached.
        *   **Total documented PT:** 12 weeks (36 sessions).
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Gabapentin 600mg TID for 5 months (partial relief), Duloxetine 60mg daily for 3 months (partial neuropathic benefit).
    *   **Interventional Treatment:** Right C6 epidural steroid injection on 2024-08-28 (60% temporary relief, returned to baseline in 3 weeks).

**Appeal Review and Determination:**

This appeal was reviewed applying the three-source hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.

**1. Application of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
*   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficit and imaging findings consistent with symptoms. **MET.**
*   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:** This was the basis for the initial denial.
    *   **Physical Therapy (2.2(a)):** The plan requires a minimum of 6 weeks. The appeal successfully clarified that the member completed 12 weeks of supervised physical therapy, with documented functional plateaus from both providers. This significantly exceeds the plan's requirement. **MET.**
    *   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2(b)):** The member completed trials of two neuropathic agents (Gabapentin and Duloxetine) for durations greater than the 4-week minimum. **MET.**
    *   **Interventional Treatment (2.2(c)):** The member underwent a cervical epidural steroid injection. **MET.**
    *   **Myelopathy Exception (2.2(A)):** This exception (reducing PT to 4 weeks and waiving interventional treatment) does not apply as the member's primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, not myelopathy, and no myelopathic signs or myelomalacia are documented in the record. However, as noted above, the standard requirements are met.
*   **2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:** Current MRI (2024-08-15) demonstrates pathology consistent with symptoms and proposed surgical level. **MET.**
*   **2.4 PHYSICIAN DOCUMENTATION:** The requesting physician has provided clinical rationale and details of functional limitations. **MET.**

**Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** Based on the complete documentation provided with the appeal, Mr. Clark has satisfied all of HealthFirst's internal medical necessity criteria for cervical spinal surgery. The original reason for denial (conflicting PT documentation) has been resolved.

**2. Application of California Regulatory Framework:**
*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This state law explicitly governs physical therapy requirements.
    *   Subsection (a)(2) states that a plan cannot condition approval on PT completion where "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." Both physical therapy providers documented a functional plateau for Mr. Clark, with persistent symptoms and no improvement in neurological deficits despite 12 weeks of therapy.
    *   Subsection (a)(3) further prohibits conditioning approval where "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Mr. Clark presents with severe right C6 nerve root compression, foraminal stenosis, bicep weakness (4/5), and an absent bicep reflex, representing objective neurological compromise.
    *   Therefore, under California law, requiring further physical therapy as a prerequisite for surgery is prohibited in this case.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (Standard of Review for IMR):** This section mandates that health care services meeting "generally accepted standards of medical practice" shall be approved, regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. This reinforces that clinical standards can override plan criteria.

**3. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
*   **Diagnostic Classification:** The patient's presentation aligns with severe cervical radiculopathy.
*   **Conservative Treatment Evidence:** For radiculopathy, 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment is generally recommended before considering surgery. Mr. Clark has completed 12 weeks of physical therapy, multiple pharmacotherapy agents, and an interventional injection.
*   **Functional Plateau:** The clinical standard defines a functional plateau as a therapist documenting maximum therapeutic benefit reached or less than 10% improvement over 3 sessions. Both PT providers documented that Mr. Clark reached a functional plateau, with persistent neurological deficits and no improvement.
*   **Surgical Indications (AANS/CNS Guidelines 2023):** Mr. Clark's condition meets the TIER 2 "Reasonable Indication" for surgery: "(b) Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and "(c) Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care."

**Conclusion on State Law and Clinical Standards:** California law and generally accepted clinical standards support approval in this case, particularly given the extensive conservative treatment, documented functional plateau, and objective neurological deficits.

**Disposition:** **OVERTURN**

**Warrant:**
The initial denial for prior authorization of ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) for member Steven Clark is **OVERTURNED**. The appeal successfully addressed the plan's reason for denial by providing complete physical therapy records, demonstrating that the member completed 12 weeks of supervised physical therapy, which significantly exceeds the HealthFirst plan's minimum 6-week requirement (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a)). Furthermore, both physical therapy providers documented that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms or neurological deficits (Clinical Record, Physical Therapy Summary; Clinical Reference Summary, Section 5).

Under California Insurance Code § 10169.5, a health insurer is explicitly prohibited from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where the member has completed a course of therapy and reached a functional plateau [CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)]. This law also applies where there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address [CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)], which is present in Mr. Clark's case (severe right C6 nerve root compression, bicep weakness 4/5, absent reflex).

Finally, the requested ACDF procedure is medically necessary and meets generally accepted standards of medical practice for cervical radiculopathy with persistent symptoms and significant functional impairment after an adequate trial of conservative treatment, as outlined in Tier 2 indications of the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines for Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update) and NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020).

Therefore, based on the resolution of the documentation conflict, the satisfaction of the plan's criteria, and the overriding provisions of California state law and generally accepted clinical standards, the denial is reversed, and the requested procedure is approved.
