### I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The initial denial for the requested ACDF C5-C6 procedure was based on conflicting and unverified physical therapy (PT) documentation. The appeal provides comprehensive and verified documentation for 12 weeks of supervised physical therapy, with both providers confirming a functional plateau.

Upon review of the submitted appeal documentation, the member's clinical presentation, the HealthFirst PPO Gold plan criteria, California state regulations, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, it has been determined that the medical necessity criteria for the requested procedure are satisfied.

Therefore, the denial is **OVERTURNED**.

### II. APPEAL BASIS REVIEW

The member's appeal asserts:
1.  Both Riverside PT and Coastal PT records are authentic.
2.  The discrepancy in Coastal PT records is explained: an initial partial submission of 4 weeks, with the final 2 weeks faxed separately.
3.  Full Coastal PT records (6 weeks) are now enclosed, confirming 12 total weeks of PT.

These findings are consistent with cervical radiculopathy. *No upper motor neuron signs (e.g., Hoffman, hyperreflexia) or bilateral symptoms suggestive of myelopathy.*

### IV. APPLICATION OF HEALTHFIRST PLAN CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)

The requested procedure (ACDF C5-C6, CPT 22551) falls under HealthFirst's Spinal Surgery - Cervical Procedures policy. The plan's medical necessity criteria are reviewed as follows:

*   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficit and supporting MRI findings. **(Met)**
*   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **(a) Physical Therapy:** The plan requires a "minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy with documented functional outcomes." The member has completed 12 weeks of PT (6 weeks with Riverside PT and 6 weeks with Coastal PT). Both providers documented a functional plateau, with Coastal PT explicitly noting no improvement in neurological deficit and maximum therapeutic benefit reached. This significantly exceeds the 6-week minimum. **(Met)**
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** The plan requires a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The member completed 5 months of Gabapentin and 3 months of Duloxetine, both with only partial relief. **(Met)**
    *   **(c) Interventional Treatment:** The plan requires one interventional treatment. The member received a right C6 epidural steroid injection, which provided only temporary, partial relief for 3 weeks before symptoms returned to baseline. **(Met)**
    *   **Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A):** This exception is not applicable as the member's primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, not myelopathy.
*   **2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:** MRI dated 2024-08-15 demonstrates C5-C6 large disc herniation, severe right C6 nerve root compression, and foraminal stenosis, consistent with symptoms and the proposed surgical level. **(Met)**
*   **2.4 PHYSICIAN DOCUMENTATION:** While not explicitly detailed, the clinical record provided supports the rationale for surgical intervention, functional limitations, and demonstrates completion of conservative care. **(Assumed Met)**

**Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The appeal successfully addressed the original denial reason by providing complete PT documentation. Based on the complete clinical record, all criteria outlined in HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 for cervical radiculopathy are satisfied.

### V. APPLICATION OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK

1.  **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute explicitly states that a health insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... (2) The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
    *   **Application:** The member completed 12 weeks of PT, and both physical therapists documented that a functional plateau was reached without adequate resolution of symptoms, particularly neurological deficits.
    *   **Impact:** This California law overrides any plan requirement for further PT once a functional plateau is documented, reinforcing that the conservative treatment requirement has been met.

2.  **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This APL primarily addresses myelopathy and conditions risking irreversible harm, allowing for waiver of full conservative treatment.
    *   **Application:** While the specific provisions for *waiving* PT for myelopathy are not directly applicable here (as this is a radiculopathy case), the APL generally affirms that "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice."
    *   **Impact:** This reinforces the primacy of clinical standards over restrictive plan criteria.

3.  **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard Not Plan Criteria):** This section mandates that for IMR (and by extension, internal appeals following the same principles), the service must be approved if it meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.
    *   **Application:** Even if the plan's criteria had not been met, the service must be evaluated against generally accepted clinical standards. As demonstrated below, the service meets these standards.
    *   **Impact:** This ensures that the ultimate determination relies on sound medical practice, not solely on plan-specific rules.

### VI. APPLICATION OF CLINICAL STANDARD KNOWLEDGE (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)

1.  **Distinction: Radiculopathy vs. Myelopathy:** The clinical guidelines emphasize the critical difference. The member's presentation (unilateral dermatomal symptoms, lower motor neuron weakness, absent reflex, absence of UMN signs or myelomalacia) is classic for **cervical radiculopathy**, not myelopathy.
2.  **Conservative Treatment for Radiculopathy:** The clinical guidelines (NASS 2020, Tier 2) generally recommend surgery for radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment, especially with significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care.
    *   **Application:** The member has undergone 12 weeks of PT, 5 months of Gabapentin, 3 months of Duloxetine, and an epidural injection, all with persistent neurological symptoms and functional impairment. This trial significantly exceeds the generally accepted 6-12 week conservative treatment period.
3.  **Functional Plateau:** The clinical standard defines a functional plateau as <10% improvement over 3 sessions or therapist documentation of maximum benefit.
    *   **Application:** Both PT providers documented a functional plateau, with Coastal PT explicitly noting no improvement in neurological deficit and maximum therapeutic benefit reached. This aligns perfectly with the clinical definition.
4.  **Surgical Indications for ACDF (CPT 22551):** Per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), Tier 2 indicates surgery is a reasonable option for "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care."
    *   **Application:** The member meets these Tier 2 criteria due to persistent right C6 radiculopathy, objective weakness, absent reflex, and documented failure of an extensive course of conservative management.

### VII. CONFLICT RESOLUTION

*   **Original Denial Reason vs. Appeal:** The initial denial was based on inadequate documentation. The appeal provided the complete and verified records for 12 weeks of physical therapy, directly resolving the reason for denial.
*   **Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard vs. State Law:**
    *   The member has met all requirements of the **HealthFirst Plan Criteria** for radiculopathy, particularly the conservative treatment requirements.
    *   The member also meets the criteria for surgery under **Generally Accepted Clinical Standards of Medical Practice** for persistent radiculopathy after an adequate conservative trial.
    *   Furthermore, **California State Law (CIC 10169.5)** explicitly prohibits conditioning approval on further PT when a functional plateau has been reached, which is clearly documented in this case.

In this instance, there is no conflict. The plan's criteria are met, and both clinical standards and state law strongly support approval.

### VIII. DETERMINATION

The denial for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level) for member Steven Clark (M-101133) is **OVERTURNED**.

### IX. WARRANT

The denial is overturned because the specific reason for the initial denial – conflicting and unverified physical therapy documentation – has been directly addressed and resolved by the appeal. The provided complete records confirm 12 weeks of supervised physical therapy, exceeding the plan's 6-week minimum requirement.

Furthermore, the member's clinical presentation of cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits (right C6 dermatomal numbness, bicep weakness 4/5 right, absent bicep reflex) and imaging confirming severe nerve root compression is consistent with the diagnosis requirement of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(a) and 2.1(c).

All conservative treatment requirements under HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2 for cervical radiculopathy have been met:
1.  **Physical Therapy:** 12 weeks completed (exceeding 6-week minimum), with documented functional plateau at both providers.
2.  **Pharmacotherapy:** Trials of Gabapentin (5 months) and Duloxetine (3 months) completed, meeting the requirement of two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
3.  **Interventional:** One epidural steroid injection completed with only temporary relief.

Crucially, the physical therapy records from both Riverside PT and Coastal PT explicitly document that the member reached a functional plateau, with no improvement in neurological deficits, and maximum therapeutic benefit was achieved. Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2), a health insurer is prohibited from conditioning approval of surgery on further physical therapy once a functional plateau is documented.

Finally, the requested ACDF C5-C6 procedure is consistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice for cervical radiculopathy with persistent symptoms and objective neurological deficits after an adequate and failed trial of conservative treatment, as outlined in the AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine and Peripheral Nerves Guidelines (2023 Update) and NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), which support surgical intervention for Tier 2 indications like the one presented by the member.
