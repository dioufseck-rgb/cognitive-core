**Summary of Request and Denial:**
Mr. Santos, a 63-year-old male, requested prior authorization for a C6-C7 Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF). The request was denied by HealthFirst, citing reason code MED-003. The denial stated that while MRI showed cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia), the myelopathy exception (Section 2.2A of HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024) was not met because the member's symptoms were primarily right-sided (radicular) with only mild bilateral components, and thus "myelopathy as primary diagnosis" was not established. The denial concluded that the primary diagnosis remained radiculopathy, making the myelopathy exception inapplicable.

**Appeal Basis:**
The appeal argues that:
1.  T2 signal change (myelomalacia) is present, triggering regulatory overrides.
2.  The member presents with a mixed myelopathy/radiculopathy presentation, with both diagnoses being present.
3.  The plan cannot deny the myelopathy exception when myelomalacia is objectively documented.

---

**Clinical Record Review:**
*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-01):** C6-C7 moderate cord compression. T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the cord. Right C7 foraminal stenosis more prominent than left.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-09-05):** Predominant right C7 distribution with 4/5 right tricep weakness and right finger numbness. *Crucially, bilateral findings include bilateral hyperreflexia (right > left), positive right Hoffman sign, equivocal left Hoffman sign, and mildly wide-based gait.* Pain is predominantly right-sided 8/10.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 6 weeks (18 sessions) of myelopathy protocol. Documented "No improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms."
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Pregabalin 150mg BID for 3 months (partial right-sided pain relief); Naproxen 500mg BID for 2 months (inadequate).
    *   **Interventional:** Right C7 epidural (50% right-sided relief, fully returned in 3 weeks; no change in bilateral hyperreflexia).

---

**Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

1.  **2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:**
    *   The MRI demonstrates moderate cord compression and T2 signal change consistent with cervical myelopathy (2.1b).
    *   The neurological exam reveals objective upper motor neuron signs (hyperreflexia, Hoffman sign, gait disturbance) characteristic of myelopathy, alongside radicular symptoms.
    *   The member also has C7 foraminal stenosis and right-sided radicular symptoms consistent with cervical radiculopathy (2.1a).
    *   *Finding:* The member objectively meets the diagnostic criteria for both cervical myelopathy and cervical radiculopathy.

2.  **2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:**
    *   The plan denied the myelopathy exception (2.2A) because it deemed myelopathy not to be the "PRIMARY diagnosis." This meant the plan intended to apply the *standard* conservative treatment requirements.
    *   **Standard Requirements (if radiculopathy primary):**
        *   **(a) Physical Therapy:** Minimum 6 weeks completed (member completed 6 weeks).
        *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** At least two agents for 4+ weeks each (member completed Pregabalin for 3 months and Naproxen for 2 months).
        *   **(c) Interventional Treatment:** One procedure (member completed right C7 epidural).
    *   *Finding:* The member *met* the duration requirements for the plan's standard conservative treatment criteria (6 weeks PT, 2 pharma, 1 interventional). The denial's stated reason for *not* applying the myelopathy exception (that myelopathy was not primary) does not, by itself, justify a denial under the standard criteria, as the standard criteria *were fulfilled by duration and type of treatment*. Furthermore, the PT summary explicitly notes "No improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms," and the interventional treatment showed "No change in bilateral hyperreflexia." This indicates a failure of conservative treatment to address the myelopathic component and objective neurological deficits, meeting the "functional plateau" definition in both clinical standards and the plan's domain knowledge.

---

**Application of California Regulatory Framework:**

1.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):**
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** This APL states, "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent unless compelling clinical evidence supports otherwise." Mr. Santos's MRI unequivocally shows both cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia).
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** For such conditions, "plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when (a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia...), AND (b) Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression, AND (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines for the condition."
        *   (a) Met: MRI demonstrates myelomalacia.
        *   (b) Met: Conservative treatment has shown "No improvement in bilateral neurological signs" and only partial, temporary pain relief, indicating failure to arrest progression of neurological symptoms.
        *   (c) Met: Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines (see below).
    *   The APL specifically identifies "The presence of myelomalacia on MRI... as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements."
    *   *Finding:* DMHC APL 22-014 directly overrides the plan's conservative treatment requirements, including the restrictive "primary diagnosis" clause, due to the presence of myelomalacia and objective myelopathy.

2.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):**
    *   This statute states a plan "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... (3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
    *   *Finding:* Mr. Santos's moderate cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia) on MRI constitute objective evidence of structural neurological compromise. Physical therapy cannot address the structural cord injury indicated by myelomalacia or reverse upper motor neuron signs. This regulation therefore prohibits the plan from requiring PT completion as a prerequisite for surgery in this case.

3.  **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):**
    *   This section mandates that if a service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice," it "SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive."
    *   *Finding:* This provision allows for an override of the plan's criteria if they are more restrictive than clinical standards.

---

**Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

1.  **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy (Section 1.1):** Clinical guidelines emphasize that myelopathy and radiculopathy are distinct entities requiring different treatment algorithms. The application of radiculopathy-derived conservative treatment requirements to myelopathy is a "category error." Mr. Santos's objective signs (myelomalacia, UMN signs, gait disturbance) clearly indicate significant myelopathy.

2.  **Myelomalacia Significance (Section 1.2):** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) is a "strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials," as it indicates existing spinal cord injury and higher risk of permanent deficit if surgery is delayed.

3.  **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy (Section 2.1, 2.3):**
    *   "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." (AO Spine Guidelines 2021).
    *   The member's 6 weeks of PT showed "No improvement in bilateral neurological signs," indicating a functional plateau for the myelopathic component, consistent with clinical expectations that PT does not produce meaningful functional improvement for moderate-severe myelopathy.

4.  **Surgical Indications (Section 3.1, 3.2):** Mr. Santos meets Tier 1 (Strong Indication) criteria for ACDF, specifically:
    *   (a) Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI.
    *   (c) Myelomalacia on MRI (cord signal change).
    *   *Finding:* Current clinical standards strongly recommend surgery for this presentation and explicitly state that a minimum course of PT is not required and that delaying surgery may be harmful.

---

**Resolution of Conflicts (Hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):**

The plan's denial is based on a restrictive interpretation of its internal criteria, specifically the "myelopathy as primary diagnosis" clause. However, this interpretation and the associated conservative treatment requirements are directly overridden by higher authorities:

*   **State Law (DMHC APL 22-014 and CIC § 10169.5):** These California regulations explicitly override plan requirements for conservative treatment (especially physical therapy) when objective evidence of cervical myelopathy with cord compression and myelomalacia is present. These conditions mandate urgent treatment and recognize that PT cannot address the structural neurological compromise. The plan's "primary diagnosis" restriction cannot supersede these state mandates.
*   **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS, AO Spine):** Generally accepted standards of medical practice strongly support surgical intervention for Mr. Santos's condition due to moderate cord compression and myelomalacia, and they explicitly do not require or support extended conservative treatment, particularly PT, prior to surgery in such cases. The plan's criteria, as applied, are more restrictive than the generally accepted standards of care, making them invalid per CHSC § 1374.32(b).

**DISPOSITION: OVERTURN**

**Warrant:**
The HealthFirst Health Plan's denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) for Mr. David Santos is hereby **OVERTURNED**.

The plan denied authorization based on its internal criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2A Note), asserting that myelopathy was not the "primary diagnosis" and therefore the myelopathy exception, which modifies conservative treatment requirements, did not apply.

However, the clinical evidence unequivocally demonstrates the presence of significant cervical myelopathy, including moderate cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia) on MRI, along with objective upper motor neuron signs (bilateral hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman, gait disturbance). While radicular symptoms are also present, the objective myelopathic findings are severe and indicate actual cord injury.

This denial conflicts directly with California state law and generally accepted clinical standards:
1.  **California State Law (DMHC APL 22-014):** This All-Plan Letter mandates that cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI be treated as an urgent condition. It explicitly states that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when objective imaging (like myelomalacia) demonstrates progressive pathology, conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression of neurological symptoms, and surgery is indicated by clinical guidelines. Mr. Santos meets all these conditions. This regulation overrides the plan's restrictive "primary diagnosis" clause and conservative treatment duration requirements (Source: DMHC APL 22-014, Sections II.1, II.2).
2.  **California State Law (CIC § 10169.5):** This statute prohibits conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy where there is "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Mr. Santos's myelomalacia and cord compression clearly fall under this provision, making the requirement for further or specific PT completion invalid (Source: CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)).
3.  **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021):** Generally accepted clinical guidelines establish that myelomalacia on MRI is a strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials. For moderate-to-severe myelopathy, there is no high-quality evidence supporting mandatory physical therapy prior to surgery, and delaying surgery may lead to neurological deterioration. Mr. Santos's condition meets Tier 1 "strong surgical indication" criteria. Furthermore, his physical therapy showed "no improvement in bilateral neurological signs," indicating a functional plateau regarding his myelopathy, which is not expected to respond to PT (Source: AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines, Section 3.1, 3.2; AO Spine 2021 Guidelines, Section 2.1, 2.3).
4.  **CHSC § 1374.32(b):** As the service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, it SHALL be approved regardless of the plan's more restrictive internal criteria (Source: CHSC § 1374.32(b)).

Therefore, based on the clear objective evidence of cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and the superseding authority of California state law and generally accepted clinical standards, the requested ACDF is medically necessary.
