**1. SUMMARY OF DENIAL**

The initial prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 for Mr. David Santos was denied on 2024-10-05. The denial reason, MED-003, stated that while cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia) were present on MRI, the member's symptoms were primarily right-sided (radicular) with only mild bilateral components. The plan reviewer concluded that the primary diagnosis remained radiculopathy, and therefore, the Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2(A) Note of HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024) which modifies conservative treatment requirements, did not apply as it requires myelopathy to be the primary diagnosis.

---

**2. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND RECORD REVIEW**

Mr. Santos, a 63-year-old male, presents with a mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy picture.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-01):** Confirms C6-C7 moderate cord compression and critically, **T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord**. Right C7 foraminal stenosis is also noted, more prominent than left.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-09-05):** Reveals both radicular and clear myelopathic signs.
    *   **Radicular findings:** Predominant right C7 distribution weakness (tricep 4/5), right finger numbness, and pain (8/10).
    *   **Myelopathic findings:** **Bilateral hyperreflexia (right > left), positive right Hoffman sign, equivocal left Hoffman sign, and a mildly wide-based gait.**
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 6 weeks (18 sessions) with a myelopathy protocol. Documented "No improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms."
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Pregabalin (3 months, partial right-sided pain relief) and Naproxen (2 months, inadequate).
    *   **Interventional:** Right C7 epidural (50% right-sided relief for 3 weeks, **no change in bilateral hyperreflexia**).

**3. APPLICATION OF PLAN CRITERIA (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)**

1.  **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** Met. The member clearly has both cervical radiculopathy (right C7 symptoms, foraminal stenosis) and cervical myelopathy (cord compression, T2 signal change, UMN signs, gait disturbance).
2.  **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:** The member has completed 6 weeks of PT, two pharmacotherapy agents for >4 weeks, and one interventional procedure. While the *duration* requirements for the standard pathway were technically met, the core of the denial revolves around the application of the myelopathy exception due to the plan's interpretation of "primary diagnosis." The plan states the myelopathy exception (2.2(A)) only applies if myelopathy is the "PRIMARY diagnosis." The plan reviewer determined radiculopathy was primary due to symptom predominance.
3.  **2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:** Met. MRI within 6 months demonstrating pathology (cord compression, myelomalacia, stenosis).
4.  **2.4 PHYSICIAN DOCUMENTATION:** Assumed to be met for initial request.

**Plan's Application Analysis:** The plan's denial centers on its interpretation of the "primary diagnosis" clause within the Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2(A) Note). The reviewer acknowledges myelopathy findings (cord compression, T2 signal change) but states myelopathy is not the "primary diagnosis," thereby denying the application of the reduced PT and waived interventional requirements for myelopathy.

**4. APPLICATION OF REGULATORY AND CLINICAL STANDARDS (Hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria)**

**A. CALIFORNIA STATE LAW AND REGULATORY GUIDANCE:**

1.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This critical regulatory guidance directly addresses the present case:
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** States that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent..." Mr. Santos has both cord compression and myelomalacia. The plan's decision to classify his myelopathy as non-primary, thereby treating it less urgently, directly conflicts with this mandate.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** States that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when: (a) objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia), (b) conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression (documented 'no improvement in bilateral neurological signs' or hyperreflexia), AND (c) surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. All three conditions are met. The APL explicitly states: "**The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements** even where a full course has not been completed, when the treating physician documents urgency."
    *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** Mandates that plans "shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice." The plan's "primary diagnosis" clause, when applied to deny the myelopathy exception despite myelomalacia, is more restrictive than generally accepted standards and conflicts with this APL.

2.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute prohibits conditioning approval of surgery on PT completion where:
    *   **(a)(3) Objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address:** The presence of myelomalacia (T2 signal change) with cord compression constitutes objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (existing cord injury). Physical therapy cannot reverse existing cord damage.
    *   **(a)(2) Member reached a functional plateau:** The clinical record states, "No improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms" after 6 weeks of PT. The interventional procedure also showed "No change in bilateral hyperreflexia." This explicitly meets the definition of a functional plateau per both the statute and clinical standards, especially for myelopathic signs.
    *   **(c) Mechanical application of PT duration:** The spirit of the plan's denial, by requiring the longer standard PT duration (by not applying the myelopathy exception), amounts to mechanically applying a PT duration without reference to neurological urgency or functional plateau, which is prohibited.

**B. CLINICAL STANDARD (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

1.  **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy (Section 1.1):** Clinical guidelines emphasize that myelopathy and radiculopathy are fundamentally different conditions with distinct treatment algorithms. Applying radiculopathy-derived conservative treatment requirements to myelopathy is a "category error."
2.  **Myelomalacia Significance (Section 1.2):** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) signifies existing spinal cord injury, a higher risk of permanent deficits, and reduced recovery. It is considered a strong surgical indication and a **contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials**. This directly contradicts the plan's attempt to enforce standard conservative requirements through its "primary diagnosis" clause.
3.  **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy (Section 2.1):** AO Spine Guidelines (2021) state: "**There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration.**" Mr. Santos exhibits objective signs of moderate myelopathy.
4.  **Surgical Indications (Section 3.1):** Mr. Santos meets multiple TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATIONS for ACDF per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023):
    *   (a) Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI.
    *   (c) Myelomalacia on MRI (cord signal change).
    *   The clinical standard **does NOT require completion of a minimum course of physical therapy** as a prerequisite for surgery for this presentation, and requiring it is inconsistent with accepted medical practice.

**5. CONFLICT RESOLUTION**

The plan's denial is based on its internal criteria requiring myelopathy to be the "primary diagnosis" to apply its myelopathy exception. This directly conflicts with California State Law (DMHC APL 22-014, CIC § 10169.5) and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine, NASS).

*   **State law (DMHC APL 22-014)** explicitly mandates that myelopathy with myelomalacia and cord compression be treated as urgent, and specifically identifies myelomalacia as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements. It also prohibits internal criteria more restrictive than clinical standards.
*   **State law (CIC § 10169.5)** overrides any plan requirement for physical therapy duration when objective neurological compromise (myelomalacia) or a functional plateau has been reached.
*   **Clinical standards** overwhelmingly support surgical intervention for myelopathy with myelomalacia as a strong Tier 1 indication, with no evidence for mandatory PT duration and clear warnings against delaying surgery.

Per the established hierarchy (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria), the state laws and clinical standards override the HealthFirst Plan's restrictive interpretation of its "primary diagnosis" clause. The objective findings of myelomalacia, cord compression, and upper motor neuron signs conclusively establish the presence and urgency of myelopathy, irrespective of the predominance of radicular pain.

**6. DISPOSITION**

**OVERTURN**

**7. WARRANT**

The denial of prior authorization for CPT 22551 for Mr. David Santos is hereby OVERTURNED. The initial denial inappropriately applied the plan's internal "primary diagnosis" clause to deny the myelopathy exception, which is in direct conflict with California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.

Specifically:

1.  **California Regulatory Override:** DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 mandates that cases with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI **shall be treated as urgent** due to the potential for irreversible harm. The APL further specifies that plans **shall not require completion of a full course of conservative treatment** (including standard PT duration) when myelomalacia is present, conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression, and surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines. Mr. Santos clearly meets these criteria with documented myelomalacia, failed conservative treatment to resolve myelopathic signs, and strong surgical indications. The plan's "primary diagnosis" clause is more restrictive than the APL and is therefore superseded. (DMHC APL 22-014, Sections II.1, II.2, II.3).
2.  **California Statutory Override for PT Requirements:** California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 prohibits conditioning approval of surgery on physical therapy completion where (a)(3) objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address is present, or (a)(2) the member has reached a functional plateau. Myelomalacia constitutes objective evidence of structural neurological compromise. The documented "No improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms" after 6 weeks of PT, and "No change in bilateral hyperreflexia" after injection, clearly demonstrates that Mr. Santos has reached a functional plateau in terms of neurological improvement. (CIC § 10169.5 (a)(2), (a)(3), (c)).
3.  **Clinical Standard Override:** Generally accepted clinical guidelines from leading spine societies (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) classify moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia as a **TIER 1 strong surgical indication**. These guidelines explicitly state there is **no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for moderate-severe myelopathy**, and that **delaying surgery may result in neurological deterioration**. Myelomalacia is a strong predictor of poor outcome if surgery is delayed and indicates existing cord injury. The plan's emphasis on symptoms being "primarily radicular" is inconsistent with the urgency and treatment algorithm dictated by the presence of myelopathy and myelomalacia. (AANS/CNS 2023 Section 3.1, AO Spine 2021 Section 2.1, NASS 2020 Section 2.1, Section 1.2, Section 2.3).

Based on the three-source hierarchy, state law and clinical standards supersede the HealthFirst plan's internal criteria regarding the "primary diagnosis" for myelopathy in this instance. The requested ACDF procedure is medically necessary for Mr. Santos's condition given the objective evidence of cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and failed conservative treatment to address his myelopathic signs.
