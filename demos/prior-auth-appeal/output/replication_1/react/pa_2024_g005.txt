**I. BACKGROUND**

Mr. Gerald Obi, a 58-year-old member with HealthFirst PPO Gold, was denied prior authorization for an ACDF at C6-C7. The denial cited insufficient completion of physical therapy (5 weeks instead of the required 6) and poorly controlled Type 2 Diabetes (HbA1c 8.2%) as grounds for denial, citing perioperative risks. The member's appeal asserts that the physical therapy duration was miscounted and that diabetes control is not a valid authorization criterion under the plan's policy.

This appeal has been reviewed in accordance with the HealthFirst PPO Gold plan documents, applicable California state laws and regulations, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice. The hierarchy of authority applied is State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.

---

**II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION & PLAN CRITERIA ASSESSMENT**

**Diagnosis:**
The clinical record documents a presenting diagnosis of **cervical radiculopathy C6-C7 with functional impairment**.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-20):** Large disc herniation C6-C7 with moderate left C7 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis. Importantly, the MRI notes "No cord contact. No myelomalacia."
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-09-25):** Left C7 dermatomal numbness, diminished left tricep reflex, 4/5 left tricep weakness, 4+/5 left grip. Member is working reduced hours due to pain and hand weakness.
*   **Assessment:** Based on the unilateral symptoms, specific dermatomal distribution, nerve root compression on MRI, and absence of upper motor neuron signs or myelomalacia, Mr. Obi's primary diagnosis is **Cervical Radiculopathy**, not Myelopathy. This distinction is critical as it dictates the applicable conservative treatment requirements and clinical urgency.
    *   **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(a) and 2.1(c):** Member meets diagnosis requirements for Cervical Radiculopathy and Cervical Disc Herniation with objective neurological deficit.

**Conservative Treatment Assessment:**
The initial denial cited failure to meet the conservative treatment requirement for physical therapy (5 weeks completed vs. 6 required).
*   **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2:** For cervical radiculopathy, standard conservative treatment includes:
    *   **(a) Physical Therapy (minimum 6 weeks):** The clinical record's "physical_therapy_summary" clearly documents **7 weeks of continuous supervised physical therapy** from 2024-07-01 through 2024-08-16, with 21 total sessions. The denial's claim of 5 weeks is a factual error. Furthermore, the physical therapist documented a "functional plateau" at discharge, stating "no further functional improvement expected."
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy (at least two agents for minimum 4 weeks each):** Member completed a 5-month trial of Pregabalin 150mg BID (partial pain relief) and a 3-month trial of Duloxetine 60mg (moderate neuropathic benefit). **This criterion is met.**
    *   **(c) Interventional Treatment (minimum one):** Member underwent a Left C7 Epidural on 2024-10-10, providing 45% temporary relief that returned to baseline in 4 weeks. **This criterion is met.**
*   **Assessment:** The member has demonstrably met and exceeded all of HealthFirst's conservative treatment requirements for cervical radiculopathy. The denial's premise regarding PT duration is factually incorrect.
    *   **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Exception 2.2(A) Myelopathy Pathway:** This exception does not apply, as the member has cervical radiculopathy, not myelopathy.

**Diabetes Control as a Denial Criterion:**
The denial cited "poorly controlled type 2 diabetes (HbA1c 8.2%) which presents perioperative risk and wound healing concerns."
*   **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024:** A thorough review of HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria for Cervical Procedures (CC-SPINE-2024, Sections 2.0 and 3.0) reveals **no criteria or exclusions related to diabetes control, HbA1c levels, or general perioperative risk assessment** for the authorization of ACDF. The treating surgeon's note also clarifies that "Diabetes management will be optimized preoperatively. This is standard perioperative protocol. Diabetes control is a surgical planning consideration, not a PA criterion."
*   **Assessment:** This ground for denial is based on a criterion not present in HealthFirst's published medical necessity policy.

**III. REGULATORY & CLINICAL STANDARDS REVIEW**

**A. California State Law (Hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria)**

1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2) — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This statute states that a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
    *   **Application:** The clinical record confirms Mr. Obi completed 7 weeks of PT and the therapist documented a functional plateau, rendering further PT unlikely to benefit. This state law reinforces that the PT requirement was satisfied and cannot be a basis for denial.
2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 — Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions:** This APL primarily addresses urgent neurological conditions such as myelopathy with myelomalacia.
    *   **Application:** Given Mr. Obi's diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy without myelomalacia or cord contact, this APL's provisions regarding waiver of conservative treatment for urgency are not directly applicable.
3.  **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) — Standard of Review for IMR:** This section mandates that if a service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, it SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.
    *   **Application:** This provides a crucial safeguard, requiring approval if clinical standards are met, even if plan criteria were more restrictive (which, in this case, the member met).

**B. Clinical Standard (Hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria)**

1.  **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021:**
    *   **Diagnosis:** Mr. Obi's presentation is consistent with Cervical Radiculopathy, distinct from Myelopathy.
    *   **Conservative Treatment for Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines generally recommend a trial of conservative treatment (6-12 weeks of PT, pharmacotherapy, injections) for radiculopathy prior to considering surgery. Mr. Obi has completed 7 weeks of PT, 5 months of pharmacotherapy, and an epidural injection, with persistent symptoms and documented functional impairment.
    *   **Surgical Indications (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023, Section 3.1):**
        *   **TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION:** "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care."
    *   **Application:** Mr. Obi's condition, persistent C7 radiculopathy with objective neurological deficit (weakness, diminished reflex) and functional impairment after a documented and adequate trial of conservative therapy, clearly meets the Tier 2 "Reasonable Indication" for ACDF per generally accepted clinical guidelines.

---

**IV. CONCLUSION**

The initial denial was based on two grounds, both of which are found to be invalid upon review of the submitted documentation, HealthFirst's own criteria, and applicable state regulations:

1.  **Physical Therapy Shortfall:** The denial incorrectly stated the member completed only 5 weeks of PT. The clinical record clearly documents that Mr. Obi completed **7 weeks of supervised physical therapy**, exceeding HealthFirst's 6-week requirement (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a)). Furthermore, the physical therapist documented that the member reached a functional plateau, which under California Insurance Code (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)) precludes a health plan from denying based on further PT requirements. This ground for denial is factually incorrect and legally insufficient.

2.  **Diabetes Control as a Denial Criterion:** The denial cited the member's HbA1c level (8.2%) as a perioperative risk. HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria for Cervical Procedures (CC-SPINE-2024) does not include diabetes control or HbA1c levels as a medical necessity criterion or exclusion for ACDF authorization. While perioperative diabetes management is an important aspect of patient safety and surgical planning, it is not a valid basis for a prior authorization denial under HealthFirst's policy. This ground for denial is not consistent with the plan's own published criteria.

Furthermore, Mr. Obi meets all other applicable medical necessity criteria under HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, including diagnosis, imaging, and completion of pharmacotherapy and interventional treatment. The requested ACDF for cervical radiculopathy with persistent symptoms and functional impairment after a comprehensive conservative treatment trial is consistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023, Tier 2 indications).

---

**V. DISPOSITION**

The denial for prior authorization for ACDF C6-C7 (CPT 22551) for Mr. Gerald Obi (M-G005) is hereby **OVERTURNED**.

**WARRANT:** The initial denial was based on (1) a factual error regarding the duration of physical therapy completed by the member, who completed 7 weeks against a 6-week requirement, and (2) the application of a criterion (diabetes control/HbA1c) that is not included in HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria for Cervical Procedures (CC-SPINE-2024). The member has met all conservative treatment requirements as per HealthFirst's policy and California state law (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)). The requested surgical procedure is considered medically necessary and appropriate under generally accepted standards of medical practice for cervical radiculopathy with persistent symptoms and functional impairment after failed conservative management (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023).
