**HEALTHFIRST HEALTH PLAN — APPEAL DETERMINATION**

**Case ID:** PA-2024-G006
**Member:** Theresa Kamau, M-G006
**Procedure:** ACDF C5-C6, one level (CPT 22551)
**Date of Denial:** 2024-11-20
**Date of Appeal Review:** 2024-11-25

**Disposition:** **OVERTURNED**

**Warrant:**

The initial denial for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 for Ms. Theresa Kamau is OVERTURNED based on a comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), relevant California state laws, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.

**I. Procedural Flaw in Initial Denial:**
The initial denial itself is procedurally defective. The denial letter stated the reason as "Member has a prior authorization denial history for cervical spine surgery (three-level fusion denied May 2024). Review of current request in context of prior denial pattern suggests the member's cervical pathology has not materially changed since the prior denial. Current request does not demonstrate sufficient change in clinical status to warrant reconsideration." The case documentation explicitly notes this denial basis as "Prior PA history — NOT a valid criterion under CC-SPINE-2024" and flags the denial as "procedurally_flawed": true.

California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b) mandates that a denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reason for the denial. Denying a request based on a "prior PA history" or a "prior denial pattern" is not a valid clinical criterion outlined in HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 policy or a generally accepted clinical reason. Therefore, the original denial is procedurally inadequate.

**II. Member Meets HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
Despite the procedurally flawed denial, a thorough review confirms that Ms. Kamau's current request for ACDF C5-C6 meets all applicable criteria within HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 policy.

*   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** Ms. Kamau has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy at C5-C6 with objective neurological deficit. The October 2024 neurological exam documents right C6 dermatomal numbness, absent right bicep reflex, and right bicep weakness (3+/5), with pain rated 8/10. The current MRI (October 2024) shows a "large disc herniation with moderate-severe right C6 nerve root compression" and "new foraminal stenosis at C5-C6," consistent with her symptoms and the proposed surgical level. This meets criteria 2.1(a) and 2.1(c).
*   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **(a) Physical Therapy:** Ms. Kamau completed a NEW 8-week course of supervised physical therapy from July-September 2024, exceeding the policy's minimum 6-week requirement. Furthermore, the therapist explicitly documented a "functional plateau," noting "no further improvement expected, neurological deficit persistent and worsening."
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** Ms. Kamau completed a trial of Gabapentin 600mg TID for 6 months (partial pain relief, deficit persists) and two courses of Methylprednisolone dose packs (temporary relief). This meets the requirement for at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
    *   **(c) Interventional Treatment:** Ms. Kamau underwent a right C6 epidural injection in November 2024, which provided only "50% relief for 2 weeks, returned to baseline." This meets the requirement for one interventional treatment.
    *   **Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A):** This exception is not applicable as the primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, not myelopathy (based on the absence of spinal cord compression, upper motor neuron signs, bilateral symptoms, or myelomalacia). However, Ms. Kamau has met the standard conservative treatment requirements.
*   **2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:** The October 2024 MRI clearly demonstrates structural pathology (C5-C6 large disc herniation, severe C6 nerve root compression, new foraminal stenosis) consistent with her symptoms and the proposed surgical level, satisfying this criterion.
*   **2.4 PHYSICIAN DOCUMENTATION:** This is presumed to be met as no deficiencies were cited in the initial denial for this criterion.

**III. Compliance with California State Law and Regulations:**
Even if the plan's criteria were not explicitly met (which they were), California state law would mandate approval:

*   **CHSC § 1374.31(b) (Defective Denial):** As noted, the denial is procedurally defective.
*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute prohibits a health plan from conditioning surgical approval on completion of PT where:
    *   **(a)(2) A functional plateau has been reached:** The clinical record clearly documents that Ms. Kamau completed an 8-week PT course and reached a functional plateau, with "no further improvement expected" and "neurological deficit persistent and worsening." This explicitly satisfies CIC § 10169.5(a)(2).
    *   **(a)(3) Objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address:** The new MRI showing an "interval increase in C5-C6 herniation size" with "moderate-severe right C6 nerve root compression" and "interval worsening" of bicep weakness constitutes objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot resolve. This satisfies CIC § 10169.5(a)(3).
    *   Therefore, the plan cannot deny based on conservative treatment or PT duration.
*   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Conservative Treatment Exceptions for Progressive Pathology):** For conditions with progressive pathology, plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when (a) objective imaging shows progressive pathology (met by the interval increase in herniation size), AND (b) the treating physician documents conservative treatment failure (met by functional plateau and worsening deficit), AND (c) surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. This APL reinforces that the strict conservative treatment requirements should not delay care in such cases, even for radiculopathy.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This statute clarifies that services meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.

**IV. Alignment with Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:**
The requested ACDF is medically necessary and indicated under generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice:

*   **AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine Guidelines (2023):** Ms. Kamau's presentation meets:
    *   **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION:** (b) Progressive neurological deficit despite conservative treatment. Her right bicep weakness (3+/5) has worsened, and the C5-C6 herniation has increased in size. Her pain is 8/10, and she is unable to work full-time, demonstrating significant functional impairment.
    *   **TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION:** (b) Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment. Ms. Kamau has undergone 8 weeks of PT, 6 months of pharmacotherapy, and an injection, with persistent symptoms and progressive deficits.
*   The clinical reference summary emphasizes that a patient who achieves partial pain reduction but no improvement in neurological symptoms after an adequate course of PT has reached a functional plateau. This aligns with Ms. Kamau's documentation.

**Conclusion:**
The HealthFirst denial of prior authorization for ACDF C5-C6 for Ms. Theresa Kamau is procedurally flawed and unsupported by the clinical evidence and applicable regulatory guidance. Ms. Kamau meets all of HealthFirst's stated clinical criteria for the requested procedure, and California state law further protects her right to receive care given her progressive neurological deficit and documented failure of conservative treatment. Surgical intervention is medically necessary and supported by generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.

Therefore, the prior authorization denial is **OVERTURNED**, and authorization for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level) is hereby approved.
