Dear Ms. Morrison and Dr. Nguyen,

This letter serves as the appeal determination for the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C3-C4 for Angela Morrison (Member ID: M-778899), initially denied on October 1, 2024.

Upon thorough review of the submitted appeal, the member's clinical record, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), relevant California regulatory framework (California Insurance Code (CIC), California Health & Safety Code (CHSC), Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL)), and generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine), we have reached the following determination:

**DENIAL STATUS: OVERTURNED**

**WARRANT:**

The initial denial, stating "imaging does not show sufficient pathology at C3-C4 to support surgical intervention. MRI shows mild degenerative changes. Procedure not medically necessary," is not supported by the comprehensive clinical evidence, HealthFirst's own criteria when fully applied, state law, or generally accepted clinical standards.

1.  **Diagnosis and Imaging Requirements (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Sections 2.1 & 2.3):**
    *   The member's presenting symptoms (left C4 dermatomal numbness, deltoid weakness 3+/5, diminished bicep reflex) and the CT myelogram findings clearly establish a diagnosis of **cervical radiculopathy** due to nerve root compression and significant foraminal stenosis at C3-C4. This satisfies HealthFirst's Diagnosis Requirement 2.1(a).
    *   The initial denial's reliance on the MRI finding of "mild degenerative changes" is incomplete. The clinical record explicitly states the MRI was limited by "SIGNIFICANT ARTIFACT from C4-C5 hardware limiting evaluation." To address this limitation, a **CT myelogram** was performed (2024-09-10), which definitively confirmed "moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression" and "significant foraminal stenosis" at C3-C4, noting that the "MRI underestimated pathology due to hardware artifact." This CT myelogram demonstrates clear structural pathology consistent with symptoms and the proposed surgical level, satisfying HealthFirst's Imaging Requirement 2.3. The initial reviewer's assertion of "insufficient pathology" is directly contradicted by the CT myelogram, which explicitly clarified the pathology not adequately visualized on MRI.

2.  **Conservative Treatment Requirements (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2):**
    *   The initial denial correctly stated, "Member meets conservative treatment requirements." This is consistent with the clinical record:
        *   **Physical Therapy:** The member completed 10 weeks (30 sessions), exceeding the 6-week minimum. The documentation states "No improvement in left C4 radicular symptoms," indicating a functional plateau.
        *   **Pharmacotherapy:** The member completed trials of Pregabalin (6 months, partial relief) and Duloxetine (3 months, minimal benefit), meeting the requirement for two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
        *   **Interventional Treatment:** A left C4 epidural steroid injection was administered (2024-09-25), providing good initial relief (60%) that returned to baseline by week 3, fulfilling the single interventional treatment requirement.
    *   The "Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A)" is not applicable as the primary diagnosis is radiculopathy, not myelopathy.

3.  **California Regulatory Requirements (CIC § 10169.5, CHSC § 1374.32):**
    *   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute prohibits a health plan from conditioning surgery approval on further physical therapy where:
        *   (a)(2) The member has completed PT and reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms. The clinical record's note of "No improvement in left C4 radicular symptoms" after 10 weeks of PT unequivocally meets the definition of a functional plateau (as also supported by clinical standards, Section 5).
        *   (a)(3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. The CT myelogram confirming "moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression" and "significant foraminal stenosis" demonstrates such structural compromise. Therefore, under state law, further PT cannot be required.
    *   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (Standard of Review for IMR):** This critical provision mandates that services meeting "generally accepted standards of medical practice" SHALL be approved regardless of whether a plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. As demonstrated below, the procedure meets generally accepted clinical standards.

4.  **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
    *   The member's presentation of persistent left C4 radicular symptoms (numbness, weakness 3+/5) with objective nerve root compression and significant foraminal stenosis on CT myelogram, despite a comprehensive and documented course of conservative treatment lasting several months, aligns with **TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION** for surgery under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) for "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx."
    *   The clinical evidence base clarifies that physical therapy has limited efficacy for neurological symptoms like weakness in the presence of structural compression (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 5). The member has met the clinical definition of a functional plateau.

**CONCLUSION:**

The initial denial was based on an incomplete assessment of the imaging evidence, failing to consider the clarifying CT myelogram which directly contradicted the denial's reasoning. Furthermore, the member has fully satisfied both HealthFirst's conservative treatment requirements and the criteria for proceeding to surgical intervention under California state law and generally accepted clinical standards. The requested ACDF C3-C4 is medically necessary for Ms. Angela Morrison.

Therefore, the prior authorization for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C3-C4) is **OVERTURNED**, and the service is **APPROVED**.
