**Date of Denial:** 2024-10-01 (Initial Level)

**Disposition:** OVERTURNED

**Warrant:** The initial denial for ACDF C3-C4 is **OVERTURNED**. The requested procedure is medically necessary as the member meets HealthFirst's own clinical coverage criteria when all available imaging and clinical information are considered, and it is consistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice for cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits that have failed extensive conservative treatment. The initial denial's premise of "insufficient pathology" is directly contradicted by the definitive findings of the CT myelogram.

---

**I. Case Summary**

Angela Morrison, a 67-year-old member of HealthFirst PPO Gold, presented with symptoms of adjacent segment disease at C3-C4 following a prior C4-C5 ACDF in 2019. Symptoms include new onset since 2023 of left C4 dermatomal numbness, 3+/5 deltoid weakness, and diminished left bicep reflex. Dr. Paul Nguyen, MD, requested prior authorization for ACDF C3-C4.

The initial authorization request was denied on 2024-10-01 by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, with reason code MED-003, stating: "Member meets conservative treatment requirements. However, imaging does not show sufficient pathology at C3-C4 to support surgical intervention. MRI shows mild degenerative changes. Procedure not medically necessary."

The appeal was submitted based on:
1.  Member completed all conservative treatment requirements.
2.  Surgeon states C3-C4 is symptomatic level based on clinical examination.
3.  CT myelogram confirms compression not visible on MRI due to hardware artifact.

**II. Clinical Record Review Findings**

*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Adjacent segment disease at C3-C4 following C4-C5 ACDF (2019). The clinical presentation is consistent with symptomatic **cervical radiculopathy**.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-08-20):** Objective findings include left C4 dermatomal numbness, deltoid weakness of 3+/5 on the left, and diminished left bicep reflex. These symptoms are new since 2023.
*   **Imaging:**
    *   **MRI (2024-08-15):** Reported "SIGNIFICANT ARTIFACT from C4-C5 hardware limiting evaluation. Mild degenerative signal changes visible. Cannot exclude moderate compression due to artifact."
    *   **CT Myelogram (2024-09-10):** Performed specifically due to MRI artifact limitation. Findings explicitly state: "**C3-C4: moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression confirmed. Significant foraminal stenosis.** MRI underestimated pathology due to hardware artifact from prior fusion."
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 10 weeks, 30 sessions. Documented "No improvement in left C4 radicular symptoms." This exceeds the plan's 6-week minimum.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Pregabalin 300mg BID for 6 months (partial relief); Duloxetine 60mg for 3 months (minimal benefit). This meets the plan's requirement of two agents for 4+ weeks each.
    *   **Interventional:** Left C4 Epidural on 2024-09-25. "Good initial relief (60%), returned to baseline by week 3." This meets the plan's requirement of one interventional procedure.

**III. Analysis Against HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)**

The plan's denial was based on Section 3.3 (Imaging does not demonstrate pathology). We will assess the case against all applicable criteria:

*   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** **MET.** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (2.1(a)) with objective neurological deficits (left C4 dermatomal numbness, 3+/5 deltoid weakness, diminished bicep reflex) and supporting imaging (CT myelogram demonstrating moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression at C3-C4).
*   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:** **MET.** As acknowledged in the initial denial, and confirmed by clinical record review:
    *   (a) Physical therapy: 10 weeks of supervised PT with documented "No improvement in left C4 radicular symptoms" significantly exceeds the 6-week minimum.
    *   (b) Pharmacotherapy: Two neuropathic agents (Pregabalin and Duloxetine) were trialed for 6 and 3 months respectively, exceeding the 4-week minimum.
    *   (c) Interventional treatment: One cervical epidural steroid injection was performed, meeting the requirement.
*   **2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:** **MET.** The initial denial incorrectly stated "imaging does not show sufficient pathology." While the MRI was limited by artifact, the subsequent CT myelogram (dated 2024-09-10, within 6 months of request) definitively demonstrates "moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression" and "significant foraminal stenosis" at the C3-C4 level, which is consistent with the member's symptoms. The CT myelogram explicitly clarified that the MRI had "underestimated pathology due to hardware artifact."
*   **2.4 PHYSICIAN DOCUMENTATION:** Documentation supports the request, including the surgeon's clinical rationale and attestation of conservative treatment completion and symptomatic level.

**Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The requested ACDF C3-C4 fully meets HealthFirst's medical necessity criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Sections 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, and 2.4). The initial denial's basis (lack of sufficient imaging pathology) is factually incorrect given the complete imaging record including the CT myelogram.

**IV. Analysis Against California Regulatory Framework (State Law)**

California state law provides crucial protections and standards for prior authorization appeals, which take precedence over plan criteria.

*   **CHSC § 1374.31(b) — Denial Notice Adequacy:** While the denial notice stated a specific clinical reason, it based this reason on an incomplete and ultimately misleading assessment of the imaging record, failing to adequately consider the impact of hardware artifact on the MRI and the definitive findings of the CT myelogram. This calls into question the procedural adequacy of the initial denial process.
*   **CIC § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This statute states a plan shall not condition approval of surgery on PT completion where: (2) the member reached a functional plateau, OR (3) there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address.
    *   The member's 10 weeks of PT with "No improvement in left C4 radicular symptoms" clearly demonstrates a functional plateau (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)).
    *   The objective evidence of "moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression" and 3+/5 deltoid weakness constitutes "structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address" (CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)).
    *   Therefore, even if HealthFirst had denied for insufficient PT (which it did not, as it conceded conservative treatment was met), state law would prohibit such a denial.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) — Standard of Review for IMR:** This is the overarching legal principle. It mandates that in an appeal, the service must be determined medically necessary if it is "in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice," regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. This standard governs the final determination.

**V. Analysis Against Clinical Evidence Base (Generally Accepted Standards of Medical Practice)**

*   **Diagnosis and Severity:** The member presents with persistent, symptomatic cervical radiculopathy at C3-C4, confirmed by objective neurological deficit (3+/5 deltoid weakness, diminished reflex, numbness) and definitive imaging showing moderate-severe nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis.
*   **Conservative Treatment Failure:** The member has undergone an extensive and documented course of conservative therapy including 10 weeks of physical therapy, trials of two neuropathic medications for 3-6 months, and an epidural steroid injection that provided only temporary relief. Critically, PT yielded "no improvement in left C4 radicular symptoms," demonstrating failure of conservative measures to address the neurological deficit.
*   **Surgical Indications (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine Guidelines):**
    *   According to the AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine: Guidelines for Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update) and NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), surgery for cervical radiculopathy is indicated in cases of persistent symptoms and objective neurological deficits that have failed an adequate course of conservative treatment.
    *   The member meets **TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION** for surgery (AANS/CNS 2023, Section 3.1):
        *   (b) "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" - The member has persistent C4 radicular symptoms with objective weakness after extensive conservative treatment well exceeding 12 weeks combined.
        *   (c) "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care" - The 3+/5 deltoid weakness and numbness represent significant functional impairment unresponsive to conservative management.
*   **Conclusion on Clinical Standard:** The requested ACDF C3-C4 is medically necessary and fully consistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice for symptomatic cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits that have failed an extensive and appropriate course of conservative therapy.

**VI. Resolution of Conflicts and Final Determination**

The initial denial was based on an inaccurate assessment of the imaging findings. The CT myelogram conclusively demonstrated significant pathology (moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression and significant foraminal stenosis) at the C3-C4 level, which was obscured by artifact on the prior MRI. When the complete clinical picture, including all imaging studies, is considered, the member clearly meets HealthFirst's own medical necessity criteria.

Furthermore, under California Insurance Code § 1374.32(b), the standard for this appeal is whether the service is consistent with "generally accepted standards of medical practice." The robust clinical evidence and guidelines (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) strongly support surgical intervention for persistent, symptomatic cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits that have failed extensive conservative treatment, as is the case for Angela Morrison.

Therefore, applying the hierarchy of State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria, the denial cannot be sustained.
