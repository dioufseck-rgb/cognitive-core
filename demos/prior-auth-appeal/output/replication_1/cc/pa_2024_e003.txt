HealthFirst Appeal Determination Services has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a Revision Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT code 22551). Based on a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical documentation submitted, California regulatory statutes, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, the initial decision to deny coverage is hereby OVERTURNED. The requested surgical procedure is approved.

I. CLINICAL HISTORY AND PRESENTING PATHOLOGY
Mr. George Harris is a 48-year-old male who underwent a prior C5-C6 ACDF in 2021. After an initial period of symptom relief, he experienced a recurrence of severe symptoms approximately 18 months postoperatively. 
A neurological examination dated September 20, 2024, documented objective neurological deficits consistent with right C6 radiculopathy, including:
- Right bicep motor weakness graded at 4/5;
- Diminished right bicep deep tendon reflex;
- Sensory loss/numbness in a right C6 dermatomal distribution.
These objective findings represent a significant clinical deterioration compared to his preoperative baseline.

II. DIAGNOSTIC IMAGING ANALYSIS
To evaluate these symptoms, the following imaging studies were performed:
1. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) dated September 1, 2024: The radiologist noted that significant hardware artifact severely limited the assessment of the C5-C6 level. While a non-union was suspected, the MRI was inconclusive, and recurrent disc herniation or residual disc material could not be definitively excluded.
2. Computed Tomography (CT) Scan (without contrast) dated September 15, 2024: This study was unaffected by hardware artifact and demonstrated incomplete bridging bone formation across the C5-C6 interspace, along with lucency surrounding the surgical hardware. The radiologist interpreted these findings as highly consistent with pseudarthrosis (failed fusion). The hardware itself appeared intact without fracture.

III. CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT HISTORY
The clinical record demonstrates that Mr. Harris has completed an exhaustive course of conservative management that failed to resolve his structural pathology:
1. Physical Therapy: Completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy. The treating therapist documented a functional plateau with no clinical improvement, noting that physical therapy cannot resolve structural fusion failure.
2. Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 4-month trial of Naproxen (500mg BID) and a 3-month trial of Gabapentin (300mg TID), yielding only partial and temporary pain relief.
3. Interventional Treatment: Received a cervical epidural steroid injection on October 10, 2024, which resulted in minimal, transient improvement.

IV. STATUTORY AND CLINICAL ANALYSIS
The initial denial dated October 30, 2024, cited HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), stating that revision surgery requires documentation of hardware failure, pseudarthrosis, or new pathology, and that the submitted documentation did not confirm hardware failure. 

1. Resolution of the Contractual Imaging Modality Deficit via CHSC § 1374.32
HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1) states that supporting documentation for a cervical diagnosis must consist of an "MRI or CT myelogram within 6 months." The plan's legal counsel may argue that because the patient submitted a standard CT scan (rather than a CT myelogram) and the MRI was non-diagnostic due to hardware artifact, the contractual criteria for diagnostic confirmation were not met.

However, under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), the statutory standard for Independent Medical Review (IMR) and medical necessity determinations explicitly overrides restrictive internal plan criteria. CHSC § 1374.32(b) mandates that a health care service must be approved if it is medically necessary under generally accepted standards of medical practice, "regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive."

Generally accepted clinical standards established by the American Association of Neurological Surgeons / Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine (2023) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020) recognize that a standard CT scan is the gold standard and clinically appropriate imaging modality for evaluating suspected pseudarthrosis (failed fusion) in patients with existing spinal hardware. MRI is frequently rendered non-diagnostic by metallic artifact, as occurred in this case. Forcing a patient to undergo an invasive CT myelogram—which carries risks of spinal headache, infection, and radiation—when a standard, non-invasive CT scan has already clearly and unambiguously confirmed pseudarthrosis is clinically inappropriate and contrary to the clinical standard of care. 

Therefore, pursuant to CHSC § 1374.32, the clinical standard of care overrides the plan's restrictive imaging modality requirement. The CT scan dated September 15, 2024, is accepted as contractually and clinically sufficient to confirm the diagnosis of C5-C6 pseudarthrosis.

2. Correction of Procedural Defect Characterization under CHSC § 1374.31(b)
A prior review suggested that the denial notice was procedurally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b) for "ignoring" the CT scan. Upon legal and clinical re-evaluation, we correct this characterization. CHSC § 1374.31(b) defines a defective notice as one that "fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial." Because the denial notice dated October 30, 2024, explicitly specified both the criteria applied (CC-SPINE-2024) and the clinical reason (non-confirmation of hardware failure), the notice was procedurally compliant with CHSC § 1374.31(b).

Instead, the plan's denial represents a substantive clinical error in medical necessity determination. The plan's reviewer focused exclusively on the absence of "hardware failure" (i.e., hardware fracture or displacement), while completely failing to address the documented "pseudarthrosis" (failed fusion). Pseudarthrosis is a distinct structural pathology and is itself an explicit, independent indication for revision surgery under both the plan's stated revision criteria and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS Tier 2 Indications). By failing to evaluate the CT scan's clear evidence of pseudarthrosis, the plan committed a substantive clinical error, not a procedural statutory violation.

3. Compliance with Physical Therapy Requirements and CIC § 10169.5
The patient fully satisfied the 6-week physical therapy requirement under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a) by completing 8 weeks of supervised therapy. Furthermore, under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3), a health plan is prohibited from mechanically conditioning surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (such as pseudarthrosis with progressive radiculopathy and motor weakness) that physical therapy cannot address. Mr. Harris's 4/5 bicep weakness and structural non-union constitute structural compromise exempting him from further conservative therapy demands under California law.

V. CONCLUSION
The clinical record establishes that Mr. George Harris suffers from documented C5-C6 pseudarthrosis with corresponding right C6 radiculopathy and objective motor deficits. He has exhausted conservative treatment. Under CHSC § 1374.32, the clinical standard of care overrides the plan's restrictive imaging criteria, validating the standard CT scan as diagnostic confirmation of pseudarthrosis. The plan's initial denial was a substantive clinical error. 

Accordingly, the prior authorization denial for a Revision ACDF at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED, and the procedure is APPROVED.
