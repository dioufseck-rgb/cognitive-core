DISPOSITION: UPHOLD (Denial Confirmed)

Dear Mr. Flores,

HealthFirst has completed its formal first-level clinical appeal review of the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical records, plan coverage criteria, California statutory requirements, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, the decision to deny coverage for this procedure has been UPHELD.

Below is a detailed explanation of the clinical, contractual, and legal bases for this determination.

1. CLINICAL RECORD SUMMARY
The clinical records submitted for review establish the following clinical facts:
- Clinical Presentation: You present with axial neck pain (rated 5/10). A physical and neurological examination performed on September 20, 2024, was completely normal. There is no documented cervical radiculopathy (nerve root compression causing pain, numbness, or weakness in a specific arm distribution) and no cervical myelopathy (spinal cord compression causing gait disturbance, hand clumsiness, hyperreflexia, or abnormal reflexes such as a positive Hoffman sign).
- Imaging Findings: A cervical spine MRI dated September 15, 2024, demonstrates severe central canal stenosis at C5-C6 with a canal diameter of 6mm (normal is >13mm). There is spinal cord contact, but no cord compression, no T2 signal change, and no myelomalacia (spinal cord scarring or bruising).
- Conservative Treatment: You have completed a robust course of conservative management, including 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy (24 sessions), a 3-month trial of Naproxen 500mg BID, a 6-week trial of Cyclobenzaprine, and a C5-C6 epidural steroid injection on October 10, 2024, which provided 60% pain relief lasting 6 weeks.
- Clinical Indication: Your surgeon, Dr. Karen Davis, requested this procedure as a "prophylactic decompression." Dr. Davis notes that given your occupation as a construction worker operating heavy equipment, you are at an elevated risk for cervical trauma, and a single traumatic event could cause catastrophic spinal cord injury given your narrow 6mm canal.

2. EVALUATION OF CLINICAL COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement), surgical intervention is authorized only when the member has a confirmed diagnosis of at least one of the following:
(a) Cervical radiculopathy with correlating radicular symptoms;
(b) Cervical myelopathy with correlating myelopathic symptoms; or
(c) Cervical disc herniation with an objective neurological deficit.

Because your neurological examination on September 20, 2024, was completely normal, you do not exhibit symptoms or signs of radiculopathy, myelopathy, or objective neurological deficits. Consequently, the diagnostic requirements of Section 2.1 are not satisfied.

3. EVALUATION UNDER CALIFORNIA STATUTORY LAW (CHSC § 1374.32)
On appeal, your provider argues that the surgery is medically necessary to prevent a catastrophic spinal cord injury, and that California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(a) defines "medically necessary" to include services provided for the purpose of "preventing" an injury or disease.

We explicitly acknowledge that CHSC § 1374.32(a) supersedes internal plan criteria and includes "preventing" an injury within the statutory definition of medical necessity. However, CHSC § 1374.32(a) mandates that any such preventive service must also satisfy the following statutory requirements:
(1) It must be "In accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice" (CHSC § 1374.32(a)(1)); and
(2) It must be "Clinically appropriate, in terms of type, frequency, extent, site, and duration, and considered effective for the patient's illness, injury, or disease" (CHSC § 1374.32(a)(2)).

To determine whether prophylactic ACDF for an asymptomatic 6mm canal meets these statutory requirements, we evaluated the request against generally accepted standards of medical practice.

4. EVALUATION OF GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS
Generally accepted standards of medical practice are established by major national neurosurgical and orthopedic spine societies, including the American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS), the Congress of Neurological Surgeons (CNS), and the North American Spine Society (NASS).

Under the AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine: Guidelines for Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update) and the NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), surgical indications for cervical decompression are classified into three tiers:
- Tier 1 (Strong Indication): Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression, progressive neurological deficits, or myelomalacia (cord signal change) on MRI.
- Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication): Mild myelopathy with stable symptoms after conservative trial, or persistent radiculopathy.
- Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated): Axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy, or asymptomatic imaging findings (including severe stenosis) without correlating clinical symptoms or signs.

According to these guidelines, prophylactic surgical decompression (ACDF) is classified as Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated) for patients who have asymptomatic cervical stenosis or axial neck pain only, even when the canal is critically narrow (6mm) and the patient is engaged in a high-risk occupation. Clinical evidence demonstrates that the perioperative risks of cervical fusion (including adjacent segment disease, pseudarthrosis, dysphagia, and nerve injury) outweigh the theoretical, unproven benefit of preventing a traumatic spinal cord injury in an asymptomatic individual. The standard of care for such patients is activity modification, close clinical monitoring, and immediate re-evaluation if neurological symptoms develop.

Because prophylactic decompression in this clinical scenario is not supported by generally accepted standards of medical practice, it does not meet the statutory definition of medical necessity under CHSC § 1374.32(a)(1).

5. CLARIFICATION OF EXCLUSION 3.1
We note that the initial denial dated October 25, 2024, referenced Exclusion 3.1, stating the surgery was excluded because it was requested solely for axial neck pain. On appeal, we have corrected this characterization. We recognize that Dr. Davis requested this procedure for "prophylactic decompression" of a severely stenotic canal, not as a treatment for your axial neck pain. However, even when evaluated under this correct clinical indication, the request remains not medically necessary because prophylactic decompression of asymptomatic stenosis is clinically contraindicated under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023).

CONCLUSION
While we understand your concern regarding the risk of trauma associated with your occupation, we must follow generally accepted standards of medical practice to ensure your safety. Because prophylactic surgery is not clinically indicated or medically necessary under CHSC § 1374.32 or CC-SPINE-2024, the denial of prior authorization for CPT 22551 must be upheld.
