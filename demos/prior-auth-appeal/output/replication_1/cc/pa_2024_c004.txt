DETERMINATION: UPHOLD DENIAL
The HealthFirst Appeal Committee has reviewed the prior authorization appeal for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, plan coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, the decision to deny prior authorization is UPHELD. Prophylactic surgical decompression is not medically necessary under California law or clinical guidelines in the absence of objective neurological deficits.

CLINICAL HISTORY AND FINDINGS
Mr. Raymond Flores is a 54-year-old male employed as a construction worker specializing in heavy equipment operation. He presents with a history of axial neck pain (rated 5/10).
1. Imaging: A cervical spine MRI dated September 15, 2024, demonstrates severe central canal stenosis at C5-C6 with a canal diameter of 6mm (normal is >13mm). The MRI notes spinal cord contact at this level but explicitly states there is no cord compression, no T2 signal change, and no evidence of myelomalacia.
2. Neurological Examination: A comprehensive neurological examination performed on September 20, 2024, was completely normal. There was no evidence of cervical radiculopathy (no dermatomal pain, numbness, or weakness) and no signs of cervical myelopathy (normal gait, no bilateral upper extremity weakness, normal reflexes, negative Hoffman sign, and no bowel/bladder dysfunction). No upper motor neuron (UMN) signs were present.
3. Conservative Treatment: The clinical record documents a completed course of conservative management:
   - Physical Therapy: 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy for axial neck pain, resulting in modest pain improvement (from 7/10 to 5/10) but no change in neurological status (as no neurological deficits were present).
   - Pharmacotherapy: Naproxen 500mg BID for 3 months (moderate pain relief) and Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks (sleep improvement).
   - Interventional: A C5-C6 epidural steroid injection was performed on October 10, 2024.

EVALUATION OF APPEAL AND CLINICAL ANALYSIS

1. CORRECTION OF CLINICAL RECORD TEMPORAL CONTRADICTION
The clinical record submitted with the appeal on November 8, 2024, states that the C5-C6 epidural performed on October 10, 2024, resulted in "60% pain relief lasting 6 weeks." We must explicitly note a severe temporal contradiction in this documentation: because the appeal was filed on November 8, 2024—only 29 days (approximately 4 weeks) after the injection—it is physically impossible to have observed or documented a completed 6-week duration of relief at the time of filing. This represents either a documentation error or a projected clinical outcome. For the purposes of this review, the plan acknowledges and evaluates the clinical fact that the patient experienced 60% pain relief sustained at a 4-week follow-up as of the appeal date. This positive response to conservative interventional therapy demonstrates that the patient's axial pain is responsive to non-surgical management.

2. CORRECTION OF SURGICAL INDICATION CHARACTERIZATION
The initial denial notice dated October 25, 2024, characterized the surgical request as being "solely for axial neck pain" to trigger Exclusion 3.1 of the HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 criteria. On appeal, we explicitly correct this characterization. We recognize that the primary clinical indication for the requested ACDF is prophylactic decompression of a critically narrow 6mm canal to prevent catastrophic spinal cord injury, as detailed in Dr. Davis's clinical notes, and is not a treatment for axial neck pain. Accordingly, we have evaluated this request under generally accepted standards of medical practice for preventive spine surgery rather than dismissing it under axial pain guidelines.

3. EVALUATION UNDER CALIFORNIA LAW (CHSC § 1374.32) AND CLINICAL STANDARDS
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(a), "medically necessary" health care services include those provided for the purpose of "preventing" an illness, injury, disease, or its symptoms. The appellant's counsel and treating surgeon argue that prophylactic decompression is medically necessary to prevent catastrophic spinal cord injury (such as quadriplegia) from minor trauma, given the member's critically narrow 6mm canal with cord contact and his high-risk occupation as a heavy equipment operator.

However, CHSC § 1374.32(a)(1) mandates that any such preventive service must be "in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice." To determine whether prophylactic ACDF meets this standard, we reference the AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine Guidelines for Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Clinical Guidelines (2020):
   - Under these clinical standards, surgical decompression (such as ACDF, CPT 22551) is indicated for patients with moderate-to-severe myelopathy (Tier 1) or mild progressive myelopathy/persistent radiculopathy (Tier 2).
   - For patients with severe cervical stenosis and cord contact but a completely normal neurological examination (no radiculopathy, no myelopathy, and no upper motor neuron signs) and no T2 signal changes or myelomalacia on MRI, prophylactic surgical decompression is not indicated (Tier 3 - Surgery Not Indicated).
   - Clinical evidence does not support prophylactic ACDF to prevent spinal cord injury in asymptomatic or non-myelopathic patients, even those with a narrow 6mm canal and high-risk occupations. The risk of perioperative complications from an ACDF outweighs the statistical risk of acute spinal cord injury from minor trauma in an asymptomatic patient.
   - Therefore, while CHSC § 1374.32 explicitly includes preventive services, the specific prophylactic surgery requested does not meet the clinical standard of care required to establish medical necessity under California law.

PLAN CRITERIA EVALUATION (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1, surgical authorization requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with nerve root compression, cervical myelopathy with myelopathic symptoms, or cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit.
   - The member does not meet Section 2.1(a) because there is no documented cervical radiculopathy (neurological exam on September 20, 2024, was completely normal).
   - The member does not meet Section 2.1(b) because there is no documented cervical myelopathy (no gait disturbance, no bilateral upper extremity weakness, normal reflexes, negative Hoffman sign).
   - The member does not meet Section 2.1(c) because there is no objective neurological deficit.
   - Section 3.1 excludes surgery requested solely for axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy. While the surgeon's intent is prophylactic, the patient's only clinical symptom is axial neck pain, and there are no objective neurological deficits to justify surgical intervention.

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS
While we acknowledge the severity of Mr. Flores's canal stenosis (6mm) and the physical demands of his occupation as a heavy equipment operator, prophylactic ACDF is not medically necessary under generally accepted standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS Guidelines) or California law (CHSC § 1374.32). The prior authorization denial is therefore UPHELD.

We recommend the following clinical management plan:
1. Activity Modification: Discussion with the treating physician regarding occupational safety and potential temporary or permanent modification of heavy equipment operation to minimize the risk of cervical trauma.
2. Clinical Monitoring: Close clinical surveillance with repeat neurological examinations every 6 to 12 months, or immediately upon the development of any new neurological symptoms (such as radiating pain, numbness, hand clumsiness, or gait instability).
3. Conservative Management: Continued non-surgical management of axial neck pain, which has shown positive response to physical therapy and interventional epidural therapy.
