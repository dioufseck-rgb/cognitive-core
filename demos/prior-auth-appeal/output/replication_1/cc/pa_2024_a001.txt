Dear Ms. Torres and Dr. Kim,

HealthFirst has completed its clinical and regulatory review of the first-level appeal submitted on November 15, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) to be performed at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center. 

After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria, California statutory and regulatory mandates, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial denial issued on November 1, 2024, has been OVERTURNED. The requested surgical procedure is APPROVED.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND HISTORY
The clinical record establishes that Ms. Diana Torres is a 61-year-old female with a progressive history of cervical myelopathy. 
- On initial presentation (August 10, 2024), she exhibited a 4-month history of bilateral hand clumsiness and gait instability. 
- A neurological examination on August 10, 2024, documented objective upper motor neuron signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia in both the upper and lower extremities, bilateral positive Babinski signs, and gait ataxia.
- A cervical spine MRI performed on August 15, 2024, demonstrated severe central cord compression at C5-C6 with T2 hyperintensity within the spinal cord, confirming the presence of myelomalacia (existing spinal cord injury).
- Ms. Torres initiated physical therapy at Valley PT on September 1, 2024. However, after 2 weeks and 6 sessions, her symptoms worsened. On September 14, 2024, Dr. Kim issued a written order to discontinue physical therapy due to symptom exacerbation.
- Pharmacotherapy consisted of a 2-week trial of a methylprednisolone taper, which provided only temporary mild improvement with no sustained benefit. No interventional treatments (such as epidural steroid injections) were performed.
- In a formal declaration letter dated October 15, 2024, Dr. Kim stated that physical therapy is strictly contraindicated in this patient due to the presence of myelomalacia on the initial MRI, noting that continuing physical therapy risks irreversible spinal cord injury and that surgery is urgently indicated.

II. ANALYSIS OF PLAN CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria Document CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2, standard authorization for cervical spinal surgery requires:
(a) A minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy.
(b) Trial of at least two specified pharmacotherapy agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
(c) One interventional treatment (e.g., cervical epidural steroid injection).

Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway Exception) modifies these requirements for members with confirmed cervical myelopathy (Criterion 2.1(b)) as follows:
- The physical therapy requirement is reduced to a minimum of 4 weeks.
- The interventional treatment requirement is waived if the treating physician documents that it poses a risk of neurological deterioration.
- The pharmacotherapy requirement (trial of two agents for 4 weeks each) remains in full.

The initial denial on November 1, 2024, cited reason code MED-003, stating that the member completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy (failing the modified 4-week requirement) and trialed only one pharmacotherapy agent (methylprednisolone taper), thereby failing to meet the conservative treatment requirements of CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. 

Strictly applying the plan's internal criteria, the clinical record does not satisfy CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 or Section 2.2(A) due to the incomplete physical therapy course (2 weeks completed vs. 4 weeks required) and incomplete pharmacotherapy (1 agent trialed vs. 2 agents required).

III. REGULATORY OVERRIDE AND LEGAL ANALYSIS (CALIFORNIA LAW)
Because Ms. Torres is a California resident enrolled in a California plan, the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria are subject to, and superseded by, the California Insurance Code (CIC) and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) regulations. 

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a) — Physical Therapy Prerequisites:
Under CIC § 10169.5(a), a health insurer is legally prohibited from conditioning the approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy under the following circumstances:
- CIC § 10169.5(a)(1): The member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition. Dr. Kim's written declaration dated October 15, 2024, explicitly states that physical therapy is contraindicated and poses a risk of irreversible spinal cord injury.
- CIC § 10169.5(a)(3): The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Ms. Torres's MRI dated August 15, 2024, provides objective evidence of severe central cord compression and myelomalacia (structural neurological compromise).
Consequently, under California law, the plan's physical therapy requirement (whether the standard 6 weeks or the modified 4 weeks) is legally unenforceable and cannot be applied to deny this request.

2. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 — Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions:
APL 22-014, Section II.1, establishes that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated, which plans must treat as urgent. 
Section II.2 prohibits plans from requiring the completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when:
(a) Objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia),
(b) The treating physician documents that conservative treatment has been attempted and failed to arrest progression (or is contraindicated), and
(c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines.
Ms. Torres meets all three conditions of APL 22-014 Section II.2. Therefore, the plan's conservative treatment requirements (including both physical therapy and the multi-agent pharmacotherapy trial) are waived under this regulatory mandate.

3. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) — Standard of Review:
CHSC § 1374.32(b) mandates that medical necessity determinations must be made in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice, and that a plan's internal coverage criteria shall not be the sole or determinative basis for a decision when they are more restrictive than these clinical standards.

IV. CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARD ANALYSIS
Generally accepted clinical standards strongly support immediate surgical decompression for Ms. Torres's clinical presentation, without requiring conservative treatment trials:

1. AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update):
Under Section 3.1, Ms. Torres's presentation meets Tier 1 — Strong Indication (surgery recommended) due to:
- Confirmed moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI.
- The presence of myelomalacia (cord signal change) on MRI.
- Objective upper motor neuron deficits (Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, Babinski signs, gait ataxia).
The guidelines state that conservative treatment is not recommended as a primary treatment for moderate-to-severe myelopathy, and that surgical decompression is the standard of care.

2. AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021) & NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020):
The AO Spine Guidelines (2021) explicitly state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." Ms. Torres's clinical record of symptom worsening during her brief physical therapy trial (Valley PT, September 1–14, 2024) directly validates this clinical risk.

V. RESOLUTION OF CROSS-SOURCE CONFLICTS
A direct conflict exists between the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, which requires 4 weeks of physical therapy and 2 pharmacotherapy agents) and both California law (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine, NASS guidelines).

Applying the established hierarchy of clinical review:
1. California State Law and Regulatory Mandates (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) hold the highest authority and strictly prohibit the application of physical therapy and conservative treatment prerequisites in the presence of documented structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) and written physician contraindication.
2. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021) hold the secondary authority and establish that immediate surgical decompression is the medically necessary standard of care, while mandatory conservative trials are clinically unsupported and dangerous.
3. Plan Internal Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) hold the lowest authority and are legally unenforceable and clinically inapplicable to the extent they conflict with state law and clinical standards.

Therefore, the conflict is resolved in favor of California State Law and generally accepted clinical standards. The plan's conservative treatment requirements are waived, and the requested procedure is determined to be medically necessary.

VI. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION DETAILS
The prior authorization denial issued on November 1, 2024, is OVERTURNED.
