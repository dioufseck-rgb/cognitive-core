I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its clinical and regulatory review of the first-level appeal submitted on behalf of Diana Torres for prior authorization of a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). The initial prior authorization request was denied on November 1, 2024, on the grounds that the member did not complete the plan's standard conservative treatment requirements under Clinical Coverage Criteria Document CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2.

Upon comprehensive review of the clinical record, California statutory law, and generally accepted clinical guidelines, the initial denial is OVERTURNED. Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter APL 22-014, the plan is legally prohibited from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy or conservative treatment when objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) is present and physical therapy is documented as contraindicated. Furthermore, generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023) establish that immediate surgical decompression is the standard of care for progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia, and delaying surgery poses a severe risk of irreversible neurological harm.

II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND HISTORY
The clinical record establishes the following clinical facts:
1. Presenting Diagnosis: Progressive cervical myelopathy C5-C6 with myelomalacia (Clinical Record).
2. Clinical History: The member is a 61-year-old female presenting with a 4-month history of progressive bilateral hand clumsiness and gait instability (Clinical Record - Initial Presentation, 2024-08-10).
3. Neurological Examination: Documented on August 10, 2024, demonstrating objective upper motor neuron signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia in the bilateral upper and lower extremities, positive bilateral Babinski signs, and documented gait ataxia (Clinical Record - Neurological Exam).
4. Objective Imaging: A cervical spine MRI performed on August 15, 2024, revealed severe central cord compression at C5-C6 with T2 hyperintensity within the spinal cord, diagnostic of myelomalacia (Clinical Record - Imaging).
5. Conservative Treatment Trial: The member attempted physical therapy at Valley PT from September 1, 2024, to September 14, 2024, completing 6 sessions over 2 weeks. Physical therapy was discontinued by written order of the treating neurosurgeon, Dr. James Kim, because the therapy caused severe symptom exacerbation (Clinical Record - Conservative Treatment).
6. Pharmacotherapy: The member trialed a single agent, a 2-week methylprednisolone taper, which provided only temporary mild improvement with no sustained benefit (Clinical Record - Pharmacotherapy).
7. Physician Declaration: In a formal declaration dated October 15, 2024, Dr. James Kim documented that physical therapy is strictly contraindicated in this patient due to the presence of myelomalacia on the initial MRI, stating that continuing physical therapy risks irreversible spinal cord injury and that surgical decompression is urgently indicated (Clinical Record - Physician Declaration).

III. PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS
The plan's initial reviewer, Dr. Alan Foster, MD, denied the procedure under Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, citing that the member completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy (standard pathway requires 6 weeks) and trialed only one pharmacotherapy agent (standard pathway requires two).

Under CC-SPINE-2024:
1. Section 2.1(b) (Diagnosis Requirement) is fully satisfied. The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy supported by objective clinical findings (gait ataxia, hyperreflexia, Hoffman sign) and a cervical MRI within 6 months demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms.
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement) requires 6 weeks of physical therapy, a trial of two pharmacotherapy agents for 4 weeks each, and one interventional treatment.
3. Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway) modifies these requirements for members with confirmed cervical myelopathy as the primary diagnosis. Under this exception, the physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks, the interventional treatment requirement is waived if documented as posing a risk of neurological deterioration, but the pharmacotherapy requirement (two agents) remains in full.

The member completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy and trialed only one pharmacotherapy agent (methylprednisolone). Therefore, the clinical record does not strictly satisfy the conservative treatment requirements under either the standard pathway or the modified Myelopathy Pathway of CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. However, as detailed below, these internal plan criteria are legally superseded and rendered unenforceable by California regulatory mandates.

IV. REGULATORY ANALYSIS AND LEGAL OVERRIDES
Under California law, internal health plan coverage criteria are subordinate to statutory and regulatory mandates. In this case, three distinct regulatory provisions prohibit the application of the plan's conservative treatment requirements:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) (Physical Therapy Override):
   This statute explicitly states that a health insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... [t]he condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The member's MRI dated August 15, 2024, provides objective, indisputable evidence of severe central cord compression and myelomalacia (T2 spinal cord signal change). This constitutes structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot resolve. Therefore, the plan's physical therapy requirement is legally inapplicable.

2. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1) (Contraindication Override):
   This statute prohibits conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy completion where "[t]he member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition." Dr. James Kim's written declaration dated October 15, 2024, explicitly states that physical therapy is contraindicated and poses a severe risk of irreversible spinal cord injury. This written documentation triggers a complete statutory waiver of the physical therapy requirement.

3. DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Conservative Treatment Waiver):
   This regulatory directive addresses prior authorization for time-sensitive neurological conditions. It states that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when: (a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia...), AND (b) Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression [or is contraindicated], AND (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines."
   The member's clinical presentation satisfies all three prongs:
   - Objective imaging (MRI, 2024-08-15) documents myelomalacia (cord signal change).
   - Dr. Kim documented that physical therapy caused symptom exacerbation, was discontinued after 2 weeks, and is contraindicated.
   - Surgical intervention is strongly indicated under clinical guidelines (see Section V below).
   Consequently, requiring the member to complete further physical therapy or trial a second pharmacotherapy agent violates DMHC APL 22-014.

4. CIC § 10169.5(c) (Mechanical Application Prohibition):
   The statute mandates that a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy "shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether... the condition involves neurological urgency." The initial reviewer's mechanical application of the 6-week PT requirement to a patient with active myelomalacia and upper motor neuron signs directly violates this statutory provision.

V. CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARD ANALYSIS
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, the standard for medical necessity is governed by generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersede restrictive internal plan criteria.

1. Surgical Indications for Cervical Myelopathy:
   According to the AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine Guidelines for Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Clinical Guidelines (2020), surgical decompression is the primary recommended treatment for patients with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy. The member's presentation—characterized by gait ataxia, bilateral hand clumsiness, hyperreflexia, positive Babinski and Hoffman signs, and MRI-documented myelomalacia—places her in Tier 1 (Strong Indication) for surgical intervention.

2. Inefficacy and Risks of Conservative Treatment:
   The AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021) state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
   Furthermore, clinical evidence demonstrates that myelomalacia (T2 signal change) indicates active spinal cord injury. Each month of delay in surgical decompression for moderate-to-severe myelopathy is associated with a decreased likelihood of neurological recovery and an increased risk of permanent, irreversible neurological deficits (AANS/CNS Guidelines 2023).

Therefore, requiring the member to undergo further conservative treatment (such as a second 4-week pharmacotherapy trial or additional physical therapy) is contrary to generally accepted standards of medical practice and poses an unacceptable risk of permanent harm.

VI. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS
Where a conflict exists between internal plan criteria, clinical evidence, and state regulatory frameworks, the following hierarchy of authority governs:
1. California Statutory and Regulatory Law (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) — Controlling Authority
2. Generally Accepted Standards of Medical Practice (AANS/CNS Guidelines 2023) — Governing Clinical Authority
3. HealthFirst Internal Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) — Subordinate Reference

While the member does not meet the conservative treatment requirements of CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, the plan's criteria are legally unenforceable under CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014 due to the presence of objective structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) and written physician documentation of contraindication. Because the requested ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is medically necessary and strongly indicated under generally accepted clinical standards, the clinical standard and regulatory overrides govern.

VII. DISPOSITION AND AUTHORIZATION
The prior authorization denial for ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is hereby OVERTURNED.
