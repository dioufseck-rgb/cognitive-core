HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of the first-level expedited appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an emergency Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) at UCSF Medical Center. After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory mandates, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has determined that the initial denial issued on November 18, 2024, must be OVERTURNED. The requested surgical procedure is APPROVED.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND CASE HISTORY
According to the clinical records submitted, Mr. Robert Chang, a 38-year-old male, presented emergently to the Emergency Room (ER) on November 10, 2024, with an acute onset of bilateral arm weakness and difficulty walking progressing rapidly over a 72-hour period. He had no prior history of cervical spine pathology.

An urgent neurological examination performed on November 10, 2024, revealed severe neurological deficits consistent with an incomplete spinal cord syndrome, including:
- Bilateral upper extremity (arm) weakness graded at 2/5;
- Bilateral lower extremity (leg) weakness graded at 3/5;
- Hyperreflexia in all extremities;
- Bilateral positive Babinski signs; and
- New-onset urinary urgency.

An urgent cervical spine MRI performed on November 10, 2024, demonstrated severe central cord compression at the C5-C6 level accompanied by extensive T2 hyperintensity throughout the spinal cord, indicating extensive myelomalacia (acute spinal cord injury). Neurosurgery was emergently consulted. In the ER, the patient was immediately started on an inpatient 24-hour intravenous (IV) methylprednisolone protocol for acute spinal cord injury. Dr. Elena Marchetti, MD, documented that physical therapy (PT) was absolutely contraindicated, as any physical manipulation or delay in surgical decompression posed an immediate risk of converting the patient's incomplete spinal cord syndrome into permanent, complete paralysis. Dr. Marchetti submitted an emergency declaration requesting immediate surgical authorization.

II. INITIAL DENIAL AND PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS
On November 18, 2024, the initial prior authorization request was denied by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, citing Reason Code MED-003. The denial was based on the plan's Clinical Coverage Criteria document CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2, which requires a documented course of conservative treatment prior to surgical authorization, including:
1. Physical Therapy: A minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy (Section 2.2(a));
2. Pharmacotherapy: A trial of at least two agents (NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each (Section 2.2(b)); and
3. Interventional Treatment: At least one cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point injection series (Section 2.2(c)).

The plan's criteria do contain "EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY," which modifies these requirements for members with confirmed cervical myelopathy by reducing the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks and waiving the interventional treatment requirement if the physician documents that it poses a risk of neurological deterioration. However, the initial reviewer noted that the patient had completed 0 weeks of physical therapy, had no documented outpatient pharmacotherapy, and had no interventional procedures, thereby failing to satisfy even the modified conservative treatment requirements under Section 2.2(A). Consequently, the request was denied under Section 3.2 of the plan's guidelines.

III. REGULATORY FRAMEWORK AND STATUTORY OVERRIDE
Under California law, health plans are prohibited from mechanically applying internal clinical coverage criteria when doing so conflicts with statutory protections or clinical standards. In this case, the plan's conservative treatment requirements are explicitly overridden by California statutory and regulatory mandates:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3): This statute explicitly mandates that a health insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... [t]he condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Mr. Chang's emergency MRI of November 10, 2024, provides indisputable, objective evidence of severe structural neurological compromise (severe C5-C6 central cord compression with extensive myelomalacia). Conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy in this clinical scenario directly violates CIC § 10169.5(a)(3).
2. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1): This provision further prohibits conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy when the treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member. Dr. Marchetti's written emergency declaration explicitly stated that physical therapy was "absolutely contraindicated" and "dangerous," risking permanent complete paralysis.
3. California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014: This regulatory directive addresses prior authorization for time-sensitive neurological conditions. Section II.1 establishes that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated" and mandates that plans treat such cases as urgent. Section II.2 explicitly states that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization" when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents urgency.
4. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b): This statute governs the standard of review for Independent Medical Reviews (IMR). It dictates that the medical necessity standard "does NOT require that the service be consistent with the plan's internal coverage criteria." If the service is clinically appropriate and in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice, it must be approved, and the plan's internal criteria "shall not be the sole or determinative basis" for a decision.

IV. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND MEDICAL NECESSITY ANALYSIS
Generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice strongly support immediate surgical decompression and contraindicate conservative treatment for patients presenting with acute, progressive cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia:

1. AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine Guidelines (2023 Update): These guidelines classify patients with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy, objective cord compression, and myelomalacia (cord signal change) on MRI under "Tier 1 — Strong Indication," where immediate surgical decompression is the recommended primary treatment. The guidelines state that conservative treatment has no role in the management of established, progressive myelopathy with structural cord injury.
2. North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020): NASS guidelines recommend surgical decompression as the primary treatment for moderate-to-severe myelopathy. They note that the evidence supporting physical therapy is limited to mild myelopathy and that delaying surgery in patients with progressive deficits or myelomalacia is associated with significantly worse long-term neurological outcomes.
3. AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021): These guidelines state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
4. Clinical Risk of Delay: Clinical literature demonstrates that the presence of myelomalacia (T2 hyperintensity) on MRI is the strongest predictor of incomplete neurological recovery, and each month of delay in surgical decompression increases the risk of permanent, irreversible spinal cord damage.

V. RESOLUTION OF CROSS-SOURCE CONFLICTS
In determining this appeal, HealthFirst applies the established legal and clinical hierarchy: California State Law and Regulatory Mandates > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Plan's Internal Clinical Coverage Criteria.

A direct conflict exists between the plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, which mechanically demands physical therapy and pharmacotherapy) and California law (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014, which prohibit such requirements in the presence of structural neurological compromise and documented contraindications). 

Applying the hierarchy, California state law and generally accepted clinical standards override the plan's internal criteria. Because Mr. Chang presented with an acute, progressive incomplete spinal cord syndrome with objective MRI evidence of severe cord compression and myelomalacia, and because his treating neurosurgeon documented that physical therapy was strictly contraindicated and dangerous, the plan's conservative treatment requirements are legally and clinically inapplicable. Conditioning surgical approval on conservative treatment in this emergency situation is clinically hazardous and unlawful.

VI. AUTHORIZATION AND DECISION
Based on the statutory protections under California Insurance Code § 10169.5, the regulatory mandates of DMHC APL 22-014, and the clinical standards established by the AANS/CNS and NASS, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED.
