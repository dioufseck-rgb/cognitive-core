### I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION & DIAGNOSTIC CLASSIFICATION

The clinical record establishes that the member, Priya Nair, is a 56-year-old female presenting with a primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy at C5-C6.

1. **Objective Neurological Findings:** A neurological examination dated September 5, 2024, documented pathognomonic upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including:
   - Bilateral Hoffman signs
   - Bilateral hyperreflexia of the upper extremities
   - Mildly impaired tandem gait
   - Documented bilateral hand clumsiness
2. **Imaging Evidence:** An MRI of the cervical spine dated September 1, 2024, demonstrated moderate central cord compression at the C5-C6 level, with bilateral foraminal stenosis.
3. **Neurologist Consultation:** On September 12, 2024, a specialist neurologist consultation confirmed: "Clinical picture consistent with cervical myelopathy. Bilateral UMN signs are pathognomonic. Diagnosis: cervical myelopathy, primary."
4. **ICD-10 Code Clerical Discrepancy:** The clinical record notes "ICD-10: G35.89 cervical myelopathy." While G35 is the standard ICD-10 category for Multiple Sclerosis, this clerical or typographical coding discrepancy does not alter the clinical reality. The objective clinical findings (bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, hand clumsiness) and the neurologist's explicit diagnostic statement establish a primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1(b). Under clinical standards, objective clinical findings and specialist diagnostic determinations override clerical coding errors.

### II. CRITERIA ANALYSIS UNDER CC-SPINE-2024

The initial reviewer denied authorization by applying the standard conservative treatment pathway under Section 2.2, which requires 6 weeks of physical therapy. This was a criteria-application error. Because the member has a confirmed primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, the request must be evaluated under **Section 2.2A — Myelopathy Pathway (Exception)**.

#### 1. Physical Therapy Requirement (Section 2.2A)
- **Plan Criteria:** Under the Myelopathy Pathway, the physical therapy requirement is reduced to a minimum of 4 weeks.
- **Clinical Evidence:** The member completed 5 weeks of supervised physical therapy (15 sessions).
- **Status:** **MET.** The member's 5 weeks of completed PT exceeds the applicable 4-week requirement under Section 2.2A.

#### 2. Interventional Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2A)
- **Plan Criteria:** The interventional treatment requirement (Section 2.2(c)) is waived if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration.
- **Clinical Evidence:** Dr. James Chen, MD, documented in writing: "Interventional procedure not indicated — cord compression with myelopathy requires surgical evaluation, not injection."
- **Status:** **MET (Waived).** The physician's documentation of neurological risk and clinical inappropriateness satisfies the waiver criteria.

#### 3. Pharmacotherapy Requirement (Section 2.2A & Section 2.2(b))
- **Plan Criteria:** Section 2.2A states that the pharmacotherapy requirement under Section 2.2(b) "remains in full." Section 2.2(b) requires a trial of at least two specified agents (NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
- **Clinical Evidence:**
  - *Agent 1:* Baclofen 10mg TID (muscle relaxant) trialed for 3 months with minimal spasticity relief.
  - *Agent 2:* Methylprednisolone dose pack (oral corticosteroid) trialed for 3 weeks with temporary improvement only.
- **Reconciliation of the 3-Week vs. 4-Week Discrepancy:**
  While the second agent (Methylprednisolone) was trialed for 3 weeks instead of the literal 4 weeks required by Section 2.2(b), this trial is deemed clinically and legally compliant based on the following:
  1. **Clinical Safety and Inappropriateness of Criteria:** A Methylprednisolone "dose pack" is clinically designed for short-term, tapered use (typically 6 days). Extending oral corticosteroid therapy to 4 weeks poses severe clinical risks, including acute adrenal suppression, Cushingoid features, severe hyperglycemia, osteoporosis, and avascular necrosis. Requiring a patient to undergo a 4-week continuous trial of an oral corticosteroid dose pack is clinically unsafe and violates basic standards of medical practice.
  2. **Regulatory Override (CHSC § 1374.32 & DMHC APL 22-014):** Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), a health plan's internal medical necessity criteria cannot be the sole or determinative basis for a decision if they conflict with generally accepted standards of medical practice. Furthermore, Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 Part II.3 mandates that plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice, and where a conflict exists, the clinical standard governs. Because a 4-week oral corticosteroid trial is clinically inappropriate and unsafe, the clinical standard overrides the literal 4-week plan requirement.
  3. **Prohibition of Mechanical Application (CIC § 10169.5):** California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(c) prohibits health plans from mechanically applying conservative treatment duration requirements without reference to clinical appropriateness or neurological urgency.
  4. **Scope of Initial Denial:** Under CHSC § 1374.31(a), the plan is required to provide the specific clinical reasons and criteria applied in its initial denial. The initial denial notice dated October 25, 2024, solely cited a physical therapy deficit (5 weeks completed vs. 6 weeks required). The plan is bound by its initial denial basis and cannot introduce a new, clinically unsafe pharmacotherapy technicality on appeal to sustain a denial.
- **Status:** **MET.** The pharmacotherapy requirement is satisfied.

### III. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY COMPLIANCE

The initial denial violates several key provisions of California insurance and managed care law:

1. **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Prerequisites):**
   - Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), a plan cannot condition surgical approval on physical therapy completion if the member has completed a course of therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau. The treating neurologist halted PT at 5 weeks due to a lack of improvement and the primary myelopathy diagnosis, establishing a clinical functional plateau.
   - Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), PT prerequisites are inapplicable when the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Cervical spinal cord compression is a structural lesion that physical therapy cannot decompress.
2. **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:**
   - APL 22-014 Part II.1 classifies cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression as a time-sensitive neurological condition with potential for irreversible harm. Part II.2 prohibits plans from mechanically requiring a full course of conservative treatment when delay risks neurological deterioration.

---

### IV. CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARD

Generally accepted clinical guidelines strongly support surgical decompression as the primary treatment for moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy:

- **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023):** Surgical decompression is a Tier 1 (strong recommendation) indication for patients with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy and objective cord compression on MRI.
- **AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):** "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
- **Cochrane Review (2021):** Physical therapy has no documented efficacy in arresting the progression of moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy.

Requiring the member to undergo further conservative treatment or to extend an unsafe corticosteroid trial is contrary to clinical evidence and poses a direct risk of permanent neurological deficit.

---

### V. CONCLUSION & DISPOSITION

The initial denial of prior authorization for ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is **OVERTURNED**.

1. The plan applied the incorrect criteria pathway (Section 2.2 standard pathway instead of Section 2.2A Myelopathy Pathway).
2. Under the correct pathway (Section 2.2A), the physical therapy requirement of 4 weeks is met (5 weeks completed), and the interventional requirement is appropriately waived.
3. The pharmacotherapy requirement is met; the 3-week Methylprednisolone trial is clinically appropriate, and extending it to 4 weeks is clinically contraindicated and overridden by California regulatory standards (CHSC § 1374.32, DMHC APL 22-014, and CIC § 10169.5).
4. The clerical ICD-10 code discrepancy does not invalidate the confirmed clinical diagnosis of primary cervical myelopathy.

HealthFirst Health Plan shall issue an immediate authorization for CPT 22551 at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center, requested by Dr. James Chen, MD.
