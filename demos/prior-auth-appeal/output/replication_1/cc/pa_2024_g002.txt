APPEAL DETERMINATION: OVERTURN INITIAL DENIAL

Dear Ms. Nair and Dr. Chen,

The Appeals and Grievances Department of HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of the prior authorization appeal for the requested single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California statutory and regulatory mandates, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has determined that the initial denial must be OVERTURNED.

The requested procedure is hereby APPROVED.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & DISPOSITION RESOLUTION

The initial denial issued on October 25, 2024, was based on a mechanical application of the standard conservative treatment pathway under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, which requires a minimum of 6 weeks of physical therapy. The initial review failed to recognize that the member has a confirmed primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, which triggers the specialized Myelopathy Pathway under CC-SPINE-2024 EXCEPTION 2.2(A).

Under the correct Myelopathy Pathway (Section 2.2A):
1. The physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks. The member has completed 5 weeks of physical therapy, which fully satisfies this requirement.
2. The interventional treatment requirement is waived. Dr. Chen documented that interventional procedures are not indicated due to cord compression, satisfying the waiver criteria.
3. The pharmacotherapy requirement (Section 2.2(b)) remains in full, requiring a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.

The clinical record shows that the member completed a 3-month trial of Baclofen (satisfying the first agent requirement) but completed only a 3-week trial of Methylprednisolone (which is technically 1 week short of the plan's 4-week minimum requirement for the second agent).

While a strict, mechanical reading of the plan's internal criteria would uphold the denial based on this 1-week pharmacotherapy deficit, California law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice strictly prohibit delaying necessary spinal surgery for a patient with objective, progressive upper motor neuron signs and structural spinal cord compression.

Pursuant to California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 and California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter APL 22-014, health plans cannot mechanically enforce conservative treatment durations when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise and a risk of irreversible harm. Furthermore, generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023 and AO Spine 2021) establish that moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy is a strong indication for primary surgical decompression, and extending conservative trials (such as oral steroids) in the face of progressive neurological deficits is clinically contraindicated.

Therefore, the 1-week deficit in the Methylprednisolone trial is legally and clinically overridden by California regulatory mandates and established medical standards. The initial denial is overturned, and the surgery is authorized.

II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION & OBJECTIVE FINDINGS

The clinical record establishes a clear, primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy supported by objective clinical and imaging findings:
1. Presenting Symptoms & Neurological Exam (2024-09-05): The member exhibits classic upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia of the upper extremities, mildly impaired tandem gait, and documented bilateral hand clumsiness. These findings are pathognomonic for cervical myelopathy.
2. Imaging (MRI 2024-09-01): Demonstrates moderate central cord compression at C5-C6 and bilateral foraminal stenosis.
3. Diagnosis Confirmation (2024-09-12): A formal neurologist consultation confirmed the clinical picture, stating: "Clinical picture consistent with cervical myelopathy. Bilateral UMN signs are pathognomonic. Diagnosis: cervical myelopathy, primary."
4. Coding Note: The clinical record lists the primary diagnosis code as "ICD-10: G35.89 cervical myelopathy." While G35.89 is technically an atypical code for cervical myelopathy (as G35 codes are typically reserved for demyelinating diseases like multiple sclerosis, and cervical myelopathy is more standardly coded under M50.0x or M47.1x), the clinical narrative, objective neurological exam, and MRI findings of moderate central cord compression establish the clinical diagnosis of primary cervical myelopathy beyond any administrative or coding ambiguity.

III. PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)

1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): Satisfied. The member has a confirmed primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy under Section 2.1(b), supported by an MRI within 6 months demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms.
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement) & EXCEPTION 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway):
   - Applicability: Because cervical myelopathy is documented as the primary diagnosis, EXCEPTION 2.2(A) applies.
   - Physical Therapy (2.2A): Reduced to 4 weeks minimum. Met. The member completed 5 weeks (15 sessions) of supervised physical therapy under a myelopathy protocol. The therapy was halted by the treating physician at 5 weeks due to a lack of improvement and the clinical necessity of surgical evaluation.
   - Interventional Treatment (2.2A): Waived. Dr. Chen documented in writing that "Interventional procedure not indicated — cord compression with myelopathy requires surgical evaluation, not injection." This satisfies the waiver provision for cases where interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration or is clinically inappropriate.
   - Pharmacotherapy (2.2b): Requires a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
     - Agent 1: Baclofen 10mg TID for 3 months (Met — exceeds 4-week minimum).
     - Agent 2: Methylprednisolone dose pack for 3 weeks (Not Met — 1 week short of the 4-week minimum).

IV. REGULATORY AND CLINICAL OVERRIDE OF THE PHARMACOTHERAPY DEFICIT

Under the hierarchy of clinical review authority, California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice supersede a health plan's internal coverage criteria when a conflict arises.

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy and Conservative Treatment as Prerequisite):
   - Under § 10169.5(a)(3), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on conservative treatment trials where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that conservative treatment cannot address.
   - Under § 10169.5(c), a plan's internal requirements for conservative treatment duration shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the condition involves neurological urgency. The member's moderate central cord compression and progressive upper motor neuron signs (hand clumsiness, gait impairment) constitute objective structural neurological compromise and clinical urgency.

2. DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Prior Authorization for Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions):
   - Section II.1: Cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans must treat these cases as urgent.
   - Section II.2: Plans shall NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology/compression, the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression, and surgery is indicated under clinical guidelines.
   - Section II.3: Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. Where a conflict exists, the clinical standard governs.

3. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards of Medical Practice:
   - The AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023) and AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021) state that for moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and corresponding neurological deficits, surgical decompression is the primary recommended treatment (Tier 1 indication).
   - The AO Spine Guidelines (2021) explicitly state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy [or conservative treatment] prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
   - Forcing a patient with progressive upper motor neuron signs to delay surgery for an additional week solely to complete an arbitrary 4-week oral steroid trial is clinically contraindicated, lacks scientific support, and exposes the patient to an unacceptable risk of permanent spinal cord injury.

V. CONCLUSION & AUTHORIZATION DETAILS

Because the member's clinical presentation represents an urgent, time-sensitive neurological condition with objective structural compromise, the plan's mechanical 4-week pharmacotherapy duration requirement is overridden by California Insurance Code § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.

The initial denial is OVERTURNED.
