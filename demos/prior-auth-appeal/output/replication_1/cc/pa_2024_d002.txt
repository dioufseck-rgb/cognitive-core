APPEAL DETERMINATION: OVERTURN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Dear Mr. Green and Dr. Kim,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its clinical and regulatory review of the appeal submitted on November 22, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). 

After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's internal coverage criteria, California statutory requirements, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has determined that the prior authorization denial issued on November 08, 2024, must be OVERTURNED. Authorization for the requested procedure is hereby APPROVED.

This determination is based on two independent and sufficient grounds: (1) the clinical record fully satisfies all medical necessity criteria under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, and (2) the initial denial notice was procedurally defective under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION I: CLINICAL EVALUATION UNDER PLAN COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 (Section 2) outlines the requirements for authorizing cervical spinal surgery. The clinical record demonstrates that the member fully satisfies every applicable criterion:

1. DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT (Section 2.1):
   - Criteria: The member must have a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy, defined as nerve root compression with radicular symptoms (pain, numbness, weakness in a dermatomal distribution), supported by an MRI within 6 months demonstrating correlating structural pathology.
   - Clinical Evidence: The clinical record documents a presenting diagnosis of "Cervical radiculopathy C5-C6 with functional impairment." A neurological examination dated September 25, 2024, reveals objective deficits: left C6 dermatomal numbness, a diminished left bicep reflex, and left bicep weakness graded at 4/5. A cervical MRI dated September 20, 2024 (within the 6-month window), demonstrates a "C5-C6 large disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression." This establishes a direct correlation between the structural pathology and the clinical symptoms, fully satisfying Section 2.1(a) and (c).

2. CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT (Section 2.2):
   - Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): The plan requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy directed toward the cervical condition. The clinical record documents that the member completed 7 weeks (total of 21 sessions) of supervised physical therapy, with notes documenting a functional plateau at week 6 and persistent radicular symptoms. This satisfies Section 2.2(a).
   - Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): The plan requires a trial of at least two specified agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The clinical record documents that the member trialed Gabapentin 600mg TID for 4 months (neuropathic agent, partial relief) and Naproxen 500mg BID for 3 months (NSAID, inadequate response). This satisfies Section 2.2(b).
   - Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): The plan requires at least one interventional treatment. The clinical record documents that the member underwent a left C6 epidural steroid injection on October 28, 2024, which provided 40% temporary relief before symptoms returned to baseline within 3 weeks. This satisfies Section 2.2(c).

3. IMAGING REQUIREMENT (Section 2.3):
   - Criteria: Current imaging within 6 months must demonstrate structural pathology consistent with symptoms and the proposed surgical level.
   - Clinical Evidence: The MRI dated September 20, 2024, confirms a large disc herniation at C5-C6 with moderate left C6 nerve root compression, which directly correlates with the left-sided C6 radicular symptoms and bicep weakness. This satisfies Section 2.3.

4. PHYSICIAN DOCUMENTATION (Section 2.4):
   - Criteria: The treating physician must provide clinical rationale, functional limitations, and surgical risks/alternatives.
   - Clinical Evidence: Dr. Sarah Kim, MD, has provided documentation detailing the member's functional impairment, clinical rationale for ACDF, and failure of conservative measures. This satisfies Section 2.4.

Because the member met all clinical criteria at the time of the initial review, the denial stating that the "Member does not meet criteria" was clinically incorrect on the merits.

SECTION II: REGULATORY COMPLIANCE AND PROCEDURAL DEFECTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Under California law, the initial denial notice issued on November 08, 2024, contained critical procedural defects that independently invalidate the denial:

1. VIOLATION OF CHSC § 1374.31(a) (FAILURE TO PROVIDE CRITERIA AND IMR NOTICE):
   - California Health & Safety Code § 1374.31(a)(2) mandates that a health plan's medical necessity denial notice must include "the specific criteria applied, the specific clinical reason for the denial, and notice of IMR [Independent Medical Review] rights."
   - The initial denial notice issued by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, referenced an "attached criteria document" but failed to attach it. Both the member and the provider confirmed that no criteria document was received. Furthermore, the plan failed to include the mandatory IMR notice.
   - Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason is "DEFECTIVE and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."

2. COMPLIANCE WITH CALIFORNIA INSURANCE CODE § 10169.5 (PHYSICAL THERAPY PLATEAU):
   - CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy if the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a "functional plateau" without adequate resolution of symptoms.
   - The clinical record explicitly documents that the member reached a functional plateau at week 6 of his 7-week physical therapy course, with persistent radicular symptoms. Mechanically denying the procedure under plan guidelines while ignoring this documented plateau violates CIC § 10169.5(c).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION III: CLINICAL EVIDENCE BASE AND MEDICAL NECESSITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Under California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32(b), the standard for Independent Medical Review (IMR) is based on generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersede a plan's internal criteria if the plan's criteria are more restrictive.

According to the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Radiculopathy (2023) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), surgical decompression via ACDF (CPT 22551) is a Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) standard of care for patients presenting with cervical radiculopathy, objective neurological deficits (such as 4/5 bicep weakness and diminished reflex), and persistent functional impairment who have failed an appropriate course of conservative management (6–12 weeks of physical therapy, pharmacotherapy, and interventional injections).

The member's clinical presentation and extensive conservative treatment history (7 weeks of PT, 4 months of Gabapentin, 3 months of Naproxen, and a cervical epidural steroid injection) fully align with these national clinical standards. Delaying surgical intervention in the presence of objective motor weakness (4/5 bicep strength) and a documented functional plateau is not supported by clinical evidence and risks permanent neurological deficit.
