Dear Mr. Green and Dr. Kim,

The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeal Review Committee has completed its review of the prior authorization appeal for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, we have determined that the initial denial issued on November 08, 2024, was incorrect both procedurally and clinically. 

Accordingly, the initial denial is OVERTURNED, and the requested procedure is hereby APPROVED.

I. PROCEDURAL DEFECTS AND REGULATORY COMPLIANCE
Under California law, health plans must adhere to strict procedural standards when issuing prior authorization denials. A review of the initial denial notice dated November 08, 2024, reveals critical statutory violations:

1. Failure to Provide Clinical Criteria (CHSC § 1374.31(b)): The initial denial notice stated that the member did not meet criteria under the plan policy and referenced an "attached criteria document." However, no criteria document was attached, and both the member and provider confirmed that no attachment was received. Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied is legally defective and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines.
2. Omission of Independent Medical Review (IMR) Notice (CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2)): The initial denial failed to include the mandatory notice of Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights. This omission directly violates CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2), which requires all medical necessity denials to include notice of IMR rights.

Because of these procedural defects, the initial denial was legally non-compliant. On procedural grounds alone, the denial is invalid.

II. CLINICAL COMPLIANCE WITH PLAN COVERAGE CRITERIA
In addition to the procedural defects, a clinical review of the member's medical records demonstrates that Mr. Green fully satisfies all medical necessity criteria outlined in the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery — Cervical Procedures (Document: CC-SPINE-2024, Effective: January 1, 2024):

1. Section 2.1 — Diagnosis Requirement: Mr. Green has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with correlating objective neurological deficits. 
   - Clinical Evidence: The neurological exam dated September 25, 2024, documents left C6 dermatomal numbness, a diminished left bicep reflex, and left bicep weakness graded at 4/5. 
   - Imaging Evidence: The cervical MRI dated September 20, 2024 (within the required 6-month window), demonstrates a large disc herniation at C5-C6 causing moderate left C6 nerve root compression, directly correlating with the member's left-sided C6 radicular symptoms. This satisfies Section 2.1(a) and Section 2.3.
2. Section 2.2 — Conservative Treatment Requirement: Mr. Green has completed a comprehensive course of conservative therapy that meets and exceeds all plan requirements:
   - Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): The member completed 7 weeks (total of 21 sessions) of supervised physical therapy directed at his cervical condition. The physical therapy notes document that the member reached a functional plateau at week 6, with persistent radicular symptoms. This satisfies the plan's 6-week minimum.
   - Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): The member completed trials of two distinct classes of medications for at least 4 weeks each:
     - Gabapentin (neuropathic agent): 600mg TID for 4 months, yielding only partial relief.
     - Naproxen (NSAID): 500mg BID for 3 months, yielding inadequate relief.
   - Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): The member underwent a left C6 cervical epidural steroid injection on October 28, 2024, which provided 40% temporary relief before symptoms returned to baseline within 3 weeks. This satisfies the requirement for at least one interventional treatment.
3. Section 2.4 — Physician Documentation: Dr. Sarah Kim, MD, has provided the necessary clinical rationale, documenting persistent functional impairment and radicular symptoms despite exhaustive conservative management, satisfying all documentation requirements.

III. ALIGNMENT WITH CLINICAL STANDARDS AND CALIFORNIA INSURANCE CODE
The clinical evidence further aligns with generally accepted standards of medical practice and California statutory protections:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Plateau): Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), a health insurer cannot condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy if the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a "functional plateau" without adequate resolution of symptoms. Mr. Green's physical therapy records explicitly document a functional plateau at week 6, meaning any further insistence on conservative therapy would violate California insurance law.
2. Generally Accepted Clinical Guidelines: Under the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020) and the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023), surgical decompression via ACDF is a Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) standard of care for patients with cervical radiculopathy who exhibit objective neurological deficits (such as Mr. Green's 4/5 bicep weakness and diminished reflex) and have failed to respond to 6 to 12 weeks of conservative management. Mr. Green's 7 weeks of physical therapy, 4 months of pharmacotherapy, and epidural injection constitute an exhaustive and failed conservative trial, making surgical intervention highly appropriate.

IV. AUTHORIZATION DETAILS
Based on the clinical and procedural findings, the prior authorization request for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level) is APPROVED.
