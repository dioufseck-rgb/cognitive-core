Dear Jessica Turner,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of the appeal filed on October 30, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551). 

After a comprehensive evaluation of the administrative record, the applicable California regulatory framework, and the plan's clinical coverage criteria, HealthFirst has determined that the initial denial issued on October 15, 2024, is procedurally invalid. By operation of California law, the requested surgical procedure is hereby DEEMED APPROVED, and the initial denial is OVERTURNED.

I. PROCEDURAL HISTORY AND IDENTIFIED DEFECTS

On October 15, 2024, HealthFirst issued an initial determination denying the request for CPT 22551. The plan's written denial notice stated: "Request denied based on peer-to-peer review. Plan medical director spoke with requesting physician on 10/12/2024. Based on that discussion, clinical criteria for surgery not met. Detailed criteria review provided verbally during peer-to-peer call."

Our administrative review has identified several critical procedural defects in this initial determination:
1. Failure to Provide Written Criteria: The plan failed to include the specific clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reasons for the denial in the written notice, relying instead on verbal communication during a peer-to-peer call.
2. Missing Independent Medical Review (IMR) Notice: The plan failed to include the mandatory state-required notice of Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights.
3. Critical Documentation Failure (Template Placeholder): The written denial notice contained an unpopulated template placeholder '10/12/2024' instead of the actual date of the peer-to-peer call. While plan records indicate that the peer-to-peer discussion between Plan Medical Director Dr. Alan Foster, MD, and requesting physician Dr. Robert Chen, MD, actually occurred on October 15, 2024, the propagation of this unpopulated template placeholder in the official written notice constitutes a severe documentation failure that invalidates the written record of the call.

II. STATUTORY ANALYSIS AND DEEMED APPROVAL MANDATE

Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a), when a health plan issues a denial based on medical necessity, it must provide a written notice that includes the specific clinical criteria applied, the specific clinical reasons for the denial, and notice of the member's IMR rights. 

CHSC § 1374.31(b) explicitly dictates the consequence of failing to meet these requirements:
"A denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is DEFECTIVE and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."

Because HealthFirst's October 15, 2024 denial notice failed to specify the clinical criteria in writing, omitted the IMR notice, and contained an unpopulated template placeholder '10/12/2024', the notice is legally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b) and must be treated "as if no denial was issued."

Under CHSC § 1367.01 and California Code of Regulations (CCR) Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1, a health plan is statutorily required to issue a decision on a standard prior authorization request within five (5) business days of receiving all necessary information. 

CCR Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1 and CHSC § 1367.01 further establish that if a plan fails to issue a timely, valid decision within this 5-business-day window, the requested service is "DEEMED APPROVED" by operation of law for the period of the delay.

Because the defective October 15, 2024 notice is treated as legally non-existent ("as if no denial was issued"), the plan has exceeded its statutory 5-business-day decision window. A "Remand" to allow the plan to reissue a compliant denial is legally untenable under California law; attempting to reissue a denial after the expiration of the statutory decision window is barred by the "Deemed Approval" trap. The statutory timeline has expired, and the request has been approved by operation of law.

III. CLINICAL MERITS AND CONFLICT RESOLUTION

We acknowledge that on the clinical merits, the patient's clinical record does not satisfy the plan's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) or generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020) for primary cervical radiculopathy. Specifically:
1. Physical Therapy: The patient completed 5 weeks of physical therapy (15 sessions), whereas CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a) and generally accepted clinical standards require a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy.
2. Pharmacotherapy: The patient trialed Meloxicam (3 months) and a Prednisone taper (2 weeks). Under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b), a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each is required; the Prednisone trial did not meet the 4-week duration threshold.
3. Interventional Treatment: The patient has no documented history of interventional treatment (such as a cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point injection series) as required by CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(c).
4. Myelopathy Exception: The patient's primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, not cervical myelopathy. Therefore, the conservative treatment exceptions under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a) and DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 do not apply.

However, under the hierarchy of authority governing health plan determinations, California statutory mandates and procedural protections (CHSC § 1374.31 and CHSC § 1367.01) strictly supersede the plan's internal clinical criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) and clinical guidelines. A health plan cannot enforce clinical criteria through a legally void and non-compliant denial process. 

Because the plan's procedural violations resulted in a statutory Deemed Approval, the clinical shortfalls are rendered legally moot.

IV. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION

The prior authorization denial for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C6-C7, one level (CPT 22551) is hereby OVERTURNED on procedural grounds. The procedure is DEEMED APPROVED by operation of California law.
