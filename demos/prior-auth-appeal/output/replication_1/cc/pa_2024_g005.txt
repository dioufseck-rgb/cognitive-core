Dear Mr. Obi and Dr. Diallo,

The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) at Stanford Medical Center. Based on a comprehensive review of the clinical documentation, the plan's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, the initial prior authorization denial issued on November 8, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED, and the procedure is APPROVED.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The initial denial was based on two grounds: (1) an alleged physical therapy (PT) shortfall (claiming only 5 weeks of the required 6 weeks were completed), and (2) poorly controlled type 2 diabetes (HbA1c 8.2%) presenting perioperative and wound healing risks. 

Upon appellate review, both grounds are determined to be invalid for denying prior authorization:
1. Physical Therapy Factual Error: The initial denial's claim of a PT shortfall is factually incorrect. Clinical records demonstrate that the member completed 7 weeks of continuous, supervised physical therapy (from July 1, 2024, through August 16, 2024, totaling 21 sessions) with a documented functional plateau. This fully satisfies the 6-week requirement under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a) and is protected under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2).
2. Preoperative Diabetes Management: While CC-SPINE-2024 does not condition prior authorization on specific HbA1c thresholds, clinical safety and appropriateness under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 require preoperative glycemic optimization to mitigate wound healing and infection risks. Because the clinical record confirms that the treating surgeon (Dr. Diallo) and the patient's endocrinologist (Dr. Patel) are actively coordinating this optimization preoperatively (targeting HbA1c < 7.5% or < 8.0% per clinical standards), this is a standard perioperative safety protocol rather than a barrier to prior authorization. The procedure is approved with the clinical directive that the surgical team proceed with the documented preoperative glycemic optimization prior to performing the surgery.

Additionally, this review identified a technical shortfall under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b) regarding conservative pharmacotherapy, as the member trialed two neuropathic agents (Pregabalin and Duloxetine) which represent a single category rather than two separate categories. However, because the plan did not deny on this ground, and because requiring further conservative trials in the face of objective structural neurological compromise (moderate left C7 nerve root compression with correlating 4/5 tricep weakness and diminished reflex) is clinically contraindicated under generally accepted standards of medical practice and legally protected under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), this technical shortfall does not bar approval.

DETAILED CLINICAL AND REGULATORY ANALYSIS

I. Physical Therapy Requirement (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a) & CIC § 10169.5)
The plan's initial denial letter dated November 8, 2024, asserted that the member completed only 5 weeks of physical therapy and therefore failed to meet the 6-week conservative treatment requirement. 

A review of the submitted physical therapy records from Stanford Medical Center reveals this assertion is factually incorrect. The records document continuous, supervised physical therapy directed at the cervical spine from July 1, 2024, through August 16, 2024, representing a total of 7 weeks and 21 completed sessions. This exceeds the 6-week minimum required under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a).

Furthermore, the physical therapy discharge summary explicitly notes a functional plateau: "no further functional improvement expected." Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms. Mechanically enforcing a minimum duration or requiring additional physical therapy under these circumstances is a violation of CIC § 10169.5(c). Therefore, the physical therapy requirement is fully satisfied and cannot serve as a basis for denial.

II. Pharmacotherapy Requirement and Technical Shortfall (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b) & CIC § 10169.5(a)(3))
Under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b), members are required to complete a trial of at least two of the specified medication categories (NSAIDs/COX-2 inhibitors, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each. 

The clinical record indicates that the member trialed Pregabalin 150mg BID for 5 months (partial pain relief) and Duloxetine 60mg daily for 3 months (moderate neuropathic benefit). Because both Pregabalin and Duloxetine are classified as neuropathic agents, they represent only a single category under Section 2.2(b). The member did not trial a second category (such as NSAIDs or muscle relaxants), resulting in a technical shortfall under the plan's internal criteria.

However, this technical shortfall does not bar prior authorization for the following reasons:
1. Procedural Binding: Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a)(2), a health plan must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for a denial. The plan's initial denial notice did not cite a pharmacotherapy shortfall as a ground for denial. The plan cannot retroactively raise new conservative treatment objections on appeal to sustain a denial.
2. Clinical Contraindication: The member's neurological examination on September 25, 2024, documented objective structural neurological compromise, including left C7 dermatomal numbness, a diminished left tricep reflex, left tricep weakness graded at 4/5, and left grip weakness graded at 4+/5. The member's MRI on September 20, 2024, confirmed a correlating C6-C7 large disc herniation with moderate left C7 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis. Under generally accepted standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023), delaying surgical decompression to perform additional conservative pharmacotherapy trials in a patient with objective, correlating motor weakness (4/5 tricep weakness) is clinically inappropriate and contraindicated, as it places the patient at significant risk of permanent, irreversible neurological deficit.
3. Statutory Protection: Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on conservative treatment (such as physical therapy or mechanical conservative trials) where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that conservative therapy cannot address. The patient's 4/5 tricep weakness and moderate C7 nerve root compression constitute objective structural neurological compromise. Therefore, enforcing a trial of a second class of medication is legally and clinically impermissible.

III. Preoperative Diabetes Management and Clinical Safety (CHSC § 1374.32)
The initial denial cited the member's poorly controlled type 2 diabetes (HbA1c 8.2% on October 15, 2024) as a ground for denial, noting elevated perioperative and wound healing risks.

Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, medical necessity determinations must ensure that requested services are "clinically appropriate" and "in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice." The patient's endocrinologist, Dr. Patel, noted on October 20, 2024, that the patient's HbA1c was suboptimally controlled and recommended glycemic optimization prior to elective surgery due to elevated wound healing risks with HbA1c > 8.

The plan recognizes that preoperative glycemic optimization (targeting HbA1c < 7.5% or < 8.0% per clinical standards) is a critical clinical safety requirement to minimize the risk of surgical site infection, wound dehiscence, and fusion failure. However, the clinical record demonstrates that this safety concern is already being actively addressed. The treating surgeon, Dr. Diallo, explicitly documented on October 25, 2024, that "diabetes management will be optimized preoperatively" in accordance with standard perioperative protocol.

Because perioperative glycemic management is a standard clinical planning and safety responsibility that is actively being coordinated between the surgical team and the patient's endocrinologist, it is not a valid basis to deny prior authorization for a procedure that is otherwise medically necessary. Denying or delaying prior authorization for a necessary spinal decompression based on a manageable comorbidity that is already undergoing active clinical optimization is clinically inappropriate.

Therefore, the prior authorization is approved, with the explicit clinical directive and expectation that the surgical and endocrinology teams will proceed with the documented preoperative glycemic optimization (targeting HbA1c < 7.5% or < 8.0% per clinical standards) prior to the physical performance of the surgery.

IV. Diagnostic, Imaging, and Interventional Requirements (CC-SPINE-2024 Sections 2.1, 2.3, 2.2(c))
All other medical necessity criteria under CC-SPINE-2024 are fully satisfied:
1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1(a)): The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy at C6-C7 with functional impairment, supported by radicular symptoms (pain, numbness, and weakness in the left C7 dermatomal distribution).
2. Imaging Requirement (Section 2.3): A current MRI of the cervical spine dated September 20, 2024 (within 6 months of the request) demonstrates a large disc herniation at C6-C7 with moderate left C7 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis, which directly correlates with the member's clinical symptoms and physical exam findings.
3. Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): The member completed a left C7 epidural steroid injection on October 10, 2024, which provided 45% temporary relief before symptoms returned to baseline, satisfying the interventional treatment requirement.
4. Physician Documentation (Section 2.4): Dr. Diallo has provided the required clinical rationale, description of functional limitations (including working reduced hours due to pain and hand weakness), and attestation of conservative treatment.

CONCLUSION & AUTHORIZATION DETAILS

Based on the clinical evidence and California regulatory standards, the prior authorization denial for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED.
