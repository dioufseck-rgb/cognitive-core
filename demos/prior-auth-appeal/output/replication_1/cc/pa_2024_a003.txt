The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the appeal submitted on October 28, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at the C4-C5 level (CPT 22551) to be performed at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center.

DETERMINATION:
The initial denial issued on October 15, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED. Prior authorization for CPT 22551 is FULLY APPROVED. This determination is based on a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery (Document: CC-SPINE-2024), California statutory and regulatory mandates, and generally accepted clinical practice guidelines.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The initial prior authorization request was denied on October 15, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, citing a failure to meet the conservative treatment requirements under Section 2.2 of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024. Specifically, the denial noted that the member completed only 5 weeks of physical therapy (6 weeks required) and had no record of a cervical epidural steroid injection.

Upon appellate review, this denial is overturned based on three independent and compounding grounds:
1. Compliance with Plan Criteria: The member has a primary diagnosis of progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia, which triggers "EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY" of CC-SPINE-2024. Under this pathway, the physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks (member completed 5 weeks), and the interventional treatment requirement is waived due to documented medical contraindications (permanent pacemaker).
2. California Regulatory Overrides: Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, the plan is legally prohibited from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of standard physical therapy or interventional trials when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) and documented medical contraindications. State law supersedes any conflicting or more restrictive plan criteria.
3. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards: National clinical guidelines, including the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) and AO Spine Guidelines (2021), classify progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia as a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) surgical emergency. Forcing conservative treatment in this clinical scenario is contraindicated and poses a severe risk of irreversible neurological deterioration.

II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND HISTORY
According to the clinical record submitted for review:
- Presenting Diagnosis: Progressive cervical myelopathy C4-C5 with myelomalacia.
- Objective Imaging: An MRI dated August 20, 2024 (performed using a pacemaker-compatible protocol) demonstrated severe central cord compression at C4-C5 with confirmed T2 signal change (myelomalacia), indicating progressive spinal cord injury.
- Neurological Examination: A physical exam on August 25, 2024, revealed significant upper motor neuron signs, including bilateral lower extremity spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor skills in both hands, and a positive Lhermitte sign.
- Comorbidities: The member has a permanent pacemaker (implanted in 2019, Boston Scientific), Type 2 diabetes, and hypertension.
- Conservative Treatment History:
  - Physical Therapy: Completed 5 weeks (15 sessions). Therapy was discontinued due to pacemaker precautions limiting specific modalities and a clinical determination by the treating physician that further physical therapy was not addressing, and could potentially worsen, the member's progressive neurological decline.
  - Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 3-month trial of Baclofen 10mg TID (muscle relaxant) with only partial relief of spasticity, and two separate 10-day courses of a Methylprednisolone taper (oral corticosteroid) which yielded only temporary improvement.
  - Interventional Treatment: Not performed. A fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injection was determined to be medically contraindicated per an anesthesiology consultation by Dr. Rivera on September 10, 2024, due to the patient's permanent pacemaker. This written contraindication was documented in the member's medical chart.

III. ANALYSIS OF PLAN CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
The initial reviewer applied the standard conservative treatment criteria under Section 2.2, which requires 6 weeks of physical therapy and at least one interventional procedure (such as an epidural injection). This was a critical administrative error.

1. Diagnosis Confirmation (Section 2.1):
The member's clinical record and MRI findings confirm a diagnosis of Cervical Myelopathy under Section 2.1(b), characterized by spinal cord compression with objective myelopathic symptoms (gait disturbance/spasticity, bilateral hand weakness, hyperreflexia, and a positive Lhermitte sign).

2. Application of Exception 2.2(A) — Myelopathy Pathway:
Because cervical myelopathy is the member's primary diagnosis, the conservative treatment requirements are modified as follows:
- Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): The minimum duration is reduced from 6 weeks to 4 weeks. The member completed 5 weeks (15 sessions) of supervised physical therapy, thereby fully satisfying this modified requirement.
- Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): The requirement is waived if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration or is otherwise contraindicated. Dr. Torres documented a written contraindication in the chart, supported by an anesthesiology consultation (Dr. Rivera, September 10, 2024) confirming that a fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injection is contraindicated due to the member's permanent pacemaker.
- Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): The member completed trials of two covered classes of medications: a muscle relaxant (Baclofen for 3 months) and an oral corticosteroid (Methylprednisolone taper, two courses), satisfying the pharmacotherapy requirement.

Consequently, the member fully satisfies the modified medical necessity criteria under Section 2.2 of CC-SPINE-2024. The initial denial was based on an incorrect application of the standard radiculopathy pathway.

IV. ANALYSIS OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY REQUIREMENTS
Under California law, the plan's mechanical application of conservative treatment requirements is invalid and legally overridden.

1. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:
APL 22-014 explicitly addresses "Prior Authorization Requirements for Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions." The Department of Managed Health Care has determined that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Under Section II.2 of the APL, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization" when:
- Objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (confirmed by the August 20, 2024 MRI showing T2 signal change/myelomalacia and progressive cord injury);
- The treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression (documented by Dr. Torres); and
- Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines.
The presence of myelomalacia on Ms. Osei's MRI triggers this regulatory waiver, rendering the plan's conservative treatment requirements legally unenforceable.

2. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5:
CIC § 10169.5(a) prohibits health insurers from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy under several circumstances:
- Subdivision (a)(1): When the treating physician documents in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member. Dr. Torres documented that PT was stopped because pacemaker precautions limited modalities and it was failing to address neurological progression.
- Subdivision (a)(3): When the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Severe central cord compression with myelomalacia is a structural spinal cord injury that physical therapy cannot anatomically resolve.
- Subdivision (c): Prohibits plans from applying a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy "mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or whether the condition involves neurological urgency." Denying coverage because the member completed 5 weeks instead of 6 weeks is a direct violation of this statutory provision.

V. ANALYSIS OF GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS
Pursuant to California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 and DMHC APL 22-014, where a conflict exists between internal plan criteria and generally accepted standards of medical practice, the clinical standard governs.

1. AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023):
The Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine recommends surgical decompression (such as ACDF) as a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) intervention for patients presenting with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy, objective cord compression, and myelomalacia (T2 signal change) on MRI.

2. AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):
The guidelines state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."

3. Clinical Significance of Myelomalacia:
Myelomalacia represents active, irreversible cellular damage to the spinal cord. Continued mechanical compression at C4-C5 poses an imminent threat of permanent quadriplegia or severe, irreversible neurological deficits. Delaying surgery to satisfy arbitrary conservative treatment durations is a violation of the standard of care.

VI. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS (HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY)
In resolving conflicts between the plan's internal clinical criteria, generally accepted clinical standards, and California state law, the following hierarchy of authority is applied:
1. California State Law & Regulatory Mandates (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) — Highest Authority
2. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine Guidelines) — Intermediate Authority
3. HealthFirst Internal Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) — Lowest Authority

Even if the plan's internal criteria did not contain the Myelopathy Pathway Exception (Exception 2.2(A)), the restrictive 6-week physical therapy and interventional requirements would be completely overridden by CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014. Furthermore, under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the clinical standard of care governs over restrictive internal plan criteria. Because the requested ACDF is medically necessary under generally accepted clinical standards, it must be approved.

CONCLUSION:
The prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED. The request for anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 (CPT 22551) is APPROVED.
