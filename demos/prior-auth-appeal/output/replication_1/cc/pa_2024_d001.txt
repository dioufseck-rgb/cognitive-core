Dear Ms. White,

The HealthFirst Appeals Department has completed its review of the appeal submitted on your behalf by Dr. Paul Lee regarding the prior authorization denial for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) at UCLA Medical Center.

Based on a comprehensive review of the clinical documentation, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, and the California regulatory framework, the initial denial is REMANDED. The initial denial notice dated October 5, 2024, is procedurally defective under California law. The plan is directed to immediately vacate the defective denial and reissue a legally compliant determination notice that addresses the clinical merits and includes all mandatory statutory disclosures, as detailed below.

I. PROCEDURAL DEFECTS AND REGULATORY VIOLATIONS
Under California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a), when a health plan issues a denial based on medical necessity, it is statutorily required to:
1. Specify the exact clinical criteria or guidelines applied;
2. Provide the specific clinical reasons for the denial; and
3. Include a mandatory notice of the member's right to an Independent Medical Review (IMR).

CHSC § 1374.31(b) explicitly states that a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is "DEFECTIVE and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."

The original denial notice issued by Dr. Alan Foster on October 5, 2024, contained only a generic reference to plan criteria ("Does not meet criteria. Conservative treatment incomplete. Physical therapy and medication requirements not satisfied") without citing any specific section numbers, specific criteria, or the plan document name (CC-SPINE-2024). Furthermore, the notice completely omitted the mandatory IMR rights disclosure, and the "imr_notice_included" field was marked false. Because the denial notice failed to meet these statutory requirements, it is legally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b). A member cannot be expected to meaningfully appeal a denial when the plan fails to disclose the specific criteria that were not met.

II. SUBSTANTIVE CLINICAL ANALYSIS UNDER PLAN CRITERIA
While the procedural defect requires a remand, a review of the clinical record against HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria Document CC-SPINE-2024 reveals that the clinical denial was substantively correct based on the plan's internal guidelines:

1. Diagnosis and Imaging (Section 2.1 & 2.3): The member meets these requirements. The MRI dated August 15, 2024, confirms a C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate right C7 nerve root compression, which correlates with the neurological exam on August 20, 2024, showing right C7 dermatomal numbness, tricep weakness (4/5), and a diminished tricep reflex.
2. Conservative Treatment (Section 2.2): The clinical record demonstrates a shortfall in the plan's standard conservative treatment requirements:
   - Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): The plan requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. The member completed only 4 weeks (12 sessions).
   - Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): The plan requires a trial of at least two agents from different classes (NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The member only trialed one agent (Ibuprofen 800mg TID for 6 weeks).
   - Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): The plan requires at least one interventional treatment (epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point injection series). The member has had no interventional treatments.
3. Myelopathy Pathway Exception (Section 2.2(A)): This exception does not apply. Although the exception reduces the PT requirement to 4 weeks and waives the interventional requirement, it applies only when cervical myelopathy is the primary diagnosis. The member's primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, and the MRI explicitly notes "No cord contact. No myelomalacia."

III. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARDS
Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3), a health plan cannot mechanically condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy if the condition involves "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(c) prohibits the mechanical application of a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or has neurological urgency.

In this case, the member exhibits objective structural neurological compromise, including 4/5 tricep weakness and a diminished tricep reflex, which correlates with moderate right C7 nerve root compression on MRI. Under generally accepted clinical standards, specifically the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023), surgical decompression (ACDF) is supported as a Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) for radiculopathy with persistent objective neurological deficits.

However, because the plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) are more restrictive than the clinical standard regarding the duration of physical therapy and the requirement for interventional injections, a conflict exists. Under California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 and CHSC § 1374.32(b), the Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard of medical necessity is based on generally accepted standards of medical practice, not the plan's internal criteria.

IV. CONCLUSION AND REMAND INSTRUCTIONS
Because the original denial notice was procedurally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b), the member's right to a fair and transparent appeal process was compromised. The plan cannot uphold a substantively correct denial that is procedurally defective.

Therefore, this case is REMANDED to the initial determination unit. The plan is directed to:
1. Vacate the defective denial notice dated October 5, 2024.
2. Re-evaluate the request, taking into account the provisions of CIC § 10169.5 regarding structural neurological compromise and the clinical standards under the AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines.
3. Within five (5) business days of this order, issue a new, legally compliant determination notice. If the plan chooses to maintain the denial, the new notice MUST:
   - Cite the specific plan document (CC-SPINE-2024) and the exact section numbers (e.g., Section 2.2) being applied.
   - Detail the specific clinical reasons for the denial (e.g., the specific shortfall of 4 weeks of PT vs. 6 weeks, the single pharmacotherapy agent, and the lack of interventional treatment).
   - Include the mandatory Independent Medical Review (IMR) application and notice of rights as required by CHSC § 1374.31.
