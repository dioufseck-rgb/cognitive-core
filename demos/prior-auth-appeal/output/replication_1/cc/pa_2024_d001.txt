Dear Sandra White,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of your first-level internal appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C6-C7 level (CPT 22551). 

After a comprehensive evaluation of your clinical records, the plan's internal coverage criteria, and applicable California statutory mandates, HealthFirst has determined that the initial denial of coverage dated October 5, 2024, must be OVERTURNED. Full authorization is hereby granted for the requested surgical procedure to be performed by Dr. Paul Lee at UCLA Medical Center.

This determination is based on the following procedural and clinical findings:

1. PROCEDURAL DEFECTS AND DEEMED APPROVAL UNDER CALIFORNIA LAW
Under California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a), when a health plan issues a prior authorization denial based on medical necessity, it is statutorily required to provide a written notice that specifies the exact clinical criteria applied, the specific clinical reasons for the denial, and a notice of the member's right to an Independent Medical Review (IMR). 

Your initial denial notice dated October 5, 2024, contained only a generic reference to plan criteria without citing any specific section or criterion, failed to provide the specific clinical reasons for the denial, and completely omitted the mandatory IMR rights notice.

Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reasons is legally defective and "shall be treated as if no denial was issued" for the purposes of appeal and decision timelines. Because the initial notice was defective, legally no denial was issued within the statutory 5-business-day prior authorization window mandated by CHSC § 1367.01. Under CHSC § 1367.01 and California Code of Regulations (CCR) Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1, when a plan fails to issue a timely, valid decision, the requested service is "Deemed Approved" by operation of law. Consequently, the procedural defects in the initial notice invalidate the denial and require an immediate overturn and authorization of the procedure.

2. CLINICAL ANALYSIS AND STATUTORY PHYSICAL THERAPY PROTECTIONS
The initial denial was substantively based on your failure to meet the conservative treatment requirements under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a), which requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. Your clinical records indicate that you completed 4 weeks (12 sessions) of physical therapy.

However, California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 supersedes the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria and prohibits the mechanical application of physical therapy duration requirements. Specifically:
- Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), a health plan shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms.
- Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), a plan is prohibited from conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address.

Your clinical record demonstrates both conditions:
- Objective Structural Neurological Compromise: Your MRI dated August 15, 2024, reveals a C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate right C7 nerve root compression. Your neurological examination dated August 20, 2024, documents objective neurological deficits, including right C7 dermatomal numbness, 4/5 tricep weakness, and a diminished tricep reflex. Physical therapy cannot resolve this structural nerve root compression.
- Documented Functional Plateau: You completed 4 weeks (12 sessions) of supervised physical therapy and reached a documented functional plateau with persistent, severe radicular symptoms.

Under CIC § 10169.5(c), the plan's internal requirement for a minimum of 6 weeks of physical therapy cannot be applied mechanically without reference to your functional plateau and objective neurological compromise. Therefore, the remaining 2 weeks of the physical therapy requirement are waived by operation of law, and the clinical criteria for ACDF are met.
