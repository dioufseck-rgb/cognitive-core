SUBJECT: APPEAL DETERMINATION — PARTIAL OVERTURN / PARTIAL UPHOLD

Dear Ms. Park and Dr. Wilson,

The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its clinical review of the first-level appeal submitted on November 10, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial for a two-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 and C6-C7 levels (CPT 22551 x2).

After a comprehensive review of the clinical documentation, plan coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has reached a PARTIAL determination:

1. OVERTURN: The prior authorization denial for the single-level ACDF at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED. This portion of the procedure is APPROVED.
2. UPHOLD: The prior authorization denial for the second-level ACDF at C6-C7 (CPT 22551 / 22552) is UPHELD. This portion of the procedure is DENIED.

A detailed clinical and regulatory rationale for this determination is provided below.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
CLINICAL BACKGROUND
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The clinical record indicates that Ms. Linda Park is a 45-year-old female presenting with a diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy at C5-C6 with adjacent C6-C7 degenerative disease.

- Neurological Examination (dated September 5, 2024): Documented right C6 dermatomal numbness and tingling, a diminished right bicep reflex, and right grip strength of 4/5. No C7 neurological findings, bilateral findings, or upper motor neuron signs were documented.
- Cervical Spine MRI (dated September 1, 2024): Demonstrated a large disc herniation at C5-C6 with moderate-to-severe nerve root compression and moderate foraminal stenosis, which clearly correlates with the patient's right C6 radicular symptoms. At the C6-C7 level, the MRI demonstrated a mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing, with no cord contact and no identifiable nerve root compression.
- Conservative Treatments Completed:
  - Physical Therapy: 8 weeks (24 sessions) across two providers, with persistent C6 radicular symptoms and no documented C7 symptoms (Physical Therapy Summary).
  - Pharmacotherapy: Gabapentin 600mg TID for 4 months (partial relief) and Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks (minimal benefit).
  - Interventional: A right C6 epidural steroid injection on October 1, 2024, which provided 50% temporary relief of right C6 symptoms that fully returned by week 4.
- Surgeon Note: Dr. James Wilson, MD, recommended a two-level fusion (C5-C6 and C6-C7) to address the C5-C6 pathology and to prophylactically stabilize the C6-C7 level to prevent adjacent segment disease, noting a 25% 5-year incidence of adjacent segment disease after single-level ACDF.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
CLINICAL AND REGULATORY ANALYSIS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

I. C5-C6 LEVEL — OVERTURN (APPROVED)
The request for ACDF at the C5-C6 level satisfies all applicable plan criteria, clinical standards, and regulatory requirements:

1. Plan Criteria Alignment: Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2, surgical authorization requires a confirmed diagnosis, completed conservative treatment, and correlating imaging.
   - Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): Satisfied by a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with correlating right C6 dermatomal numbness, diminished bicep reflex, and 4/5 grip strength.
   - Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): Satisfied by the completion of 8 weeks of physical therapy (exceeding the 6-week requirement in Section 2.2(a)), a trial of two qualifying medications (Gabapentin and Cyclobenzaprine) for at least 4 weeks each (satisfying Section 2.2(b)), and one interventional cervical epidural steroid injection on October 1, 2024 (satisfying Section 2.2(c)).
   - Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement): Satisfied by the September 1, 2024 MRI demonstrating a large disc herniation and moderate-to-severe nerve root compression at C5-C6, which directly correlates with the documented C6 radicular symptoms.
2. Clinical Standards: Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Section 3.1, surgical decompression and fusion is a Tier 2 (reasonable) indication for patients with cervical radiculopathy and persistent symptoms after 6 to 12 weeks of conservative treatment. Ms. Park has persistent symptoms despite 8 weeks of physical therapy, 4 months of neuropathic medication, and an epidural injection.
3. Regulatory Standard: Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(a), a service is medically necessary when a provider, exercising prudent clinical judgment, provides it to treat an illness, injury, or its symptoms in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice. The clinical evidence overwhelmingly supports the medical necessity of ACDF at C5-C6.

Therefore, the denial of the C5-C6 level is overturned.

II. C6-C7 LEVEL — UPHOLD (DENIED)
The request for ACDF at the C6-C7 level does not satisfy plan criteria, clinical standards, or regulatory medical necessity requirements:

1. Plan Criteria Non-Alignment: Under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.3, imaging must demonstrate structural pathology consistent with the member's symptoms and the proposed surgical level. Furthermore, Section 3.3 explicitly excludes coverage when imaging does not demonstrate pathology at the proposed surgical level consistent with the clinical presentation.
   - The September 1, 2024 MRI shows only a mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing at C6-C7, with no cord contact or nerve root compression.
   - The clinical record contains no documented C7 radicular symptoms (such as pain, numbness, or weakness in a C7 distribution) and no correlating physical exam findings (such as diminished triceps reflex or C7 dermatomal sensory loss).
2. Clinical Standards: Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Section 3.1, surgical intervention is classified as Tier 3 (not indicated) when imaging findings lack correlating clinical symptoms. Prophylactic fusion of an adjacent asymptomatic level to prevent adjacent segment disease is not supported by high-quality clinical evidence as a medically necessary intervention. While Dr. Wilson notes a 25% 5-year incidence of adjacent segment disease, clinical guidelines do not support fusing an asymptomatic, non-compressed level solely for prophylactic stabilization.
3. Regulatory Standard: Under CHSC § 1374.32(a), a service must be clinically appropriate in terms of site and extent, and not primarily for the convenience of the provider. Fusing an asymptomatic level with minimal degenerative changes does not meet the statutory definition of medical necessity.

Therefore, the denial of the C6-C7 level is upheld.

III. PROCEDURAL COMPLIANCE AND DEFECT RESOLUTION
Under CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2), health plans are required to include the specific clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons in all written denial notices. A review of the initial denial notice dated October 28, 2024, reveals that the plan failed to cite the specific sections of the Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) applied to the review.

Pursuant to CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied is procedurally defective. HealthFirst acknowledges this procedural defect. However, under California's regulatory framework, procedural defects do not automatically mandate the approval of non-medically necessary services. Under CHSC § 1374.32, the substantive clinical standard of medical necessity remains the governing factor for the final appeal determination. Because the C6-C7 level is clinically non-indicated, the substantive clinical standard requires that the denial for the C6-C7 level be upheld despite the initial procedural omission.
