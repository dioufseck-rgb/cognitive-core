HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its clinical and regulatory review of the appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a two-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 and C6-C7 levels (CPT 22551 x2).

Based on a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, we have made the following determination:

1. C5-C6 LEVEL: OVERTURNED (APPROVED)
The initial denial of the ACDF at the C5-C6 level is OVERTURNED. Prior authorization is APPROVED for CPT 22551 at the C5-C6 level.

2. C6-C7 LEVEL: UPHELD (DENIED)
The initial denial of the ACDF at the C6-C7 level is UPHELD. Prior authorization is DENIED for the C6-C7 level. Prophylactic fusion of this level does not meet medical necessity criteria.

This decision is final at the health plan level. Because a portion of your request has been denied, you have the right to appeal this decision to the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) for an Independent Medical Review (IMR). Detailed instructions on how to file for an IMR are provided at the end of this letter.

I. ANALYSIS OF THE C5-C6 LEVEL (OVERTURNED / APPROVED)

The clinical record demonstrates that the C5-C6 level fully satisfies all medical necessity criteria outlined in HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria Document CC-SPINE-2024:

1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1): The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy. The neurological exam dated September 5, 2024, documents objective neurological deficits, including right C6 dermatomal numbness and tingling, a diminished right bicep reflex, and right grip strength of 4/5. The MRI dated September 1, 2024, demonstrates a large disc herniation with moderate-to-severe nerve root compression and moderate foraminal stenosis at C5-C6, which directly correlates with the right C6 radicular symptoms.
2. Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2):
   - Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): The member completed 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy (24 sessions total) across two providers, exceeding the plan's 6-week minimum.
   - Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): The member completed trials of two qualifying agents: Gabapentin 600mg TID for 4 months (neuropathic agent) and Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks (muscle relaxant), satisfying the 4-week minimum trial duration.
   - Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): The member underwent a right C6 epidural steroid injection on October 1, 2024, which provided 50% temporary relief before symptoms fully returned by week 4.
3. Imaging Requirement (Section 2.3): The MRI dated September 1, 2024 (within 6 months of the request) demonstrates structural pathology (large disc herniation and moderate-severe nerve root compression) consistent with the member's right C6 radicular symptoms.
4. Physician Documentation (Section 2.4): Dr. Wilson has provided the necessary clinical rationale, functional limitations, and risk discussion.

Because all criteria under CC-SPINE-2024 are met, the denial for the C5-C6 level is overturned and approved.

II. ANALYSIS OF THE C6-C7 LEVEL (UPHELD / DENIED)

The clinical record does not support the medical necessity of extending the fusion to the C6-C7 level. The denial is upheld based on the following criteria:

1. Failure to Meet Imaging and Symptom Correlation (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.3 & Section 3.3):
   - Section 2.3 requires that current imaging demonstrate structural pathology consistent with the member's symptoms and proposed surgical level.
   - Section 3.3 explicitly excludes coverage when imaging does not demonstrate pathology at the proposed surgical level consistent with the clinical presentation.
   - The MRI dated September 1, 2024, shows only a "mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing, no cord contact, and no identifiable nerve root compression" at C6-C7.
   - Furthermore, the neurological exam dated September 5, 2024, and physical therapy records document no C7 dermatomal symptoms, no C7 reflex changes, and no C7-related weakness. There are no clinical symptoms or objective neurological findings attributable to the C6-C7 level.
2. Prophylactic Fusion is Not Medically Necessary:
   - Dr. Wilson's clinical rationale states that the C6-C7 level should be fused "prophylactically" to prevent adjacent segment disease, citing a 25% 5-year incidence after single-level ACDF.
   - Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(a), "medically necessary" services must be in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice and clinically appropriate in terms of extent and site.
   - Generally accepted clinical standards, including the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy and Radiculopathy (2023 Update), classify surgical intervention for imaging findings without correlating symptoms as Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated).
   - Prophylactic fusion of an asymptomatic adjacent level with mild degenerative changes does not meet the clinical standard of medical necessity. It unnecessarily increases surgical risk, blood loss, and recovery time without proven clinical benefit for the patient's active symptoms.

Therefore, because the C6-C7 level fails to meet CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.3 and falls under the Section 3.3 exclusion, and because prophylactic fusion violates generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS Tier 3), the denial of the C6-C7 level is UPHELD.

III. CORRECTION OF PROCEDURAL DEFECTS AND REGULATORY COMPLIANCE

1. Correction of Initial Denial Defect: Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the denial. The initial denial notice dated October 28, 2024, failed to explicitly cite the plan's clinical coverage criteria document (CC-SPINE-2024). This appeal determination letter corrects that procedural defect by explicitly citing and applying the relevant sections of CC-SPINE-2024.
2. Regulatory Compliance on Appeal Dispositions: Under California Health & Safety Code § 1367.01, a health plan must issue a definitive decision (approve, modify, or deny) within 30 days for a standard appeal. A "remand" is not a legally valid appeal disposition under California law, as it would unlawfully delay the member's access to care and their right to an Independent Medical Review (IMR) under CHSC § 1374.30. Accordingly, HealthFirst has issued this definitive partial determination (Partial — Overturn for C5-C6, Uphold for C6-C7) to ensure full compliance with California regulatory timelines and to preserve the member's immediate right to seek IMR for the denied C6-C7 component.
