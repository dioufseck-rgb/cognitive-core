Dear Ms. Brooks,

The Appeals and Grievances Department of HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of the appeal submitted on December 5, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial of the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 (CPT 22551). 

After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's internal coverage criteria, California regulatory statutes, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst Health Plan has determined that a genuine clinical and radiological conflict exists regarding your diagnosis. Because this conflict involves a complex dispute of clinical judgment between qualified medical specialists, a binary administrative decision to flatly uphold or overturn the denial is legally and clinically inappropriate. 

Accordingly, this case is being REMANDED and GATED for immediate referral to an Independent Medical Review (IMR) administered by the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) pursuant to California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.30.

I. CLINICAL RECORD AND RADIOLOGICAL CONFLICT

The clinical record contains a significant and unresolved conflict regarding the interpretation of your cervical spine magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) dated October 1, 2024:

1. Original MRI Interpretation (October 1, 2024): Radiologist A interpreted the MRI as demonstrating a C4-C5 disc herniation with cord contact. The radiologist noted "possible early T2 signal change" but concluded that this finding was "equivocal" and "may represent artifact," recommending clinical correlation.
2. Treating Hospital Second Read (October 15, 2024): Radiologist B at the treating hospital (UCSF Medical Center) re-evaluated the same MRI and concluded there is C4-C5 cord compression with "early myelomalacia." Radiologist B documented that the T2 hyperintensity is "reproducible on multiple sequences and represents early cord signal change, not artifact," which is consistent with early myelopathy.
3. Plan Radiologist Review (November 5, 2024): The plan's board-certified radiologist reviewed the imaging and concluded that the T2 signal change is "below the threshold for clinical significance," does not represent myelomalacia, and is instead indicative of "artifact or a normal variant."

In contrast to the disputed imaging findings, your physical neurological examination performed on October 5, 2024, reveals objective, undisputed upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including:
- Bilateral Hoffman signs;
- Bilateral hyperreflexia of the upper extremities;
- Subtle balance impairment during tandem gait testing.

II. EVALUATION UNDER PLAN COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)

Under the HealthFirst Health Plan Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery — Cervical Procedures (Document: CC-SPINE-2024):

1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1): Section 2.1(b) requires a "confirmed diagnosis" of cervical myelopathy, defined as spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms. Because the imaging findings (specifically the presence of early myelomalacia or true T2 cord signal change) are highly disputed among three qualified radiologists, the imaging component of the myelopathy diagnosis remains unconfirmed under the plan's utilization review guidelines.
2. Conservative Treatment Pathway (Section 2.2): 
   - Under the standard pathway, a member must complete 6 weeks of physical therapy, a trial of two neuropathic or anti-inflammatory agents for 4 weeks each, and at least one interventional treatment under Section 2.2(c) (such as a cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point injection series).
   - Your record indicates you completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of physical therapy and met the pharmacotherapy requirement (Gabapentin for 3 months and Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks). However, no interventional procedure was completed.
   - While your treating physician, Dr. James Park, MD, documented that an epidural steroid injection was not performed due to safety concerns regarding cord compression, the standard pathway requires at least one interventional option. The clinical record does not document why other non-contraindicated interventional options, such as a trigger point injection series, were not attempted.
3. Myelopathy Pathway Exception 2.2(A): This exception waives the interventional treatment requirement (Section 2.2(c)) and reduces the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks. However, Section 2.2(A) explicitly states that this pathway applies only to members with a "confirmed cervical myelopathy" under Section 2.1(b). Because the radiological conflict prevents the diagnosis of myelopathy from being "confirmed" under the plan's internal guidelines, a clean application of Exception 2.2(A) is legally and procedurally blocked.

III. EVALUATION UNDER CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK

1. Inapplicability of DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 II.2:
   The treating physician's appeal suggests that standard conservative treatment requirements should be waived under DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 II.2. However, APL 22-014 II.2(a) specifically requires that "objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia, or progressive herniation on serial imaging)" to mandate a waiver of conservative treatment. In this case, there is only a single MRI from October 1, 2024, which has been interpreted differently by various clinicians. Because there is no "serial imaging" demonstrating "progressive pathology" in the clinical record, APL 22-014 II.2 cannot be applied as a mandatory regulatory waiver.

2. Resolution of Clinical Conflict under CHSC § 1374.30 and § 1374.32:
   Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), the standard for determining medical necessity during an Independent Medical Review (IMR) is based on generally accepted standards of medical practice, rather than the plan's internal coverage criteria. 
   - Generally accepted clinical standards, including the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Guidelines (2020), state that surgical decompression (ACDF) is recommended as the primary treatment for patients with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy and objective cord compression, and that delaying surgery to complete conservative trials in the presence of true myelomalacia risks irreversible neurological harm.
   - Because there is a direct conflict of clinical judgment between your treating hospital's radiologist (who identifies early myelomalacia) and the plan's board-certified reviewer (who identifies the finding as an artifact), the plan's administrative appeals coordinator cannot exceed their clinical authority by resolving this complex radiological dispute. 
   - Under CHSC § 1374.30, such disputes regarding clinical judgment and medical necessity must be referred to an independent, third-party medical specialist who can review the actual imaging studies and clinical presentation to make a binding determination under generally accepted clinical standards.

IV. DISPOSITION AND NEXT STEPS

Because the clinical UMN signs are present but the imaging component of the myelopathy diagnosis remains unconfirmed due to the radiological conflict, a flat administrative OVERTURN or UPHOLD is inappropriate. 

HealthFirst Health Plan has GATED and REMANDED this case for Independent Medical Review (IMR) per CHSC § 1374.30. This ensures that an independent, board-certified neurosurgeon or neuroradiologist, exercising independent clinical judgment under CHSC § 1374.32, will resolve the radiological conflict and determine whether the requested ACDF C4-C5 (CPT 22551) is medically necessary under generally accepted standards of medical practice.
