DISPOSITION: REMAND FOR INDEPENDENT MEDICAL REVIEW (IMR)
Pursuant to California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.30, the prior authorization denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C4-C5) is hereby REMANDED for Independent Medical Review (IMR) administered by the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC). A binary internal determination to UPHOLD or OVERTURN is deferred, as a critical clinical and radiological dispute exists between qualified specialists that must be resolved by an independent, board-certified external reviewer.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
On November 20, 2024, HealthFirst initially denied prior authorization for a single-level ACDF at C4-C5 (CPT 22551) requested by Dr. James Park, MD, for member Helen Brooks. The denial, reviewed by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, was based on the "Standard Pathway" of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, citing that the member had completed physical therapy and pharmacotherapy but had not undergone an interventional procedure (such as an epidural steroid injection). The plan's reviewer determined that the "Myelopathy Exception" did not apply because the MRI T2 signal change was minimal, potentially representing an artifact, and was below the threshold for clinical significance.

On appeal, the treating physician and hospital present a second radiologist read confirming early myelomalacia (reproducible T2 hyperintensity on multiple sequences, not artifact) and document objective, correlating upper motor neuron (UMN) signs on clinical examination. Under California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32 and DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, the plan cannot unilaterally rely on its own reviewer's interpretation to deny coverage when a qualified treating hospital radiologist has confirmed myelomalacia in a clinically symptomatic patient. Because this radiological conflict directly determines whether the conservative treatment requirements are waived, this case is remanded for an expedited Independent Medical Review (IMR) under CHSC § 1374.30.

II. CLINICAL RECORD & PRESENTING PRESENTATION
According to the clinical record provided:
1. Clinical Examination (Date: October 5, 2024): The member exhibits objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia of the upper extremities, and subtle balance impairment on tandem gait. No lower extremity findings are documented.
2. Conservative Treatment:
   - Physical Therapy: The member completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy directed at her cervical condition. The physical therapy summary notes "no improvement in UMN signs," indicating she reached a functional plateau.
   - Pharmacotherapy: The member completed a 3-month trial of Gabapentin (400mg TID) with partial benefit, and a 6-week trial of Cyclobenzaprine with minimal benefit, satisfying the plan's requirement for two agents trialed for at least 4 weeks.
   - Interventional Treatment: No interventional procedures (e.g., epidural steroid injections) were performed. Dr. Park documented that he did not refer the member for an epidural due to myelopathy concerns, noting that epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated in the presence of spinal cord compression.

III. THE RADIOLOGICAL DISPUTE
The core clinical conflict centers on the interpretation of the member's cervical MRI:
1. Original MRI Read (Date: October 1, 2024 - Radiologist A): Findings showed a C4-C5 disc herniation with cord contact. The report noted "possible early T2 signal change — equivocal, may represent artifact" and stated "clinical correlation required."
2. Second MRI Read (Date: October 15, 2024 - Radiologist B, UCSF Medical Center): Findings showed C4-C5 cord compression with early myelomalacia. The radiologist explicitly noted that the "T2 hyperintensity is reproducible on multiple sequences and represents early cord signal change, not artifact," concluding it is "consistent with early myelopathy."
3. Plan Radiologist Review (Date: November 5, 2024): The plan's reviewer concluded that the "T2 signal change is below threshold for clinical significance. Not consistent with myelomalacia. Represents artifact or normal variant."

IV. ANALYSIS OF PLAN COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024:
1. Section 2.1(b) (Cervical Myelopathy Diagnosis): Requires spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms (including gait disturbance, hyperreflexia, and positive Hoffman sign). The member's neurological exam on October 5, 2024, documented bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia, and subtle balance impairment, satisfying the clinical diagnostic criteria.
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirements): Under the standard pathway, a member must complete 6 weeks of physical therapy, a trial of two neuropathic/muscle relaxant agents, and at least one interventional treatment (e.g., cervical epidural steroid injection).
3. Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway): For members with confirmed cervical myelopathy, the physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks, and the interventional treatment requirement (Section 2.2(c)) is waived if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration.
4. Application: If the treating hospital's MRI interpretation of "early myelomalacia" is correct, the member has confirmed cervical myelopathy as her primary diagnosis. Under Exception 2.2(A), her 8 weeks of physical therapy and 3 months of pharmacotherapy fully satisfy the modified conservative treatment requirements, and the interventional requirement is waived due to Dr. Park's documented contraindication (risk of neurological deterioration from epidural injection in the setting of cord compression). However, by relying on its own radiologist's review (which labeled the T2 signal change as an artifact), the plan applied the "Standard Pathway" and denied the request under Section 3.2 for failure to complete an interventional procedure.

V. ANALYSIS OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK
Under California law, the plan's mechanical application of its internal criteria is superseded by statutory and regulatory mandates:
1. CHSC § 1374.32 (Standard of Review for IMR): Medical necessity must be determined based on generally accepted standards of medical practice, clinical appropriateness, and effectiveness. Subdivision (b) explicitly states that the standard does not require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria, and the plan's criteria shall not be the sole or determinative basis for a decision.
2. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014: The DMHC classifies cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI as a time-sensitive neurological condition with potential for irreversible harm. Under Section II.2, plans shall not require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as cord signal change or myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents urgency. Section II.3 mandates that where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs.
3. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5: Under subdivision (a)(3), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Under subdivision (c), a plan's internal requirement for physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or exhibits neurological urgency.

VI. ANALYSIS OF CLINICAL EVIDENCE BASE
Generally accepted clinical standards do not support the plan's denial:
1. Diagnostic Significance of Myelomalacia: According to the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) and AO Spine Guidelines (2021), the presence of myelomalacia (T2 signal change) on MRI indicates existing spinal cord injury and represents a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) for surgical decompression.
2. Inefficacy and Risk of Conservative Treatment: The AO Spine Guidelines (2021) state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
3. Contraindication of Interventional Procedures: In patients with significant spinal cord compression and clinical myelopathy, epidural steroid injections carry a risk of mechanical or ischemic cord injury and are relatively contraindicated. Requiring an interventional procedure in this clinical scenario violates the basic medical standard of "do no harm."

VII. RESOLUTION OF CROSS-SOURCE CONFLICTS & JUSTIFICATION FOR REMAND
A direct conflict exists between:
- The Plan's Internal Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), which was applied to deny coverage because the plan's reviewer interpreted the MRI T2 signal change as an artifact, thereby enforcing the standard pathway requiring an injection.
- California Law (CHSC § 1374.32, DMHC APL 22-014, CIC § 10169.5) and Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021), which dictate that clinical standards override plan criteria, that conservative treatment (including injections and extended PT) is not indicated or is contraindicated for progressive myelopathy with cord compression, and that qualified specialist disagreements on imaging must be resolved in favor of patient safety.

Under the hierarchy of authority (State Law > Clinical Standards > Plan Criteria), the plan cannot resolve a three-way radiological conflict (Radiologist A: equivocal; Radiologist B: reproducible early myelomalacia; Plan Radiologist: artifact) by simply adopting its own reviewer's opinion to deny a necessary surgical procedure for a patient with clear, correlating upper motor neuron signs. 

Because the resolution of this radiological dispute is determinative of whether the member meets the criteria for surgical decompression, and because a unilateral denial by the plan violates CHSC § 1374.32 and DMHC APL 22-014, this case is REMANDED for Independent Medical Review (IMR) under CHSC § 1374.30. An independent, board-certified neurosurgeon will perform a definitive review of the MRI sequences and clinical record to determine medical necessity.
