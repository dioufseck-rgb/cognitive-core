CLINICAL AND REGULATORY ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

I. APPROVAL OF C5-C6 LEVEL (CPT 22551)

The clinical documentation submitted fully satisfies the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery — Cervical Procedures (Document: CC-SPINE-2024) and California statutory medical necessity standards under CHSC § 1374.32(a) for the C5-C6 level:

1. Diagnosis and Objective Neurological Deficit (Section 2.1):
   The clinical record documents a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with severe objective neurological deficits correlating to the C5-C6 level. The neurological examination dated September 20, 2024, reveals significant right bicep weakness (graded 3+/5), an absent right bicep reflex, and right C6 dermatomal numbness and tingling.

2. Imaging Findings (Section 2.3):
   The cervical spine MRI dated September 15, 2024, demonstrates a large disc herniation at C5-C6 causing moderate-to-severe right C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis, directly correlating with the member's clinical symptoms and neurological deficits.

3. Conservative Treatment (Section 2.2):
   The member has completed a comprehensive, documented course of conservative therapy that failed to resolve his C6 symptoms:
   - Physical Therapy: Completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy directed at the cervical spine, satisfying the 6-week minimum requirement under Section 2.2(a).
   - Pharmacotherapy: Trialed Pregabalin 150mg BID for 4 months (neuropathic agent) and Naproxen 500mg BID for 3 months (NSAID), satisfying the requirement for two agents for at least 4 weeks under Section 2.2(b).
   - Interventional Treatment: Received a right C6 selective epidural steroid injection on October 15, 2024, which provided 70% temporary relief but symptoms fully returned within 4 weeks, satisfying Section 2.2(c).

Because the C5-C6 level meets all clinical criteria and represents a standard, medically necessary intervention under CHSC § 1374.32(a), the initial "all-or-nothing" denial of this level was inappropriate. Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial must specify the exact clinical reasons and criteria applied. Denying a clearly indicated, medically necessary single-level procedure (C5-C6) solely because an adjacent level (C6-C7) was not indicated constitutes a procedural defect. Therefore, the denial for C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is overturned and approved.

II. DENIAL OF C6-C7 LEVEL (CPT 22552)

The request for the additional C6-C7 level (CPT 22552) does not meet the plan's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) or generally accepted standards of medical practice, and is denied based on the following clinical facts:

1. Lack of Correlating Symptoms and Deficits (Section 2.1 & Section 3.3):
   The neurological examination dated September 20, 2024, notes "no definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms" and only a "slightly diminished" right tricep reflex. Furthermore, the physical therapy discharge summary at week 8 explicitly notes that the member's C7 symptoms were "minimal and possibly resolving."

2. Insufficient Imaging Pathology (Section 2.3 & Section 3.3):
   The MRI dated September 15, 2024, shows only a "small disc bulge" and "mild foraminal narrowing" at C6-C7. Crucially, the radiologist noted "no nerve root compression identifiable" and "no cord contact" at the C6-C7 level. Under Section 3.3 of the plan criteria, surgical authorization must be denied when imaging does not demonstrate pathology at the proposed level consistent with the clinical presentation.

3. Refutation of the Prophylactic Adjacent-Level Fusion Argument:
   In the appeal, Dr. Park argues that both levels should be addressed simultaneously to prevent accelerated adjacent-segment degeneration. While adjacent-segment degeneration is a recognized long-term phenomenon in spine surgery, generally accepted clinical standards do not support the prophylactic fusion of asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic levels that lack objective pathology.

   According to the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines for Multidisciplinary Spine Care (2020) and the American Association of Neurological Surgeons / Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Joint Guidelines (2023), surgical decompression and fusion must be strictly limited to levels where there is a direct correlation between clinical radicular symptoms, objective neurological deficits on physical exam, and advanced structural pathology (such as severe foraminal stenosis or nerve root compression) on imaging. 

   Fusing the C6-C7 level prophylactically in a patient with resolving, non-definitive C7 symptoms and mild MRI findings (no nerve root compression) is not clinically appropriate, is not considered effective for the patient's active illness, and violates the clinical standard of conserving spinal motion segments where possible. Therefore, the C6-C7 level does not meet the definition of medical necessity under CHSC § 1374.32(a) and is excluded under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 3.3.
