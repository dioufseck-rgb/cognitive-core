HealthFirst Health Plan's Appeal Determination Services has completed its review of the appeal regarding the prior authorization denial for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). HealthFirst Health Plan will authorize the requested surgical procedure.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND DIAGNOSTIC ESTABLISHMENT

The clinical record establishes that the member, Frances Kim, has clinical cervical myelopathy. A neurological examination performed on September 5, 2024, revealed multiple objective, bilateral upper motor neuron (UMN) signs:
1. Positive Hoffman sign bilaterally
2. Hyperreflexia in the bilateral upper extremities
3. Sustained clonus at the bilateral ankles (3 beats)
4. Positive finger escape sign
5. Mildly wide-based gait

An MRI of the cervical spine dated September 1, 2024, demonstrated a C5-C6 disc herniation with "mild cord contact." The radiologist noted that cord contact is present but not quantifiable as compression on standard sequences. The MRI report explicitly states: "no measurable cord compression, no T2 signal change, no myelomalacia."

Under the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(b)), cervical myelopathy is defined as "spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms." The plan's initial reviewer denied the request on the basis that the MRI did not demonstrate measurable cord compression or myelomalacia, and therefore concluded that the UMN signs were not conclusive for myelopathy.

However, under generally accepted standards of medical practice, specifically the Joint Guidelines of the American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS) and the Congress of Neurological Surgeons (CNS) (2023 Update), a diagnosis of clinical cervical myelopathy is established by the presence of multiple positive UMN signs on physical examination. The clinical standard recognizes "imaging-negative myelopathy" as a distinct clinical entity where objective neurological signs precede gross structural changes on standard MRI sequences. The member's MRI demonstrating a C5-C6 disc herniation with mild cord contact provides sufficient structural correlation to localize the pathology to the C5-C6 level, satisfying the diagnostic threshold under generally accepted medical standards.

II. REGULATORY FRAMEWORK AND STANDARD OF REVIEW

This appeal is governed by the California Health and Safety Code (CHSC). Under CHSC § 1374.32(a), the standard of review for medical necessity determinations must be based on whether the service is in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice, is clinically appropriate, and is considered effective for the patient's illness. 

Crucially, CHSC § 1374.32(b) mandates that the medical necessity standard does NOT require that the service be consistent with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the requested service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, the reviewer must approve the service regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. Under CHSC § 1374.32(c), the plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) may be used as a reference but cannot be the sole or determinative basis for a decision when they conflict with broader clinical standards.

III. APPLICATION OF DMHC ALL-PLAN LETTER APL 22-014

In the initial appeal arguments, reference was made to the Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter APL 22-014. Upon precise legal and clinical review, we explicitly acknowledge that the specific conservative treatment waiver mandated under APL 22-014 Section II.1 and II.2 does NOT apply to this case. APL 22-014 limits its automatic conservative treatment exceptions to cases with "objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI" or "progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia...)". Because the member's MRI explicitly states "no measurable cord compression, no T2 signal change, no myelomalacia," the plan is not regulatory-mandated to waive conservative treatment under those specific sections.

However, APL 22-014 Section II.3 remains highly relevant. It states that plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for time-sensitive neurological conditions. Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs. Therefore, while a waiver is not mandated by APL 22-014 Section II.2, the clinical necessity of the procedure must still be evaluated under the broader clinical standards of the AANS/CNS and AO Spine International, as enforced by CHSC § 1374.32.

IV. RESOLUTION OF CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENTS

The plan's initial denial cited the member's failure to complete the standard 6-week physical therapy (PT) requirement, noting the member had completed only 5 weeks. Additionally, a strict review of the plan's conservative treatment criteria (Section 2.2) reveals deficits in pharmacotherapy (the member's Methylprednisolone trial was only 10 days, failing the 4-week minimum under Section 2.2(b)) and interventional treatment (the member had no interventional treatments, failing Section 2.2(c)). 

These conservative treatment requirements are resolved and overridden as follows:

1. Physical Therapy (5 Weeks Completed vs. 6 Weeks Required)
Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3), a health plan shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the treating physician documents that PT is contraindicated, or where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(c) prohibits the mechanical application of a minimum PT duration. 
In this case, the member completed 5 weeks of supervised PT (15 sessions). The treating physician halted PT, citing active myelopathy concerns. Under clinical guidelines from AO Spine International (2021), continuing physical therapy in a patient with active, progressive upper motor neuron signs (such as sustained ankle clonus and wide-based gait) is clinically contraindicated due to the risk of precipitating rapid, irreversible neurological deterioration. The physician's safety halt represents a valid clinical contraindication, and the mechanical application of the remaining 1 week of PT is legally and clinically impermissible.

2. Pharmacotherapy (10-Day Steroid Taper vs. 4-Week Requirement)
The plan's internal criteria (Section 2.2(b)) require a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The member trialed Baclofen (5mg TID) for 2 months with minimal relief, but the trial of Methylprednisolone was only 10 days. 
From a clinical standpoint, a 10-day oral steroid taper represents a completed, standard clinical course for that specific class of medication. Requiring a patient to take oral corticosteroids continuously for 4 weeks is clinically inappropriate and contrary to standard medical practice due to the high risk of systemic corticosteroid toxicity. Furthermore, because the member has progressive UMN signs, further or prolonged pharmacotherapy is clinically futile. Pharmacotherapy cannot decompress the spinal cord or reverse structural myelopathic signs. Therefore, the 10-day steroid taper is accepted as a completed trial, and further pharmacotherapy is clinically waived.

3. Interventional Treatment (None Completed)
The plan's criteria require at least one interventional treatment (e.g., cervical epidural steroid injection). The member had no interventional treatments. 
We document a retrospective clinical justification that performing cervical epidural steroid injections or medial branch blocks in a patient with active, progressive upper motor neuron signs (sustained clonus, hyperreflexia, finger escape sign) poses an unacceptable risk of direct spinal cord injury, hematoma, or rapid neurological deterioration. Under generally accepted clinical standards, interventional cervical procedures are contraindicated in the presence of active myelopathy. Thus, this requirement is clinically waived.

4. Procedural Defect under CHSC § 1374.31(b)
As an additional procedural support, we note that the plan's initial denial notice dated October 30, 2024, only cited the physical therapy duration (5 weeks vs. 6 weeks) and the lack of imaging-confirmed compression as the reasons for denial. The notice failed to raise any deficits regarding pharmacotherapy or interventional treatment. Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice must specify all clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the denial. A denial notice that fails to do so is legally defective. Because the plan failed to raise the pharmacotherapy and interventional deficits in the initial denial, they are procedurally barred from raising them now to uphold the denial. Regardless, as established above, these requirements are clinically contraindicated and overridden by generally accepted medical standards.

V. CONCLUSION

In summary, Frances Kim has established clinical cervical myelopathy localized to the C5-C6 level. Under CHSC § 1374.32, generally accepted medical standards govern this review. These standards (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021) dictate that surgical decompression (ACDF) is the primary and necessary treatment for clinical myelopathy, and that extended conservative treatment (including further physical therapy, prolonged steroid trials, and interventional injections) is clinically contraindicated and poses a risk of irreversible neurological harm. 

Accordingly, the prior authorization denial for ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED, and the procedure is approved.
