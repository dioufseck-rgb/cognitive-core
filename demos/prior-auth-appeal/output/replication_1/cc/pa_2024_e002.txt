Dear Ms. Mills and Dr. Torres,

The HealthFirst Appeal Review Department has completed its review of the prior authorization appeal for the requested three-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C3-C4, C4-C5, and C5-C6 (CPT 22551 x3) to be performed at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center. 

Based on a comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, we have issued a PARTIAL OVERTURN. Specifically, surgical intervention is authorized for the C4-C5 and C5-C6 levels. The denial for the C3-C4 level is upheld.

Below is the detailed clinical, regulatory, and criteria-based analysis for each requested level.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. PROCEDURAL COMPLIANCE AND DEFECT IN INITIAL DENIAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), a health plan's denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the denial. A denial notice that fails to do so is considered defective.

The initial denial notice issued on November 25, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, failed to cite the specific clinical coverage criteria applied (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024) and failed to provide a detailed clinical rationale for denying the C5-C6 level despite documented neurological deficits and completed conservative treatment. This procedural defect has been corrected in this appeal review, and a level-by-level substantive analysis has been performed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. LEVEL-BY-LEVEL SUBSTANTIVE ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A. C4-C5 LEVEL: OVERTURNED (APPROVED)
- Clinical Presentation: The clinical record dated October 5, 2024, documents severe cervical myelopathy. Objective findings include bilateral hyperreflexia, bilateral Hoffman sign, a wide-based gait, and bilateral deltoid weakness (4/5) at the C5 level.
- Imaging Findings: The cervical spine MRI dated October 1, 2024, demonstrates a large disc herniation at C4-C5 causing severe spinal cord compression and T2 signal change within the spinal cord (myelomalacia).
- Plan Criteria Alignment: This level fully satisfies HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1(b) (Cervical myelopathy with spinal cord compression and myelopathic symptoms). Under the Myelopathy Pathway Exception 2.2(A), the physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks, and the interventional treatment requirement is waived if there is a risk of neurological deterioration. The patient completed 9 weeks of physical therapy (27 sessions) and completed the pharmacotherapy requirement (Baclofen 10mg TID for 3 months and Pregabalin 300mg BID for 4 months), satisfying the plan's conservative treatment criteria.
- Regulatory Override: Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) and California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, health plans are prohibited from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. The presence of severe cord compression and myelomalacia (T2 signal change) on MRI represents an urgent, irreversible structural neurological compromise. Therefore, any mechanical application of conservative treatment requirements is legally overridden.
- Clinical Standard: Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update) and NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), the presence of moderate-to-severe myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia constitutes a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) for immediate surgical decompression. Delaying surgery in this setting poses a high risk of permanent, irreversible neurological deficit.
- Conclusion: The denial of the C4-C5 level is OVERTURNED.

B. C5-C6 LEVEL: OVERTURNED (APPROVED)
- Clinical Presentation: The clinical record documents left-sided C6 radiculopathy. Objective neurological findings on October 5, 2024, include left bicep weakness (4-/5) and a diminished left bicep reflex.
- Imaging Findings: The MRI dated October 1, 2024, demonstrates a moderate disc herniation at C5-C6 with moderate left C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis.
- Plan Criteria Alignment: This level satisfies CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1(a) (Cervical radiculopathy with nerve root compression and dermatomal weakness/reflex changes) and Section 2.1(c) (Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit). The patient has fully completed the conservative treatment requirements under Section 2.2:
  1. Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): Completed 9 weeks (27 sessions) of supervised physical therapy, exceeding the 6-week requirement. The physical therapy summary notes that C5-C6 radicular symptoms persisted and the patient reached a functional plateau.
  2. Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): Completed trials of two neuropathic/muscle relaxant agents for at least 4 weeks each (Baclofen for 3 months, Pregabalin for 4 months).
  3. Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): Completed a cervical epidural steroid injection on October 20, 2024, which provided only modest, temporary relief of radicular symptoms.
- Clinical Standard: Under the NASS Guidelines (2020) and AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), surgical decompression (ACDF) is a Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) for patients with cervical radiculopathy and objective neurological deficits (weakness and reflex changes) who have failed a comprehensive 6-to-12 week course of conservative management.
- Conclusion: The denial of the C5-C6 level is OVERTURNED.

C. C3-C4 LEVEL: UPHELD (DENIED)
- Clinical Presentation: The clinical record does not document any objective radicular or myelopathic findings attributable to the C3-C4 level. The neurological exam on October 5, 2024, notes "C4 level: unclear — neck pain with C3-C4 palpation but no specific radicular pattern." Furthermore, the physical therapy summary notes "C3-C4 contribution unclear."
- Imaging Findings: The MRI dated October 1, 2024, shows only a mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing at C3-C4.
- Plan Criteria Alignment: This level fails to meet CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1, as there is no confirmed diagnosis of radiculopathy, myelopathy, or disc herniation with objective neurological deficit correlating to this level. Under Section 3.1, surgical authorization is excluded when requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy. Under Section 3.3, authorization must be denied when imaging does not demonstrate pathology at the proposed surgical level consistent with the clinical presentation.
- Clinical Standard: Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), surgical intervention for mild imaging findings without correlating clinical symptoms or for isolated axial neck pain is classified as Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated). There is no clinical evidence supporting prophylactic fusion of a mild, asymptomatic adjacent level.
- Conclusion: The denial of the C3-C4 level is UPHELD.

4. NOTICE OF INDEPENDENT MEDICAL REVIEW (IMR) RIGHTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Because a portion of your requested service (the C3-C4 level) was upheld as denied, you have the right to request an Independent Medical Review (IMR) from the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.30.

An IMR is an objective, independent review of medical necessity decisions made by your health plan. If the IMR is decided in your favor, HealthFirst must authorize the service. The IMR application is free to you. You may submit an application for an IMR if your health plan has denied, modified, or delayed a health care service based on a decision that the service is not medically necessary.

To apply for an IMR:
1. Complete the IMR Application Form provided by the DMHC (available at www.dmhc.ca.gov or by calling 1-888-466-2219).
2. Attach copies of any letters from your health plan regarding this denial, as well as any additional clinical records or supportive statements from your treating physician.
3. Mail or fax the completed form to the DMHC as instructed on the application.

For urgent cases involving an imminent and serious threat to your health, you may request an expedited IMR directly from the DMHC without completing the internal appeal process first, as provided under CHSC § 1374.30(c).
