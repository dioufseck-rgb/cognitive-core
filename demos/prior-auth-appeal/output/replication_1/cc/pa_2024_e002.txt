2. PROCEDURAL DEFECT ANALYSIS (CALIFORNIA LAW)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a)(2), a health plan's denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reason for the denial. The initial denial notice issued on November 25, 2024, stated: "Three-level ACDF not approved. Review of imaging and clinical record supports surgical intervention at one level (C4-C5) only. C3-C4 and C5-C6 do not demonstrate pathology of sufficient severity to warrant surgical intervention at this time."

This notice is procedurally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b) because:
1. It failed to identify or cite the specific clinical coverage criteria applied (HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024).
2. It failed to provide a specific clinical reason for denying the C5-C6 level, which demonstrated moderate left C6 nerve root compression and corresponding neurological deficits on exam.

Pursuant to CHSC § 1374.31(b), a defective denial notice shall be treated as if no denial was issued for the purposes of appeal timelines, and the plan must correct these deficiencies on appeal. This determination letter provides the required detailed clinical and criteria-based analysis.

3. LEVEL-BY-LEVEL CLINICAL & REGULATORY ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 LEVEL C4-C5: OVERTURNED (APPROVED)
- Clinical Findings: MRI dated October 1, 2024, demonstrates a large disc herniation, severe spinal cord compression, and T2 signal change (myelomalacia) at C4-C5. Neurological exam dated October 5, 2024, reveals objective myelopathy findings: bilateral hyperreflexia, bilateral Hoffman sign, wide-based gait, and bilateral deltoid weakness (4/5) at the C5 level.
- Plan Criteria Analysis: The clinical presentation satisfies HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1(b) (Cervical Myelopathy), which requires spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms (gait disturbance, bilateral upper extremity weakness, hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign).
- Conservative Treatment & Regulatory Override: The patient completed 9 weeks of physical therapy (27 sessions), 3 months of Baclofen, 4 months of Pregabalin, and a cervical epidural steroid injection on October 20, 2024. This exceeds the plan's standard conservative treatment requirements under Section 2.2. Furthermore, under California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI is a time-sensitive neurological condition. Plans are prohibited from requiring a full course of conservative treatment when delay risks irreversible harm. Additionally, California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) prohibits conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address.
- Clinical Standard: Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Section 3.1, moderate-to-severe myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) for surgical decompression. Delaying surgery is contraindicated due to the risk of permanent neurological deficit (AO Spine Guidelines, 2021).
- Conclusion: The denial of the C4-C5 level is overturned.

3.2 LEVEL C5-C6: OVERTURNED (APPROVED)
- Clinical Findings: MRI dated October 1, 2024, demonstrates a moderate disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression, and foraminal stenosis at C5-C6. Neurological exam dated October 5, 2024, documents left bicep weakness (4-/5) and a diminished left bicep reflex, which directly correlates with a C6 radiculopathy.
- Plan Criteria Analysis: This level satisfies CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1(a) (Cervical radiculopathy with nerve root compression and radicular symptoms) and Section 2.1(c) (Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit).
- Conservative Treatment & Regulatory Override: The patient completed 9 weeks of physical therapy (27 sessions), satisfying Section 2.2(a). She completed a 4-month trial of Pregabalin and a 3-month trial of Baclofen, satisfying Section 2.2(b). She underwent a cervical epidural steroid injection on October 20, 2024, satisfying Section 2.2(c). The physical therapy summary notes that "C5-C6 radicular symptoms persist" and the patient has reached a "functional plateau." Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on physical therapy where the member has completed physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms.
- Clinical Standard: Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Section 3.1, radiculopathy with persistent symptoms and objective neurological deficits (weakness and reflex changes) after 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment constitutes a Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) for surgical decompression.
- Conclusion: The denial of the C5-C6 level is overturned.

3.3 LEVEL C3-C4: UPHELD (DENIED)
- Clinical Findings: MRI dated October 1, 2024, demonstrates a mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing at C3-C4. Neurological exam dated October 5, 2024, notes "C4 level: unclear — neck pain with C3-C4 palpation but no specific radicular pattern." The physical therapy summary dated October 5, 2024, states "C3-C4 contribution unclear."
- Plan Criteria Analysis: Under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 3.1, authorization is denied when surgery is requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy. Under Section 3.3, authorization is denied when imaging does not demonstrate pathology at the proposed surgical level consistent with the clinical presentation. The mild imaging findings and lack of correlating radicular or myelopathic symptoms at C3-C4 fail to meet the diagnostic requirements of Section 2.1.
- Clinical Standard: Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Section 3.1, axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy, and imaging findings without correlating symptoms, are classified as Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated). There is no clinical evidence supporting surgical fusion at a level with only mild, asymptomatic degenerative changes (NASS Guidelines, 2020).
- Conclusion: The denial of the C3-C4 level is upheld.

4. CONFLICT RESOLUTION & HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In evaluating this appeal, HealthFirst applies the mandatory California regulatory hierarchy of authority: California State Law (CIC and CHSC) > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Plan Coverage Criteria.

1. Myelopathy Conservative Treatment Conflict: HealthFirst Plan Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 Note restricts the myelopathy conservative treatment exception only to cases where myelopathy is the "primary" diagnosis. In this mixed myeloradiculopathy case, this restriction conflicts with DMHC APL 22-014 and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS Guidelines, 2023), which mandate immediate surgical consideration for any patient with objective myelomalacia and cord compression, regardless of whether radiculopathy is also present. Under the hierarchy, DMHC APL 22-014 and the clinical standard override the plan's restrictive "primary diagnosis" note, requiring approval of the C4-C5 level.
2. Physical Therapy Mechanical Application Conflict: The plan's initial reviewer mechanically denied the C5-C6 level. This mechanical denial conflicts with CIC § 10169.5(c), which prohibits the mechanical application of physical therapy duration requirements without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau. Because the clinical record documents a functional plateau at C5-C6, state law overrides any plan-level denial, requiring approval of the C5-C6 level.
