I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION & OBJECTIVE FINDINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Mr. Santos is a 63-year-old male presenting with a mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy. The clinical record contains the following objective findings:
1. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) dated September 1, 2024: Demonstrates moderate spinal cord compression at the C6-C7 level, accompanied by T2-weighted signal change within the spinal cord, diagnostic of myelomalacia. Right C7 foraminal stenosis is also noted.
2. Neurological Examination dated September 5, 2024: Reveals a mixed presentation. Radicular findings include right triceps weakness (4/5), right finger numbness, and severe right-sided pain (8/10) in a C7 distribution. Objective myelopathic (upper motor neuron) findings include bilateral hyperreflexia (right greater than left), a positive right Hoffman sign, an equivocal left Hoffman sign, and a mildly wide-based gait disturbance.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
II. EVALUATION OF THE INITIAL DENIAL & TEMPORAL TIMELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The initial denial issued on October 5, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, was based on HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2 (Exception 2.2(a)), which requires that cervical myelopathy be the "primary diagnosis" to modify standard conservative treatment requirements. Because the member's symptoms were predominantly right-sided (radicular), the reviewer classified the primary diagnosis as radiculopathy and applied standard conservative treatment requirements (Section 2.2), which require a completed 6-week course of physical therapy and a completed interventional trial.

This appeal review explicitly acknowledges that at the time of the initial denial on October 5, 2024, the standard conservative treatment requirements were not yet complete, and their failure could not have been documented:
1. Physical Therapy Timeline: The MRI showing cord signal change was performed on September 1, 2024, and the myelopathy-specific PT protocol was initiated shortly thereafter. As of the initial denial on October 5, 2024, only 34 days (4.8 weeks) had elapsed. It was mathematically and temporally impossible for the member to have completed the standard 6-week physical therapy course prior to the initial denial.
2. Interventional Treatment Timeline: The member underwent a right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 28, 2024. As of the denial on October 5, 2024 (7 days post-procedure), the clinical outcome of this trial was not yet established. The clinical record subsequently documented that the 50% right-sided relief fully returned in 3 weeks (by October 19, 2024). This outcome could not have been known or documented at the time of the initial review.

Therefore, the initial denial was procedurally and clinically consistent with the plan's standard internal criteria based on the clinical data available on October 5, 2024. However, on appeal, we must evaluate the case under newly available post-denial clinical data and California regulatory overrides.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
III. REGULATORY OVERRIDES GOVERNING THIS APPEAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Under California law, a health plan's internal clinical coverage criteria are subordinate to state statutory mandates. The plan's standard conservative treatment requirements are legally overridden in this case by the following provisions:

1. Primary Legal Override: California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3)
Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where "the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." 

Mr. Santos's MRI on September 1, 2024, provides objective evidence of moderate spinal cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord. This is accompanied by objective upper motor neuron signs (bilateral hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign, and gait disturbance) documented on September 5, 2024. Myelomalacia represents irreversible structural spinal cord injury. Physical therapy cannot decompress the spinal cord or resolve this structural compromise. Therefore, under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), HealthFirst cannot legally condition the approval of this ACDF on the completion of a physical therapy trial.

2. Supporting Regulatory Guidance: DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014
APL 22-014, Section II.1, establishes that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated," and mandates that plans treat such cases with clinical urgency. 

While the specific conservative treatment waiver under APL 22-014 Section II.2 requires progressive pathology on serial imaging (which is not present here, as only a single MRI from September 1, 2024, is documented), Section II.3 explicitly prohibits plans from applying internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. The plan's internal restriction requiring myelopathy to be the "primary diagnosis" to qualify for a modified conservative treatment pathway is more restrictive than generally accepted clinical standards and is therefore invalid under Section II.3.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IV. GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Generally accepted clinical guidelines do not support delaying surgical decompression to complete conservative treatment in patients with objective myelomalacia and upper motor neuron signs:

1. AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update): Classifies moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI as a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) for surgical decompression. Extended conservative treatment trials are not recommended.
2. AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021): States, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
3. NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020): Recommends surgical decompression as the primary treatment for patients with moderate-to-severe myelopathy or those with objective cord signal changes, as preoperative myelomalacia is the strongest predictor of incomplete neurological recovery if surgery is delayed.

Applying a "primary diagnosis" distinction to deny surgical decompression when both myelopathic and radicular findings are objectively present (mixed presentation) is a clinical category error. The presence of radiculopathy does not lessen the neurological urgency of the co-existing myelomalacia.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
V. STATUS OF CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT AT THE TIME OF APPEAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Even if conservative treatment requirements were applied, the clinical record submitted for this appeal on October 20, 2024, demonstrates that the member has now fully completed and failed the conservative regimen:
1. Physical Therapy: As of October 20, 2024, 49 days (7 weeks) have elapsed since the September 1, 2024 MRI. The member has completed 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy (18 sessions) using a myelopathy protocol. The treating therapist documented a functional plateau, with no improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms.
2. Interventional Treatment: As of October 19, 2024 (3 weeks post-injection), the right C7 epidural steroid injection performed on September 28, 2024, has officially failed, with the clinical record documenting that the temporary 50% right-sided relief has fully returned to baseline (8/10 pain) and produced no change in bilateral hyperreflexia.
3. Pharmacotherapy: The member completed a 3-month trial of Pregabalin (150mg BID) with only partial relief, and a 2-month trial of Naproxen (500mg BID) which was inadequate, satisfying the 4-week trial of two agents required under Section 2.2(b).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VI. CONCLUSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
While the initial denial on October 5, 2024, was temporally consistent with the incomplete conservative treatment data available at that time, the clinical evidence available at the time of this appeal (October 20, 2024) establishes that the conservative treatment trials have been completed and have failed. 

Furthermore, under California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(3), the plan is legally prohibited from conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy due to the objective presence of structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia with cord compression and upper motor neuron signs). Generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021) dictate that surgical decompression is the medically necessary and urgent standard of care for this presentation.

Accordingly, the initial denial is OVERTURNED and the request for ACDF C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is APPROVED.
