I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The prior authorization denial for a one-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) requested by Dr. Lisa Wong, MD, for member David Santos must be OVERTURNED. The initial denial by HealthFirst Health Plan on October 5, 2024, was based on the rationale that the member's primary diagnosis was radiculopathy rather than myelopathy, thereby failing to meet the plan's internal "myelopathy exception" criteria (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2).

A comprehensive review of the clinical record, plan criteria, California statutory law, and generally accepted clinical standards reveals that:
1. The member fully satisfied the plan's standard conservative treatment requirements under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, making the denial a misapplication of the plan's own standard criteria.
2. Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3), conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy is legally prohibited when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise, which is documented here by MRI-confirmed myelomalacia and cord compression.
3. Under California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter APL 22-014, cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm, and plans are prohibited from applying restrictive internal criteria that delay surgical intervention.
4. Generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) classify the member's presentation as a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) surgical candidate, where conservative therapy is not recommended and delays risk permanent neurological deficit.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
II. CLINICAL HISTORY AND FINDINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Mr. David Santos is a 63-year-old male presenting with a mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy.
- Clinical Presentation & Neurological Exam (Date: September 5, 2024): The member exhibits a mixed presentation. Radicular findings include predominant right-sided pain (rated 8/10), right tricep weakness (4/5), and right finger numbness in a C7 distribution. Myelopathic findings include bilateral hyperreflexia (right greater than left), a positive right Hoffman sign, an equivocal left Hoffman sign, and a mildly wide-based gait.
- Objective Imaging (MRI Date: September 1, 2024): MRI of the cervical spine demonstrates moderate cord compression at C6-C7, T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord, and prominent right C7 foraminal stenosis.
- Conservative Treatment History:
  - Physical Therapy: Completed 6 weeks (18 sessions) of a supervised physical therapy myelopathy protocol. The treating provider documented no improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms.
  - Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 3-month trial of Pregabalin (150mg BID) with only partial right-sided pain relief, and a 2-month trial of Naproxen (500mg BID) with inadequate response.
  - Interventional Treatment: Underwent a right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 28, 2024, which provided 50% right-sided relief that fully returned within 3 weeks, and resulted in no change to bilateral hyperreflexia.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
III. ANALYSIS OF PLAN CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The plan's denial letter states that the "myelopathy exception" was applied but criteria were not met because the member's symptoms are primarily right-sided (radicular) and the exception requires myelopathy as the primary diagnosis. This analysis is procedurally and clinically flawed under the plan's own Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):

1. Standard Criteria Met: Under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1, the member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (nerve root compression with radicular symptoms) and cervical myelopathy (spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms), supported by an MRI within 6 months demonstrating structural pathology. Under Section 2.2, the standard conservative treatment requirements consist of:
   - (a) Physical therapy: minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy (Member completed 6 weeks/18 sessions).
   - (b) Pharmacotherapy: trial of at least two specified agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each (Member completed 3 months of Pregabalin and 2 months of Naproxen).
   - (c) Interventional treatment: at least one cervical epidural steroid injection (Member completed a right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 28, 2024).
   Because the member fully completed the standard conservative treatment pathway, the plan's reliance on the "myelopathy exception" restriction (which modifies/waives conservative treatment for those who have not completed it) is entirely irrelevant. The member met the standard criteria, and the denial under Section 3.2 is a misapplication of the plan's own policy.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IV. ANALYSIS OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY OVERRIDES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Even if the member had not completed the standard conservative treatment, California law prohibits the plan from denying this procedure based on conservative treatment requirements:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3): This statute explicitly states that a health insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... [t]he condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The member's MRI dated September 1, 2024, objectively documents moderate cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord. This constitutes severe structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Under CIC § 10169.5(c), the plan's physical therapy requirements cannot be applied mechanically to deny coverage in the presence of such neurological compromise.
2. DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014: The California Department of Managed Health Care has determined that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI "constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated." Under Section II.2, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization" when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as cord signal change/myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression.
3. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b): During clinical review, the standard of review does not require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, the reviewer must recommend approval regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
V. ANALYSIS OF GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The plan's insistence on delaying surgery to enforce conservative treatment for a patient with objective myelomalacia is directly contrary to generally accepted standards of medical practice:

1. AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update): Under these guidelines, the presence of moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia (T2 signal change) on MRI is classified as a Tier 1 — Strong Indication for surgical decompression. Conservative treatment is not recommended as a primary treatment for moderate-to-severe myelopathy or when myelomalacia is present.
2. AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021): The guidelines state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
3. Clinical Significance of Myelomalacia: As documented in the Clinical Reference Summary, T2 signal change (myelomalacia) indicates existing spinal cord injury and a high risk of permanent, irreversible neurological deficit if compression is allowed to continue. Delaying surgical decompression to complete conservative trials is clinically contraindicated.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VI. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A direct conflict exists between the plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 Note, which restricts the myelopathy exception to cases where myelopathy is the "primary" diagnosis) and California state law (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014), as well as generally accepted clinical standards.

Applying the established hierarchy of authority (State Law > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Plan Criteria):
1. California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(3) and DMHC APL 22-014 supersede the plan's internal primary diagnosis restriction. The statutory prohibition against conditioning surgery on physical therapy applies based on the objective presence of structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia), regardless of whether the plan classifies radiculopathy or myelopathy as the "primary" diagnosis.
2. Generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021) govern over the plan's restrictive criteria under DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3. These standards dictate that surgical decompression is indicated immediately without conservative treatment delays when cord compression and myelomalacia are present.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VII. CONCLUSION AND ORDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The member, David Santos, has documented mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy with objective MRI evidence of moderate cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia) at C6-C7, accompanied by corresponding clinical deficits (weakness, hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign, and gait disturbance). The member has completed a full 6-week course of physical therapy, multiple pharmacotherapy trials, and an epidural injection, thereby fully satisfying the plan's standard criteria. Furthermore, California statutory and regulatory provisions prohibit the plan from conditioning surgical approval on conservative treatment in the presence of objective structural neurological compromise.

Therefore, the initial prior authorization denial is hereby OVERTURNED. HealthFirst Health Plan is ordered to authorize Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C6-C7, one level (CPT 22551), as requested by Dr. Lisa Wong, MD, to be performed at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center.
