DETERMINATION: UPHOLD DENIAL
After a comprehensive review of your clinical records, imaging reports, and the appeal arguments submitted by Dr. Walsh, HealthFirst has determined that the requested surgical procedure is not medically necessary under your plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory standards, or generally accepted clinical guidelines. Therefore, the initial denial issued on October 15, 2024, is UPHELD.

CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND RECORD SUMMARY
Your clinical records indicate a presenting diagnosis of right-sided cervical radiculopathy at the C5-C6 level. 
1. Imaging: A cervical spine MRI dated September 10, 2024, demonstrated a mild-to-moderate disc herniation at C5-C6 with right C6 nerve root contact, but without measurable nerve root compression. The radiologist noted no spinal cord contact, no spinal cord compression, no T2 signal change, and no myelomalacia. There was mild right foraminal narrowing.
2. Neurological Examination: A clinical examination performed on September 15, 2024, documented right shoulder and neck pain rated at 6/10, and mild tingling in the right C6 dermatomal distribution. However, your motor strength was fully intact (grip strength 5/5 bilaterally), reflexes were normal and symmetric (bicep reflex 2+ bilaterally), and there were no upper motor neuron (UMN) signs. Your functional assessment indicated that you are working full-time with no activity limitations.
3. Conservative Treatment:
   - Physical Therapy: Completed 4 weeks (12 sessions) of supervised physical therapy, which resulted in mild improvement in your pain. No neurological deficits were identified to track. Your physical therapy was halted by Dr. Walsh to seek surgical authorization.
   - Pharmacotherapy: Completed an 8-week trial of Naproxen 500mg twice daily (BID), which provided moderate pain relief.
   - Interventional Treatment: No cervical epidural steroid injections, medial branch blocks, or trigger point injections have been performed.

PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2, surgical authorization for CPT 22551 requires that all medical necessity criteria be satisfied. The requested procedure fails to meet these criteria in the following areas:
1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1): Section 2.1(a) requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy defined as nerve root compression with radicular symptoms (pain, numbness, or weakness in a dermatomal distribution). Your MRI dated September 10, 2024, shows nerve root "contact" but explicitly states there is "no measurable compression." Section 2.1(c) allows for cervical disc herniation with an objective neurological deficit; however, your neurological exam on September 15, 2024, documented no weakness, normal reflexes, and no objective neurological deficits.
2. Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2):
   - Section 2.2(a) requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. Your records document only 4 weeks of physical therapy.
   - Section 2.2(b) requires a trial of at least two specified pharmacotherapy agents (such as NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each. You have only trialed one agent (Naproxen).
   - Section 2.2(c) requires at least one interventional treatment (cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point injection series). You have had no interventional treatments.
3. Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2 Exception): The modified conservative treatment pathway for myelopathy does not apply because your primary diagnosis is radiculopathy, and you have no clinical or imaging evidence of cervical myelopathy (no cord compression, no myelomalacia, and no upper motor neuron signs).
Consequently, under Section 3.2 of CC-SPINE-2024, authorization must be denied because the conservative treatment requirements have not been met.

CALIFORNIA REGULATORY ANALYSIS
We evaluated your request against California regulatory mandates to determine if any statutory provisions override the plan's internal criteria:
1. Physical Therapy Prerequisite (California Insurance Code [CIC] § 10169.5): Under CIC § 10169.5(a), a plan cannot condition surgical approval on physical therapy if: (1) PT is documented as contraindicated or unlikely to benefit; (2) the member reached a functional plateau; or (3) there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise. 
   - Your records do not document that PT is contraindicated.
   - Your PT records do not show a functional plateau; rather, they note "mild improvement in pain" before therapy was prematurely halted.
   - There is no objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (your MRI shows no nerve root or spinal cord compression, and your neurological exam shows no motor weakness). Therefore, the statutory exceptions under CIC § 10169.5 do not apply, and the 6-week PT requirement remains legally enforceable.
2. Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions (DMHC All-Plan Letter [APL] 22-014): DMHC APL 22-014 mandates that plans waive conservative treatment for urgent neurological conditions, specifically citing the presence of myelomalacia or progressive cord compression on MRI. Because your MRI dated September 10, 2024, explicitly notes "no cord compression" and "no myelomalacia," and your clinical exam shows no progressive neurological deficits, your condition does not qualify as urgent under APL 22-014.

CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND GUIDELINE ANALYSIS
Under generally accepted standards of medical practice, specifically the Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine of the American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Guidelines (2023):
1. Tier 1 — Strong Indication (Surgery Recommended): Surgery is strongly recommended for moderate-to-severe myelopathy, progressive neurological deficits, or myelomalacia. Your clinical presentation does not meet any Tier 1 criteria.
2. Tier 2 — Reasonable Indication (Surgery Reasonable Option): Surgery is a reasonable option for cervical radiculopathy with persistent symptoms only after an adequate conservative trial of 6 to 12 weeks. Because you have only completed 4 weeks of physical therapy, trialed only one medication, and had no interventional treatments, your presentation does not yet meet Tier 2 criteria.
3. Tier 3 — Surgery Not Indicated: The AANS/CNS guidelines classify mild radiculopathy that has not yet undergone a complete trial of conservative treatment under Tier 3 (surgery not indicated). Delaying surgery to complete conservative management is the standard of care and does not pose a risk of neurological deterioration in the absence of cord compression or motor deficits.

RESOLUTION OF PHYSICIAN DISPUTE
In his appeal letter dated October 18, 2024, Dr. Walsh strongly disputed the initial denial, arguing that his 25 years of spine surgery experience, personal examination of you, and clinical judgment should take precedence over a remote reviewer. He asserted that you would suffer "permanent disability" if the surgery were not immediately authorized.

While HealthFirst respects Dr. Walsh's extensive experience and clinical dedication, we must resolve conflicting medical opinions by evaluating the objective clinical evidence against established medical standards:
- Objective Evidence vs. Subjective Urgency: Dr. Walsh's assertion of imminent "permanent disability" is directly contradicted by your objective clinical findings. Your MRI shows no spinal cord contact or compression, and your neurological exam demonstrates completely intact motor strength (5/5 grip strength), normal reflexes, and full functional capacity (working full-time with no activity limitations).
- Standard of Care: Established clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS, 2023) and California regulatory frameworks (CIC § 10169.5) require objective evidence of structural compromise or a completed conservative trial before invasive spinal surgery is indicated. Professional authority and emotional appeals cannot substitute for objective clinical data or override the requirement to complete safe, non-invasive conservative therapies.
Therefore, the objective clinical record supports the initial reviewer's determination that surgical intervention is premature and not medically necessary at this time.

HOW TO OBTAIN COVERAGE
To meet medical necessity criteria for surgical authorization in the future, the following conservative treatment steps should be completed under the direction of your treating providers:
1. Complete an additional 2 weeks of supervised physical therapy to satisfy the 6-week requirement (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a)).
2. Trial a second class of pharmacotherapy (such as a muscle relaxant or neuropathic agent) for at least 4 weeks (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b)).
3. Undergo at least one interventional treatment, such as a cervical epidural steroid injection (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(c)).

If your symptoms worsen or you develop new neurological deficits (such as weakness or reflex changes), your provider should document these findings and submit an updated clinical record for review.

NOTICE OF YOUR RIGHT TO AN INDEPENDENT MEDICAL REVIEW (IMR)
If you disagree with this appeal decision, you have the right to request an Independent Medical Review (IMR) from the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) or the California Department of Insurance, depending on your plan type. 
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.30, you may submit an application for an IMR if your health care service has been denied on the basis of medical necessity. The IMR is a review conducted by independent, board-certified physicians who are not affiliated with HealthFirst. 
- Standard of Review: Per CHSC § 1374.32, the independent reviewer will determine whether the requested service is medically necessary based on generally accepted standards of medical practice, and is not bound by the plan's internal coverage criteria.
- How to Apply: You will receive an IMR application form and addressed envelope along with this letter. There is no cost to you for an IMR. You must submit your application within 180 days of receiving this notice.
- Urgent Reviews: If your treating physician documents that your condition poses an imminent and serious threat to your health, you may request an expedited IMR, which will be decided within 3 days (CHSC § 1374.30(c)).
