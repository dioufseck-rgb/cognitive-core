Dear Ms. James and Dr. Adams,

The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the first-level clinical appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) at UCLA Medical Center. After a comprehensive evaluation of the complete clinical record, including newly submitted physical therapy documentation, the applicable HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria, California statutory mandates, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial denial issued on November 2, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED. The requested surgical procedure is APPROVED.

I. CLINICAL HISTORY AND PRESENTING DIAGNOSIS
Ms. Dorothy James is a 51-year-old female with a history of progressive, bilateral upper extremity symptoms. Her presenting clinical diagnosis is bilateral C6-C7 cervical radiculopathy. 
- Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) of the cervical spine dated September 1, 2024, demonstrates a large disc herniation at C6-C7, resulting in bilateral C7 nerve root compression, bilateral foraminal stenosis, and mild spinal cord contact without compression.
- A neurological examination performed on September 5, 2024, reveals objective neurological deficits consistent with bilateral C7 dermatomal and myotomal compromise, specifically: bilateral tricep weakness graded at 4/5, diminished bilateral tricep reflexes, and bilateral finger tingling.

II. EVALUATION OF HEALTHFIRST CLINICAL COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2, authorization for cervical spinal surgery (CPT 22551) is approved when all medical necessity criteria are satisfied.

1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1): Section 2.1(a) requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy, defined as nerve root compression with radicular symptoms (pain, numbness, weakness in a dermatomal distribution), supported by an MRI within 6 months demonstrating consistent structural pathology. Ms. James's MRI (September 1, 2024) and neurological exam (September 5, 2024) document a large C6-C7 disc herniation with bilateral C7 nerve root compression and corresponding bilateral tricep weakness (4/5) and reflex changes. This criterion is fully satisfied.

2. Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2): Section 2.2 requires a documented course of conservative treatment consisting of physical therapy, pharmacotherapy, and interventional treatment.
   - (a) Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): Requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy with documented functional outcomes directed toward the cervical condition. 
     - The initial denial (MED-003) was based on the plan's receipt of only 4 weeks of PT records from Valley Physical Therapy (June 1, 2024, to June 28, 2024; 12 sessions), which documented moderate improvement followed by a functional plateau.
     - With this appeal, records from Mountain Physical Therapy have been submitted, documenting an additional 4 weeks of supervised physical therapy (July 15, 2024, to August 9, 2024; 12 sessions). The discharge summary notes no improvement in neurological symptoms and a documented functional plateau.
     - Combined, Ms. James completed a total of 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy across two providers. Although there was a 17-day gap (June 28 to July 15, 2024) during the transition between providers, CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a) specifies a cumulative duration ("minimum 6 weeks") and does not impose a strict continuity or single-provider requirement. Therefore, the physical therapy requirement is fully satisfied.
   - (b) Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): Requires a trial of at least two specified classes of medications for a minimum of 4 weeks each. Ms. James completed:
     - Pregabalin (neuropathic agent) at 150mg BID for 3 months, yielding only partial relief.
     - Naproxen (NSAID) at 500mg BID for 2 months, which was inadequate.
     This satisfies Section 2.2(b) for two agents from different classes trialed for over 4 weeks.
   - (c) Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): Requires at least one cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point injection series. Ms. James underwent a bilateral C7 epidural steroid injection on September 20, 2024, which provided temporary relief (50% on the left, 30% on the right) before symptoms returned to baseline. This satisfies Section 2.2(c).

3. Imaging Requirement (Section 2.3): Requires current imaging (within 6 months) demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms and the proposed surgical level. Ms. James's MRI dated September 1, 2024 (within 3 months of the appeal) confirms a large C6-C7 disc herniation and bilateral C7 nerve root compression, directly correlating with her bilateral C7 radiculopathy and tricep weakness. This criterion is satisfied.

4. Physician Documentation (Section 2.4): Dr. Paul Adams, MD, has provided the necessary clinical documentation, including surgical rationale, description of functional limitations, and discussion of risks/alternatives. This criterion is satisfied.

III. REGULATORY COMPLIANCE AND STATE LAW ANALYSIS (CALIFORNIA)
Under California law, which supersedes any conflicting or more restrictive internal health plan policies, the initial denial was procedurally and substantively deficient:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as a Prerequisite:
   - CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) prohibits an insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy if the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician or physical therapist documents that the member reached a "functional plateau" without adequate resolution of symptoms.
   - CIC § 10169.5(c) explicitly mandates that a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy "shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau."
   - In this case, the records from Valley Physical Therapy (which were in the plan's possession at the time of the initial review) explicitly documented that Ms. James achieved a "moderate improvement then plateau" after 4 weeks of treatment. Mechanically denying the authorization because the duration was under 6 weeks, despite documented functional plateauing, directly violates CIC § 10169.5.

2. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31 — Plan Obligations on Denial:
   - Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons. The plan's initial denial cited a lack of records for the second 4-week block of PT (Mountain PT). However, the clinical record indicates these records were submitted with the initial request but not received or processed due to an administrative failure by the plan. Under California regulatory standards, administrative receipt failures by the plan cannot be used to penalize the member or delay medically necessary care.

IV. CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARD ANALYSIS
Pursuant to California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), the standard for medical necessity on review must align with generally accepted standards of medical practice, independent of a plan's internal criteria.
- The AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines for the Selection of Patients for Cervical Spine Surgery (2023) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020) establish that surgical decompression (ACDF) is a Tier 2 "reasonable and indicated" intervention for patients presenting with cervical radiculopathy, persistent symptoms after 6–12 weeks of conservative care, and objective neurological deficits (such as Ms. James's 4/5 tricep weakness and diminished reflexes).
- The clinical evidence base confirms that continuing conservative therapy, including physical therapy, after a patient has reached a documented functional plateau (defined clinically as a failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement over consecutive sessions, per Section 5 of the Clinical Reference Summary) is ineffective and introduces an unnecessary risk of prolonged nerve root compression and permanent neurological deficit. Ms. James's clinical course demonstrates a clear functional plateau across two separate physical therapy providers, satisfying the clinical standard for surgical intervention.

V. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS AND CONCLUSION
Applying the established hierarchy of clinical review (California State Law > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Plan Coverage Criteria):
1. California State Law (CIC § 10169.5) invalidates the mechanical application of a 6-week physical therapy requirement when a functional plateau has been documented. Ms. James reached a documented plateau at 4 weeks (Valley PT), rendering the initial denial legally non-compliant.
2. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023) support surgical intervention for Ms. James's objective neurological deficits and failed conservative therapy.
3. HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) are now fully satisfied in their entirety, as the newly submitted Mountain PT records confirm a total of 8 weeks of physical therapy, resolving the documentation gap that prompted the initial denial.

Accordingly, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED. The request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) to be performed by Dr. Paul Adams, MD, at UCLA Medical Center is APPROVED.
