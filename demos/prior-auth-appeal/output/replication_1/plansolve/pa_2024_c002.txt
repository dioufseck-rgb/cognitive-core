### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The medical necessity review of the prior authorization appeal for CPT 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) on behalf of member David Santos has been completed. The initial decision to deny coverage on October 5, 2024, is **OVERTURNED**. 

The initial denial argued that because the member's symptoms are predominantly unilateral, the primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy rather than myelopathy, thereby rendering the member ineligible for the plan’s myelopathy exception. This clinical reasoning is flawed and represents a fundamental diagnostic misclassification. 

Under the governing hierarchy of authority, **California State Regulatory Mandates** and **Generally Accepted Clinical Standards** supersede internal plan criteria. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) objectively documents moderate spinal cord compression and spinal cord signal change (**myelomalacia**), accompanied by objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs (bilateral hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign, and gait instability). 

Under **California Insurance Code § 10169.5** and **DMHC All-Plan Letter 22-014**, the presence of myelomalacia constitutes progressive structural compromise that cannot be addressed by physical therapy, legally waiving and overriding any standard conservative or physical therapy timelines. Furthermore, under **CHSC § 1374.32(b)** and national clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021), a delay in surgical decompression in the presence of myelomalacia is clinically contraindicated due to the risk of irreversible neurological injury. 

Additionally, a review of the clinical record demonstrates that the member has actually completed a comprehensive conservative trial that satisfies even the plan's standard conservative pathway. Immediate approval is mandated.

### EVALUATION STEPS & CLINICAL ANALYSIS

#### Step 1: Procedural Adequacy and Defect Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
The initial denial notice dated October 5, 2024, is procedurally adequate in form as it cited the clinical criteria applied (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2 Myelopathy Exception) and provided a specific clinical reason (symptoms are primarily unilateral/radicular, arguing myelopathy was not the primary diagnosis). However, as analyzed below, the denial is substantively invalid and fails to align with state regulatory and clinical standards.

#### Step 2: Clinical & Diagnostic Classification Review (Radiculopathy vs. Myelopathy)
The initial reviewer, Dr. Patricia Huang, classified the member's condition as "primarily radiculopathy," asserting that the plan's myelopathy exception requires myelopathy to be the sole primary diagnosis. This is an incorrect clinical distinction that ignores a classic mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy presentation.

According to the **AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023)**, cervical myelopathy is defined by objective spinal cord compression on MRI accompanied by upper motor neuron signs and functional deficits. The member's clinical record contains unambiguous objective findings of active myelopathy:
1. **Objective Imaging:** MRI dated September 1, 2024, reveals moderate cord compression at C6-C7 with **T2-weighted signal changes (myelomalacia)** within the spinal cord.
2. **Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) Signs:** Neurological examination dated September 5, 2024, documents bilateral hyperreflexia (right greater than left), a positive right Hoffman sign, an equivocal left Hoffman sign, and a mildly wide-based gait.
3. **Mixed Component:** The presence of concurrent right-sided C7 radicular symptoms (triceps weakness 4/5, finger numbness, and 8/10 dermatomal pain) is due to right C7 foraminal stenosis, which is more prominent than the left.

The co-existence of radicular pain does not negate the active, progressive spinal cord injury (myelomalacia) documented on the MRI and physical exam. Denying a myelopathy exception because the patient has concurrent severe radicular pain is a diagnostic category error. Active myelopathy is clinically present.

#### Step 3: Application of Plan-Level Criteria & Exceptions (CC-SPINE-2024)
Even if the plan's internal guidelines (CC-SPINE-2024) are applied strictly, the member has satisfied the standard conservative treatment requirements under Section 2.2:
*   **Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)):** The member completed **6 weeks of supervised physical therapy (18 sessions)** utilizing a myelopathy protocol. The physical therapy records note a complete lack of improvement in both bilateral neurological signs and right-sided radicular symptoms, establishing a documented **functional plateau** under Clinical Standards.
*   **Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)):** The member completed trials of two qualifying agents for well over the required 4 weeks:
    *   *Pregabalin 150mg BID* (neuropathic agent) for 3 months (offered only partial, temporary pain relief).
    *   *Naproxen 500mg BID* (NSAID) for 2 months (inadequate response).
*   **Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)):** The member underwent a **right C7 epidural steroid injection** on September 28, 2024. The procedure provided only 50% temporary relief, with pain fully returning within 3 weeks, and had no therapeutic effect on the bilateral hyperreflexia.

Thus, the member satisfied the standard 6-week PT, 2-pharmacotherapy, and 1-interventional trial criteria. The initial denial's claim that "criteria were not met" is factually incorrect based on the submitted clinical record.

#### Step 4: Evaluation of State Regulatory Overrides (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)
Under the governing hierarchy of authority, California State Law supersedes any more restrictive internal plan criteria. Two state regulatory overrides apply directly to this case:

1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3):** A health plan cannot condition the approval of a surgical procedure on the completion or duration of physical therapy when the condition "involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Moderate spinal cord compression with T2 signal change (myelomalacia) is the definition of structural neurological compromise. Physical therapy cannot decompress a stenotic spinal canal or reverse structural cord myelomalacia.
2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:** This mandate explicitly states that health plans *shall not* require standard conservative treatment where objective imaging shows progressive pathology (such as cord signal change/myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents conservative failure. The APL identifies myelomalacia as a specific finding that waives standard conservative timelines. 

Applying the plan’s conservative timelines to delay surgery for a patient with active myelomalacia violates both CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014.

#### Step 5: Evaluation of Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS & AO Spine)
Under **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b)**, the standard of review for medical necessity is determined by generally accepted clinical practices, not the plan's internal rules.
*   **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023):** Cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia) represents a **Tier 1 Strong Indication** for surgical decompression.
*   **AO Spine Clinical Guidelines (2021):** There is no clinical evidence supporting mandatory physical therapy trials prior to surgery for moderate-to-severe myelopathy. Delayed decompression in the presence of myelomalacia is highly correlated with permanent, irreversible spinal cord damage and poor long-term functional recovery.

Therefore, delaying the ACDF to satisfy a rigid interpretation of "primary diagnosis" under plan criteria violates national clinical standards of care.
