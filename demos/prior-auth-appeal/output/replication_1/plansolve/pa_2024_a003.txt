## I. FINAL DISPOSITION
**DECISION:** **OVERTURN THE DENIAL IN FULL**

The prior authorization denial issued on October 15, 2024, by HealthFirst Health Plan is **OVERTURNED**. 

The requested surgical procedure—**CPT 22551 (ACDF C4-C5, one level)** at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center—is determined to be **medically necessary** and is hereby **approved for immediate authorization**. 

The plan’s initial determination mechanically applied conservative therapy rules meant for cervical radiculopathy to a patient with severe, progressive cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia. Under California law, established clinical guidelines, and the plan's own internal myelopathy exceptions, requiring further conservative treatment is clinically contraindicated, legally impermissible, and represents a severe category error.

## II. CLINICAL & PROCEDURAL TIMELINE

* **2019:** Patient underwent permanent pacemaker implantation (Boston Scientific) due to cardiac conduction deficits.
* **March 1, 2021:** HealthFirst PPO Gold plan coverage became effective.
* **May – August 2024:** Patient experienced progressive neurological deficits, including hand clumsiness, gait instability, and bilateral lower extremity spasticity.
* **August 20, 2024:** Cervical spine MRI performed using a specialized pacemaker-compatible safety protocol. Imaging revealed severe central spinal cord compression at C4-C5 and confirmed T2-weighted signal changes consistent with **myelomalacia** (active cord injury).
* **August 25, 2024:** Neurosurgical clinical evaluation by Dr. Michael Torres, MD. Neurological exam demonstrated objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs: bilateral lower extremity spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor skills bilaterally, and a positive Lhermitte sign.

* **September 10, 2024:** Clinical consultation with Anesthesiology (Dr. Rivera). Fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injection was formally contraindicated due to the permanent pacemaker, cardiac risk profile, and potential positioning hazards in the presence of severe cord compression.
* **October 15, 2024:** HealthFirst issued an initial denial (Dr. Alan Foster, MD) citing failure to complete 6 weeks of physical therapy and failure to obtain a cervical epidural steroid injection under Plan Criteria Section 2.2.
* **October 18, 2024:** Neurosurgery division at Cedars-Sinai submitted this expedited first-level appeal.

## III. STEP-BY-STEP EVALUATION FINDINGS

### Step 1: Procedural Adequacy & Disclosure Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
* **Objective:** Verify if the initial denial notice meets state procedural disclosure standards.
* **Finding:** **Positive Finding.** The denial notice dated October 15, 2024, specified the applied clinical criteria document (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2), articulated the clinical rationale (5 weeks PT instead of 6; lack of epidural steroid injection), and included standard California IMR notification language. The notice is procedurally adequate. *Proceed to Step 2.*

### Step 2: Diagnostic Classification (Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy)
* **Objective:** Review medical records to classify the primary spinal pathology.
* **Finding:** **Positive Finding (Cervical Myelopathy).** 
  * Clinical findings on August 25, 2024, show profound upper motor neuron (UMN) signs: positive Lhermitte sign, hyperreflexia, spastic gait, and loss of fine motor coordination.
  * Imaging findings (MRI, August 20, 2024) demonstrate severe central spinal cord compression and **myelomalacia** (T2 signal hyperintensity) at C4-C5.
  * *Conclusion:* The member’s primary diagnosis is **Progressive Cervical Myelopathy with Myelomalacia** (ICD-10 M50.022). She does *not* present with simple radiculopathy. *Proceed to Step 3.*

### Step 3: State Regulatory Override & Clinical Standard Evaluation
* **Objective:** Determine if California statutory provisions and clinical guidelines override plan-specific conservative treatment rules.
* **Finding:** **Positive Finding (Regulatory and Clinical Override).**
  * **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3):** Insurers shall not condition surgical approval on PT completion where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. Myelomalacia and severe central cord compression constitute severe structural compromise. PT cannot decompress a spinal cord.
  * **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:** Plans shall not require conservative treatment prior to surgery if imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (specifically citing myelomalacia) and the physician documents conservative treatment failure. Dr. Torres documented that the patient's neurological status progressed and deteriorated despite 5 weeks of PT.
  * **AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023):** Cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and objective UMN signs constitutes a **Tier 1 (Strong) Surgical Indication**. Conservative trials in this population are contraindicated as delay increases the risk of irreversible, permanent neurological deficits (paraplegia, loss of hand function).
  * *Conclusion:* Both California law and peer-reviewed clinical standards strictly override standard conservative management requirements for this patient. *Proceed to Step 4.*

### Step 4: Plan Criteria Application — Myelopathy Pathway (Exception 2.2(A))
* **Objective:** Evaluate the request against the plan's modified criteria for cervical myelopathy.
* **Finding:** **Positive Finding.**
  * Under **HealthFirst Plan Criteria Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Exception)**, the standard conservative treatment pathway is modified:
    1. *Physical Therapy:* Minimum requirement is reduced from 6 weeks to **4 weeks**. Member completed **5 weeks** (15 sessions), satisfying this requirement.
    2. *Interventional Treatment:* Waived if the treating physician documents that interventional treatments (e.g., epidural injections) pose a risk of neurological deterioration or are clinically contraindicated. On September 10, 2024, Dr. Rivera (Anesthesiology) documented a absolute contraindication to fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injections due to the patient's permanent pacemaker. Dr. Torres further noted risk of spinal cord injury. Therefore, the injection requirement is **fully waived**.
    3. *Pharmacotherapy:* Member trialed Baclofen (3 months) and two separate 10-day courses of oral Methylprednisolone (corticosteroids), satisfying the two-agent trial expectation.
  * *Conclusion:* Even if the regulatory overrides in Step 3 did not exist, the member fully satisfies HealthFirst's internal **Exception 2.2(A)** criteria. The initial reviewer's application of the 6-week PT and injection rules was a misapplication of the plan's own policy. *Proceed to Step 6.*

### Step 6: Case Sufficiency and Record Integrity
* **Objective:** Ensure the administrative and clinical record contains sufficient objective evidence.
* **Finding:** **Positive Finding.** The file contains a complete neurosurgical consultation, a detailed MRI radiology report utilizing a pacemaker-compatible protocol, physical therapy documentation, and a formal anesthesiology consultation. The clinical record is highly robust and sufficient for a final determination.

## IV. HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY & LEGAL ANALYSIS

In accordance with the governing rules of prior authorization reviews, conflicts between guidelines are resolved using the following established hierarchy:

$$\text{California State Law (CIC, CHSC, DMHC APL)} > \text{Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine)} > \text{Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)}$$

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------+
|               CALIFORNIA STATE LAW (Highest)                |
|  - CIC § 10169.5(a)(3): Waives PT for structural injury     |
|  - DMHC APL 22-014: Prohibits conservative delay in         |
|    cases with myelomalacia                                  |
+------------------------------+------------------------------+
                               |
                               v
+-------------------------------------------------------------+
|                 CLINICAL STANDARDS (Tier 1)                 |
|  - AANS/CNS 2023: Moderate-to-severe myelopathy with        |
|    cord compression is a Tier 1 surgical indication         |
|  - AO Spine 2021: PT trial is medically contraindicated     |
+------------------------------+------------------------------+
                               |
                               v
+-------------------------------------------------------------+
|                 PLAN CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)               |
|  - HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(A) (Exception):    |
|    Reduces PT to 4 weeks & waives injection for high risk   |
+-------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1. Statutory Override (California Insurance Code § 10169.5)
The initial denial letter asserted that the member failed to complete a 6th week of physical therapy. However, under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)**, a health plan is legally prohibited from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy if there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise. The member’s MRI dated August 20, 2024, reveals severe central spinal cord compression and myelomalacia at C4-C5. Myelomalacia represents physical, structural softening and ischemia of the spinal cord tissue. Physical therapy cannot mechanically relieve bony or disc herniation-induced spinal cord compression. Conditioning surgical decompression on continued PT violates California statutory code.

### 2. Regulatory Compliance (DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014)
The California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) issued **APL 22-014** specifically to address time-sensitive neurological conditions. The directive states that health plans *shall not* mandate standard conservative treatment protocols when objective imaging shows progressive pathology (noting myelomalacia explicitly) and the physician documents that conservative management has failed to halt neurological decline. 
* Dr. Torres documented that the patient’s spasticity, gait instability, and loss of hand dexterity were actively progressing.
* Compelling the patient to undergo further conservative treatment in the face of active myelomalacia violates DMHC directive APL 22-014.

### 3. Clinical Standard Superiority (CHSC § 1374.32(b))
Under **California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32(b)**, medical necessity determinations must align with generally accepted standards of medical practice, overriding restrictive internal plan criteria. 
* The **AANS/CNS 2023 Joint Guidelines** and the **AO Spine 2021 Clinical Practice Guidelines** state that for patients with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective spinal cord compression, surgical decompression is the recommended **first-line treatment**.
* The literature demonstrates that each month of delay in decompression for patients with myelomalacia is linked to permanent, irreversible spinal cord damage and diminished post-operative functional recovery. There is no evidence supporting a mandatory physical therapy trial for moderate-to-severe myelopathy. 

### 4. Correct Application of Internal Plan Criteria (Exception 2.2(A))
The initial denial by Dr. Alan Foster incorrectly evaluated this patient under the standard radiculopathy criteria (6 weeks PT + injection). Had the reviewer evaluated the request under **Plan Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Exception)**, the request would have qualified for immediate approval:
* **PT Requirement Met:** Section 2.2(A) reduces the PT requirement to **4 weeks**. The member completed **5 weeks**.
* **Injection Waived:** The member’s permanent pacemaker (implanted 2019) posed an electrical/cardiac risk during fluoroscopy-guided interventional procedures, documented by Dr. Rivera, MD on September 10, 2024. Therefore, the injection requirement was contractually eligible for waiver under Section 2.2(A).
