### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY OF DETERMINATION

This appeal concerns the medical necessity of a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) for a 56-year-old female with a confirmed primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy. HealthFirst denied the initial authorization on October 25, 2024, citing a failure to complete the plan's standard 6-week conservative physical therapy (PT) protocol under Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. The member completed 5 weeks (15 sessions) of physical therapy, which was halted by her physician due to a lack of clinical response and the inherent risks of delaying surgery for a structural spinal cord compression.

This independent review evaluated the case using the **California Hierarchy of Authority** (California State Law > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Plan Clinical Criteria). The evaluation reveals that:
1.  **Plan Criteria Error:** The health plan misapplied its own criteria. Under Clinical Policy CC-SPINE-2024 **Section 2.2A (Exception: Myelopathy Pathway)**, members with a primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy are subject to a reduced physical therapy requirement of **4 weeks** and a waiver of interventional procedures. The member’s completed 5 weeks of physical therapy exceeds this requirement.
2.  **Statutory Override:** California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 prohibits health plans from conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy where there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address, or where a clinical plateau has been reached. The member has objective spinal cord compression and bilateral Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) signs that physical therapy cannot correct.
3.  **Clinical Standards Override:** According to the American Association of Neurological Surgeons / Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS 2023) and AO Spine (2021) guidelines, moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective spinal cord compression constitutes a **Tier 1 (strong) surgical indication**. There is no clinical evidence supporting mandatory preoperative physical therapy for this presentation, as delay risks irreversible spinal cord injury. Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), generally accepted clinical standards override restrictive plan criteria.

Accordingly, the prior authorization denial is overturned, and the surgical procedure is approved.

### DETAILED EVALUATION & ANALYSIS

#### Step 1: Procedural Adequacy and Disclosure Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
*   **Statutory Requirement:** California Health & Safety Code § 1374.31(b) mandates that a health plan's written denial notice must explicitly cite the specific clinical coverage policies, sections, and exact clinical reasons used to make an adverse determination. 
*   **Evaluation:** The health plan’s initial denial notice dated October 25, 2024, cited CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 and detailed the exact clinical reason (completion of 5 weeks of physical therapy instead of the 6 weeks required under the standard pathway).
*   **Finding:** The denial notice is procedurally adequate under state law. Because the notice was not procedurally defective, the review proceeds to a substantive clinical evaluation under Step 2.

#### Step 2: Diagnostic Classification and Plan Criteria Exception Review (CC-SPINE-2024 § 2.2A)
*   **Diagnostic Facts:** 
    *   **Clinical Presentation:** A neurological examination on September 5, 2024, documented bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia in the upper extremities, mild tandem gait impairment, and bilateral hand clumsiness. 
    *   **Imaging:** A cervical spine MRI on September 1, 2024, demonstrated moderate central spinal cord compression at the C5-C6 level.
    *   **Neurological Consultation:** On September 12, 2024, a consulting neurologist formally documented: *"Clinical picture consistent with cervical myelopathy. Bilateral UMN [upper motor neuron] signs are pathognomonic. Diagnosis: cervical myelopathy, primary" (ICD-10: G35.89).*
    *   **Conclusion:** The diagnostic criteria for cervical myelopathy under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1(b) are fully satisfied. The condition is documented as the **primary diagnosis**.
*   **Plan Criteria Application:**
    *   The health plan denied the request under the *Standard Pathway* (Section 2.2), which requires a minimum of 6 weeks of physical therapy.
    *   However, CC-SPINE-2024 contains **Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway)**, which explicitly states: *"For members with confirmed cervical myelopathy (criterion 2.1(b)), the conservative treatment requirement is MODIFIED... Physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks minimum."*
    *   The member completed **5 weeks** (15 sessions) of physical therapy under a specialized myelopathy protocol before her treating neurologist halted sessions due to a lack of improvement. Five weeks exceeds the plan's 4-week modified requirement.
    *   Additionally, Section 2.2(A) waives the interventional treatment requirement (such as epidural injections) if the physician documents that the procedure poses a risk of neurological deterioration. Dr. James Chen, MD, provided this written waiver, noting: *"Interventional procedure not indicated — cord compression with myelopathy requires surgical evaluation, not injection."*
    *   The member also completed the required pharmacotherapy, trialing Baclofen (10mg TID) for 3 months (muscle relaxant) and a Methylprednisolone dose pack for 3 weeks (oral corticosteroid), satisfying Section 2.2(b).
*   **Finding:** The health plan made a clear factual error by applying the Standard Pathway instead of the Myelopathy Pathway (Exception 2.2A). Because cervical myelopathy was established as the primary diagnosis, the member was subject to a 4-week PT minimum. Having completed 5 weeks, the member fully satisfied the plan's medical necessity criteria. This constitutes a positive clinical finding under Step 2.

#### Step 3: Statutory Physical Therapy Override Evaluation (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)
Even if the health plan's internal criteria required a 6-week physical therapy trial without exception, California statutory law overrides mechanical prior authorization requirements.

*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 Analysis:**
    *   Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)**, a plan cannot condition surgical approval on physical therapy if the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a **functional plateau**. The clinical record notes that the member completed 15 sessions over 5 weeks with *"no improvement in UMN signs,"* leading the neurologist to halt therapy. This represents a clear clinical plateau.
    *   Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)**, physical therapy requirements must be waived if the condition involves *"objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."* Spinal cord compression caused by a herniated disc or bony stenosis is a mechanical, structural lesion. Physical therapy cannot physically decompress the spinal cord. The member's MRI and physical exam provide objective, diagnostic evidence of structural compromise.
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 Analysis:**
    *   APL 22-014 dictates that plans **shall not** require a full course of conservative treatment where delaying surgery risks irreversible neurological harm. Although myelomalacia (T2 cord signal change) was not yet present on this member's MRI, her clinical presentation includes progressive, functional upper motor neuron deficits (bilateral hand clumsiness, gait impairment, and hyperreflexia). 
    *   Dr. Chen documented that conservative treatment had failed and that delaying surgical decompression posed an unacceptable risk of neurological deterioration.
*   **Finding:** Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) and (3), the health plan is legally prohibited from enforcing its physical therapy duration requirements. The clinical evidence of structural spinal cord compression and the documented functional plateau legally invalidate the plan's PT requirement.

#### Step 4: Generally Accepted Clinical Standards and Medical Necessity Assessment (CHSC § 1374.32)
*   **Clinical Standards of Care:**
    *   **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023):** Moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective spinal cord compression and functional upper motor neuron deficits is classified as a **Tier 1 (strong) surgical indication**. Immediate or early surgical decompression is recommended to prevent permanent neurological deficits.
    *   **AO Spine Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):** These guidelines explicitly state: *"There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."*
*   **Application of California Law:**
    *   **California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32(b)** establishes that the standard for medical necessity during an appeal is whether the service meets generally accepted clinical standards, **not** whether it adheres to the plan's internal criteria.
    *   The standard clinical practice for symptomatic cervical myelopathy is surgical decompression (ACDF). Enforcing a mandatory, arbitrary 6-week physical therapy trial is not supported by evidence-based medicine and deviates from the standard of care.
*   **Finding:** Under generally accepted standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS and AO Spine guidelines), the requested C5-C6 ACDF is medically necessary and clinically urgent. Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), these clinical standards override the plan's restrictive 6-week physical therapy requirement.

### HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY RESOLUTION

In resolving this appeal, the **California Hierarchy of Authority** was applied:
$$\text{California State Law (CIC, CHSC)} > \text{Generally Accepted Clinical Standards} > \text{Plan Clinical Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)}$$

1.  **State Law (CIC § 10169.5):** Outlaws the mechanical application of a physical therapy prerequisite in the presence of structural neurological compromise. This statutorily invalidates the health plan's PT requirement.
2.  **Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine):** Define moderate cervical myelopathy with spinal cord compression as a primary indication for surgical decompression, stating that delaying surgery for physical therapy is medically inappropriate. Under CHSC § 1374.32, these clinical standards override the plan's guidelines.
3.  **Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):** Even if the plan's criteria could be legally enforced, the plan's clinical reviewer failed to apply the myelopathy exception (Section 2.2A). The member met the correct internal criteria.

The health plan's initial denial is medically and legally indefensible across all three tiers of authority.
