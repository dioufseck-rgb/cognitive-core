The initial denial of prior authorization for the requested Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is **OVERTURNED**. ### II. CLINICAL CASE SUMMARY

The member is a 51-year-old female with a clinical presentation of persistent bilateral cervical radiculopathy at the C6-C7 level. 

*   **Symptomatology & Clinical Exam (September 5, 2024):** Neurological examination by Dr. Paul Adams, MD, reveals objective bilateral C7 dermatomal symptoms. Key findings include bilateral tricep muscle weakness graded at 4/5, bilaterally diminished tricep reflexes, and bilateral finger tingling.
*   **Diagnostic Imaging (September 1, 2024):** A cervical spine MRI demonstrates a large C6-C7 disc herniation, resulting in bilateral C7 nerve root compression and bilateral foraminal stenosis. Mild spinal cord contact is noted without spinal cord compression or myelomalacia.
*   **Conservative Management History:**
    1.  **Physical Therapy (Valley Physical Therapy):** Completed June 1, 2024, to June 28, 2024 (4 weeks, 12 sessions). The patient experienced moderate initial improvement but subsequently reached a clinical plateau.
    2.  **Physical Therapy (Mountain Physical Therapy):** Completed July 15, 2024, to August 9, 2024 (4 weeks, 12 sessions). Outcome documentation notes no improvement in neurological symptoms, with a definitive functional plateau documented by the physical therapist at discharge.
    3.  **Pharmacotherapy:** Completed a 3-month trial of Pregabalin 150mg BID (partial relief) and a concurrent 2-month trial of Naproxen 500mg BID (inadequate relief).
    4.  **Interventional Treatment:** Underwent bilateral C7 epidural steroid injections on September 20, 2024, which yielded temporary relief (50% on the left, 30% on the right) before symptoms returned to baseline.

### III. STEP-BY-STEP EVALUATION PLAN EXECUTION

#### Step 1: Procedural Adequacy and Defect Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
The initial denial notice dated November 2, 2024, cited reason code **MED-003** (Physical therapy requirement not met). The notice explicitly outlined the clinical basis of the denial, noting that the plan had received only 4 weeks of physical therapy records (from Valley Physical Therapy) and was missing the documented clinical records from the secondary provider (Mountain Physical Therapy) to verify the claimed 8-week duration. Because the notice provided a clear clinical reason and identified the specific missing records necessary to satisfy the criteria, the denial is deemed **procedurally adequate**. We proceed to Step 2.

#### Step 2: Clinical Classification and Diagnosis Verification
A critical clinical distinction must be made between cervical myelopathy and cervical radiculopathy, as they follow distinct clinical pathways.
*   **Myelopathy Indicators:** Spinal cord compression on MRI, upper motor neuron signs (e.g., positive Hoffman sign, hyperreflexia, clonus), bilateral symptoms (hand clumsiness, gait disturbance), and T2 cord signal changes (myelomalacia).
*   **Radiculopathy Indicators:** Nerve root compression with unilateral/bilateral dermatomal symptoms (pain, sensory changes, or lower motor neuron weakness) corresponding to a specific nerve root distribution.
*   **Findings:** The member’s MRI on September 1, 2024, shows "mild cord contact *without compression*" and no evidence of myelomalacia. The neurological exam on September 5, 2024, shows lower motor neuron signs (tricep weakness 4/5, diminished reflexes) consistent with a bilateral C7 radiculopathy, with no upper motor neuron signs or gait deficits.
*   **Classification:** **Negative Finding (Cervical Radiculopathy Only)**. Under the Evaluation Plan, we bypass Step 3 and Step 4 and proceed directly to **Step 5 (Standard Plan Criteria Pathway)**.

#### Step 5: Plan Criteria and Myelopathy Exception Verification (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)
Because the primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, the standard conservative treatment requirements under **Section 2.2** of the HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 guidelines apply.

1.  **Diagnosis and Imaging (Section 2.1 & 2.3):** The diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy is objectively confirmed by the September 1, 2024, MRI showing bilateral C7 nerve root compression and correlating clinical findings (C7 dermatomal symptoms/weakness).
2.  **Physical Therapy Requirement (Section 2.2(a)):** The plan criteria require a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy with documented functional outcomes.
    *   *Initial Review Status:* The plan had only received 4 weeks of records from Valley Physical Therapy.
    *   *Appeal Review Status:* The appellant has enclosed clinical records from Mountain Physical Therapy, documenting an additional 4 weeks (12 sessions) from July 15 to August 9, 2024.
    *   *Analysis:* Combined, the member has completed **8 total weeks of physical therapy (24 sessions)**, which exceeds the plan's 6-week minimum. Furthermore, both providers documented a clear **functional plateau** (less than 10% clinical improvement and failure to arrest neurological symptoms).
3.  **Pharmacotherapy Requirement (Section 2.2(b)):** The plan criteria require a trial of at least two covered classes of agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
    *   The member completed 3 months of Pregabalin 150mg BID (neuropathic agent) and 2 months of Naproxen 500mg BID (NSAID). This fully satisfies the two-agent pharmacotherapy trial requirement.
4.  **Interventional Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2(c)):** The plan criteria require a minimum of one cervical epidural steroid injection (or equivalent).
    *   The member underwent bilateral C7 epidural steroid injections on September 20, 2024, satisfying this requirement.

**Finding:** **Positive Finding (Standard Plan Criteria Satisfied)**. Upon review of the supplemental clinical records submitted with this appeal, the member meets all medical necessity criteria outlined in HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.

### IV. REGULATORY AND STATUTORY COMPLIANCE

This determination is reinforced by the governing hierarchy of California state law, which supersedes restrictive administrative policies applied by health plans:

1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5:** Under California law, a health plan cannot mechanically apply a physical therapy duration requirement to deny a surgical procedure if the treating physician and physical therapist document that the member has already completed a course of physical therapy and reached a **functional plateau** without adequate resolution of symptoms. 
    *   The records from Mountain Physical Therapy explicitly state that the member reached a clinical and functional plateau at discharge on August 9, 2024, with no improvement in objective neurological deficits (bilateral tricep weakness and finger tingling). Forcing an additional trial of physical therapy violates CIC § 10169.5(a)(2).
2.  **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 (Clinical Standards):** Under California IMR guidelines, medical necessity must be evaluated in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice. The clinical guidelines of the North American Spine Society (NASS, 2020) and the American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS, 2023) state that surgical decompression is indicated for cervical radiculopathy when objective neurological deficits (e.g., motor weakness 4/5) persist despite 6 to 12 weeks of conservative care. Delaying surgical decompression in the presence of motor weakness and an established functional plateau is clinically contraindicated and risks permanent neurological impairment.

---

### V. CONCLUSION

The plan's initial denial was based on a lack of clinical documentation to verify the 8 weeks of physical therapy claimed by the member. With the submission of the Mountain Physical Therapy records on appeal, the missing clinical documentation has been supplied, confirming 8 total weeks of physical therapy with an established functional plateau. All standard conservative treatment requirements under HealthFirst policy CC-SPINE-2024, including physical therapy, pharmacotherapy, and interventional blocks, have been fully satisfied. 

Accordingly, under California law and clinical evidence standards, the surgical procedure is medically necessary.

The initial denial is **OVERTURNED**. The plan is directed to authorize CPT 22551 for the requested ACDF C6-C7 at UCLA Medical Center.
