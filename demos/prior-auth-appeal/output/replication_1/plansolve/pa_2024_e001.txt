## 1. FORMAL DETERMINATION

Pursuant to California regulatory requirements and generally accepted clinical standards, a comprehensive review of the clinical record, imaging, and plan criteria has been conducted. 

The determination is a **PARTIAL APPROVAL**:
*   **APPROVED:** Single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the **C5–C6** level (**CPT Code 22551**).
*   **DENIED:** Additional adjacent-level fusion at the **C6–C7** level (**CPT Code 22552**).

---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The medical necessity review supports surgical decompression and fusion at the C5–C6 level. The member presents with severe, clinically correlating right C6 radiculopathy and concordant, high-grade structural pathology on MRI. The member has fully exhausted the plan’s conservative management requirements for this level, including physical therapy, multi-agent pharmacotherapy, and an interventional epidural steroid injection, with return of debilitating symptoms. 

However, surgical intervention at the adjacent C6–C7 level is not medically necessary. Objective clinical imaging demonstrates only mild degenerative changes at C6–C7 with no nerve root compression or spinal cord contact. Correspondingly, there are no definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms or objective neurological deficits correlating to this level. Treating an adjacent, asymptomatic, radiographically mild level "prophylactically" is not supported by clinical evidence or plan criteria. 

Therefore, the initial denial is modified to a **Partial Approval**, authorizing the C5–C6 surgical intervention (CPT 22551) and upholding the denial of the C6–C7 surgical intervention (CPT 22552).

---

## 3. STEP-BY-STEP EVALUATION ANALYSIS

### Step 1: Procedural Adequacy & Defect Check
Under **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b)**, a denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and detail the precise clinical reasons for the determination to be procedurally sound. 
*   **Evaluation:** The initial denial notice issued on November 1, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang explicitly cited the relevant clinical coverage criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024) and detailed the clinical findings (that the C5–C6 level met criteria but C6–C7 showed mild changes only, and that two-level fusions are not approved when one level fails to meet criteria). 
*   **Finding:** The initial denial notice is **procedurally adequate and sound**. No procedural defects exist under CHSC § 1374.31(b) to warrant an administrative overturn.

### Step 2: Diagnostic Classification (Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy)
Determining the correct diagnostic pathway is critical, as treatment algorithms and conservative trial requirements differ fundamentally between radiculopathy and myelopathy.
*   **Evaluation:** The clinical record demonstrates:
    *   *Neurological Exam (Sept 20, 2024):* Right bicep weakness (3+/5), absent right bicep reflex, and right C6 dermatomal numbness and tingling.
    *   *Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) Signs:* There is no documentation of hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign, clonus, bilateral upper extremity symptoms, or gait disturbances.
    *   *Imaging:* MRI shows nerve root compression at C5–C6 but specifically notes "no cord contact" or central canal stenosis at either level.
*   **Finding:** The member's primary pathology is classified as **Cervical Radiculopathy** (nerve root compression with dermatomal symptoms and matching lower motor neuron deficits), *not* cervical myelopathy.

### Step 3: Multi-Level Imaging & Pathology Assessment
The radiologist's interpretation of the cervical spine MRI dated September 15, 2024 (within the 6-month regulatory window) was analyzed for both requested levels:
*   **C5–C6 Level:** MRI shows a large disc herniation, moderate-to-severe right C6 nerve root compression, and foraminal stenosis. This represents high-grade, surgical-level structural pathology.
*   **C6–C7 Level:** MRI shows a small disc bulge, mild foraminal narrowing, no identifiable nerve root compression, and no cord contact. 
*   **Finding:** Objective imaging confirms **single-level surgical-grade pathology at C5–C6** and demonstrates only **mild, clinically insignificant degenerative changes at C6–C7**.

### Step 4: Plan Criteria Compliance & Myelopathy Exception Review
The member’s treatment history was evaluated against **HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2**:
*   **Conservative Trial Completed (for C5–C6):**
    *   *Physical Therapy (2.2a):* Completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised PT with persistent C6 symptoms. This exceeds the 6-week plan requirement.
    *   *Pharmacotherapy (2.2b):* Completed >4 weeks of Pregabalin 150mg BID (4 months) and Naproxen 500mg BID (3 months). This satisfies the requirement of two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks.
    *   *Interventional (2.2c):* Completed a right C6 epidural steroid injection on October 15, 2024, providing 70% temporary relief before symptoms fully returned. This satisfies the interventional requirement.
*   **Exception 2.2(A) Applicability:** Because the diagnostic classification in Step 2 confirmed radiculopathy and ruled out myelopathy, the modified Myelopathy Pathway criteria do not apply.
*   **Adjacent Level (C6–C7) Compliance:** There has been no C7-specific interventional therapy. Furthermore, the physical therapy notes at week 8 explicitly state that minor C7-associated symptoms were "minimal and possibly resolving," and the physical exam notes that C7 symptoms were "not clearly documented" or "possibly present" but not definitive.
*   **Finding:** Clinical criteria are **fully satisfied for C5–C6** but are **not satisfied for C6–C7** due to a lack of correlating symptomatology and severe structural pathology (violating Section 3.3).

### Step 5: Application of California Statutory Overrides
We evaluated whether statutory protections override the plan's criteria to mandate approval of the second level:
*   **Evaluation:** Under **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3)** and **DMHC APL 22-014**, conservative treatment requirements (such as physical therapy) may be legally waived if there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (such as myelomalacia or progressive spinal cord compression) that physical therapy cannot address.
*   **Application:** The MRI explicitly shows "no cord contact" and "no nerve root compression" at the C6–C7 level. There is no evidence of myelomalacia, progressive spinal cord compression, or UMN deficits. Additionally, the treating physical therapist documented that C7-related symptoms were resolving, meaning PT was beneficial rather than contraindicated.
*   **Finding:** **No statutory waivers or overrides apply** to the C6–C7 level. Standard criteria and clinical necessity guidelines remain fully active.

### Step 6: Clinical Standard Evaluation & Conflict Resolution
Where plan criteria and clinical standards conflict, California law (**CHSC § 1374.32(b)**) mandates that generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine) govern:
*   **Clinical Standards for C5–C6:** Under **AANS/CNS (2023) Guidelines**, surgical decompression and fusion is a **Tier 2 (Reasonable) to Tier 1 (Strong) indication** for patients with cervical radiculopathy who exhibit severe objective neurological deficits (3+/5 bicep weakness, absent reflex) and fail a comprehensive 6-to-12-week conservative trial. Surgical intervention at C5–C6 is clinically necessary.
*   **Clinical Standards for C6–C7:** The appeal argues that the C6–C7 level must be addressed simultaneously to prevent "accelerated adjacent-segment degeneration." However, under **AANS/CNS (2023)** and **NASS (2020) clinical standards (Tier 3)**, surgical fusion is *not indicated* for levels with mild, non-compressing radiographic findings and absent or resolving clinical symptoms. Prophylactic fusion of a radiographically mild, asymptomatic adjacent level lacks high-quality evidentiary support, increases perioperative morbidity, and does not prevent long-term adjacent segment disease.
*   **Finding:** Clinical standards support the medical necessity of a **single-level C5–C6 ACDF** but actively contraindicate the inclusion of the C6–C7 level.

## 4. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND REGULATORY CITATIONS

1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5**: Establishes standard criteria for physical therapy waiver. The member’s C6–C7 level has no objective structural compromise (myelomalacia/cord compression) and C7 symptoms resolved with therapy, meaning no statutory waiver of conservative criteria applies to C6–C7.
2.  **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b)**: Dictates that medical necessity must be determined in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice.
3.  **AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines for Degenerative Cervical Spine Disorders (2023 Update)**: Identifies persistent radiculopathy with objective motor deficits matching radiographic compression after conservative failure as a strong surgical indication. Conversely, asymptomatic or radiographically mild degenerative disc disease without nerve root or cord compression is classified as Tier 3 (surgery not clinically indicated).
4.  **HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024**: Section 2.2 criteria for conservative management are met for the C5–C6 level. Section 3.3 mandates denial when imaging does not demonstrate pathology at the proposed surgical level consistent with the clinical presentation, which applies to the C6–C7 level.

## 5. CONCLUSION AND NEXT STEPS

The final determination is a **PARTIAL APPROVAL**:

1.  **Surgical decompression and fusion at C5–C6 (CPT 22551) is APPROVED.** The clinical documentation demonstrates a severe, matching C6 deficit, corresponding high-grade compression on MRI, and failure of a comprehensive conservative trial.
2.  **Surgical decompression and fusion at C6–C7 (CPT 22552) is DENIED.** Imaging shows only mild degenerative changes with no nerve root compression or cord contact, and there are no corresponding clinical symptoms or neurological deficits at this level.
