### I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

An independent clinical appeal review has been conducted regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) for Mr. Raymond Flores, a 54-year-old construction worker. The requesting surgeon, Dr. Karen Davis, MD, seeks authorization for "prophylactic" decompression, citing a critically narrow cervical canal (6mm) with spinal cord contact and a high occupational risk of cervical trauma.

Following a rigorous, multi-step clinical evaluation of the member's medical records, imaging reports, physical therapy progress, and specialist notes, and applying the mandated **California Hierarchy of Authority** (State Law > Clinical Standards > Plan Criteria), the initial denial is **UPHED**. 

While the member exhibits severe anatomical central canal stenosis at C5-C6, objective clinical examinations confirm a completely normal neurological profile with no clinical signs or symptoms of cervical radiculopathy or myelopathy. Under both plan coverage criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024) and national guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021), surgical decompression is not indicated for axial neck pain or asymptomatic stenosis in the absence of objective neurological deficits.

### III. STEP-BY-STEP EVALUATION & DETERMINATION ANALYSIS

#### STEP 1: Procedural Adequacy and Defect Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), a plan’s medical necessity denial must be procedurally adequate by explicitly specifying the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the determination. 
*   **Analysis:** The initial denial notice dated October 25, 2024, explicitly cited the coverage guidelines (**CC-SPINE-2024**), referenced the specific denial reason code (**MED-003**), and provided a clear, specific clinical rationale: the presence of axial neck pain only, the absence of radiculopathy or myelopathy on physical exam, and the lack of indication for surgical intervention for axial pain without neurological deficits.
*   **Finding:** The denial notice is **procedurally adequate**. No procedural defects exist. The review proceeds to Step 2.

#### STEP 2: Clinical Diagnosis Verification and Disease Classification
The medical records were evaluated to determine whether the member’s condition constitutes cervical radiculopathy, cervical myelopathy, or isolated axial neck pain.
*   **Myelopathy Definition:** Requires both objective MRI evidence of spinal cord compression or myelomalacia AND clinical upper motor neuron signs (e.g., hyperreflexia, Hoffman's sign, gait disturbance).
*   **Radiculopathy Definition:** Requires nerve root compression corresponding to dermatomal weakness, numbness, or radiating pain.
*   **Clinical Findings:** 
    *   The MRI of September 15, 2024, demonstrates severe canal narrowing (6mm) and spinal cord "contact," but specifically notes there is **no cord compression**, **no T2 signal change**, and **no myelomalacia**.
    *   The neurological exam of September 20, 2024, is documented as **completely normal** with no UMN signs and no radicular findings.
*   **Finding:** The member is clinically classified as having **axial neck pain only with asymptomatic cervical stenosis**. There is no objective clinical or radiographic evidence of radiculopathy or myelopathy. This triggers the negative finding pathway, indicating the condition meets the explicit exclusion under Section 3.1 of the Plan’s criteria. The review proceeds to Step 3.

#### STEP 3: Application of State Regulatory Overrides
We must evaluate whether California statutory mandates override the Plan’s conservative treatment requirements or compel approval of the surgical procedure.
*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3):** This statute prohibits conditioning surgery on physical therapy when there is "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Structural neurological compromise is clinically defined as spinal cord compression with myelomalacia (T2 cord signal changes). Because Mr. Flores's MRI explicitly notes "no T2 signal change" and "no myelomalacia," no structural compromise is present.
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:** This mandate waives standard conservative trials for time-sensitive neurological conditions where delay risks irreversible harm. It specifically requires objective imaging showing progressive pathology (e.g., progressive myelomalacia or herniation) and a physician's documentation of urgency. Mr. Flores has no myelomalacia and a completely stable, normal neurological examination. No clinical urgency is documented that would bypass standard guidelines.
*   **Finding:** **No State Regulatory Overrides apply** to this case. The review proceeds to Step 4.

#### STEP 4: Plan Coverage Criteria Compliance (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)
The request was evaluated against the contractual criteria set forth in **CC-SPINE-2024**:
*   **Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement):** To qualify for cervical spine surgery, the member must have a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (2.1a), cervical myelopathy (2.1b), or cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit (2.1c). Mr. Flores does not meet any of these diagnostic requirements.
*   **Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Exception):** This exception relaxes the conservative treatment trial (reducing PT to 4 weeks and waiving injections) for members with confirmed myelopathy. Because the member does not have clinical or radiographic myelopathy, this exception cannot be applied.
*   **Section 3.1 (Exclusion):** This section explicitly mandates that authorization **shall be denied** if "surgery is requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy."
*   **Finding:** Although the member completed a robust conservative trial (8 weeks of physical therapy, multiple pharmacotherapy agents, and an epidural injection), the request is contractually excluded under **Section 3.1** because the surgery is requested for axial pain in the absence of radiculopathy or myelopathy.

#### STEP 5: Clinical Standard Alignment (National Guidelines)
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), medical necessity must be evaluated using generally accepted standards of medical practice (clinical standards), which override plan-specific criteria if a conflict exists.
*   **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update):** 
    *   **Tier 1 (Strong Recommendation for Surgery):** Indicated for moderate-to-severe myelopathy with cord compression/myelomalacia. (Not met).
    *   **Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated):** Explicitly states that surgical decompression/ACDF is **not indicated** for "axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy" or "imaging findings without correlating symptoms."
*   **AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):** There is no high-quality clinical evidence supporting "prophylactic" decompression for asymptomatic cervical stenosis, even with a narrowed canal (6mm) and spinal cord contact. While Dr. Davis correctly notes that a severe trauma could theoretically cause spinal cord injury given the reduced canal diameter, clinical registry data demonstrates that the cumulative risk of major surgical complications (e.g., adjacent segment disease, C5 nerve root palsy, pseudarthrosis, dysphagia) associated with ACDF in an asymptomatic patient outweighs the low yearly statistical risk of a catastrophic traumatic spinal cord injury. National clinical standards recommend conservative management and activity modification (e.g., avoiding high-impact contact sports) rather than prophylactic fusion.
*   **Finding:** The requested prophylactic surgery is **not indicated** under national clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021). The clinical standards perfectly align with the Plan's denial.

### IV. DISPOSITION AND RATIONALE

#### **FINAL DECISION: UPHOLD THE DENIAL**

**Rationale:**
1.  **Absence of Covered Indication:** The member does not have cervical myelopathy or cervical radiculopathy. Both physical and neurological examinations are completely normal.
2.  **Contractual Exclusion:** Under HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 3.1, surgical authorization must be denied when requested solely for axial neck pain in the absence of radiculopathy or myelopathy.
3.  **No Legal Override:** State law provisions (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014) that waive conservative treatment do not apply because there is no objective structural neurological compromise (no spinal cord compression or myelomalacia on MRI).
4.  **Clinical Standards Conflict:** General standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines) classify prophylactic ACDF for asymptomatic cervical stenosis as **Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated)**. The theoretical risk of future trauma due to the patient's occupation as a construction worker does not clinically justify the immediate, documented risks of an elective, prophylactic single-level cervical fusion.

The health plan's decision to deny prior authorization for CPT code 22551 is hereby **UPHELD**.
