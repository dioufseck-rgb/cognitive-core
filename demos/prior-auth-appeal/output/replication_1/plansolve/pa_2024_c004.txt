### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This formal appeal review evaluates the medical necessity of a proposed prophylactic single-level anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) for a 54-year-old male construction worker. The initial prior authorization request was denied on October 25, 2024, on the basis that the member exhibits axial neck pain only, with no documented radiculopathy or myelopathy. The appeal asserts that prophylactic surgery is indicated due to severe spinal canal stenosis (6mm canal diameter) with cord contact, placing the member at elevated risk for catastrophic spinal cord injury due to his high-trauma occupation. 

Applying the mandatory **California Hierarchy of Authority (California State Law > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards of Practice > Health Plan Criteria)**, a systematic evaluation of the clinical record was conducted. The clinical records, including a completely normal neurological examination and MRI findings demonstrating cord contact *without* compression, T2 signal changes, or myelomalacia, confirm a clinical presentation of isolated axial neck pain. Because prophylactic surgery for asymptomatic or axial-pain-only stenosis is excluded under both plan criteria and generally accepted clinical guidelines, **the decision to deny coverage for CPT 22551 is UPHELD**.

---

### EVALUATION AND ANALYSIS

#### Step 1: Procedural Defect & Notice Adequacy Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
*   **Objective:** Review the initial denial notice dated 2024-10-25 to determine if the plan met its statutory obligations under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b).
*   **Analysis:** The initial denial notice issued by Dr. Patricia Huang explicitly cited the clinical criteria applied (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024), provided specific clinical reasoning (cervical stenosis with axial neck pain only; absence of radiculopathy or myelopathy on examination), and included the mandatory notice of Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights.
*   **Finding:** **Adequate (Positive Finding).** The denial notice is procedurally valid and legally compliant. There are no procedural defects. The review proceeds to Step 2.

#### Step 2: Diagnostic Classification & Clinical Reality Check
*   **Objective:** Review clinical records, physical therapy notes, and imaging to classify the member's true clinical presentation (Cervical Myelopathy vs. Cervical Radiculopathy vs. Isolated Axial Pain).
*   **Analysis of Clinical Record:**
    *   **Neurological Examination (2024-09-20):** Dr. Davis documented a "completely normal neurological examination." There are no upper motor neuron (UMN) signs (e.g., negative Hoffman sign, normal reflexes, no clonus, no gait instability) and no signs of radiculopathy (no dermatomal sensory, motor, or reflex deficits). The member's symptom is isolated to axial neck pain, rated 5/10.
    *   **Cervical Spine MRI (2024-09-15):** The imaging reveals severe central canal stenosis at C5-C6 with a canal diameter of 6mm. However, the radiologist explicitly noted "cord contact without compression," "no T2 signal change," and "no myelomalacia."
*   **Clinical Synthesis:** Under standard diagnostic definitions (AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines), cervical myelopathy requires objective evidence of spinal cord compression on MRI *accompanied by* objective clinical upper motor neuron signs or myelopathic symptoms (gait disturbances, bilateral extremity weakness, clumsiness). Radiculopathy requires objective nerve root compression correlated with dermatomal sensory or motor deficits. Raymond Flores exhibits neither. His clinical presentation is strictly classified as **isolated axial neck pain with asymptomatic/prophylactic severe cervical stenosis**.
*   **Finding:** **Axial Pain Only Confirmed (Negative Finding).** The clinical presentation lacks any objective myelopathic or radicular symptoms. The request is solely for prophylactic decompression. 
*   **Action:** Pursuant to Step 2 of the evaluation plan, **UPHOLD** the denial under HealthFirst Plan Exclusion 3.1 and Clinical Standard Tier 3(a).

#### Step 3: Regulatory Override & Safe Harbor Analysis
*   **Objective:** Assess if California state statutes override the plan's conservative treatment prerequisites.
*   **Analysis:**
    *   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3):** This statute waives physical therapy requirements only when there is "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Structural neurological compromise is clinically defined as spinal cord compression, nerve root compression with correlating deficit, or myelomalacia. The MRI explicitly notes "cord contact without compression" and "no myelomalacia," and the physical exam confirms zero neurological deficits. Hence, CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) is not triggered.
    *   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:** This guidance restricts plans from requiring conservative therapy when objective imaging shows progressive pathology (e.g., myelomalacia, cord signal changes) and the physician documents that delay risks irreversible decline. As Raymond Flores has no myelomalacia, no cord signal changes, and no clinical myelopathy, this safe harbor is inapplicable.
*   **Finding:** **No Regulatory Override Triggered (Negative Finding).** The statutory safe harbors for neurological urgency do not apply to asymptomatic, structurally stable (non-compressed) stenosis.

#### Step 4: Clinical Standard Alignment (CHSC § 1374.32(b))
*   **Objective:** Evaluate if prophylactic ACDF for asymptomatic severe stenosis meets generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) regardless of plan criteria.
*   **Analysis:**
    *   **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023):** These guidelines classify surgical interventions for cervical spine conditions into three tiers. **Tier 1 (Strong Indication)** is reserved for moderate-to-severe myelopathy with objective cord compression, progressive deficits, or myelomalacia. **Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated)** explicitly includes "axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy" and "imaging findings without correlating symptoms." Prophylactic surgery for asymptomatic stenosis does not meet Tier 1 or Tier 2 standards.
    *   **AO Spine International Guidelines (2021):** Clinical guidelines do not support prophylactic surgical decompression for spinal canal narrowing in the absence of clinical myelopathy, even when minor trauma risks are hypothesized due to occupation. The evidence demonstrates that the vast majority of patients with asymptomatic stenosis do not develop acute spinal cord injuries, and the morbidity of prophylactic multi-level or single-level fusion outweighs the statistical risk of catastrophic trauma-induced cord injury.
*   **Finding:** **Fails Clinical Standards (Negative Finding).** The clinical standard aligns with the plan's exclusion: surgery is not indicated for isolated axial neck pain in the absence of neurological compromise (Tier 3(a)).

#### Step 5: Plan Criteria Compliance Check (CC-SPINE-2024)
*   **Objective:** Determine if the member met the standard conservative treatment pathways.
*   **Analysis:** 
    *   **Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement):** The criteria require a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (2.1(a)), cervical myelopathy (2.1(b)), or cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit (2.1(c)). Raymond Flores has none of these diagnoses.
    *   **Section 3.1 (Exclusions):** The plan criteria state: *"Authorization will be DENIED when... Surgery is requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy."*
*   **Finding:** **Plan Criteria Unmet & Exclusion Triggered (Negative Finding).** The clinical facts fall squarely within the Section 3.1 exclusion.

### ADJUDICATION OF APPEAL ARGUMENTS

1.  **Appellant Argument:** *Member has severe cervical stenosis with cord contact — risk of myelopathy from minor trauma is present, and prophylactic surgery is indicated.*
    *   **Clinical Evaluation:** While a 6mm canal diameter represents significant anatomic narrowing, clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021) do not support prophylactic spinal fusion for "cord contact" without cord compression, T2 signal changes, or clinical myelopathy. Decompression and fusion carry inherent surgical risks (dysphagia, adjacent segment disease, pseudarthrosis, neurological injury) that outweigh the theoretical risk of trauma-induced myelopathy in an asymptomatic patient.
2.  **Appellant Argument:** *The member's occupation as a construction worker with heavy equipment operation exposes him to high trauma risk, justifying prophylactic surgery.*
    *   **Clinical Evaluation:** Occupational exposure to trauma is not a medically necessary indication for prophylactic cervical fusion under any generally accepted clinical guidelines or California regulatory provisions. Prudent clinical management for individuals with asymptomatic stenosis and high-exposure occupations involves activity modification, ergonomic counseling, and close clinical monitoring, rather than elective spinal fusion.

---

### FINAL DISPOSITION AND ORDER

Based on a comprehensive review of the clinical evidence, California regulatory statutes, and generally accepted clinical standards of practice, the health plan's initial decision is upheld.

*   **Determination:** **UPHOLD the denial of coverage for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6).**
*   **Primary Basis:** 
    1.  The requested procedure is excluded under **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 3.1**, as it is requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy.
    2.  The requested prophylactic surgery is classified as **Tier 3 (Not Indicated)** under generally accepted clinical standards of practice (**AANS/CNS 2023 Joint Guidelines** and **AO Spine 2021 Guidelines**).
    3.  No statutory overrides under **California Insurance Code § 10169.5** or **DMHC APL 22-014** are active, as there is no objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (spinal cord compression or myelomalacia) and no clinical myelopathy.
