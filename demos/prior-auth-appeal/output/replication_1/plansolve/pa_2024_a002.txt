## I. FINAL DETERMINATION
### **DISPOSITION: OVERTURN IN FULL**

The initial clinical denial issued on November 5, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, is **OVERTURNED IN FULL**. Prior authorization for a single-level anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) at UCSF Medical Center is **APPROVED**.

Under California regulatory hierarchy, the plan's rigid requirement for a 6-week trial of physical therapy is **preempted and waived** by California statutory overrides (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014) and generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023 and AO Spine 2021). Furthermore, the member meets the substantive clinical requirements of the plan's own Myelopathy Exception (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2 Exception A). The administrative denial based on the sequencing of billing codes was clinical error.

---

## II. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The initial denial was based on HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2, which requires a minimum of 6 weeks of physical therapy (PT). The initial reviewer concluded that the member only completed 4 weeks of PT and did not qualify for the Plan's 4-week Myelopathy Exception because the administrative billing code listed on the referral was "cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy" (ICD-10: M47.812) rather than myelopathy.

An independent medical review of the clinical record reveals that the member has **cervical spondylotic myelopathy with objective spinal cord compression and early myelomalacia (T2-weighted MRI hyperintensity)** at C6-C7, accompanied by objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs (hyperreflexia, bilateral Hoffman's sign, gait impairment, and 3/5 bilateral hand weakness). 

Under California law and national clinical standards:
1. **Physical therapy is clinically contraindicated** for progressive spinal cord compression with myelomalacia, and continuing PT risks irreversible quadriplegia or permanent neurological deficits.
2. **California Insurance Code § 10169.5** and **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014** legally mandate the waiver of conservative treatment requirements when objective imaging demonstrates structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents that further conservative care is contraindicated.
3. **Generally Accepted Clinical Standards** (AANS/CNS 2023 and AO Spine 2021 guidelines) designate moderate-to-severe myelopathy with cord signal changes as a Tier 1 strong surgical indication with no minimum physical therapy trial requirement. These clinical standards supersede restrictive plan policies under California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32(b).

## III. CLINICAL CHRONOLOGY & RECORD REVIEW

A comprehensive review of the clinical record provided by Dr. Sarah Chen (MD, Spine Surgery) and UCSF Medical Center demonstrates the following clinical course:

*   **July 1, 2024 (Initial Neurological Evaluation):** The member presented with bilateral hand weakness, coordination problems, and clumsy fine-motor movements. A physical exam revealed objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including bilateral 3/5 grip strength, positive bilateral Hoffman's sign, hyperreflexia in the bilateral upper extremities, and impaired tandem gait. Due to administrative workflows, the primary diagnostic billing code assigned was ICD-10 M47.812 ("cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy"), but the narrative clinical charting explicitly described active cervical myelopathy.
*   **July 10, 2024 (Diagnostic Imaging — MRI):** MRI of the cervical spine performed at UCSF Medical Center revealed a **C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate spinal cord compression** and **T2-signal intensity changes within the spinal cord**, diagnostic of **early myelomalacia** (active, irreversible cord injury).
*   **Physical Therapy Trial:** The member completed 4 weeks (12 sessions) of supervised physical therapy. Upon receipt and review of the July 10 MRI confirming myelomalacia, Dr. Sarah Chen immediately halted physical therapy, documenting that further cervical distraction or manipulation was strictly contraindicated due to the risk of worsening spinal cord damage. Dr. Chen issued a formal letter stating that further PT was contraindicated.
*   **Pharmacotherapy Trial:** The member completed a 3-month trial of Gabapentin (300mg TID), which provided only partial pain relief and zero improvement in motor weakness. Additionally, the member completed a 2-month trial of Naproxen (500mg BID), which was clinically inadequate.
*   **Interventional Treatment:** On September 15, 2024, the member underwent a cervical epidural steroid injection. The response was documented as minimal transient pain improvement, with no change or recovery in motor deficits.

## IV. STEP-BY-STEP EVALUATION ANALYSIS

Applying the mandated California Regulatory Evaluation Plan, we resolve this appeal as follows:

### STEP 1: Procedural Defect Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
*   **Requirement:** Evaluate if the denial notice of November 5, 2024, meets the disclosure requirements of California Health & Safety Code § 1374.31(b).
*   **Finding:** The initial denial notice explicitly cited Plan Document CC-SPINE-2024 (Section 2.2), detailed the clinical rationale (failure to complete 6 weeks of PT and lack of a primary myelopathy diagnosis code), and outlined the member's Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights.
*   **Conclusion:** The denial notice is procedurally adequate. **Action: Proceed to Step 2.**

### STEP 2: Clinical Diagnosis & Pathology Verification
*   **Requirement:** Review medical records to verify if the pathology is cervical radiculopathy (standard pathway) or cervical myelopathy/mixed pathology (exception pathways).
*   **Finding:** The physical examination of July 1, 2024, demonstrates a classic, objective myelopathic presentation: bilateral grip weakness (3/5), hyperreflexia, positive bilateral Hoffman's sign, and impaired tandem gait (UMN signs). The UCSF Medical Center MRI dated July 10, 2024, confirms moderate spinal cord compression at C6-C7 with associated T2 signal changes consistent with early myelomalacia. 
*   **Conclusion:** Although the administrative billing code utilized on early PT referrals was M47.812 (spondylosis with radiculopathy), the clinical charting, physical exam, and objective imaging establish a definitive diagnosis of **cervical spondylotic myelopathy with active myelomalacia**. This is a myelopathic, structural neurological compromise, not a simple radiculopathy. **Action: Proceed to Step 3 (Myelopathy Pathway).**

### STEP 3: Application of California State Law Overrides (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)
*   **Requirement:** Evaluate if statutory provisions nullify the plan's 6-week physical therapy requirement.
*   **Finding:** 
    1.  **California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(1):** A health plan cannot condition surgical approval on physical therapy where the treating physician has documented in writing that PT is contraindicated. Dr. Chen explicitly halted PT after 4 weeks and documented that further PT was contraindicated because of the MRI findings.
    2.  **California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(3):** Insurers shall not require physical therapy where there is objective evidence of "structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Marcus Webb's C6-C7 moderate cord compression and myelomalacia constitute severe structural neurological compromise that PT cannot resolve.
    3.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:** This circular mandates that plans *shall not* require standard conservative treatment trials for time-sensitive neurological conditions where objective imaging shows progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia/cord signal changes) and the physician documents conservative treatment failure. The member has myelomalacia and failed to improve after 3 months of gabapentin, 2 months of naproxen, and an epidural injection.
*   **Conclusion:** The 6-week physical therapy requirement is legally waived under both CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014. Conditioning surgery on 2 additional weeks of PT in the presence of active myelomalacia is a violation of California insurance law. **Action: STATUTORY OVERRIDE TRIGGERED. Immediate OVERTURN indicated.**

### STEP 4: Clinical Standard Review (CHSC § 1374.32(b) / AANS/CNS 2023 & AO Spine 2021)
*   **Requirement:** Assess medical necessity under generally accepted clinical guidelines, which override plan criteria during appeal and Independent Medical Review (IMR) pursuant to CHSC § 1374.32(b).
*   **Finding:**
    1.  **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update):** Moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective spinal cord compression and myelomalacia (T2 cord hyperintensity) is classified as a **Tier 1 — Strong Surgical Indication**. Surgical decompression is the recommended primary treatment. Extended trials of physical therapy are not supported and are contraindicated because myelomalacia is a strong predictor of permanent, irreversible deficit if decompression is delayed.
    2.  **AO Spine Guidelines (2021):** Guidelines state: *"There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."*
*   **Conclusion:** The plan’s insistence on 6 weeks of PT for active myelopathy represents a clinical category error. The clinical evidence base for conservative management is derived from radiculopathy, not myelopathy. Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), generally accepted clinical guidelines override the plan's CC-SPINE-2024 criteria. **Action: CLINICAL OVERRIDE TRIGGERED. OVERTURN indicated.**

### STEP 5: Plan Criteria Compliance & Myelopathy Exception Analysis (CC-SPINE-2024)
*   **Requirement:** Assess if the clinical evidence satisfies the substantive criteria of the Plan's internal policy CC-SPINE-2024, including Section 2.2 Exception A.
*   **Finding:** 
    1.  **Diagnosis (Section 2.1(b)):** Confirmed cervical myelopathy is fully documented via UMN signs and cord compression on MRI.
    2.  **Modified PT (Exception 2.2(A)):** The PT trial is modified to a **4-week minimum** for confirmed myelopathy. The member completed 4 weeks (12 sessions).
    3.  **Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)):** The member completed more than 4 weeks of two agents: Gabapentin (3 months) and Naproxen (2 months).
    4.  **Interventional (Section 2.2(c)):** The interventional requirement was exceeded (cervical epidural steroid injection performed on September 15, 2024).
    5.  **Administrative Dispute:** The Plan's initial reviewer denied the exception solely because the primary ICD-10 administrative billing code was listed as radiculopathy (M47.812). Under clinical peer review standards and California law, a rigid administrative coding sequence cannot override consistent, detailed clinical charting of myelopathy and objective MRI pathology.
*   **Conclusion:** The member substantively met all criteria for Plan CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 Exception A (Myelopathy Pathway). The Plan misapplied its own criteria by executing a purely administrative denial based on billing code sequence. **Action: OVERTURN indicated.**
