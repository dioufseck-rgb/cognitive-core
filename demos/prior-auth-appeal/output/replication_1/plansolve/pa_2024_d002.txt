### I. FINAL DISPOSITION
**OVERTURN IN FULL.** The initial determination by HealthFirst Health Plan to deny coverage for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) is **overturned**. ### II. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
This appeal arises from the November 8, 2024, denial of coverage for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) for a 57-year-old male member, Michael Green, to treat progressive, functionally impairing cervical radiculopathy. The plan’s initial reviewer, Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, denied the request utilizing generic reason code MED-003, stating that the member did not meet clinical criteria under plan policy, and referenced an attached criteria document. 

A formal review of the appeal records reveals that the initial denial was **procedurally defective** under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b) because the plan failed to provide the applied clinical criteria or specific clinical reasons to the member and provider, and omitted the legally mandated Independent Medical Review (IMR) notice. 

Furthermore, a comprehensive clinical evaluation reveals that the member **fully satisfies** all requirements of the plan’s own clinical coverage criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024) for cervical radiculopathy, as well as generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS and NASS Guidelines) and California statutory insurance mandates (CIC § 10169.5). The plan’s denial is clinically and legally unsustainable and is overturned on both procedural and substantive clinical grounds.

### III. HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY & CONFLICT RESOLUTION
In accordance with California regulatory mandates, this appeal has been evaluated under a strict hierarchy of authority:
1. **Highest Authority:** California State Law & Regulatory Mandates (California Health & Safety Code [CHSC], California Insurance Code [CIC], Department of Managed Health Care All-Plan Letters [DMHC APL]).
2. **Intermediate Authority:** Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines, North American Spine Society [NASS] Guidelines, AO Spine Guidelines).
3. **Lowest Authority:** Plan Clinical Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024).

*Rule of Application:* Any plan clinical criterion that is more restrictive than or conflicts with state law or generally accepted clinical standards is unenforceable, and the higher authority must be applied.

### IV. PROCEDURAL ADEQUACY & DISCLOSURE CHECK (STEP 1)
Pursuant to **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b)**, when a health plan issues a medical necessity denial, it is statutorily required to:
1. Provide written notice containing the specific clinical criteria applied and the precise clinical reasons for the denial.
2. Include the mandatory Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights notice.
3. Deliver this exact information to both the enrollee and the treating provider.

CHSC § 1374.31(b) explicitly dictates that *“a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is defective and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines.”*

**Findings of Fact:**
* The plan’s initial denial letter dated November 8, 2024, stated: *"Request denied. Member does not meet criteria for requested procedure under plan policy. See attached criteria document."*
* No criteria document was attached to the denial letter. Both the member (Michael Green) and the requesting provider (Dr. Sarah Kim) confirmed that no clinical criteria attachment was ever received.
* The mandatory California IMR rights notice was entirely omitted from the denial packet.
* The clinical justification provided was restricted to a generic code (MED-003) with no individualized clinical reasoning or discussion of the member’s actual medical records.

**Procedural Conclusion:**
The denial notice issued by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, is **facially and legally defective** under CHSC § 1374.31(b). It failed to disclose the criteria applied, lacked an individualized clinical rationale, and withheld mandatory statutory disclosures regarding IMR rights. Under California law, a defective denial is voidable and must be treated as if no denial was issued. **On procedural grounds alone, the denial must be OVERTURNED.**

### V. CLINICAL EVIDENCE & PLAN CRITERIA COMPLIANCE EVALUATION (STEPS 2–5)
Even if the denial notice had been procedurally flawless, a substantive clinical review of the medical record establishes that the member meets all contractually required and clinically accepted standards for the requested procedure.

#### 1. Diagnostic Classification and Severity Assessment
Under Section 2.1 of HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, the plan requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (nerve root compression with dermatomal pain, numbness, or weakness) supported by current imaging (within 6 months) demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms.

* **Clinical Evidence:** 
  * **Neurological Exam (2024-09-25):** Documents objective, correlating focal deficits including left C6 dermatomal numbness, a diminished left bicep reflex, and moderate left bicep motor weakness graded at 4/5. 
  * **Objective Imaging (MRI dated 2024-09-20):** Reveals a large left-sided C5-C6 disc herniation causing moderate left C6 nerve root compression. No spinal cord contact or signal changes (myelomalacia) are present, confirming a primary diagnosis of **cervical radiculopathy**.
* **Clinical Conclusion:** The member has a highly documented, objectively confirmed diagnosis of severe, single-level left C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy with correlating motor, sensory, and reflex deficits. Section 2.1 criteria are fully satisfied.

#### 2. Conservative Treatment Compliance Analysis
Section 2.2 of the plan policy requires a documented course of conservative treatment consisting of physical therapy, pharmacotherapy, and at least one interventional procedure.

* **A. Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)):** 
  * *Plan Requirement:* Minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy with documented functional outcomes.
  * *Member Experience:* The clinical record documents that the member completed **7 weeks (21 sessions)** of supervised physical therapy. The physical therapy notes document that the member reached a **functional plateau at week 6** with persistent, severe radicular symptoms.
  * *Statutory Overlay (CIC § 10169.5):* Under California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2), a health insurer is prohibited from mechanically enforcing arbitrary physical therapy durations if the member has completed therapy and reached a documented "functional plateau" without symptom resolution. The member met the standard 6-week plan requirement and achieved a documented clinical plateau, fully satisfying both plan criteria and state law.
* **B. Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)):**
  * *Plan Requirement:* Trial of at least two qualifying agents (NSAIDs, neuropathic agents, muscle relaxants, oral steroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
  * *Member Experience:* The member trialed **Gabapentin 600mg TID for 4 months** (neuropathic agent, resulting in only partial relief) and **Naproxen 500mg BID for 3 months** (NSAID, resulting in inadequate relief). 
  * *Clinical Conclusion:* The member completed a multi-month trial of two distinct, first-line therapeutic classes, far exceeding the plan’s 4-week minimum. Section 2.2(b) criteria are fully satisfied.
* **C. Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)):**
  * *Plan Requirement:* At least one interventional procedure (cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point series).
  * *Member Experience:* The member underwent a **left C6 cervical epidural steroid injection on October 28, 2024**, which provided temporary 40% relief before symptoms returned to baseline within 3 weeks.
  * *Clinical Conclusion:* The member completed the required interventional trial. Section 2.2(c) criteria are fully satisfied.

### VI. GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS EVALUATION
Under **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b)**, the standard for determining medical necessity must align with generally accepted standards of medical practice, independent of any more restrictive internal guidelines a health plan may seek to enforce.

According to the **American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) 2023 Guidelines** and the **North American Spine Society (NASS) 2020 Guidelines**:
1. Single-level ACDF (CPT 22551) is a highly effective, standard-of-care, Tier 1/Tier 2 indicated surgical intervention for patients presenting with cervical radiculopathy due to disc herniation who exhibit objective neurological deficits (motor weakness, sensory loss, reflex changes) and have failed a reasonable course of conservative management (typically 6 to 12 weeks).
2. The presence of **progressive motor weakness (4/5 bicep strength)** is a critical clinical finding. Delaying surgical decompression in a patient with active motor deficits and an objective structural correlate (C5-C6 herniation compressing the C6 nerve root) risks permanent, irreversible axonotmesis and long-term functional loss.
3. The member's conservative trial of 7 weeks of physical therapy, 3 to 4 months of dual-agent pharmacotherapy, and a targeted epidural steroid injection represents an exhaustive, standard-of-care conservative regimen. 

Therefore, according to national neurosurgical clinical guidelines, the requested ACDF is highly indicated, medically necessary, and urgent to prevent permanent neurological damage.
