### STEP 2: CLINICAL CLASSIFICATION VERIFICATION (MYELOPATHY VS. RADICULOPATHY)
Under generally accepted spinal guidelines, cervical myelopathy (spinal cord pathology) and cervical radiculopathy (nerve root pathology) are distinct clinical entities requiring completely different treatment algorithms.

*   **Imaging Review (MRI dated September 10, 2024):** 
    *   Findings: Mild-to-moderate disc herniation at C5-C6 with right C6 nerve root contact *without* measurable compression.
    *   Cord Status: **No cord contact. No cord compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia.**
*   **Clinical Exam Review (Neurological Exam dated September 15, 2024):**
    *   Symptoms: Right shoulder and neck pain (6/10), mild right C6 dermatomal tingling.
    *   Objective Findings: Biceps reflex is normal and symmetric (2+ bilaterally). Grip strength is intact (5/5 bilaterally). **No weakness. No upper motor neuron (UMN) signs** (e.g., no Hoffman sign, no hyperreflexia, no clonus, no gait disturbance).
    *   Functional Status: Member is working full-time with "no activity limitations."
*   **Finding:** **Cervical Radiculopathy Confirmed (Negative for Myelopathy).** There is no objective imaging evidence of spinal cord compression or myelomalacia, and there are no clinical signs of myelopathy. The patient’s pathology is strictly classified as a stable, unilateral C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy with mild sensory symptoms and zero motor or functional deficits.

### STEP 3: PLAN CRITERIA COMPLIANCE & EXCEPTION APPLICABILITY (CC-SPINE-2024)
Because the member’s pathology is classified as radiculopathy, her request must be evaluated under the standard criteria set forth in **HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2**:

1.  **Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)):** Requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy with documented functional outcomes.
    *   *Actual:* Member completed only 4 weeks (12 sessions). Dr. Walsh elected to halt PT prematurely to seek surgical authorization. **(Criterion Not Met)**
2.  **Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)):** Requires a trial of at least two approved agents (NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
    *   *Actual:* Member trialed only Naproxen 500mg BID for 8 weeks (1 agent), which provided moderate pain relief. No second agent was attempted. **(Criterion Not Met)**
3.  **Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)):** Requires at least one interventional procedure (cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point injection).
    *   *Actual:* No interventional treatments have been performed. **(Criterion Not Met)**

*   **Applicability of Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway):** 
    Dr. Walsh argues that the patient requires immediate surgery to prevent permanent disability. However, the plan's expedited Myelopathy Pathway (which reduces PT to 4 weeks and waives interventional therapy) **does not apply**. Under CC-SPINE-2024, this exception is strictly reserved for cases where cervical myelopathy is the primary diagnosis. As verified in Step 2, the patient has no cord compression, no myelomalacia, and no myelopathic symptoms.
*   **Finding:** **Non-Compliant (Negative Finding).** The member has failed to satisfy any of the three standard conservative treatment criteria required by the plan's medical policy.

### STEP 4: STATUTORY OVERRIDE ANALYSIS (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)
We must evaluate whether California statutory protections override the plan's conservative treatment requirements, notwithstanding the failure to meet CC-SPINE-2024.

1.  **Waiver for Structural Neurological Compromise (CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) & DMHC APL 22-014):**
    *   *Statute:* A plan cannot require PT if objective imaging shows structural neurological compromise (e.g., spinal cord compression or myelomalacia) that PT cannot address.
    *   *Application:* The member’s MRI explicitly states: "No cord contact. No cord compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia." There is only mild right C6 nerve root contact without compression. Therefore, no structural compromise of the spinal cord exists to trigger this statutory waiver. **(Waiver Inapplicable)**
2.  **Medical Contraindication (CIC § 10169.5(a)(1)):**
    *   *Statute:* A plan cannot require PT if the treating physician documents in writing that PT is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member due to the severity of the condition.
    *   *Application:* Dr. Walsh's letter of October 18, 2024, asserts that the patient has "significant cervical pathology" and risks "permanent disability." However, he provides no clinical or physiological reasoning explaining why physical therapy is medically contraindicated or harmful. The physical therapy notes from the actual therapist state the patient had "mild improvement in pain" and "no neurological deficit to track." Dr. Walsh's election to halt PT was administrative, not clinically contraindicated. **(Waiver Inapplicable)**
3.  **Functional PT Plateau (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)):**
    *   *Statute:* A plan cannot require further PT if the member reached a "functional plateau" (defined as <10% improvement over 3 sessions or therapist-documented maximum benefit).
    *   *Application:* The PT summary indicates "mild improvement in pain" and does not document a functional plateau or failure to progress. PT was halted solely because the surgeon chose to seek surgery. **(Waiver Inapplicable)**

*   **Finding:** **No Statutory Override Applies.** The clinical presentation does not meet the legal thresholds established by California law to bypass conservative management.

### STEP 5: GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS EVALIGATION (CHSC § 1374.32(b))
Under **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**, clinical standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) supersede restrictive plan criteria if a conflict exists. We must evaluate if these standards support immediate surgery for this member.

*   **Clinical Guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023 & NASS 2020):**
    *   **Tier 1 (Strong Indication):** Surgery is recommended as a primary treatment for moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with cord compression, myelomalacia, or progressive motor deficits. This member has none of these findings.
    *   **Tier 2/3 (Conservative Care Standard):** For mild-to-moderate cervical radiculopathy without motor deficit, the standard of care is conservative management. NASS (2020) and AANS/CNS (2023) guidelines state that surgical decompression is only indicated for radiculopathy when there are *persistent, medically refractory symptoms after a minimum of 6 to 12 weeks of active conservative therapy*, or in the presence of progressive motor weakness.
    *   **Risk Analysis:** The member has normal bicep reflexes (2+), full grip strength (5/5), and no weakness. She is working full-time without limitations. There is no evidence of clinical urgency, and delaying surgery to complete conservative care poses no risk of irreversible injury.
*   **Finding:** **Clinical Standards Not Met.** Standard medical practice dictates that a patient with stable, non-deficit C5-C6 radiculopathy must complete a full trial of conservative therapy before undergoing an invasive anterior cervical fusion (ACDF).

### STEP 6: DISPOSITION & RESOLUTION

Applying the clinical facts to the **Disposition Decision Tree**:
1.  **Procedural Defect?** No. (Procedurally sound under CHSC § 1374.31).
2.  **Is Myelopathy/Myelomalacia present?** No. (Imaging and clinical exam confirm stable radiculopathy).
3.  **Does it meet Standard Plan Conservative Criteria for Radiculopathy?** No. (Member has only completed 4 weeks of PT, 1 medication trial, and 0 interventional procedures).
4.  **Does it meet CA Law or Clinical Standards for PT Waiver?** No. (No cord compression, no documented contraindication, no functional plateau, and clinical guidelines recommend conservative care as the primary standard of care).

While Dr. Walsh's 25 years of surgical experience and clinical judgment are respected, his assertion that this patient is at risk of "permanent disability" is completely contradicted by his own objective physical exam (5/5 strength, normal reflexes, working full-time) and the radiologist's MRI interpretation (no cord contact, no cord compression). Prudent clinical judgment and evidence-based medicine require that conservative measures be exhausted before exposing a neurologically intact patient to the risks of cervical spine fusion.

### FINAL DECISION
The request for **CPT Code 22551 (ACDF C5-C6)** is **DENIED** as not medically necessary.

To establish medical necessity for future review, the following clinical documentation must be submitted:
1.  Completion of an additional 2 weeks of supervised physical therapy (to satisfy the standard 6-week trial).
2.  Trial and failure of a second pharmacotherapy agent (such as a muscle relaxant or neuropathic agent) for at least 4 weeks.
3.  Documented consideration or completion of at least one cervical interventional procedure (e.g., epidural steroid injection), or a clear, clinical explanation from the physician detailing why an injection is physiologically contraindicated in the absence of spinal cord compression.
