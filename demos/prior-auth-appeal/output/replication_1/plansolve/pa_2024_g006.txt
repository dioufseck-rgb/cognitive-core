### II. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
On November 20, 2024, HealthFirst Utilization Management denied prior authorization for a single-level ACDF (C5-C6) for Theresa Kamau. The sole basis of the plan’s denial (Reason Code: MED-003) was the member's prior authorization history—specifically, the denial of a three-level cervical fusion in May 2024—asserting that the member's clinical status had not "materially changed" to warrant reconsideration. 

Upon comprehensive clinical and regulatory review, this determination is **overturned** on two independent grounds:
1. **Procedural Defect (CHSC § 1374.31(b)):** The plan’s initial denial notice is procedurally and legally defective under California law. It failed to evaluate the current clinical request against active clinical coverage criteria, relying instead on an unauthorized, extra-contractual "prior PA history" exclusion.
2. **Clinical and Statutory Compliance (CIC § 10169.5 & CC-SPINE-2024):** The clinical record demonstrates that the member has a primary diagnosis of progressive C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy with objective, worsening neurological deficits (3+/5 motor weakness, absent bicep reflex) and matching current MRI pathology. The member has fully exhausted all conservative treatment requirements—including physical therapy, pharmacotherapy, and an interventional epidural steroid injection—and has documented a formal functional plateau. The request satisfies all requirements of the plan's internal medical policy (CC-SPINE-2024) and meets the clinical standard under California Insurance Code § 10169.5.

#### 1. Procedural Adequacy and Denial Notice Validity (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
Under **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b)**, any medical necessity denial notice issued by a health plan must specify the clinical criteria applied and provide a specific, patient-centric clinical reason for the denial. 

* **Evaluation:** The plan’s denial notice dated November 20, 2024, failed to cite or apply any clinical criteria from CC-SPINE-2024. Instead, the plan denied the single-level ACDF request because a *different* procedure (a three-level fusion) was denied six months prior in May 2024. 
* **Ruling:** "Prior PA history" is not a recognized clinical criterion under CC-SPINE-2024, nor is it a legally permissible basis to deny medical care under California law. Furthermore, the plan’s clinical assertion that the patient's pathology has not changed is flatly contradicted by objective imaging and exam findings. The denial notice is **legally defective** and treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of statutory compliance.

#### 2. Statutory Physical Therapy Override (CIC § 10169.5)
Under **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2)**, a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy if:
> *"The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."*

* **Evaluation:** The member completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of physical therapy ending September 2024. A functional plateau was explicitly documented by the therapist and physician, showing worsening motor weakness (3+/5) and persistent severe pain.
* **Ruling:** The plan's mechanic application of a prior denial to block surgical authorization violates **CIC § 10169.5**. The completed PT course and documented functional plateau legally exhaust any physical therapy prerequisites.

### VI. EVIDENCE-BASED CLINICAL STANDARDS ANALYSIS

Under **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**, the standard of review requires alignment with generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersede restrictive internal plan interpretations.

1. **North American Spine Society (NASS) Guidelines (2020):** Recommend surgical decompression (ACDF) for patients with cervical radiculopathy who present with objective neurological deficits (motor weakness, reflex changes) and matching imaging pathology who have failed a structured course of conservative management.
2. **Joint AANS/CNS Guidelines (2023):** State that progressive focal motor deficits (such as 3+/5 bicep weakness) in the presence of matching, progressive structural nerve root compression represent a **Tier 1 / Tier 2 Surgical Indication**. Delaying surgical decompression in patients with progressive motor weakness and a documented functional plateau on conservative care is clinically contraindicated due to the increased risk of permanent, irreversible motor axonal loss.

The medical record clearly indicates that the size of the C5-C6 herniation has increased, the nerve root compression has worsened, and the corresponding motor deficit has deteriorated from a mild impairment to a significant 3+/5 weakness. Initiating further conservative measures or upholding a denial based on a historical, unrelated 3-level request is a direct violation of clinical standards.
