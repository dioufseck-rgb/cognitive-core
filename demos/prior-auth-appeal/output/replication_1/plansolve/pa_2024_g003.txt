### FINAL DISPOSITION: UPHOLD

Following a comprehensive clinical and regulatory review conducted in accordance with the established Hierarchy of Authority, the HealthFirst Utilization Management Initial Denial is hereby **UPHELD**. 

While the treating neurologist has asserted clinical urgency, the objective clinical evidence, functional assessments, and radiographic findings demonstrate a mild, stable cervical myelopathy without cord signal changes (myelomalacia). Under both California regulatory standards and generally accepted clinical guidelines, initial conservative management remains appropriate, and the member has not completed the clinical prerequisites defined in the plan’s coverage criteria.

**Objective:** Evaluate if the initial denial notice dated November 5, 2024, complies with California procedural standards under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b).

*   **Findings:** The initial denial notice issued on November 5, 2024, is procedurally sound. It specifies the denial code **MED-003**, outlines the exact clinical reasons for the determination, and details the specific outstanding requirements under the plan's clinical policy (3 weeks of physical therapy completed versus 6 weeks required under the standard pathway, only one pharmacotherapy agent utilized, and no interventional procedures on record). The notice also includes the required disclosures regarding the member’s right to an Independent Medical Review (IMR) and internal appeal rights.
*   **Procedural Status:** **Negative Finding (Procedurally Adequate).** There are no procedural defects under CHSC § 1374.31(b). The review must proceed to a substantive clinical evaluation.

### Step 3: Evaluation of State Law Overrides (Informational Summary)

*Although Step 2 directs the review to bypass Step 3, the statutory claims raised in the appeal basis are addressed here for regulatory completeness:*
*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5:** Under subdivision (a)(3), a plan cannot require physical therapy if there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. In this case, there is no MRI evidence of cord signal change, myelomalacia, or structural compromise (such as a cord injury) that precludes a safe, monitored trial of physical therapy.
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:** This regulatory waiver of conservative treatment applies when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (e.g., progressive herniation or cord signal change on serial imaging) and the physician documents that conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression. This condition is not met here; serial imaging is not present, symptoms have been stable for 6 months, and the MRI explicitly denies myelomalacia or signal changes.
*   **Functional Plateau (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)):** The completed 3 weeks of physical therapy did not document a functional plateau (<10% improvement over 3 sessions or therapist attestation of maximum benefit). Instead, physical therapy was halted prematurely by Dr. Osei citing general myelopathy concerns.

### Step 5: Assessment Under Plan-Specific Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)

**Objective:** Evaluate the prior authorization request against the plan's internal medical policy criteria.

#### 1. Standard Pathway (Section 2.2)
*   **Physical Therapy:** Requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. *Member completed only 3 weeks (9 sessions).* **(Unmet)**
*   **Pharmacotherapy:** Requires a trial of at least two covered agents (NSAIDs/COX-2 inhibitors, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each. *Member trialed only one agent (Baclofen 5mg BID for 6 weeks).* **(Unmet)**
*   **Interventional Treatment:** Requires at least one interventional procedure (cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point injection series). *None completed.* **(Unmet)**

#### 2. Exception 2.2(A) — Myelopathy Pathway
The plan’s clinical criteria provide a modified pathway when cervical myelopathy is the primary diagnosis:
*   **Physical Therapy:** Requirement is reduced to a **4-week minimum** (or documentation of a functional plateau). *Member completed only 3 weeks, and no functional plateau was documented.* **(Unmet)**
*   **Pharmacotherapy:** The requirement for a trial of **two agents** for a minimum of 4 weeks each remains in full. *Member trialed only one agent (Baclofen).* **(Unmet)**
*   **Interventional Treatment:** Waived if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration. *While Dr. Osei documented a general risk, the failure of both the physical therapy and pharmacotherapy requirements prevents approval under this exception.* **(Unmet)**

*   **Plan Criteria Status:** **Negative Finding (Plan Criteria Deficient).** The member fails to satisfy either the Standard Pathway (Section 2.2) or the Myelopathy Exception Pathway (Section 2.2(A)) of Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024.

### Clinical Standard and Medical Necessity Review (AANS/CNS & AO Spine Guidelines)

Under **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**, health plan appeals must be evaluated against generally accepted standards of medical practice. 

1.  **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update):** Under these guidelines, surgical decompression (ACDF) for mild cervical myelopathy is classified as a **Tier 2 (Reasonable)** indication, but not a Tier 1 (Strong/Urgent) indication. Immediate surgical authorization and urgency classification require the presence of acute clinical progression or radiographic myelomalacia (T2 cord signal changes). Neither finding is present in this record.
2.  **AO Spine Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):** While AO Spine guidelines state that mandatory physical therapy is not supported by high-quality evidence for patients with *moderate-to-severe* myelopathy, they support conservative management with close clinical monitoring for patients presenting with *mild, stable myelopathy* (mJOA score ≥ 15, stable for 6 months) and no signal intensity changes on MRI. 
3.  **Clinical Correlation:** Dr. Osei’s letter dated November 1, 2024, asserts "surgical urgency" and states that "further delay risks irreversible neurological deterioration." However, this assertion is directly contradicted by the objective clinical findings in the record. The member’s symptoms have been stable for 6 months, his gait is fully intact, his ADLs are fully independent, his mJOA score is 15/18 (mild), and his MRI shows no myelomalacia or T2 signal change. Therefore, there is no clinical evidence of an active, progressive, or threatened irreversible neurological deficit that would contraindicate completing a standard, monitored trial of conservative therapy.

---

### DECISION SUMMARY

1.  The initial denial was procedurally adequate under California law (**Step 1 Negative**).
2.  The clinical and imaging findings confirm mild, stable myelopathy without myelomalacia, which does not trigger statutory conservative treatment waivers (**Step 2 Negative**, bypassing Step 3).
3.  The proposed procedure fails to satisfy the conservative treatment criteria of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, failing both the Standard Pathway and the Myelopathy Exception under Section 2.2(A) due to incomplete physical therapy and an insufficient pharmacotherapy trial (**Step 5 Negative**).
4.  Generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine) support conservative management as an initial step for mild, stable myelopathy with an mJOA score of 15 and no MRI signal changes.

The clinical and regulatory requirements for authorization have not been met. The decision of the health plan to deny the prior authorization request for CPT 22551 is **UPHELD**.
