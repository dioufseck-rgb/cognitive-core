### **FINAL DETERMINATION: REMAND TO UTILIZATION MANAGEMENT**

Following a comprehensive procedural and clinical review of the prior authorization appeal for Mr. Bernard Achebe, the appeal reviewer finds that the initial denial notice issued by HealthFirst on November 5, 2024, is **procedurally defective and legally non-compliant** under California law. 

Pursuant to the governing **Hierarchy of Authority**—wherein California State Law and Regulations supersede internal plan coverage criteria—this case is hereby **REMANDED** to HealthFirst Utilization Management with instructions to immediately rescind the initial denial, correct the procedural violations, apply the correct clinical pathway (Section 2.2(A) Myelopathy Exception Pathway), and issue a revised, legally compliant determination within the statutory timeframe.

A detailed analysis of the procedural defects, state law overrides, clinical standards, and plan criteria is provided below to guide the re-evaluation.

---

### **PART I: EVALUATION STEPS & FINDINGS**

#### **Step 1: Procedural Adequacy and Regulatory Compliance Audit**
*   **Regulatory Standard**: California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b) mandates that health plans must provide written notice of denials that explicitly specify the clinical criteria applied, the specific clinical reasons for the denial, and a clear disclosure of the member’s right to an Independent Medical Review (IMR).
*   **Evaluation of Initial Denial**: 
    1.  The denial letter dated November 5, 2024, failed to cite the active clinical coverage policy (**CC-SPINE-2024**).
    2.  The denial letter mechanically applied the "Standard Pathway" conservative treatment criteria (demanding 6 weeks of PT and interventional procedures), completely failing to address or apply **Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Exception Pathway)**, despite the clinical records prominently identifying "cervical myelopathy" as the primary diagnosis.
    3.  The denial letter failed to provide the mandatory California IMR disclosure language.
*   **Finding (Procedural Defect)**: The denial notice is legally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b). Under California law, a defective denial must be treated as if no valid denial was issued for the purpose of regulatory enforcement.
*   **Action**: This finding triggers an immediate **REMAND** to Utilization Management to cure the procedural defects. To ensure clinical clarity upon remand, a substantive clinical review was performed under Steps 2 through 5.

---

#### **Step 2: Diagnosis and Clinical Classification Verification**
*   **Clinical Question**: Does the patient's presentation represent Cervical Radiculopathy or Cervical Myelopathy?
*   **Clinical Records Evaluation**:
    *   **Objective Imaging**: MRI dated October 1, 2024, shows "C5-C6 moderate central cord compression" with a canal diameter of 8mm. The radiologist notes "cord contact present without compression" and explicitly states **"No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia."**
    *   **Clinical Examination**: Dr. Osei’s neurological exam dated October 5, 2024, demonstrates bilateral upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including **bilateral Hoffman signs** and **mild hyperreflexia**. However, tandem gait is intact, grip strength is 4+/5 bilaterally (no frank weakness), and there are no dermatomal sensory deficits.
    *   **Functional Severity**: The patient’s modified Japanese Orthopaedic Association (mJOA) score is **15/18**, which is classified as **mild cervical myelopathy** (scores of 15–17 indicate mild category). The patient remains fully independent in activities of daily living (ADLs).
*   **Finding (Confirmed Myelopathy - Mild)**: Objective MRI findings of spinal cord contact/compression combined with clear upper motor neuron signs (bilateral Hoffman, hyperreflexia) confirm the diagnosis of **Cervical Myelopathy**. 

---

#### **Step 3: State Law Override Analysis (Statutory PT Waivers)**
*   **Statutory Standards**:
    *   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3)** prohibits plans from requiring physical therapy (PT) if there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot anatomically address.
    *   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014** states that plans cannot require standard conservative treatment if objective imaging shows progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia or progressive herniation) and the physician documents that delay risks irreversible harm.
*   **Evaluation of the Record**:
    *   The patient’s MRI explicitly notes **no myelomalacia** and **no T2 cord signal changes** (which represents existing, irreversible spinal cord injury).
    *   The symptoms have been documented as **stable for 6 months**. There is no serial imaging or clinical documentation demonstrating "progressive pathology" or acute neurological decline.
    *   There is no documentation in the physical therapy records showing the patient reached a "functional plateau" at 3 weeks (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)). Instead, Dr. Osei unilaterally halted PT after 3 weeks (9 sessions) citing general myelopathy concerns.
*   **Finding**: Because the myelopathy is clinically classified as mild and stable, and **no myelomalacia** is present on MRI, the clinical presentation does not meet the high-risk threshold required to trigger an automatic statutory waiver under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) or DMHC APL 22-014. State law does not automatically override the conservative therapy requirements in this specific clinical state.

---

#### **Step 4: Clinical Standard Compliance Review (IMR Standard)**
*   **Clinical Standards**: Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the Independent Medical Review standard relies on generally accepted standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS Guidelines 2023, AO Spine Guidelines 2021, NASS Guidelines 2020) rather than a plan's internal criteria.
    *   **AANS/CNS 2023 & AO Spine 2021**: Surgical decompression is a **Tier 1 (Strong Recommendation)** as initial treatment for *moderate-to-severe* cervical myelopathy or cases with objective *myelomalacia* on MRI. 
    *   For **mild cervical myelopathy** (mJOA 15-17) without myelomalacia, guidelines state that a trial of conservative treatment (including physical therapy and medical management) with close clinical monitoring is a **reasonable and supported initial pathway** (NASS Grade B Evidence; AANS/CNS Tier 2).
*   **Finding**: Immediate surgical intervention is not mandated by generally accepted clinical standards for stable, mild myelopathy in the absence of myelomalacia or progressive deficits. Dr. Osei's declaration of surgical "urgency" is not supported by the objective clinical findings (stable 6-month history, mJOA of 15, fully intact ADLs, and lack of T2 signal changes on MRI). Therefore, the plan may clinically require a modified conservative trial.

---

#### **Step 5: Plan Criteria and Exception Application (CC-SPINE-2024)**
*   **Plan Criteria Analysis**: Because the patient has a primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, **Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway)** of the CC-SPINE-2024 policy must be applied instead of the standard radiculopathy pathway.
*   **Exception Criteria vs. Clinical Facts**:
    1.  **Physical Therapy**: The Myelopathy Pathway reduces the PT requirement to a **minimum of 4 weeks**. The member has only completed **3 weeks** (9 sessions) before the trial was halted. *Criterion not met (1 week remaining).*
    2.  **Pharmacotherapy**: The policy requires a trial of at least **two agents** (e.g., NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents) for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The member has only trialed **one agent** (Baclofen 5mg BID for 6 weeks). *Criterion not met (second agent required).*
    3.  **Interventional Procedures**: This requirement is **waived** under Section 2.2(A) because Dr. Osei documented a clinical concern of neurological deterioration from interventional procedures. *Criterion met (waived).*
*   **Finding**: The member does not fully satisfy the plan’s Myelopathy Exception Pathway criteria, as they lack 1 additional week of physical therapy and a trial of a second pharmacotherapy agent.

---

### **PART II: DISPOSITION DETERMINATION**

#### **DISPOSITION: REMAND**
The initial denial dated November 5, 2024, is **REVERSED and REMANDED** to HealthFirst Utilization Management. 

#### **RATIONALE FOR REMAND:**
The health plan committed a critical procedural error by failing to apply its own internal exception pathway (**CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(A) Myelopathy Exception Pathway**) and failing to cite the governing coverage policy or IMR rights, rendering the denial legally defective under **California Health & Safety Code § 1374.31(b)**. 

While a substantive clinical review confirms that the patient's mild, stable myelopathy (mJOA 15, no myelomalacia) does not yet qualify for an automatic clinical override under **CIC § 10169.5** or **APL 22-014**, the plan cannot mechanically demand a standard 6-week PT course and interventional procedures. The plan must evaluate the case solely under the modified Myelopathy Pathway.

---

### **PART III: INSTRUCTIONS ON REMAND**

#### **To HealthFirst Utilization Management:**
1.  **Rescind the Denial**: Immediately vacate the denial dated November 5, 2024.
2.  **Apply correct criteria**: Re-evaluate the request under **CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Exception Pathway)**.

    *   Acknowledges that the interventional procedure requirement (2.2(c)) is waived.
    *   Clearly states that only **1 additional week of physical therapy** and **1 additional pharmacotherapy agent trial** (for 4 weeks) are required to satisfy the policy.

#### **To the Requesting Provider (Dr. Kwame Osei, MD):**
To secure immediate authorization upon the plan's re-review, please provide one of the following:
1.  **Documentation of Completion**: Evidence that the patient has completed at least 1 additional week of physical therapy (bringing the total to 4 weeks) and has trialed a second qualifying pharmacotherapy agent (e.g., an NSAID or gabapentin) for 4 weeks; **OR**
2.  **Documentation of a Functional Plateau/Contraindication**: Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) or (2)**, submit a written statement from the physical therapist or your office confirming that the patient reached an objective "functional plateau" (<10% improvement) during the initial 3 weeks of PT, or that continuing PT poses a direct clinical hazard to this patient; **OR**
3.  **Evidence of Clinical Progression**: If a repeat neurological exam or serial imaging shows clinical deterioration (e.g., drop in mJOA score, new gait instability, or new T2 cord signal changes/myelomalacia on MRI), submit these findings to immediately trigger the statutory overrides under **DMHC APL 22-014**, which would waive all remaining conservative treatment requirements.
