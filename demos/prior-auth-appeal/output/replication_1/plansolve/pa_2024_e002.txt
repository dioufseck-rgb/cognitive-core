### Step 2: Diagnostic Classification & Pathology Localization

A rigorous clinical distinction must be maintained between the member's dual diagnoses of **cervical myelopathy** (spinal cord compression with upper motor neuron signs) and **cervical radiculopathy** (nerve root compression with lower motor neuron signs). 

#### A. C4-C5 Level: Cervical Myelopathy (Primary Diagnosis)
*   **Imaging (MRI 10/01/2024):** Large disc herniation, severe cord compression, and T2 signal change (myelomalacia).
*   **Neurological Exam (10/05/2024):** Clear upper motor neuron (UMN) myelopathic findings, including bilateral hyperreflexia, bilateral positive Hoffman sign, and a wide-based gait. Bilateral C5 deltoid weakness is graded 4/5.
*   **Classification:** Primary Cervical Myelopathy with structural spinal cord injury (myelomalacia).

#### B. C5-C6 Level: Cervical Radiculopathy
*   **Imaging (MRI 10/01/2024):** Moderate disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression, and foraminal stenosis. No spinal cord compression or myelomalacia is noted at this level.
*   **Neurological Exam (10/05/2024):** Left C6 radicular deficits, including left bicep weakness (4-/5) and a diminished left bicep reflex.
*   **Classification:** Left-sided C6 Cervical Radiculopathy.

#### C. C3-C4 Level: Axial Neck Pain / Unclear Clinical Correlation
*   **Imaging (MRI 10/01/2024):** Mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing. No spinal cord compression, myelomalacia, or nerve root compression.
*   **Neurological Exam (10/05/2024):** Non-specific neck pain elicited by palpation of the C3-C4 region. No correlating radicular pattern (no C4 dermatomal or myotomal deficits) and no localized myelopathic signs.
*   **Classification:** Axial Neck Pain without myelopathy or radiculopathy.

### Step 3: Application of California Statutory Overrides (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)

#### A. Application to C4-C5 (Myelopathy)
Under **DMHC APL 22-014**, cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI represents a time-sensitive neurological condition with a high risk of irreversible harm. Furthermore, **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)** prohibits health plans from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy when there is "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." 
The presence of myelomalacia on the member's MRI represents an active spinal cord injury that cannot be rehabilitated by physical therapy. Consequently, all conservative treatment requirements (including standard physical therapy trials) are legally waived for this level. *The plan correctly approved this level.*

#### B. Application to C5-C6 (Radiculopathy)
While standard conservative therapy is required for radiculopathy under Plan Criteria Section 2.2, **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)** overrides these requirements if "the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau."
*   **Conservative Trial Documentation:** 
    *   **Physical Therapy:** The member completed **9 weeks (27 sessions)** of supervised physical therapy. This exceeds the plan’s standard 6-week requirement (Section 2.2(a)). The treating physical therapist documented that while myelopathic symptoms remained stable, "C5-C6 radicular symptoms persist," establishing a clinical functional plateau.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** The member completed a 4-month trial of Pregabalin 300mg BID (neuropathic agent) and a 3-month trial of Baclofen 10mg TID (muscle relaxant), satisfying Plan Section 2.2(b).
    *   **Interventional:** The member underwent a cervical epidural steroid injection on October 20, 2024, resulting in only "modest improvement in radicular symptoms," satisfying Plan Section 2.2(c).
*   **Statutory Conclusion:** The member completed an exhaustive, 9-week conservative treatment trial and reached a documented functional plateau. Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)**, the plan cannot legally mandate further conservative treatment or deny surgery for the C5-C6 level based on conservative trial failure.

#### C. Application to C3-C4 (Axial Neck Pain)
There is no objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (myelopathy or radiculopathy) localizing to the C3-C4 level. The member's symptoms at this level are limited to localized neck pain upon palpation. The physical therapy notes state that the "C3-C4 contribution [is] unclear." Therefore, no statutory overrides apply to waive or modify medical necessity criteria for this level.

### Step 4: Clinical Standards and Medical Necessity Validation (AANS/CNS & AO Spine Guidelines)

#### A. C5-C6 Level (Approved)
*   **Plan Criteria CC-SPINE-2024:** Section 2.1(a) requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with correlating symptoms and imaging. Section 2.2 requires a conservative trial. As documented in Step 3, the member has a correlating left C6 radiculopathy (disc herniation, C6 root compression, 4-/5 bicep weakness, diminished reflex) and has fully completed a 9-week conservative trial, reaching a functional plateau.
*   **Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023 & NASS 2020):** These guidelines classify persistent radiculopathy with correlating motor deficits (4-/5 bicep weakness) and failed conservative treatment of greater than 6 weeks as a **Tier 2 Strong Surgical Indication**. Delaying surgical decompression at this level in the presence of an active motor deficit risks permanent neurological dysfunction.
*   **Determination:** The medical necessity for decompression and fusion at C5-C6 is established. The initial denial of this level is **OVERTURNED**.

#### B. C3-C4 Level (Denied)
*   **Plan Criteria CC-SPINE-2024:** Section 3.1 explicitly states that "Authorization will be DENIED when... Surgery is requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy." Section 3.3 further excludes levels where imaging does not demonstrate pathology consistent with clinical presentation. The member's C3-C4 level shows only a mild disc bulge, minimal narrowing, and local palpation tenderness, with no clinical or electrical radicular/myelopathic signs.
*   **Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023 & NASS 2020):** Under these guidelines, surgical decompression and fusion for mild imaging findings with purely axial neck pain is classified as **Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated)**. Clinical evidence demonstrates that ACDF for axial pain alone has poor outcomes and is not a generally accepted standard of medical practice.
*   **Determination:** The request for the C3-C4 level does not meet plan criteria or clinical standards. The initial denial of this level is **UPHELD**.

## IV. FORMAL DETERMINATION

Pursuant to **California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32**, which mandates that health care services meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice shall be approved regardless of plan criteria, and in accordance with **California Insurance Code § 10169.5**, the initial determination of HealthFirst is **PARTIALLY OVERTURNED**.

1.  **ACDF at C4-C5:** **APPROVED** (Myelopathy with myelomalacia; Tier 1 indication; statutory conservative waiver applies).
2.  **ACDF at C5-C6:** **APPROVED** (Radiculopathy with correlating motor deficit; conservative treatment completed to a functional plateau under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2); Tier 2 clinical standard met).
3.  **ACDF at C3-C4:** **DENIED** (Axial neck pain only; fails Plan Section 3.1 and Tier 3 clinical guidelines; surgical intervention at this level is not medically necessary).

HealthFirst is directed to authorize a **two-level anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 and C5-C6** (CPT 22551 and appropriate additional-level codes) to be performed by Dr. James Torres, MD at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center.
