### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & FINAL DISPOSITION

Pursuant to California regulatory requirements and generally accepted clinical standards, a comprehensive clinical and administrative review of the medical necessity for a three-level ACDF has been conducted. 

#### FINAL DISPOSITION: PARTIAL OVERTURN
*   **APPROVED:** Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at **C4-C5 and C5-C6** (CPT 22551 and CPT 22552).
*   **UPHELD (DENIED):** Surgical intervention at **C3-C4**.

**Summary of Rationale:** 
1.  **C4-C5 (Previously Approved):** The plan’s initial approval of the C4-C5 level is confirmed. The member exhibits severe cervical myelopathy with corresponding severe spinal cord compression and myelomalacia (T2 signal change) on MRI, presenting a Tier 1 strong surgical indication.
2.  **C5-C6 (Overturned and Approved):** The plan’s denial of C5-C6 is overturned. The member has a documented moderate disc herniation with moderate left C6 nerve root compression and correlating left bicep weakness (4-/5) and diminished reflex. The member has fully completed a 9-week course of physical therapy, trialed two neuropathic/muscle-relaxant medications, and failed an epidural injection, thereby meeting all HealthFirst standard plan criteria. Furthermore, under California law (CIC § 10169.5) and professional guidelines, surgical decompression of this symptomatic adjacent level is clinically indicated.
3.  **C3-C4 (Upheld and Denied):** The plan's denial of C3-C4 is upheld. Imaging demonstrates only mild degenerative changes without cord or nerve root compression. Clinical exams and physical therapy records confirm the clinical contribution of this level is unclear, representing axial neck pain only. Under both plan criteria and national neurosurgical standards, fusing this level is not medically indicated and poses unnecessary surgical risk.

### I. SOURCE HIERARCHY (CONFLICT RESOLUTION FRAMEWORK)
In accordance with California regulatory requirements, this determination is resolved using the following strict hierarchy of authority:
1.  **State Law & Regulatory Mandates:** California Health & Safety Code (CHSC), California Insurance Code (CIC), and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letters. (Highest Authority)
2.  **Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:** National professional society guidelines (AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines 2023, AO Spine Clinical Practice Guidelines 2021, NASS Guidelines 2020).
3.  **Plan Coverage Criteria:** HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024). (Lowest Authority)

*Rule of Application:* Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), plan criteria cannot be applied mechanically if they are more restrictive than generally accepted medical standards or state statutory overrides. Where a conflict exists, the higher authority must be applied.

### II. EVALUATION STEP-BY-STEP

#### Step 1: Procedural Adequacy and Defect Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
The initial denial notice issued on November 25, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster was evaluated for statutory compliance under **California Health & Safety Code § 1374.31(a)**.
*   **Finding:** The denial notice stated: *"Review of imaging and clinical record supports surgical intervention at one level (C4-C5) only. C3-C4 and C5-C6 do not demonstrate pathology of sufficient severity..."* 
*   **Procedural Defect Identified:** The plan failed to cite the specific clinical coverage criteria applied (e.g., CC-SPINE-2024) and failed to provide a detailed, level-by-level clinical reason explaining the deficiency for the C5-C6 level, which actually met all conservative trials. Under **CHSC § 1374.31(b)**, a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied is legally defective. 
*   **Resolution:** While a procedural defect technically allows for an immediate administrative overturn of the timelines, this reviewer must execute a substantive clinical review of each level to ensure patient safety and prevent inappropriate surgical fusion at non-pathological levels (C3-C4).

#### Step 2: Clinical Diagnosis & Pathology Classification (Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy)
The clinical record was analyzed to classify the pathology at each requested level:
*   **C4-C5 (Myelopathy):** The MRI dated October 1, 2024, reveals a large disc herniation, severe cord compression, and T2-weighted cord signal change (**myelomalacia**). The physical exam on October 5, 2024, confirms severe upper motor neuron (UMN) signs: bilateral hyperreflexia, bilateral Hoffman sign, wide-based gait, and bilateral deltoid weakness (4/5). This is classified as **severe cervical myelopathy with existing structural cord injury**.
*   **C5-C6 (Radiculopathy):** MRI reveals moderate disc herniation with moderate left C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis. Physical exam reveals left bicep weakness (4-/5) and a diminished left bicep reflex. This is classified as **symptomatic C6 radiculopathy**.
*   **C3-C4 (Axial Pain/Mild Degeneration):** MRI shows a mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing with no cord compression. The physical exam notes "neck pain with C3-C4 palpation but no specific radicular pattern," and the physical therapy summary states "C3-C4 contribution unclear." This is classified as **isolated axial neck pain**.

#### Step 3: Evaluation of Plan Criteria Compliance (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)
The clinical records were assessed against Section 2 of HealthFirst's internal criteria:
*   **C5-C6 Compliance (MET):** 
    *   *Diagnosis (2.1a):* Confirmed cervical radiculopathy with correlating MRI and exam.
    *   *PT (2.2a):* Completed 9 weeks of physical therapy (27 sessions), exceeding the 6-week requirement.
    *   *Pharmacotherapy (2.2b):* Completed a 3-month trial of Baclofen and a 4-month trial of Pregabalin (two agents, both exceeding the 4-week requirement).
    *   *Interventional (2.2c):* Completed a cervical epidural steroid injection on October 20, 2024.
    *   *Conclusion:* **The plan's denial of C5-C6 directly violated its own CC-SPINE-2024 clinical criteria.** All conservative requirements were met, and the level is highly symptomatic.
*   **C3-C4 Compliance (UNMET):**
    *   *Exclusion (3.1):* Under Section 3.1, surgery is excluded if requested solely for axial neck pain. Because there is no documented radiculopathy or myelopathy at C3-C4, this level fails to meet plan criteria.

#### Step 4: Application of Statutory Overrides (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)
*   **C5-C6 Level:** Under **California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2)**, a plan cannot condition surgical approval on additional physical therapy if the member has completed a course of therapy and achieved a **functional plateau**. The therapist documented: *"9 weeks PT... C5-C6 radicular symptoms persist."* This constitutes a functional plateau, waiving any further conservative requirements. Additionally, under **DMHC APL 22-014**, because the adjacent C4-C5 level exhibits severe progressive myelopathy with myelomalacia, standard conservative timelines must be waived to prevent irreversible neurological harm.
*   **C3-C4 Level:** No statutory overrides apply. There is no objective structural neurological compromise, myelomalacia, or radiculopathy at C3-C4 that would warrant a waiver of standard clinical requirements.

#### Step 5: Alignment with Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (CHSC § 1374.32(b))
Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the Independent Medical Review (IMR) medical necessity standard must prioritize generally accepted guidelines over restrictive plan rules:
*   **C5-C6 Medical Necessity (MET):** Under **AANS/CNS 2023** and **AO Spine 2021** guidelines, when a patient requires surgical decompression for severe cervical myelopathy (C4-C5), adjacent levels with symptomatic pathology (C5-C6 radiculopathy) should be addressed simultaneously. Decompressing C5-C6 is medically necessary to resolve the active left C6 motor deficit (bicep weakness) and to prevent rapid adjacent segment degeneration post-fusion.
*   **C3-C4 Medical Necessity (UNMET):** Under **AANS/CNS 2023** guidelines, multi-level fusion at an asymptomatic level exhibiting only mild degenerative changes is classified as **Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated)**. Fusing C3-C4 is not clinically appropriate, does not treat the patient's myelopathic or radicular symptoms, and significantly increases the surgical risks of pseudarthrosis, dysphagia, and restriction of neck range of motion.

### III. DETERMINATION DETAILS BY SURGICAL LEVEL

#### 1. C4-C5 LEVEL: APPROVED (Plan's initial approval confirmed)
*   **Pathology:** Severe cord compression with T2 signal change (myelomalacia).
*   **Clinical Findings:** Bilateral hyperreflexia, bilateral Hoffman, wide-based gait, 4/5 deltoid weakness.
*   **Regulatory Support:** DMHC APL 22-014. Myelomalacia is a time-sensitive neurological emergency with high risk of permanent deficit. Delayed surgery is contraindicated.

#### 2. C5-C6 LEVEL: OVERTURNED (APPROVED)
*   **Pathology:** Moderate disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression.
*   **Clinical Findings:** Left bicep weakness (4-/5), diminished left bicep reflex.
*   **Conservative History:** Failed 9 weeks of PT (functional plateau reached per CIC § 10169.5), failed Baclofen (3 months) and Pregabalin (4 months), failed epidural steroid injection.
*   **Clinical Standards:** **AANS/CNS 2023** and **AO Spine 2021** support surgical decompression of symptomatic adjacent-level radiculopathy in the setting of primary myelopathy decompression. Delaying surgery for this level is medically inappropriate and inconsistent with standard neurosurgical practice.

#### 3. C3-C4 LEVEL: UPHELD (DENIED)
*   **Pathology:** Mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing, no cord compression, no myelomalacia.
*   **Clinical Findings:** Localized neck pain on palpation only, no radicular pattern, "C3-C4 contribution unclear."
*   **Clinical Standards:** Fusing a non-pathological, asymptomatic level is a **Tier 3 (Not Indicated)** procedure. Fusing C3-C4 solely because it is adjacent to a pathological level is clinical over-treatment and is denied to protect the member from unnecessary surgical morbidity.
