### **FINAL DETERMINATION: OVERTURN IN FULL**

Following a rigorous, step-by-step clinical and regulatory review of the medical records, imaging reports, physical examination findings, and California statutory provisions, the initial decision by HealthFirst to deny authorization for CPT Code 22551 (ACDF C4-C5) is **OVERTURNED**. 

The health plan is hereby **DIRECTED** to immediately authorize the requested surgical procedure. Delaying surgical decompression in this clinical presentation is contraindicated under generally accepted standards of care and is a direct violation of California regulatory mandates.

---

### **I. GOVERNING HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY**
In accordance with California regulatory frameworks, this determination has been reached by applying the following descending hierarchy of authority:
1. **California State Law and Regulatory Mandates** (California Insurance Code [CIC] § 10169.5; California Health & Safety Code [CHSC] § 1374.30–.32; DMHC All-Plan Letter [APL] 22-014).
2. **Generally Accepted Clinical Standards of Medical Practice** (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023; AO Spine Clinical Practice Guidelines 2021; NASS Guidelines 2020).
3. **Plan Clinical Coverage Criteria** (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024).

Under **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**, the standard of review for Independent Medical Review (IMR) requires alignment with generally accepted clinical standards and state law. The plan's internal coverage criteria are reference points but cannot serve as the sole or determinative basis for a denial when they are more restrictive than clinical standards or state statutory overrides.

### **II. STEP-by-STEP EVALUATION & FINDINGS**

#### **Step 1: Procedural Adequacy of Initial Denial**
The initial denial notice issued on November 20, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, cited the specific clinical policy applied (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024) and articulated a clear, albeit clinically flawed, rationale (interpreting the T2 signal change as an artifact and noting the lack of an interventional procedure). The notice included the required statutory disclosures. 
*   **Finding:** The denial is procedurally adequate under **CHSC § 1374.31(b)**. Therefore, this appeal is evaluated on its clinical and legal merits under Step 2.

#### **Step 2: Diagnostic Classification (Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy)**
The plan's denial incorrectly subjected this member to a standard conservative treatment algorithm typically reserved for *radiculopathy* (axial pain/nerve root compression).
*   **Neurological Examination Findings (Date: October 5, 2024):** 
    *   Bilateral upper extremity Hoffman's signs (objective Upper Motor Neuron [UMN] indicator).
    *   Bilateral hyperreflexia in the upper extremities.
    *   Subtle balance impairment on tandem gait (early gait ataxia).
*   **Clinical Determination:** The presence of bilateral UMN signs paired with objective balance impairment establishes a primary diagnosis of **cervical myelopathy** under both clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023) and the plan's own clinical coverage criteria (Section 2.1(b)). Subjecting a patient with myelopathy to the standard radiculopathy pathway constitutes a critical clinical diagnostic error. 

#### **Step 3: Imaging Analysis & Resolution of Clinical Dispute**
A conflict exists between the plan's reviewer and the treating institution's board-certified radiologist regarding the C4-C5 MRI performed on October 1, 2024:
*   *Original Interpretation (10/01/2024):* C4-C5 disc herniation with cord contact. Equivocal/possible early T2 signal change.
*   *Plan Radiologist Review (11/05/2024):* Claimed T2 signal change was "below threshold," "artifact," or "normal variant."
*   *Treating Hospital Second Read (10/15/2024 - Radiologist B):* Confirmed C4-C5 cord compression with **early myelomalacia**. Explicitly noted that the T2 hyperintensity is **reproducible on multiple sequences**, representing true early spinal cord signal change, not artifact.
*   **Resolution:** Under **DMHC APL 22-014**, where objective imaging demonstrates progressive or structural pathology verified by a treating specialist, the clinical dispute must be resolved in favor of the patient's active clinical protection. The reproducible T2 hyperintensity confirms **myelomalacia**—an objective sign of permanent, structural spinal cord injury.

#### **Step 4: California Statutory Overrides and Conservative Treatment Exceptions**
The plan denied the surgery because the member had not undergone a cervical interventional procedure (such as an epidural steroid injection) under Section 2.2(c) of CC-SPINE-2024. This requirement is legally and clinically superseded by California statutory provisions:

1.  **Contraindication and Harm (CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) & DMHC APL 22-014):** Dr. James Park, MD, documented that an epidural steroid injection was withheld because the patient has active spinal cord compression and myelomalacia. Epidural steroid injections introduce fluid volume into an already compromised spinal canal, creating a relative contraindication due to the risk of acute neurological deterioration. Forcing an interventional procedure under these circumstances is a direct violation of **DMHC APL 22-014**, which prohibits plans from demanding conservative therapies that pose a risk of irreversible harm.
2.  **Structural Neurological Compromise (CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)):** California law prohibits conditioning surgical approval on conservative therapy (including physical therapy or interventional trials) when there is "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Myelomalacia (intrinsic cord damage) is a structural lesion that physical therapy or injections cannot anatomically reverse.
3.  **Functional Plateau Achieved (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)):** The member completed a highly compliant course of physical therapy (8 weeks, 24 sessions). The physical therapist's discharge summary noted "no improvement in UMN signs." By completing 8 weeks of therapy with zero improvement in objective neurological signs, the member achieved a documented clinical **functional plateau**. 

Therefore, the plan's conservative interventional requirement is legally waived under California law.

#### **Step 5: Clinical Standard Medical Necessity Alignment**
Under **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**, the health plan must approve services that align with generally accepted standards of medical practice, even if the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive:
*   **AANS/CNS Guidelines (2023 Update):** Establish that moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia) is a **Tier 1 — Strong Surgical Indication**. Delaying surgical decompression to pursue conservative therapy is contraindicated, as prolonged cord compression is the single greatest predictor of permanent, irreversible neurological deficit.
*   **AO Spine Guidelines (2021):** Confirm there is no high-quality evidence supporting mandatory physical therapy or interventional injections prior to surgical intervention for patients with cervical myelopathy. Requiring these trials represents a "category error" that misapplies radiculopathy clinical data to spinal cord compression.
