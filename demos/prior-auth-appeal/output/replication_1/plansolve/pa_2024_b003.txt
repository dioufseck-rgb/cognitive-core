## II. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
This appeal reviews the medical necessity of a single-level C3-C4 ACDF for a 67-year-old female with severe adjacent segment disease, presenting as progressive left C4 cervical radiculopathy with documented motor deficit (3+/5 deltoid weakness) following a successful 2019 C4-C5 ACDF. 

The health plan denied the procedure on 2024-10-01, asserting that conservative treatment requirements were met but that magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) showed only "mild degenerative changes" insufficient to support surgical intervention. 

This review finds that the initial denial was **procedurally defective** and **clinically incorrect**. The initial reviewer failed to account for a severe magnetic susceptibility artifact on the cervical spine MRI caused by existing C4-C5 hardware, which obscured the C3-C4 level. Furthermore, the plan failed to evaluate the definitive diagnostic test in the clinical record—a **CT Myelogram** dated 2024-09-10—which resolved the artifact and confirmed severe left C4 nerve root compression and significant foraminal stenosis, directly correlating with the member’s clinical deficit. 

Because the member has fully completed and failed an extensive course of conservative therapy (10 weeks of physical therapy, multiple neuropathic drugs, and a targeted epidural steroid injection) and exhibits a progressive motor deficit, the requested procedure is medically necessary under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California State Law (CIC § 10169.5 and CHSC § 1374.32), and national clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023).

## III. CLINICAL-LEGAL EVALUATION STEPS

### STEP 1: Procedural Adequacy and Regulatory Compliance Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), a plan’s medical necessity denial must explicitly specify the clinical criteria applied, reference the specific policy guidelines, and detail the precise clinical reasoning. 

* **Evaluation:** The denial notice dated 2024-10-01 simply cited reason code "MED-003" and stated that "imaging does not show sufficient pathology." It failed to reference Plan Document **CC-SPINE-2024**, did not cite the specific section of the policy applied (such as Section 2.3), and failed to acknowledge or address the clinical-imaging conflict documented by the treating neurosurgeon (the hardware artifact).
* **Finding:** The initial denial notice is **procedurally defective** under CHSC § 1374.31(b). A defective notice fails to satisfy state-mandated administrative requirements. This procedural failure constitutes independent grounds to overturn or correct the plan's determination, as the member was denied a legally compliant explanation of benefits.

*Action: Proceed to clinical review to establish substantive criteria compliance.*

### STEP 2: Diagnostic Classification (Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy)
To determine the correct clinical pathway, the member's clinical presentation must be classified.
* **Clinical Presentation:** 
  * **Neurological Examination (2024-08-20):** Reveals left C4 dermatomal distribution sensory deficits (shoulder and lateral arm numbness), significant focal motor weakness (left deltoid weakness graded at 3+/5), and a diminished left biceps reflex. These symptoms are new since 2023. No upper motor neuron (UMN) signs (such as hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman's sign, clonus, gait instability, or bilateral symptoms) are documented.
  * **Imaging:** The CT myelogram confirms moderate-to-severe *left C4 nerve root compression* and significant *foraminal stenosis*. There is no objective evidence of spinal cord compression or myelomalacia (T2-weighted cord signal change) at the C3-C4 level.
* **Finding:** The member's primary pathology is classified as **Cervical Radiculopathy** with objective motor deficit, secondary to adjacent segment disease. 
* *Note: Because this is classified as cervical radiculopathy rather than cervical myelopathy, Step 3 (the Myelopathy statutory waiver under DMHC APL 22-014) is not directly triggered. The case is evaluated under the standard Cervical Radiculopathy criteria in Step 4.*

### STEP 4: Plan Criteria Compliance Evaluation (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)
The plan's standard clinical coverage criteria for cervical spinal surgery under Section 2 require that the member meet Diagnosis (2.1), Conservative Treatment (2.2), and Imaging (2.3) standards.

#### A. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1)
* **Policy Requirement:** Confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (nerve root compression with radicular symptoms such as pain, numbness, and weakness in a dermatomal distribution), documented by MRI or CT Myelogram within 6 months.
* **Clinical Evidence:** The neurological exam on 2024-08-20 confirmed left C4 dermatomal numbness and 3+/5 deltoid weakness. The CT Myelogram on 2024-09-10 confirmed moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression.
* **Finding:** **MET.** The diagnostic criteria of Section 2.1(a) are fully satisfied.

#### B. Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2)
* **Policy Requirement (a) — Physical Therapy:** Minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy with documented functional outcomes.
  * **Clinical Evidence:** The member completed **10 weeks (30 sessions)** of structured physical therapy. The physical therapy summary notes no improvement in her left C4 radicular symptoms.
  * **Finding:** **MET** (exceeded by 4 weeks).
* **Policy Requirement (b) — Pharmacotherapy:** Trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
  * **Clinical Evidence:** 
    1. *Pregabalin 300mg BID* for 6 months (partial relief).
    2. *Duloxetine 60mg* for 3 months (minimal benefit).
  * **Finding:** **MET** (two targeted neuropathic agents trialed and failed over multiple months).
* **Policy Requirement (c) — Interventional Treatment:** At least one cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point series.
  * **Clinical Evidence:** The member underwent a *left C4 epidural steroid injection* on 2024-09-25, which provided temporary relief (60%) but returned to baseline by week 3.
  * **Finding:** **MET.**

#### C. Imaging Requirement (Section 2.3)
* **Policy Requirement:** Current imaging within 6 months demonstrating structural pathology consistent with the symptoms and proposed surgical level.
* **Clinical Evidence:** 
  * The initial reviewer denied coverage because the MRI dated 2024-08-15 showed "mild degenerative changes." 
  * However, the MRI radiologist explicitly noted: *"SIGNIFICANT ARTIFACT from C4-C5 hardware limiting evaluation. Cannot exclude moderate compression due to artifact."*
  * Because the MRI was non-diagnostic due to metallic susceptibility artifact from the adjacent 2019 fusion hardware, Dr. Nguyen appropriately ordered a **CT Myelogram** on 2024-09-10. 
  * The CT Myelogram findings state: *"C3-C4: moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression confirmed. Significant foraminal stenosis. MRI underestimated pathology due to hardware artifact."*
* **Finding:** **MET.** The CT Myelogram constitutes a standard, validated diagnostic imaging modality under Section 2.1 and Section 2.3. It definitively demonstrates severe structural pathology at C3-C4 that correlates perfectly with the member's left C4 radiculopathy and deltoid weakness.

The initial reviewer's finding that imaging was insufficient is a clinical error resulting from the failure to review the CT Myelogram and the disregard of the documented MRI hardware artifact.

## IV. CLINICAL STANDARD & REGULATORY REVIEW (IMR STANDARD)

### 1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 Compliance
Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), a health insurer cannot mechanically condition the approval of surgery on the continued completion of physical therapy if the member has completed a course of physical therapy and has reached a **functional plateau** without adequate symptom resolution. 

The member completed 10 weeks (30 sessions) of physical therapy with documented failure to improve her left C4 radicular symptoms or reverse her 3+/5 deltoid motor deficit. By clinical standards, a motor deficit secondary to mechanical nerve root compression represents a functional plateau that physical therapy cannot resolve. Denying surgery to demand additional conservative care violates CIC § 10169.5.

### 2. Independent Medical Review (IMR) Standard — CHSC § 1374.32(b)
Under California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32(b), the standard for medical necessity during an appeal is whether the requested service meets **generally accepted standards of medical practice**, independent of the plan's restrictive internal criteria.

* **National Guidelines (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023 & NASS 2020):** Surgical decompression (ACDF) is a Tier 1/Tier 2 standard of care recommendation for patients with cervical radiculopathy who present with:
  1. Correlating objective imaging pathology (CT Myelogram showing moderate-severe C4 compression);
  2. Objective neurological deficits (3+/5 deltoid motor weakness); and
  3. Failure of a comprehensive multi-modal conservative trial (10 weeks of PT, 2 neuropathic medications, and a selective epidural injection).
* **Clinical Risk of Delay:** Prolonging conservative treatment in the presence of a significant, active motor deficit (3+/5 strength) risks permanent, irreversible axonal damage and long-term deltoid muscle wasting. Proceeding to surgical decompression is clinically urgent to preserve motor function.

## V. FORMAL DETERMINATION

Based on a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's coverage documents, and governing California statutes, the initial denial is **OVERTURNED** based on the following findings:

1. **Procedural Defect:** The plan's denial was procedurally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b) for failing to cite the specific clinical coverage policies or provide detailed clinical reasoning addressing the imaging discrepancy.
2. **Criteria Met:** The member fully satisfied all medical necessity criteria under HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024. She completed 10 weeks of PT (Section 2.2a), trialed two neuropathic agents (Section 2.2b), and failed a targeted epidural steroid injection (Section 2.2c).
3. **Imaging Error Overturned:** The initial reviewer’s finding of "insufficient pathology" was clinically flawed. The reviewer relied on an MRI that was explicitly labeled as non-diagnostic due to hardware artifact, while ignoring a definitive CT Myelogram (2024-09-10) that confirmed moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression.
4. **Standard of Care:** Under CHSC § 1374.32(b) and AANS/CNS guidelines, a single-level ACDF (CPT 22551) is the established, medically necessary standard of care to treat adjacent segment disease with progressive motor weakness.

HealthFirst shall immediately authorize the requested C3-C4 ACDF.
