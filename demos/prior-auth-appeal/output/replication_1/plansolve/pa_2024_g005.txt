### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

On November 8, 2024, HealthFirst Utilization Management issued a denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) requested by Dr. Amina Diallo on behalf of Mr. Gerald Obi. The denial was based on two grounds: 
1. **Ground 1:** A purported physical therapy (PT) shortfall, claiming the member only completed 5 of the 6 required weeks of conservative treatment.
2. **Ground 2:** Suboptimal glycemic control (HbA1c of 8.2%), which the plan asserted presented an unacceptable perioperative and wound-healing risk.

This clinical and regulatory appeal review has evaluated the denial against the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (**CC-SPINE-2024**), California statutory mandates (**California Insurance Code [CIC] § 10169.5** and **California Health & Safety Code [CHSC] § 1374.32**), and generally accepted clinical standards (**AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines**). 

The review finds that:
1. **The plan factually miscounted the member's physical therapy.** Objective clinical records demonstrate the member completed **7 weeks** (21 sessions) of PT, satisfying the plan's 6-week requirement. Furthermore, a functional plateau was documented at discharge.
2. **The plan applied extra-contractual criteria.** HealthFirst's approved clinical policy (CC-SPINE-2024) contains no glycemic or HbA1c thresholds for surgical authorization. Under California law, applying non-plan criteria to deny a clinically indicated procedure is invalid. 

Consequently, the initial denial is **OVERTURNED IN FULL**, and CPT 22551 is **AUTHORIZED**.

---

### I. PROCEDURAL ADEQUACY CHECK (Step 1)
Under **CHSC § 1374.31(b)**, a denial notice must explicitly cite the clinical criteria applied, provide specific clinical reasons for the denial, and disclose state Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights. 
* **Finding:** The initial denial notice dated November 8, 2024, complied with these baseline procedural standards by citing CC-SPINE-2024 and referencing the specific clinical reasons (PT shortfall and HbA1c level). 
* **Action:** Because the denial is procedurally adequate but substantively contested, we proceed to a clinical and statutory evaluation of the grounds for denial.

---

### II. DIAGNOSTIC CLASSIFICATION & PLAN CRITERIA EVALUATION (Step 2)

#### 1. Primary Diagnosis and Imaging Profile
* **Clinical Presentation:** Mr. Obi presents with progressive left-sided C7 radiculopathy causing functional impairment, including Left C7 dermatomal numbness, a diminished left tricep reflex, left tricep motor weakness (4/5), and diminished left grip strength (4+/5). The symptoms have forced him to work reduced hours.
* **Objective Imaging:** An MRI dated September 20, 2024, confirms a **C6-C7 large disc herniation with moderate left C7 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis**. There is no spinal cord contact and no myelomalacia.
* **Classification:** Because there is no spinal cord compression or upper motor neuron signs (e.g., Hoffman sign, hyperreflexia, gait disturbance), the primary diagnosis is **Cervical Radiculopathy (Section 2.1(a))**, not Cervical Myelopathy. The standard conservative treatment pathway under **Section 2.2** is active, requiring 6 weeks of physical therapy, 2 pharmacotherapy trials, and 1 interventional procedure.

#### 2. Evaluation of Conservative Treatment Requirements
The plan denied the request under Section 2.2(a), alleging that the member completed only 5 weeks of physical therapy. A review of the structured medical records reveals this assertion is **factually incorrect**:

| Conservative Care Type | Plan Requirement (CC-SPINE-2024) | Member's Actual Treatment | Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Physical Therapy** | Minimum 6 weeks | **7 weeks** (2024-07-01 through 2024-08-16), totaling 21 sessions. | **MET (Exceeded)** |
| **Pharmacotherapy** | $\ge 2$ agents for $\ge 4$ weeks each | (1) Pregabalin 150mg BID for 5 months;<br>(2) Duloxetine 60mg daily for 3 months. | **MET** |
| **Interventional** | Minimum 1 cervical epidural | Left C7 epidural injection on 2024-10-10 (45% temporary relief). | **MET** |

* **PT Detail:** The physical therapy discharge summary dated August 16, 2024, explicitly documents that Mr. Obi completed **7 weeks of therapy** and reached a **functional plateau** ("no further functional improvement expected").
* **Conclusion:** The member successfully completed all conservative treatment requirements of the standard pathway. Ground 1 of the denial is factually incorrect and clinically unsubstantiated.

---

### III. STATUTORY PHYSICAL THERAPY OVERRIDES (Step 3)
Under California law, even if the plan's 6-week requirement had not been fully met, the plan is legally prohibited from enforcing a rigid physical therapy timeline under the current clinical circumstances:

* **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2):** A health plan shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where *"the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."*
* **Application:** The physical therapy records explicitly document a functional plateau as of August 16, 2024, with persistent 4/5 tricep weakness and functional limitations. Forcing the member to undergo additional physical therapy when a professional therapist has certified that no further improvement is expected violates **CIC § 10169.5**.

---

### IV. VALIDITY OF NON-PLAN CRITERIA / DIABETES CONTROL (Step 4)
The plan's second ground for denial was Mr. Obi's HbA1c of 8.2%, citing perioperative and wound-healing risks.

1. **Absence in Plan Policy:** A rigorous review of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria **CC-SPINE-2024** shows that **there are no exclusion criteria, medical necessity thresholds, or limitations related to HbA1c levels, diabetes status, or glycemic control** for cervical spine surgery.
2. **California Regulatory Prohibition (CHSC § 1374.32(b)):** Under California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) standards, a health plan is legally required to evaluate medical necessity based on its officially approved, filed, and disclosed clinical criteria. Utilizing extra-contractual or "shadow" guidelines (such as an arbitrary HbA1c cutoff of <8.0% or <7.5%) to deny an otherwise clinically indicated procedure is **unlawful**.
3. **Clinical Standards vs. Prior Authorization:** Generally accepted clinical standards, including the **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023)** and **NASS Guidelines (2020)**, recognize that diabetes management is a critical preoperative consideration. However, both clinical guidelines and the treating surgeon, Dr. Diallo, clarify that perioperative optimization is the *clinical responsibility of the surgical and endocrinology teams*, not a gatekeeping prior authorization criterion. 
   * *Note:* Dr. Diallo has already documented that the patient's diabetes management will be optimized preoperatively, and the patient is followed quarterly by an endocrinologist (Dr. Patel) for this purpose. 

Therefore, Ground 2 of the denial is invalid under both plan policy and California law.

---

### V. CLINICAL STANDARDS VS. PLAN CRITERIA CONFLICT RESOLUTION (Step 5)
In this case, there is no direct conflict between the plan's written coverage criteria and generally accepted clinical standards, because the member **fully satisfied** the plan's written criteria under CC-SPINE-2024:
* He has a documented diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits (motor weakness and reflex loss) matching a C6-C7 herniation on MRI (Sections 2.1(a), 2.3).
* He completed 7 weeks of physical therapy (Section 2.2(a)), 2 qualifying pharmacotherapy trials (Section 2.2(b)), and a selective epidural steroid injection (Section 2.2(c)).

The conflict arose solely because the plan's utilization reviewer (1) factually miscalculated the physical therapy duration and (2) fabricated an extra-contractual barrier regarding diabetes control. Where such application conflicts with California law and generally accepted medical standards, the clinical standards and statutory mandates must override the reviewer's erroneous application.

---

### FINAL DISPOSITION

Pursuant to the California Health & Safety Code and the clinical evidence of record, the initial decision to deny coverage for CPT 22551 is **reversed**.

* **Authorized Facility:** Stanford Medical Center
* **Requesting Provider:** Dr. Amina Diallo, MD
* **Authorization Validity Period:** 90 Days from the date of this letter (per CC-SPINE-2024, Section 3.5)
