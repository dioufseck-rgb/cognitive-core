DISPOSITION: OVERTURNED (The prior authorization denial is reversed in full, and the requested revision ACDF C5-C6 under CPT code 22551 is APPROVED.)

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
George Harris, a 48-year-old male with a history of a C5-C6 ACDF performed in 2021, is appealing the health plan's October 30, 2024, denial of a prior authorization request for a revision ACDF at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). The initial denial was issued under reason code MED-003, stating that revision criteria require documentation of hardware failure, pseudarthrosis, or new pathology, and that the submitted documentation did not confirm hardware failure.

Upon comprehensive clinical and regulatory review of the complete medical record, the health plan's denial is OVERTURNED. The clinical documentation establishes that the patient has symptomatic, objective pseudarthrosis (failed fusion) at C5-C6 with associated right C6 radiculopathy and objective neurological deficits. The patient has fully satisfied all medical necessity criteria outlined in HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2. Furthermore, the plan's initial review procedurally erred by ignoring the diagnostic CT scan that confirmed pseudarthrosis, relying instead on a limited MRI. Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b) and California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5, the denial is defective and inconsistent with state regulatory mandates and generally accepted clinical standards.

II. CLINICAL HISTORY AND PRESENTING EVIDENCE
The patient underwent an initial C5-C6 ACDF in 2021 with an initially good outcome. However, symptoms recurred approximately 18 months postoperatively. The patient presented in late 2024 with a return of severe right C6 radicular symptoms.
1. Neurological Examination (Dated September 20, 2024): Documents objective right C6 radiculopathy, including right bicep weakness (graded 4/5), a diminished right bicep reflex, and C6 dermatomal numbness. These findings represent a clinical deterioration worse than the patient's preoperative baseline.
2. Diagnostic Imaging:
   - Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) (Dated September 1, 2024): Demonstrated hardware artifact that limited the assessment. The radiologist noted a "possible non-union at C5-C6 but cannot confirm" and that "residual disc material or recurrent herniation cannot be excluded."
   - Computed Tomography (CT) Scan (Dated September 15, 2024): Performed specifically to overcome the MRI hardware artifact. The CT scan demonstrated "incomplete bridging bone formation" and "lucency around hardware suggesting pseudarthrosis." The radiologist concluded that these findings are "consistent with failed fusion/pseudarthrosis," while noting that the "hardware appears intact without fracture."
3. Conservative Treatment History:
   - Physical Therapy: Completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy directed at the cervical spine. The treating physical therapist and physician noted no improvement and documented that physical therapy cannot address structural hardware or fusion failure.
   - Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 4-month trial of Naproxen 500mg BID (NSAID) and a 3-month trial of Gabapentin 300mg TID (neuropathic agent), both yielding only partial and inadequate pain relief.
   - Interventional Treatment: Received a cervical epidural steroid injection on October 10, 2024, which resulted in minimal improvement. The treating physician noted that this lack of response is expected in the presence of structural fusion failure.

III. ANALYSIS OF PLAN COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery — Cervical Procedures (Document: CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2) outlines the requirements for authorizing cervical spinal surgery (CPT 22551). The patient's clinical record satisfies ALL applicable criteria:

1. Section 2.1 — Diagnosis Requirement: Satisfied. The patient has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy under Section 2.1(a), characterized by nerve root compression with radicular symptoms (right bicep weakness 4/5, diminished bicep reflex, and C6 dermatomal numbness). The supporting documentation includes a CT scan dated September 15, 2024 (within the required 6-month window), demonstrating structural pathology (pseudarthrosis) consistent with these symptoms.
2. Section 2.2 — Conservative Treatment Requirement: Satisfied.
   - Section 2.2(a) (Physical Therapy): The patient completed 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy (24 sessions), exceeding the plan's minimum requirement of 6 weeks.
   - Section 2.2(b) (Pharmacotherapy): The patient completed trials of two qualifying agents for more than the required 4 weeks each: Naproxen (NSAID) for 4 months and Gabapentin (neuropathic agent) for 3 months.
   - Section 2.2(c) (Interventional Treatment): The patient underwent a cervical epidural steroid injection on October 10, 2024, satisfying the requirement of at least one interventional treatment.
3. Section 2.3 — Imaging Requirement: Satisfied. The CT scan dated September 15, 2024, is within 6 months of the request and demonstrates objective structural pathology (incomplete bridging bone and lucency around hardware consistent with pseudarthrosis) at the proposed surgical level (C5-C6) that correlates directly with the patient's right C6 radiculopathy.
4. Section 2.4 — Physician Documentation: Satisfied. Dr. Karen Williams, MD, provided the necessary clinical documentation, including an attestation of completed conservative treatment, the clinical rationale for revision surgery (failed fusion/pseudarthrosis with progressive neurological deficit), a description of functional limitations, and a discussion of surgical risks and alternatives.

IV. RESOLUTION OF CROSS-SOURCE CONFLICTS AND REGULATORY COMPLIANCE
The initial denial by Dr. Patricia Huang on October 30, 2024, cited reason code MED-003, stating: "Revision surgery not covered under standard ACDF criteria. Member had prior C5-C6 ACDF in 2021. Revision criteria require documentation of hardware failure, pseudarthrosis, or new pathology at previously treated level. Documentation submitted does not confirm hardware failure." This denial is clinically and procedurally invalid under California law and the plan's own guidelines:

1. Misapplication of Plan Criteria and Imaging Misinterpretation:
   The plan's denial text explicitly acknowledges that "pseudarthrosis" is an approved indication for revision surgery. However, the reviewer denied the request solely because the documentation "does not confirm hardware failure" (noting that the hardware was intact without fracture on the CT scan). This represents a critical logical and clinical error. Pseudarthrosis (failed bony fusion) and hardware failure (fracture or displacement of metal implants) are distinct clinical entities. The CT scan dated September 15, 2024, explicitly confirmed "incomplete bridging bone formation" and "lucency around hardware," which are the definitive diagnostic criteria for pseudarthrosis. By requiring hardware fracture in addition to pseudarthrosis, the plan misapplied its own criteria and ignored the objective findings of the CT scan.
2. Procedural Defect under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b):
   Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a health plan's denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the denial. The plan's reliance on the September 1, 2024, MRI report—which was explicitly noted by the radiologist to be limited by hardware artifact—while ignoring the definitive September 15, 2024, CT scan, constitutes a procedural defect. The plan failed to provide a valid clinical reason for ignoring the CT scan, which is the medically accepted gold standard for evaluating fusion status in the presence of spinal hardware.
3. Violation of California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Prerequisite):
   Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), a health plan cannot condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. The patient's pseudarthrosis and associated 4/5 bicep weakness represent a structural and neurological compromise. The treating physician and physical therapist documented that the patient reached a functional plateau because physical therapy cannot restore bony fusion. Mechanically denying the revision based on conservative treatment criteria or ignoring the structural nature of the failure violates CIC § 10169.5.
4. Application of the Independent Medical Review (IMR) Standard (CHSC § 1374.32):
   Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the standard for determining medical necessity on appeal is based on generally accepted standards of medical practice, not the plan's internal criteria. If a conflict exists, the clinical standard governs. Generally accepted clinical standards, as detailed below, dictate that symptomatic pseudarthrosis with objective neurological deficits requires surgical revision.

V. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND GENERALLY ACCEPTED STANDARDS OF MEDICAL PRACTICE
According to the American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS) and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (CNS) Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine Guidelines for Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy and Radiculopathy (2023 Update), as well as the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020):
1. Surgical Indications for Pseudarthrosis: Revision surgery (ACDF or posterior fusion) is the standard of care and strongly indicated (Tier 1/Tier 2 clinical indication) for patients who present with symptomatic pseudarthrosis, persistent radiculopathy, and objective neurological deficits (such as 4/5 motor weakness) that have failed to respond to conservative measures.
2. Inefficacy of Conservative Care for Structural Failure: The Cochrane Review (Nikolaidis et al., 2021) and AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021) establish that conservative treatments, including physical therapy and epidural injections, have no efficacy in promoting bony fusion or resolving radiculopathy caused by gross structural instability or pseudarthrosis. Prolonging conservative trials in a patient with progressive motor weakness (4/5 bicep strength) risks permanent, irreversible nerve root damage.
3. Imaging Modality Standards: In patients with prior spinal instrumentation, MRI is frequently non-diagnostic due to metal susceptibility artifacts. A thin-cut CT scan is the clinically accepted gold standard for evaluating suspected pseudarthrosis. The plan's failure to base its decision on the CT scan findings is a departure from standard radiological and neurosurgical practice.

VI. CONCLUSION
The patient, George Harris, is a 48-year-old male with objective, CT-confirmed C5-C6 pseudarthrosis and corresponding right C6 radiculopathy with motor weakness. He has completed and failed an exhaustive course of conservative therapy, including 8 weeks of physical therapy, multiple medications, and an epidural injection. He meets all plan criteria under CC-SPINE-2024 and satisfies the clinical standards of medical necessity under California law (CHSC § 1374.32 and CIC § 10169.5).

Accordingly, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED. The request for a revision ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is APPROVED.
