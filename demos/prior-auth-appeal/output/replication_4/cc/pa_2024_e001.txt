OVERALL DISPOSITION:
After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, plan coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial denial has been OVERTURNED IN PART.

1. CPT 22551 (ACDF, Single Level; C5-C6): OVERTURNED (Approved)
2. CPT 22552 (ACDF, Add-on Level; C6-C7): UPHELD (Denied)

This definitive determination allows you to proceed immediately with the authorized single-level C5-C6 ACDF (CPT 22551). Below is the detailed clinical and regulatory rationale for each level.

SECTION I: CLINICAL & REGULATORY RATIONALE FOR C5-C6 (CPT 22551) — APPROVED
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The denial of the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551) is overturned because the member fully meets all clinical coverage criteria under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 and California medical necessity standards:

1. Diagnosis and Imaging Requirements (CC-SPINE-2024, Sections 2.1 & 2.3):
   - The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits. 
   - A cervical MRI dated September 15, 2024, demonstrates a large disc herniation at C5-C6 causing moderate-to-severe right C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis.
   - A neurological examination dated September 20, 2024, documents objective deficits correlating precisely with the C5-C6 pathology: right bicep weakness (graded 3+/5), an absent right bicep reflex, and right C6 dermatomal numbness and tingling.

2. Conservative Treatment Requirements (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2):
   - Physical Therapy: The member completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy, exceeding the 6-week minimum required under Section 2.2(a). Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5, the physical therapy requirement is fully satisfied as the treating therapist documented that the member reached a functional plateau with persistent C6 symptoms.
   - Pharmacotherapy: The member completed a trial of two qualifying agents for at least 4 weeks each, satisfying Section 2.2(b) (Pregabalin 150mg BID for 4 months and Naproxen 500mg BID for 3 months).
   - Interventional Treatment: The member underwent a right C6 epidural steroid injection on October 15, 2024, which provided 70% temporary relief but symptoms fully returned within 4 weeks, satisfying Section 2.2(c).

Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, ACDF at C5-C6 is medically necessary, clinically appropriate, and aligned with generally accepted standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines, Tier 1 Strong Indication).

SECTION II: CLINICAL & REGULATORY RATIONALE FOR C6-C7 (CPT 22552) — DENIED
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The denial of the C6-C7 level (CPT 22552) is upheld because the clinical and imaging evidence does not support medical necessity at this level at this time:

1. Imaging and Clinical Discordance (CC-SPINE-2024, Sections 2.3 & 3.3):
   - The MRI dated September 15, 2024, shows only a small disc bulge and mild foraminal narrowing at C6-C7. There is no identifiable nerve root compression, cord contact, or spinal cord signal change (myelomalacia) at this level.
   - This fails Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement) and triggers the exclusion under Section 3.3, which mandates denial when imaging does not demonstrate pathology at the proposed surgical level consistent with the clinical presentation.

2. Insufficient Clinical Findings:
   - The neurological examination dated September 20, 2024, shows no definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms (no tricep weakness or middle finger sensory deficits). The right tricep reflex was only "slightly diminished."
   - Physical therapy records at week 8 note that C7-associated symptoms were minimal and possibly resolving, indicating a lack of persistent, severe radiculopathy at this level.
   - No C7-specific interventional treatment (such as a diagnostic selective C7 nerve block) has been performed to isolate C6-C7 as a symptomatic pain generator.

3. Resolution of Procedural and Administrative Constraints:
   - No "Remand" Category: Under California Health & Safety Code § 1367.01 and DMHC regulations, health plans must issue a definitive decision on appeals within 30 days. There is no legal category of "remand" for an internal health plan appeal. Any delay or failure to issue a definitive decision within the 30-day statutory timeline would result in a "DEEMED APPROVAL" of the entire request under CHSC § 1367.01 (Part IV.3), legally forcing the plan to pay for the medically unsupported C6-C7 level.
   - Clinical Workability of Add-on Codes: CPT 22552 is an add-on code that cannot be billed or performed independently of the primary code CPT 22551. Attempting to "remand" C6-C7 while approving C5-C6 is clinically and administratively unworkable, as a surgeon cannot perform a multi-level fusion if the second level is in an unresolved administrative status. 
   - Therefore, to satisfy the plan's statutory duty and allow the surgeon to proceed immediately with the authorized single-level C5-C6 ACDF, the plan is issuing a definitive denial (UPHOLD) for the C6-C7 level (CPT 22552).

This denial of C6-C7 (CPT 22552) is issued without prejudice. If the member's clinical presentation changes or symptoms progress, you may submit a new prior authorization request. The plan will review a future request for C6-C7 upon receipt of any of the following clinical evidence:
1. A diagnostic selective C7 nerve block demonstrating significant, temporary pain relief, confirming C6-C7 as a primary symptomatic level.
2. Progressive, documented C7 dermatomal symptoms (such as tricep weakness or middle finger numbness) that fail to respond to conservative management.
3. Updated cervical imaging demonstrating progressive structural pathology, foraminal stenosis, or nerve root compression at the C6-C7 level.

Because a portion of your request (C6-C7, CPT 22552) has been denied, you have the right to request an Independent Medical Review (IMR) through the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) under CHSC § 1374.30.

- The IMR process is an independent review conducted by non-affiliated, board-certified specialists.
- Under CHSC § 1374.32, the IMR reviewer is not bound by the plan's internal criteria and will determine medical necessity based on generally accepted clinical standards.
- For context, in 2023, the DMHC reviewed 847 cervical spine IMR cases. Of those, 612 cases (72.3%) resulted in the plan's denial being overturned, while 235 cases (27.7%) resulted in the plan's denial being upheld.
- To apply for an IMR, please complete the IMR Application Form available on the DMHC website (www.dmhc.ca.gov) or call the DMHC Help Center at 1-888-466-2219.
