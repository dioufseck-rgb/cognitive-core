Dear Ms. Park and Dr. Wilson,

The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the appeal submitted on November 10, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial for a two-level anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 and C6-C7 levels (CPT 22551 x2). 

After a comprehensive review of the clinical records, the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, we have issued a PARTIAL OVERTURN. We have APPROVED the surgical procedure for the C5-C6 level, but we have UPHELD the denial for the C6-C7 level.

Below is a detailed explanation of our clinical and regulatory findings.

---

### I. CLINICAL SUMMARY & PRESENTING PRESENTATION
Ms. Linda Park is a 45-year-old female with a presenting diagnosis of right-sided C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy and adjacent C6-C7 degenerative disc disease. 
- Clinical Exam (September 5, 2024): Documents right C6 dermatomal numbness and tingling, a diminished right bicep reflex, and right-sided grip strength of 4/5. There are no documented C7 dermatomal symptoms, no bilateral upper extremity findings, and no upper motor neuron signs (ruling out cervical myelopathy).
- Imaging (MRI dated September 1, 2024): Demonstrates a large disc herniation at C5-C6 with moderate-to-severe nerve root compression and moderate foraminal stenosis, which directly correlates with the patient's right C6 radicular symptoms. At C6-C7, the MRI shows only a mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing, with no cord contact or identifiable nerve root compression.
- Conservative Treatment: The clinical record documents that the member completed 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy (24 sessions across two providers), a 4-month trial of Gabapentin (600mg TID) with partial relief, a 6-week trial of Cyclobenzaprine with minimal benefit, and a right C6 epidural steroid injection on October 1, 2024, which provided 50% temporary relief before symptoms fully returned.

---

### II. ANALYSIS OF C5-C6 LEVEL (OVERTURNED / APPROVED)
The prior authorization denial for the C5-C6 level is OVERTURNED. This level meets all internal plan criteria and generally accepted clinical standards.

1. Plan Criteria Compliance (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2):
   - Section 2.1 (Diagnosis): The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits (diminished bicep reflex, 4/5 grip strength) and correlating MRI findings within 6 months.
   - Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment): The member completed 8 weeks of physical therapy (exceeding the 6-week requirement in Section 2.2(a)), trialed two formulary agents (Gabapentin and Cyclobenzaprine) for more than 4 weeks each (satisfying Section 2.2(b)), and underwent a right C6 epidural steroid injection (satisfying Section 2.2(c)).
   - Section 2.3 (Imaging): The MRI dated September 1, 2024, demonstrates severe structural pathology at C5-C6 correlating with the right C6 radicular symptoms.
2. Clinical Evidence Standard:
   - Under the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023) and NASS Guidelines (2020), the patient's presentation at C5-C6 represents a Tier 2 "Reasonable Indication" for surgery, as she has persistent radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits that failed to respond to an exhaustive course of conservative management.
3. Regulatory Compliance:
   - Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, a service is medically necessary if it is clinically appropriate and in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice. Denying the C5-C6 level, which fully meets clinical standards and plan criteria, violates this statutory standard.

---

### III. ANALYSIS OF C6-C7 LEVEL (UPHELD / DENIED)
The prior authorization denial for the C6-C7 level is UPHELD. This level does not meet plan criteria or generally accepted clinical standards of medical necessity.

1. Plan Criteria Non-Compliance (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2 & 3):
   - Section 2.1 & 2.3 (Diagnosis & Imaging): There are no documented clinical symptoms (pain, numbness, or weakness) in the C7 dermatomal distribution. The MRI dated September 1, 2024, shows only mild degenerative changes (mild disc bulge, minimal narrowing) without nerve root compression or cord contact.
   - Section 3.3 (Exclusion): The plan explicitly excludes surgical levels where "Imaging does not demonstrate pathology at the proposed surgical level consistent with clinical presentation." Because there is no correlating pathology or clinical deficit at C6-C7, this level is excluded.
2. Clinical Evidence Standard:
   - Under the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023), surgical intervention at C6-C7 is classified as Tier 3 "Surgery Not Indicated" due to the absence of correlating clinical symptoms and significant structural pathology.
   - Dr. Wilson's clinical rationale for fusing C6-C7 is prophylactic stabilization to prevent adjacent segment disease (ASD), noting a 25% 5-year incidence. However, under generally accepted standards of medical practice, prophylactic fusion of an asymptomatic level with only mild degenerative changes is not considered medically necessary. Fusing an asymptomatic level exposes the patient to unnecessary surgical risks without proven therapeutic benefit.
3. Regulatory Standard:
   - Under CHSC § 1374.32(a)(2), a service must be "clinically appropriate, in terms of type, frequency, extent, site, and duration" to be deemed medically necessary. Fusing the C6-C7 level is not clinically appropriate given the lack of symptoms and minimal imaging findings.

---

### IV. REGULATORY COMPLIANCE & PROCEDURAL DEFECTS
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), a health plan's denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reason for the denial. 

We acknowledge that the initial denial notice dated October 28, 2024, was procedurally defective because it failed to cite the specific clinical coverage criteria document (CC-SPINE-2024) applied during the review. Under California law, a defective denial notice is treated as if no denial was issued for the purposes of appeal timelines. While this procedural defect does not justify the approval of an medically unnecessary procedure (C6-C7), it has been corrected in this determination, and the medically necessary portion of the procedure (C5-C6) has been approved.

---

### V. INDEPENDENT MEDICAL REVIEW (IMR) RIGHTS
Because a portion of your requested service (the C6-C7 level) has been denied, you have the right to request an Independent Medical Review (IMR) through the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC).

- What is an IMR? An IMR is an independent review of your case conducted by expert doctors who are not associated with HealthFirst. If the IMR decides in your favor, HealthFirst must authorize the service.
- How to Apply: You may submit an application for IMR to the DMHC. The right to an IMR is in addition to any other grievance process. You do not need to exhaust further internal appeals before requesting an IMR.
- Standard of Review: Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the independent medical reviewer will determine medical necessity based on generally accepted standards of medical practice, and is not bound by HealthFirst's internal coverage criteria.
- Fee: There is no charge to you for an IMR.
