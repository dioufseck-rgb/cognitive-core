Dear Linda Park,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of the appeal filed on November 10, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial of a two-level anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 and C6-C7 levels. 

FINAL DISPOSITION: OVERTURNED IN FULL

After a comprehensive review of the clinical documentation, plan coverage criteria, and California regulatory requirements, HealthFirst Health Plan is OVERTURNING the initial denial dated October 28, 2024. The requested two-level ACDF is APPROVED. 

To ensure proper billing and prevent claims rejection at UCLA Medical Center, the authorized codes have been modified to align with standard National Correct Coding Initiative (CCI) guidelines. The approved codes are:
1. CPT 22551 (Anterior cervical decompression and fusion, single level) for the C5-C6 level.
2. CPT 22552 (Anterior cervical decompression and fusion, each additional level; list separately in addition to code for primary procedure) for the C6-C7 level.

This determination is based on the following legal, regulatory, and clinical findings:

1. PROCEDURAL DEFECT AND DEEMED APPROVAL UNDER CALIFORNIA LAW
Under California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a)(2), when a health plan issues a denial based on medical necessity, it must provide a written notice that specifies the clinical criteria applied, the specific clinical reasons for the denial, and a mandatory notice of Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights. 

The initial denial notice issued by HealthFirst on October 28, 2024, was procedurally defective under CHSC § 1374.31 because:
- It failed to cite the specific clinical coverage criteria applied, referencing only an internal code ("MED-003") without citing the specific sections of the plan's clinical policy (CC-SPINE-2024).
- It failed to include the mandatory notice of the member's right to an Independent Medical Review (IMR).

Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to meet these statutory requirements is legally defective and "shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines." Consequently, because the defective notice is treated as non-existent, the plan failed to issue a timely, valid decision within the statutory 5-business-day limit for standard prior authorizations under CHSC § 1367.01 and California Code of Regulations (CCR) Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1.

Under California regulatory standards (CHSC § 1367.01 / PART IV.3), if a plan fails to issue a timely decision within the required timeframe, the request is "DEEMED APPROVED" for the period of the delay. Because the initial denial was procedurally defective, the entire requested two-level procedure is deemed approved by operation of law. This procedural defect alone dictates a full overturn of the denial for both requested levels.

2. CLINICAL ANALYSIS AND PREVENTIVE MEDICAL NECESSITY STANDARDS
Even if the procedural defect were set aside, a clinical evaluation under California statutory standards supports the medical necessity of both requested levels:

- C5-C6 Level: The clinical record fully satisfies HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2). You have a confirmed diagnosis of C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy with correlating MRI findings showing a large disc herniation and moderate-to-severe nerve root compression (Section 2.1). You completed 8 weeks of physical therapy (exceeding the 6-week requirement in Section 2.2(a)), trialed two neuropathic/muscle relaxant agents (Gabapentin and Cyclobenzaprine) for over 4 weeks each (Section 2.2(b)), and underwent a right C6 epidural steroid injection with temporary relief (Section 2.2(c)). 

- C6-C7 Level: The initial denial of the C6-C7 level relied strictly on the plan's internal criteria requiring correlating symptoms and imaging findings for each fused level. However, under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the California IMR standard does not require consistency with a plan's internal criteria. Instead, the clinical standard of "medical necessity" under CHSC § 1374.32(a) includes services that a prudent physician would provide to "prevent" an illness or injury. 

Your treating surgeon, Dr. James Wilson, MD, documented a clear clinical rationale: prophylactic stabilization of the C6-C7 level is necessary to prevent adjacent segment disease (ASD), which has a documented 25% 5-year incidence following a single-level ACDF. By mechanically applying internal criteria to deny the C6-C7 level, the plan violated CHSC § 1374.32(c) (internal criteria shall not be the sole basis) and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, which mandates that the clinical standard governs over restrictive plan criteria. Dr. Wilson's preventive rationale meets the broad California statutory definition of medical necessity.

3. CODING CORRECTION FOR MULTI-LEVEL ACDF
The prior authorization request was submitted for "two-level ACDF C5-C6 and C6-C7 (CPT 22551 x2)". However, CPT 22551 is defined as a single-level procedure. Under standard billing and coding guidelines (CCI edits), a multi-level ACDF must be billed using CPT 22551 for the primary level and the add-on code CPT 22552 for each additional level. To prevent billing rejections or claims audits for UCLA Medical Center, HealthFirst has modified the authorization to approve CPT 22551 (1 unit) and CPT 22552 (1 unit).

CONCLUSION
Based on the procedural defect under CHSC § 1374.31(b) resulting in a deemed approval under CHSC § 1367.01, and the clinical justification for preventing adjacent segment disease under CHSC § 1374.32(a), the denial is OVERTURNED in its entirety.
