I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The Appeals and Grievances Division of HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of the first-level internal appeal filed on behalf of Helen Brooks regarding the prior authorization denial for a one-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 (CPT 22551). The initial denial issued on November 20, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, is hereby OVERTURNED. 

A final, binary determination of OVERTURN is issued. Under California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1367.01 and § 1374.31, the health plan's internal appeals division must issue a final, binary determination (Overturn or Uphold). A plan cannot "remand" its own appeal to the state-run Independent Medical Review (IMR) process; doing so would constitute a procedural failure to issue a timely decision, resulting in an automatic "deemed approved" status under CHSC § 1367.01 and California Code of Regulations (CCR) Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1.

Furthermore, delaying surgical authorization for this patient violates Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, which classifies cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia as an urgent condition with potential for irreversible neurological harm if untreated. Immediate surgical authorization is hereby granted to comply with state regulatory mandates and prevent progressive, irreversible spinal cord injury.

II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND RECORD REVIEW
The clinical record establishes the following objective findings:
1. Clinical Presentation: The patient is a 49-year-old female presenting with early cervical myelopathy. A comprehensive neurological examination on October 5, 2024, documented significant upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia of the upper extremities, and subtle balance impairment on tandem gait.
2. Imaging Findings: 
   - An MRI of the cervical spine dated October 1, 2024 (interpreted by Radiologist A), demonstrated a C4-C5 disc herniation with cord contact and noted a "possible early T2 signal change — equivocal, may represent artifact. Clinical correlation required."
   - A second read of the same MRI on October 15, 2024, by Radiologist B at the treating hospital (UCSF Medical Center), confirmed "C4-C5 cord compression with early myelomalacia." Radiologist B explicitly documented that the "T2 hyperintensity is reproducible on multiple sequences and represents early cord signal change, not artifact," which is highly consistent with early myelopathy.
   - On November 5, 2024, the plan's imaging reviewer (Dr. Alan Foster, MD) disputed these findings, asserting that the T2 signal change was "below threshold for clinical significance" and represented "artifact or normal variant."
3. Conservative Treatment:
   - Physical Therapy: The patient completed 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy (24 sessions) directed at her cervical spine, with documented failure to improve her upper motor neuron signs.
   - Pharmacotherapy: The patient completed a 3-month trial of Gabapentin (400mg TID) with partial benefit, and a 6-week trial of Cyclobenzaprine with minimal response.
   - Interventional Treatment: No interventional procedures (such as epidural steroid injections) were performed. Dr. James Park, MD (Neurosurgery), explicitly documented that a referral for an epidural steroid injection was withheld because epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated in the presence of severe spinal cord compression due to the risk of acute neurological deterioration.

III. RESOLUTION OF RADIOLOGICAL CONFLICT UNDER CALIFORNIA LAW AND CLINICAL STANDARDS
The initial denial was based on the plan radiologist's assertion that the T2 signal change was an artifact and below the threshold of clinical significance. To reach a final binary determination, this conflict must be resolved using the established regulatory hierarchy: California State Law > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Plan's Internal Criteria.

1. Standard of Review under California Law (CHSC § 1374.32):
Under CHSC § 1374.32(a), medical necessity must be determined in accordance with "generally accepted standards of medical practice." Generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023; NASS Guidelines 2020; AO Spine Guidelines 2021) dictate that when a qualified radiologist at a treating academic medical center (UCSF) identifies reproducible T2 hyperintensity on multiple sequences representing early myelomalacia, and this finding is clinically correlated with objective upper motor neuron signs (bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, and gait impairment), the diagnosis of cervical myelopathy is established. A remote plan reviewer's unilateral re-interpretation of the imaging as "artifact" to deny surgery is inconsistent with prudent clinical judgment and generally accepted medical standards. Therefore, the treating hospital's radiological interpretation controls.

2. Urgency and Risk of Delay (DMHC APL 22-014):
DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Section II.1) mandates that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans are prohibited from applying restrictive internal criteria that delay treatment for such time-sensitive neurological conditions. Delaying surgery to resolve a radiological dispute or to enforce standard conservative pathways violates APL 22-014 and exposes the patient to progressive, irreversible spinal cord injury (e.g., worsening gait or hand weakness).

3. Statutory Override of Conservative Treatment (CIC § 10169.5):
Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3), a health plan is prohibited from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy when the patient's condition involves "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The patient's MRI demonstrates objective structural spinal cord compression and myelomalacia, which are structural lesions that physical therapy cannot mechanically resolve. Insisting on further conservative therapy in the face of active spinal cord injury violates CIC § 10169.5, rendering any further physical therapy requirements legally moot.

IV. COMPLIANCE WITH PLAN CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
Even under the plan's own internal clinical coverage guidelines (CC-SPINE-2024), the patient fully satisfies the criteria for surgical authorization under Section 2.2(A) — Myelopathy Pathway (Myelopathy Exception):
1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1(b)): The patient has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, supported by objective clinical findings (bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia, and tandem gait impairment) and current MRI imaging demonstrating C4-C5 cord compression and myelomalacia.
2. Modified Conservative Treatment (Section 2.2(A)):
   - Physical Therapy: The Myelopathy Exception reduces the physical therapy requirement to a minimum of 4 weeks. The patient completed 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy (exceeding the requirement) and reached a functional plateau with no improvement in her upper motor neuron signs.
   - Pharmacotherapy: The patient completed trials of two qualifying agents (Gabapentin for 3 months and Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks), satisfying Section 2.2(b).
   - Interventional Treatment Waiver: Section 2.2(A) explicitly states that the interventional treatment requirement (2.2(c)) is WAIVED if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration. Dr. James Park, MD, documented that epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated due to the patient's spinal cord compression and the risk of acute neurological decline. The interventional requirement is therefore fully waived.

Because the patient satisfies all elements of the Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A), the plan's initial denial was an arbitrary misapplication of its own clinical guidelines, constituting a bad-faith denial of benefits.

V. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION
Applying the regulatory hierarchy and clinical standards, the radiological conflict is resolved in favor of the treating hospital's radiologist and neurosurgeon. The patient has confirmed cervical myelopathy with early myelomalacia and correlating upper motor neuron signs, representing an urgent neurological condition under DMHC APL 22-014. The patient has met all applicable plan criteria under the CC-SPINE-2024 Myelopathy Exception, and further conservative treatment is legally barred from being used as a barrier to approval under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3).

The prior authorization denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C4-C5, one level) is OVERTURNED. Immediate approval is issued.
