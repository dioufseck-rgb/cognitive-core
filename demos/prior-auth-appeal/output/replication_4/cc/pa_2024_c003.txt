Dear Helen Brooks,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its first-level internal appeal review of the prior authorization denial for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at the C4-C5 level (CPT 22551). 

After a comprehensive evaluation of your clinical records, the plan's coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has determined that a direct conflict exists between qualified medical specialists regarding the interpretation of your diagnostic imaging. Under California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, the plan cannot solely rely on its own internal reviewer's interpretation to deny coverage when such a conflict exists. Therefore, a binary decision to uphold or overturn the denial cannot be made at this level. 

HealthFirst is formally REMANDING this case for an Independent Medical Review (IMR) administered by the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) pursuant to CHSC § 1374.30. This action ensures that an independent, board-certified specialist will review the disputed MRI sequences and make a binding determination based on generally accepted standards of medical practice.

Below is a detailed analysis of the clinical, regulatory, and criteria-based factors that necessitated this remand.

---

I. CLINICAL BACKGROUND AND PRESENTING DIAGNOSIS
The clinical record indicates that you are a 49-year-old female presenting with symptoms and signs consistent with early cervical myelopathy. 
1. Neurological Examination (Dated 2024-10-05): Documented objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia of the upper extremities, and subtle balance impairment on tandem gait. No lower extremity findings were noted.
2. Conservative Treatment History:
   - Physical Therapy: Completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy directed at the cervical spine. The physical therapy summary notes a "functional plateau" with no improvement in your objective UMN signs.
   - Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 3-month trial of Gabapentin (400mg TID) with only partial benefit, and a 6-week trial of Cyclobenzaprine with minimal response.
   - Interventional Treatment: Not performed. Dr. James Park, MD, documented that a cervical epidural steroid injection was not performed due to clinical concerns regarding cord compression, as epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated in patients with active spinal cord compression and myelopathy.
3. Diagnostic Imaging (MRI dated 2024-10-01):
   - Original Read (Radiologist A, 2024-10-01): C4-C5 disc herniation with cord contact. Possible early T2 signal change — equivocal, may represent artifact. Clinical correlation required.
   - Second Read (Radiologist B, UCSF Medical Center, 2024-10-15): C4-C5 cord compression with early myelomalacia. T2 hyperintensity is reproducible on multiple sequences and represents early cord signal change, not artifact. Consistent with early myelopathy.
   - Plan Radiologist Review (Plan Radiologist, 2024-11-05): T2 signal change is below threshold for clinical significance. Not consistent with myelomalacia. Represents artifact or normal variant.

II. ANALYSIS OF PLAN COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2, prior authorization for CPT 22551 requires satisfaction of several criteria:
1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): Requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (2.1(b)), defined as spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms (gait disturbance, bilateral upper extremity weakness, hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign). Your clinical exam on 2024-10-05 confirms bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, and balance impairment, satisfying the clinical symptom criteria.
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): Standard criteria require 6 weeks of physical therapy, a trial of two medications for 4 weeks each, and at least one interventional treatment (e.g., epidural steroid injection).
   - Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway): If cervical myelopathy is the primary diagnosis, the physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks, and the interventional treatment requirement (2.2(c)) is WAIVED if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration.
   - Application: Your treating physician, Dr. Park, documented that an epidural steroid injection was withheld due to the risk of neurological deterioration associated with cord compression. If your myelopathy diagnosis is confirmed, you satisfy Exception 2.2(A) because you completed 8 weeks of PT, met the pharmacotherapy requirement (Gabapentin and Cyclobenzaprine), and had the interventional requirement appropriately waived.
3. Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement): Requires current imaging demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms, interpreted by a radiologist or treating specialist.
4. The Conflict: The plan's initial denial on 2024-11-20 by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, was based on the Plan Radiologist's review (2024-11-05), which concluded that the T2 signal change was an artifact and did not represent true myelomalacia. Consequently, the plan refused to apply the Myelopathy Pathway Exception 2.2(A) and denied the request under the standard pathway due to the lack of an interventional procedure (Section 3.2).

III. ANALYSIS OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK
California law regulates how health plans must evaluate prior authorization requests and appeals, overriding internal plan criteria when conflicts arise:
1. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 (Standard of Review for IMR):
   - Under subdivision (a), medical necessity must be determined in accordance with "generally accepted standards of medical practice" and clinical appropriateness.
   - Subdivision (b) explicitly states that the IMR standard does NOT require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the service meets generally accepted standards, it must be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.
2. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Prior Authorization for Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions):
   - Section II.1: Cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm. Plans must treat these cases as urgent.
   - Section II.2: Plans shall NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as cord signal change/myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents urgency.
   - Section II.3: Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice.
3. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3):
   - A health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Your documented upper motor neuron signs represent structural neurological compromise.
4. CHSC § 1374.31(b) (Defective Denial Notice):
   - The plan's initial denial notice applied standard pathway criteria (requiring an injection) without legally reconciling the treating hospital's radiologist's read of myelomalacia or the treating physician's documented contraindication.

---

IV. ANALYSIS OF CLINICAL EVIDENCE BASE
Generally accepted clinical standards do not support the plan's mechanical denial of surgery for a patient with clinical myelopathy and suspected myelomalacia:
1. AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) & AO Spine Guidelines (2021):
   - Decompression surgery (ACDF) is a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) recommendation for patients with moderate-to-severe myelopathy or those with objective myelomalacia (T2 cord signal change) on MRI.
   - There is no high-quality clinical evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy or interventional injections prior to surgical intervention for patients with cervical myelopathy and cord compression.
   - Delaying surgery to complete conservative trials in the presence of myelomalacia is associated with a high risk of irreversible neurological deterioration (AO Spine Guidelines, 2021).
2. Cochrane Review (2021) & NASS Guidelines (2020):
   - Physical therapy has low-to-moderate efficacy for mild myelopathy and does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement in patients with established upper motor neuron signs.
   - Epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated in patients with severe cord compression due to the risk of acute spinal cord injury or hematoma, which could cause permanent paralysis.

---

V. RESOLUTION OF CROSS-SOURCE CONFLICTS & JUSTIFICATION FOR REMAND
A profound conflict exists between three qualified radiologists regarding the same MRI scan:
- Radiologist A (Original): "Possible early T2 signal change — equivocal, may represent artifact."
- Radiologist B (UCSF Medical Center): "T2 hyperintensity is reproducible on multiple sequences and represents early cord signal change, not artifact. Consistent with early myelopathy."
- Plan Radiologist: "T2 signal change is below threshold... Represents artifact or normal variant."

Under CHSC § 1374.32, the plan's internal medical necessity criteria cannot be the sole or determinative basis for resolving this conflict. The plan cannot simply default to its own reviewer's interpretation to deny a time-sensitive surgical procedure when a major academic medical center's radiologist (UCSF) has identified active myelomalacia. 

Because the resolution of this radiological conflict requires independent clinical arbitration by an unbiased third-party specialist, a binary "Uphold" or "Overturn" decision by the plan's internal appeal committee is inappropriate. 

Therefore, this case is REMANDED for Independent Medical Review (IMR) under CHSC § 1374.30.
