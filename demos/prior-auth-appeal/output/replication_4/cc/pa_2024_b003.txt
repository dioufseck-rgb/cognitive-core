Dear Ms. Morrison and Dr. Nguyen,

The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the prior authorization appeal for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at the C3-C4 level (CPT 22551). Based on a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial decision to deny coverage is hereby OVERTURNED. 

The requested procedure is approved. Below is the detailed clinical and legal rationale for this determination.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND HISTORY
Ms. Angela Morrison is a 67-year-old female with a history of a successful C4-C5 ACDF performed in 2019. She presented with a new onset (since 2023) of progressive left-sided cervical radiculopathy. A neurological examination on August 20, 2024, documented objective neurological deficits in the left C4 dermatomal distribution, including shoulder and lateral arm numbness, deltoid weakness graded at 3+/5, and a diminished left bicep reflex. 

An MRI of the cervical spine performed on August 15, 2024, demonstrated mild degenerative signal changes at C3-C4 but was explicitly noted by the radiologist to have "SIGNIFICANT ARTIFACT from C4-C5 hardware limiting evaluation," stating that moderate compression could not be excluded. Due to this diagnostic limitation, a CT myelogram was performed on September 10, 2024. The CT myelogram confirmed moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression and significant foraminal stenosis at C3-C4, noting that the prior MRI had underestimated the pathology due to hardware artifact.

Ms. Morrison completed an extensive course of conservative management, including:
1. Physical Therapy: 10 weeks (30 sessions) of supervised physical therapy with no improvement in her left C4 radicular symptoms.
2. Pharmacotherapy: Pregabalin 300mg BID for 6 months (partial relief) and Duloxetine 60mg for 3 months (minimal benefit).
3. Interventional Treatment: A left C4 epidural steroid injection on September 25, 2024, which provided 60% temporary relief but symptoms returned to baseline by week 3.

II. PLAN CRITERIA COMPLIANCE ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)
The initial denial on October 1, 2024, cited reason code MED-003, stating that while conservative treatment requirements were met, imaging did not show sufficient pathology at C3-C4 to support surgical intervention based on the MRI. This analysis was clinically and procedurally flawed under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024:

1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): Ms. Morrison has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy, characterized by left C4 nerve root compression with corresponding radicular symptoms (shoulder/arm numbness, deltoid weakness, diminished reflex). Section 2.1 explicitly accepts either an "MRI or CT myelogram within 6 months of request demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms." The CT myelogram dated September 10, 2024, is within the 6-month window and confirms moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression. This criterion is fully satisfied.
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): The initial reviewer acknowledged that conservative treatment requirements were met. Ms. Morrison completed 10 weeks of physical therapy (exceeding the 6-week minimum in Section 2.2(a)), trialed two neuropathic agents (Pregabalin and Duloxetine) for more than 4 weeks each (satisfying Section 2.2(b)), and underwent a left C4 epidural steroid injection (satisfying Section 2.2(c)). This criterion is fully satisfied.
3. Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement): Current imaging must demonstrate structural pathology consistent with symptoms and the proposed surgical level. The CT myelogram (September 10, 2024) demonstrates moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression and significant foraminal stenosis at C3-C4, which perfectly correlates with the left C4 radiculopathy documented on her neurological exam. The initial reviewer's reliance on the August 15, 2024 MRI—which was explicitly documented as compromised by hardware artifact—to deny coverage represents a misapplication of Section 2.3, as the plan criteria permit the use of a CT myelogram. This criterion is fully satisfied.
4. Section 2.4 (Physician Documentation): Dr. Nguyen provided the necessary clinical rationale, description of functional limitations, and surgical risk discussion. This criterion is fully satisfied.

III. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY COMPLIANCE ANALYSIS
Under California law, which supersedes any conflicting or restrictive health plan criteria, the initial denial is unsustainable:

1. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b) (Defective Denial Notice): A denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reason for the denial. The denial notice dated October 1, 2024, failed to cite the specific clinical coverage criteria document (CC-SPINE-2024) and completely ignored the diagnostic findings of the CT myelogram. Under § 1374.31(b), this omission renders the denial notice procedurally defective.
2. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Prerequisite): Under § 10169.5(a)(2), a health plan cannot condition approval of a surgical procedure on physical therapy if the member has completed a course of physical therapy and reached a functional plateau. Ms. Morrison completed 10 weeks (30 sessions) of physical therapy with no improvement, establishing a clear functional plateau under § 10169.5(b). Furthermore, under § 10169.5(a)(3), physical therapy cannot be mandated when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (moderate-to-severe nerve root compression and deltoid weakness of 3+/5) that physical therapy cannot address.
3. CHSC § 1374.32 (Standard of Review for Independent Medical Review): Under California's IMR standard, "medically necessary" means services that a provider, exercising prudent clinical judgment, would provide to a patient in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice and clinically appropriate in terms of type, site, and duration. Section 1374.32(b) explicitly states that the IMR standard does NOT require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria. A prudent neurosurgeon would rely on a CT myelogram when an MRI is compromised by hardware artifact. Denying surgery based on an artifact-compromised MRI while ignoring a definitive CT myelogram violates this clinical standard.

IV. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND GUIDELINES COMPLIANCE
According to generally accepted clinical standards, including the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020) and the American Association of Neurological Surgeons / Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Joint Section Guidelines (2023):
1. Diagnostic Imaging in the Presence of Hardware: When a prior fusion is present, metallic hardware frequently creates significant magnetic susceptibility artifact on MRI, rendering it unreliable for evaluating adjacent segment pathology. In such cases, a CT myelogram is the gold-standard diagnostic study to evaluate nerve root and spinal cord compression. Relying on a compromised MRI over a definitive CT myelogram is contrary to standard radiological and neurosurgical practice.
2. Surgical Indications for Radiculopathy (Tier 2): Under AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Section 3.1, surgery is indicated (Tier 2 - Reasonable Indication) for cervical radiculopathy with persistent symptoms and objective neurological deficits (such as Ms. Morrison's 3+/5 deltoid weakness and sensory loss) that have failed to respond to 6–12 weeks of conservative care. Ms. Morrison's clinical presentation meets these criteria.

V. RESOLUTION OF CROSS-SOURCE CONFLICTS
To the extent that any conflict exists between the plan's internal interpretation of CC-SPINE-2024 and the clinical record, the conflict is resolved using the established hierarchy of authority: California State Law > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Plan Criteria.
- The initial reviewer's decision to ignore the CT myelogram in favor of an MRI with documented hardware artifact directly conflicts with California's medical necessity standard (CHSC § 1374.32) and standard clinical practice.
- Under California law, the CT myelogram must govern the imaging assessment because the MRI was explicitly noted to be non-diagnostic at the target level due to artifact.
- The clinical record demonstrates that Ms. Morrison meets all criteria under every level of the hierarchy.

VI. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION
Because Ms. Morrison has documented cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits, has failed an exhaustive course of conservative therapy, and has a CT myelogram confirming moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression at C3-C4, the requested ACDF is medically necessary and meets all plan and regulatory criteria.

The prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED.
