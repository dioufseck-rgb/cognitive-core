Dear Mr. Santos and Dr. Wong,

The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Division has completed its de novo review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a one-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT code 22551) at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center. 

DISPOSITION: The initial denial issued on October 5, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED. Prior authorization for CPT 22551 is APPROVED.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The initial denial of prior authorization was based on a determination that the member's primary diagnosis was cervical radiculopathy rather than cervical myelopathy, thereby rendering the plan's "Myelopathy Exception" (Section 2.2) inapplicable and requiring the completion of standard conservative treatment. 

Upon de novo review of the complete clinical record as of the appeal date (October 20, 2024), this Division finds that the denial must be overturned on two independent grounds:
1. Standard Pathway Satisfaction: The member has fully completed and documented the failure of the plan's standard conservative treatment requirements under Section 2.2 of Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024. While the initial denial on October 5, 2024, was issued prematurely before the clinical outcome of the September 28, 2024, epidural steroid injection could be determined, the de novo appeal record now documents that the epidural failed to provide lasting relief after 3 weeks (fully returning to 8/10 pain by October 19, 2024).
2. Regulatory and Clinical Standard Alignment: Under California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 Section II.3, the plan's internal criteria cannot be more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update), the presence of myelomalacia (T2 cord signal change) on MRI is a Tier 1 Strong Indication for surgical intervention, making extended conservative trials clinically inappropriate. Furthermore, under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3), the physical therapy requirement is legally waived due to objective evidence of structural neurological compromise.

II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION & RECORD REVIEW
The member is a 63-year-old male presenting with a mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy.
- Objective Imaging: An MRI dated September 1, 2024, demonstrates C6-C7 moderate cord compression, T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord, and right C7 foraminal stenosis more prominent than the left.
- Neurological Examination: A physical exam dated September 5, 2024, reveals a mixed presentation. Radicular findings include predominant right C7 distribution tricep weakness (4/5) and right finger numbness, with pain rated at 8/10. Myelopathic findings include bilateral hyperreflexia (right > left), a positive right Hoffman sign, an equivocal left Hoffman sign, and a mildly wide-based gait.
- Conservative Treatment History:
  1. Physical Therapy: Completed 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy (18 sessions) utilizing a myelopathy protocol. The treating therapist documented no improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms.
  2. Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 3-month trial of Pregabalin 150mg BID (neuropathic agent) resulting in only partial right-sided pain relief, and a 2-month trial of Naproxen 500mg BID (NSAID) which was inadequate.
  3. Interventional Treatment: Received a right C7 cervical epidural steroid injection on September 28, 2024. The clinical record notes that the injection provided 50% temporary right-sided relief, but the pain fully returned to 8/10 within 3 weeks (by October 19, 2024), with no change in bilateral hyperreflexia.

III. PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2, surgical authorization for ACDF (CPT 22551) requires a confirmed diagnosis (Section 2.1), completion of conservative treatment (Section 2.2), and consistent imaging (Section 2.3).

1. Diagnosis and Imaging Requirements (Sections 2.1 & 2.3):
The member has a confirmed diagnosis of mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy, satisfying both Section 2.1(a) (cervical radiculopathy) and Section 2.1(b) (cervical myelopathy). The MRI dated September 1, 2024, is within 6 months of the request and demonstrates structural pathology (moderate cord compression and myelomalacia at C6-C7) consistent with the member's symptoms, satisfying Section 2.3.

2. Standard Conservative Treatment Pathway (Section 2.2):
The initial denial on October 5, 2024, asserted that because radiculopathy was the primary diagnosis, the "Myelopathy Exception" (which modifies PT to 4 weeks and waives interventional treatment) did not apply, and standard conservative treatment was required. 

However, as of the de novo appeal review on October 20, 2024, the member has fully satisfied the standard conservative treatment requirements under Section 2.2:
- Section 2.2(a) (Physical Therapy): The member completed 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy (18 sessions), satisfying the standard 6-week requirement.
- Section 2.2(b) (Pharmacotherapy): The member completed a 3-month trial of Pregabalin (neuropathic agent) and a 2-month trial of Naproxen (NSAID). This satisfies the requirement for a trial of at least two qualifying agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
- Section 2.2(c) (Interventional Treatment): The member received a right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 28, 2024. At the time of the initial denial (October 5, 2024), only 7 days had elapsed, and the clinical outcome of the injection was pending. On de novo appeal review, we incorporate the documented clinical status as of October 19, 2024 (3 weeks post-injection), which confirms that the pain has fully returned to 8/10 and the interventional trial has failed. 

Because the member has completed and documented the failure of 6 weeks of PT, two qualifying medications for >4 weeks, and a cervical epidural steroid injection, the standard conservative treatment pathway is 100% satisfied. The plan's denial on the basis of incomplete conservative treatment is a misapplication of its own criteria.

IV. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY COMPLIANCE
1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) — Physical Therapy Waiver:
CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) explicitly states that a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the condition involves "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." 
The member's MRI dated September 1, 2024, provides objective evidence of moderate cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia), which represents structural neurological compromise. Therefore, the plan's 6-week physical therapy requirement (Section 2.2(a)) is legally waived and deemed satisfied under California law. 
While we explicitly acknowledge that CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) applies strictly to physical therapy and does not waive the plan's pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)) or interventional (Section 2.2(c)) requirements, those requirements were independently and fully satisfied through the completed trials of Pregabalin, Naproxen, and the failed C7 epidural steroid injection.

2. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 Section II.3 — Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:
The initial denial relied on the "Note" in CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, which restricts the myelopathy exception to cases where myelopathy is the "primary diagnosis." 
We explicitly acknowledge that the conservative treatment waiver under APL 22-014 Section II.2(a) is inapplicable because it requires "progressive pathology... on serial imaging," and the clinical record contains only a single MRI from September 1, 2024. 
However, APL 22-014 Section II.3 mandates that plans "shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice." Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the standard of review for medical necessity is governed by generally accepted standards of medical practice, not a plan's restrictive internal criteria. As detailed below, generally accepted clinical standards do not restrict surgical indications based on whether myelopathy or radiculopathy is designated as "primary" when both are objectively present with myelomalacia. Therefore, the plan's "primary diagnosis" restriction is invalid under California regulatory standards, and the clinical standard must govern.

V. CLINICAL EVIDENCE & GENERALLY ACCEPTED STANDARDS
Under the AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update), surgical decompression is the standard of care for patients with moderate-to-severe myelopathy or those with objective structural cord compromise.
- Tier 1 Strong Indication: The guidelines classify "myelomalacia on MRI (cord signal change)" as a Tier 1 Strong Indication for surgical intervention (Section 3.1). 
- Clinical Inappropriateness of Delay: The clinical evidence base, including the AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021), demonstrates that there is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of conservative treatment prior to surgery for patients with objective cord compression and myelomalacia. Delaying surgery in these patients to complete extended conservative trials is associated with a high risk of irreversible neurological deterioration, as preoperative myelomalacia is the strongest predictor of incomplete neurological recovery (Section 2.3).
- Mixed Presentation: In clinical practice, cervical cord compression frequently coexists with nerve root compression (mixed myelopathy-radiculopathy). The presence of prominent radicular pain (8/10) and right-sided weakness does not negate the objective spinal cord injury (myelomalacia) and upper motor neuron signs (bilateral hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign, wide-based gait). Under generally accepted clinical standards, any presence of myelomalacia is a critical clinical indicator that makes extended conservative trials inappropriate, regardless of which symptom is subjectively labeled as "primary."

VI. CONCLUSION
The member, David Santos, has a mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy with objective MRI evidence of C6-C7 moderate cord compression and myelomalacia. 
1. Under a de novo review of the clinical record as of October 20, 2024, the member has fully completed and failed the plan's standard conservative treatment pathway (6 weeks PT, 3 months Pregabalin, 2 months Naproxen, and a failed C7 epidural steroid injection), satisfying Section 2.2 of CC-SPINE-2024.
2. Under California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(3), the physical therapy requirement is legally waived due to objective structural neurological compromise.
3. Under DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3, the plan's internal restriction requiring myelopathy to be the "primary diagnosis" is impermissibly more restrictive than generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines), which recommend immediate surgical decompression for patients with documented myelomalacia.

Accordingly, the prior authorization denial for ACDF C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED, and the procedure is APPROVED.
