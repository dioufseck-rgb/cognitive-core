Dear Mr. Santos and Dr. Wong,

This letter serves as the formal appeal determination regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT code 22551) at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center. After a comprehensive clinical and regulatory review of the medical records, plan criteria, California statutory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has OVERTURNED the initial denial. The requested surgical procedure is hereby APPROVED.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The initial denial issued on October 05, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, was based on HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2, Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway). The denial asserted that because the member's symptoms were primarily right-sided (radicular), radiculopathy was the primary diagnosis, and therefore the "Myelopathy Exception" (which requires myelopathy to be the primary diagnosis) could not be applied to waive or modify conservative treatment requirements.

This appeal review has determined that the initial denial was clinically and procedurally incorrect on two independent grounds:
1. Standard Criteria Met: The member fully satisfied the standard, non-modified conservative treatment requirements under Section 2.2 of CC-SPINE-2024. The plan's initial review failed to evaluate the standard pathway, rendering the denial clinically and procedurally defective.
2. Regulatory and Clinical Override: Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) and California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, a health plan cannot condition surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (such as myelomalacia) that physical therapy cannot address. Furthermore, generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines) classify the member's presentation as a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) surgical candidate, overriding any restrictive plan-specific limitations.

II. CLINICAL HISTORY AND FINDINGS
Mr. David Santos is a 63-year-old male with a history of mixed cervical myelopathy and radiculopathy. 
- Clinical Presentation: The neurological examination dated September 05, 2024, documents a mixed presentation. Radicular findings include predominant right-sided pain (rated 8/10), right tricep weakness (4/5), and right finger numbness in a C7 distribution. Myelopathic findings include bilateral hyperreflexia (right greater than left), a positive right Hoffman sign, an equivocal left Hoffman sign, and a mildly wide-based gait.
- Objective Imaging: A cervical spine MRI dated September 01, 2024, demonstrates moderate cord compression at C6-C7 with associated T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord, as well as prominent right C7 foraminal stenosis.
- Conservative Treatment History:
  - Physical Therapy: Completed 6 weeks (18 sessions) of supervised physical therapy utilizing a myelopathy protocol. The physical therapist documented no improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms, establishing a functional plateau.
  - Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 3-month trial of Pregabalin (150mg BID) with only partial right-sided pain relief, and a 2-month trial of Naproxen (500mg BID) with inadequate response.
  - Interventional Treatment: Received a right C7 cervical epidural steroid injection on September 28, 2024, which provided 50% temporary relief of right-sided pain that fully returned within 3 weeks, with no change in bilateral hyperreflexia.

III. ANALYSIS OF PLAN CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
The initial denial incorrectly focused on the "Myelopathy Exception" under Section 2.2(A), which modifies conservative treatment requirements for patients who have not completed standard trials. However, a rule-by-rule review of the standard criteria demonstrates that Mr. Santos fully satisfied all standard requirements:

1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): Satisfied. The member has a confirmed diagnosis of both cervical radiculopathy (supported by right C7 radicular symptoms and right C7 foraminal stenosis on MRI) and cervical myelopathy (supported by gait disturbance, bilateral hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign, and moderate cord compression on MRI).
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): Satisfied.
   - Section 2.2(a) (Physical Therapy): Satisfied. The member completed 6 weeks (18 sessions) of supervised physical therapy.
   - Section 2.2(b) (Pharmacotherapy): Satisfied. The member completed trials of two qualifying agents for at least 4 weeks each (Pregabalin for 3 months and Naproxen for 2 months).
   - Section 2.2(c) (Interventional Treatment): Satisfied. The member underwent a right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 28, 2024.
3. Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement): Satisfied. The MRI dated September 01, 2024 (within 6 months of the request) demonstrates moderate cord compression and myelomalacia at C6-C7, which directly correlates with the clinical presentation and the proposed surgical level.
4. Section 2.4 (Physician Documentation): Satisfied. Dr. Lisa Wong, MD, provided the necessary clinical documentation, including conservative treatment history, clinical rationale, functional limitations, and surgical risk discussion.

Because the member met the standard conservative treatment criteria, the plan's denial based on the "primary diagnosis" restriction of the myelopathy exception was a misapplication of its own clinical policy.

IV. ANALYSIS OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK
Even if the member had not completed the standard conservative treatment trials, California law prohibits the plan from denying this request:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3): This statute explicitly states that a health insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... [t]he condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The presence of moderate cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia) on Mr. Santos's MRI constitutes objective structural neurological compromise. Physical therapy cannot decompress the spinal cord or resolve myelomalacia. Therefore, conditioning surgical approval on further physical therapy or denying the request based on physical therapy requirements is a direct violation of California law.
2. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014: The California Department of Managed Health Care has determined that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI is a time-sensitive neurological condition with potential for irreversible harm. Under APL 22-014, Section II.2, plans "shall not require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization" when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology or myelomalacia, and the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression.
3. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b): Under California's Independent Medical Review (IMR) standards, medical necessity determinations must be made in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice, and plan criteria "shall not be the sole or determinative basis" if they are more restrictive than clinical standards.

V. ANALYSIS OF CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARD
Generally accepted clinical standards strongly support immediate surgical decompression for Mr. Santos's presentation:

1. AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) & AO Spine Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021): These guidelines establish that patients with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy and objective cord compression or myelomalacia on MRI meet Tier 1 (Strong Indication) criteria for surgical decompression. 
2. Cochrane Review (2021): Evidence demonstrates that conservative treatment has limited to no efficacy in arresting the progression of moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgical intervention to complete conservative trials is associated with a significantly decreased likelihood of neurological recovery and risks permanent, irreversible spinal cord damage.
3. AO Spine Guidelines (2021) explicitly state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."

VI. RESOLUTION OF CROSS-SOURCE CONFLICTS
Under California regulatory hierarchy, state statutory mandates (CIC § 10169.5) and regulatory directives (DMHC APL 22-014) supersede any restrictive internal health plan coverage criteria. 
- The plan's internal guideline (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2, Note) restricting the myelopathy exception to cases where myelopathy is the "primary diagnosis" is more restrictive than both California law and generally accepted clinical standards.
- Because Mr. Santos has documented myelomalacia (structural neurological compromise), the statutory prohibition against mandatory physical therapy under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) overrides the plan's "primary diagnosis" restriction.
- Clinically, the distinction between "primary" radiculopathy and "secondary" myelopathy is artificial and dangerous when objective spinal cord injury (myelomalacia) is present. The clinical standard of care requires surgical decompression to prevent irreversible neurological decline, regardless of which symptom is subjectively more prominent.

VII. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION
Based on the clinical evidence, plan criteria, and California regulatory requirements, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED.
