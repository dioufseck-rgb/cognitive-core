The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the appeal filed on October 18, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551). 

After a comprehensive review of the clinical records, plan criteria, and California statutory requirements, HealthFirst has OVERTURNED the initial denial. The requested procedure is DEEMED APPROVED as a matter of law.

I. PROCEDURAL ANALYSIS AND STATUTORY DEEMED APPROVAL

The initial denial issued on October 5, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, was procedurally defective under California law. Specifically, the denial notice failed to include the mandatory Independent Medical Review (IMR) notice, failed to cite the specific clinical criteria applied (providing only a generic reference with no section numbers or specific criteria), and failed to provide the specific clinical reasons for the denial.

Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a), a health plan is strictly required to provide written notice of a denial that includes the specific criteria applied, the specific clinical reason for the denial, and a notice of the member's right to an Independent Medical Review (IMR). 

Pursuant to CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is legally DEFECTIVE and "shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."

Because the October 5, 2024 denial notice is legally treated as non-existent under CHSC § 1374.31(b), the statutory timeline for issuing a decision on the prior authorization request has expired. Under CHSC § 1367.01 and California Code of Regulations (CCR) Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1 (as outlined in California Regulatory Framework Part IV.1), a health plan must issue a decision on a standard prior authorization request within 5 business days of receipt of all necessary information. 

Under California Regulatory Framework Part IV.3 (CCR Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1), if a plan fails to issue a valid decision within the required statutory timeframes, the request is "DEEMED APPROVED" for the period of the delay. Because the plan failed to issue a legally compliant denial notice, the 5-business-day statutory timeline has expired, triggering an automatic "DEEMED APPROVED" status for the requested ACDF C6-C7 procedure. A remand to allow the plan to reissue a compliant notice is a legally invalid remedy under this framework, as the statutory penalty of deemed approval applies automatically.

II. SUBSTANTIVE CLINICAL AND REGULATORY ANALYSIS

Even if the plan attempted to defend the denial on its clinical merits, the substantive basis for the denial is legally unenforceable under California law. The initial reviewer denied the request on the basis that the member's conservative treatment was incomplete, specifically citing that the member completed only 4 weeks of physical therapy (PT) instead of the 6 weeks required under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a).

This mechanical application of a duration requirement violates California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5. Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms. Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(c) explicitly prohibits the mechanical application of an internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau.

The clinical record indicates that the member completed 4 weeks of physical therapy (consisting of 12 sessions) and reached a functional plateau. Under the clinical standard defined in Section 5 of the Clinical Reference Summary, a functional plateau is reached when there is a failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement in neurological symptoms. The member's neurological exam on August 20, 2024, documented objective neurological deficits, including right C7 dermatomal numbness, tricep weakness (4/5 right), and a diminished tricep reflex, which are secondary to a moderate right C7 nerve root compression from a C6-C7 disc herniation (confirmed on MRI dated August 15, 2024). 

As established in Section 5 of the Clinical Reference Summary, objective neurological deficits (such as motor weakness and reflex changes) are structural in nature and are not expected to respond to physical therapy regardless of duration. Conditioning surgical approval on an additional 2 weeks of physical therapy when a functional plateau has been reached violates CIC § 10169.5 and is inconsistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice.

III. HIERARCHY OF GOVERNING RULES

In resolving this appeal, HealthFirst applies the mandatory hierarchy of authority: California State Law (CHSC and CIC) supersedes generally accepted clinical standards, which in turn supersede the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024). 

1. California State Law (CHSC § 1374.31(b) and CHSC § 1367.01) mandates that the procedurally defective denial notice is treated as if no denial was issued, resulting in an expired decision timeline and an automatic "DEEMED APPROVED" status.
2. California State Law (CIC § 10169.5) prohibits the mechanical enforcement of the plan's 6-week physical therapy requirement (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a)) because the member completed a 4-week course (12 sessions) and reached a functional plateau.
3. Generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023 and NASS Guidelines 2020) support surgical intervention for cervical radiculopathy with persistent symptoms and objective neurological deficits (tricep weakness 4/5 and diminished reflex) when conservative therapy has failed to yield functional improvement.

Accordingly, the plan's internal criteria requiring 6 weeks of physical therapy are superseded by both state law and clinical standards.

IV. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION DETAILS

Based on the procedural defects under CHSC § 1374.31(b) and the expiration of decision timelines under CHSC § 1367.01, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED, and the requested procedure is DEEMED APPROVED.
