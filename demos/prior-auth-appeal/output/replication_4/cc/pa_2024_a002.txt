Dear Mr. Webb and Dr. Chen,

This letter serves as the formal appeal determination regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) at UCSF Medical Center. After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst clinical coverage criteria, California statutory and regulatory mandates, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, the initial prior authorization denial issued on November 5, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED. HealthFirst is directed to immediately authorize the requested surgical procedure.

Below is the detailed clinical, regulatory, and plan-based rationale for this determination.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND HISTORY
Mr. Marcus Webb is a 47-year-old male residing in California who presented on July 1, 2024, with symptoms highly characteristic of cervical myelopathy. Clinical notes from his initial presentation describe bilateral hand weakness, coordination problems, and upper motor neuron (UMN) signs. A comprehensive neurological examination on July 1, 2024, documented:
- Bilateral grip weakness graded at 3/5;
- Bilateral positive Hoffman sign;
- Bilateral upper extremity hyperreflexia;
- Impaired tandem gait.

An MRI of the cervical spine performed on July 10, 2024, revealed a C6-C7 disc herniation causing moderate spinal cord compression, accompanied by T2 signal change within the spinal cord consistent with early myelomalacia.

In terms of conservative management, the clinical record indicates:
1. Physical Therapy: Mr. Webb completed 4 weeks of supervised physical therapy (total of 12 sessions). Following the MRI confirmation of myelomalacia and cord compression, his treating physician, Dr. Sarah Chen, halted further physical therapy, documenting in writing that continued physical therapy was contraindicated due to the risk of neurological deterioration.
2. Pharmacotherapy: Mr. Webb underwent a 3-month trial of Gabapentin (300mg TID), which provided only partial pain relief and no improvement in weakness, and a 2-month trial of Naproxen (500mg BID), which was inadequate.
3. Interventional Treatment: A cervical epidural steroid injection was performed on September 15, 2024, resulting in minimal improvement and no change in motor deficits.

II. PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)
The initial denial issued by Dr. Patricia Huang on November 5, 2024, cited Reason Code MED-003, stating that the member completed only 4 weeks of physical therapy instead of the standard 6-week minimum required under Section 2.2(a) of the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024). The reviewer noted that while Section 2.2(A) provides a "Myelopathy Pathway" exception reducing the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks, this exception applies only when myelopathy is the primary diagnosis. Because the ICD-10 billing code on the physical therapy referrals and the most recent billing note was listed as "cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy" (ICD-10 M47.812), the plan determined that the member did not qualify for the myelopathy exception.

A detailed review of the clinical record reveals that this denial was based on an administrative billing discrepancy rather than clinical reality. While the ICD-10 billing code M47.812 was utilized on PT referrals (noted by the physician as an administrative artifact), Dr. Chen's narrative clinical notes consistently and unambiguously describe cervical myelopathy and objective myelomalacia. Under Section 2.1(b) of the plan's criteria, cervical myelopathy is defined by spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms, including gait disturbance, bilateral upper extremity weakness, hyperreflexia, and a positive Hoffman sign—all of which are objectively documented in Mr. Webb's clinical record. 

Furthermore, under the plan's Myelopathy Pathway (Section 2.2(A)), the 4-week physical therapy requirement was fully satisfied (4 weeks completed, 12 sessions), the pharmacotherapy requirement was met (Gabapentin for 3 months and Naproxen for 2 months), and the interventional requirement was attempted (epidural on September 15, 2024). Therefore, even under a proper clinical reading of the plan's own criteria, the clinical presentation of primary cervical myelopathy is established, and the criteria for the Myelopathy Pathway are met.

III. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK AND LEGAL OVERRIDES
Even if the plan's internal criteria were interpreted to strictly require a "primary diagnosis" label on billing forms, California law provides clear, binding overrides that supersede the plan's internal guidelines.

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy Prerequisites
Under CIC § 10169.5(a), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where:
- (1) The member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition; OR
- (3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address.
Additionally, CIC § 10169.5(c) mandates that a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the condition involves neurological urgency under subdivision (a)(3).
In this case, both statutory exceptions are triggered: Dr. Chen documented in writing that further physical therapy was contraindicated after MRI confirmation of myelomalacia, and the MRI provides objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (moderate cord compression and T2 signal change). The plan's mechanical application of the 6-week physical therapy requirement directly violates CIC § 10169.5.

2. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 — Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions
APL 22-014 states that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm. Under Section II.2, plans shall not require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when:
- (a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (including cord signal change or myelomalacia); AND
- (b) The treating physician documents that conservative treatment has been attempted and failed to arrest progression; AND
- (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines.
The presence of myelomalacia on Mr. Webb's MRI of July 10, 2024, is a specific finding that mandates the waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements. Requiring an additional 2 weeks of physical therapy in the presence of active myelomalacia and progressive upper motor neuron deficits violates APL 22-014.

3. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) — Standard of Review
CHSC § 1374.32(b) establishes that the standard for medical necessity does not require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If a service is clinically appropriate and in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice, it must be approved, regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.

IV. CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARD ANALYSIS
Generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice strongly support immediate surgical decompression for patients presenting with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy and objective myelomalacia.

1. Diagnostic Significance of Myelomalacia: As outlined in the Clinical Reference Summary (Section 1.2), T2 signal change (myelomalacia) indicates active spinal cord injury and represents the strongest predictor of incomplete neurological recovery if surgery is delayed.
2. Inefficacy of Physical Therapy: The Cochrane Review (2021) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Guidelines (2020) demonstrate that physical therapy has low-to-moderate evidence and does not produce meaningful functional improvement for moderate-to-severe myelopathy. 
3. Risk of Delay: The AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021) state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
4. Surgical Indications: Under the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023), Mr. Webb's clinical presentation—characterized by objective cord compression, myelomalacia, and progressive neurological deficits (grip weakness, gait impairment, hyperreflexia)—meets Tier 1 (Strong Indication) criteria for surgical intervention. Conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy in this clinical scenario is contrary to established medical standards.

V. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS (HIERARCHY OF RULES)
In resolving the conflict between the plan's internal billing-code-centric criteria and the clinical/regulatory requirements, we apply the established hierarchy: California State Law > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Plan Criteria.

1. State Law Override: CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) and DMHC APL 22-014 legally prohibit the plan from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of a 6-week physical therapy course when objective structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) is present. This statutory mandate completely overrides the plan's 6-week physical therapy requirement.
2. Clinical Standard Override: Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), because generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) recognize myelomalacia with objective deficits as a Tier 1 surgical indication and contraindicate physical therapy, the clinical standard governs over the plan's restrictive "primary diagnosis" billing code requirement.
3. Plan Criteria Misapplication: Even under the plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), the clinical record establishes a primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy based on objective clinical findings, satisfying the Myelopathy Pathway (Section 2.2(A)) and rendering the 4-week physical therapy course sufficient.

VI. CONCLUSION AND ORDER
The prior authorization denial was based on an administrative billing artifact that ignored severe, objective neurological pathology. The clinical record demonstrates that Mr. Marcus Webb has cervical myelopathy with active myelomalacia, a condition of high neurological urgency. Under California law (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014) and generally accepted clinical guidelines, a mandatory 6-week physical therapy trial is contraindicated and legally waived.

Therefore, the denial of prior authorization for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED. HealthFirst is ordered to approve this request immediately.
