Dear Mr. Webb and Dr. Chen,

The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its formal review of the prior authorization appeal for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) at UCSF Medical Center. 

After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California statutory mandates, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has determined that the initial prior authorization denial must be OVERTURNED. The requested surgical procedure is hereby approved.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The initial prior authorization request was denied on November 5, 2024, on the grounds that the member completed only 4 weeks of physical therapy (PT) instead of the 6 weeks required under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a). The initial review also determined that the member did not qualify for the Myelopathy Pathway Exception (Section 2.2(A)) because the primary diagnosis code listed on the PT referral and recent billing forms was "cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy" (ICD-10 M47.812) rather than cervical myelopathy.

On appeal, this determination is overturned. Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3), a health plan cannot condition surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy when the treating physician documents that PT is contraindicated or when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. Furthermore, California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 mandates that plans waive conservative treatment requirements when objective MRI evidence demonstrates progressive pathology, such as myelomalacia. 

The clinical record contains objective, indisputable evidence of cervical myelopathy with early myelomalacia (spinal cord injury) on MRI, and the treating physician has documented that further physical therapy is contraindicated. Under the hierarchy of authority, California statutory and regulatory mandates supersede the plan's internal coverage criteria. Therefore, the 6-week physical therapy requirement is waived, and the surgery is authorized.

II. CLINICAL HISTORY AND FINDINGS
The clinical record documents a severe, progressive neurological condition:
1. Clinical Presentation (Initial Exam Date: July 1, 2024): Mr. Webb presented with bilateral hand weakness, coordination problems, and upper motor neuron (UMN) signs.
2. Neurological Examination (July 1, 2024): Documented bilateral grip weakness (3/5), bilaterally positive Hoffman signs, bilateral upper extremity hyperreflexia, and an impaired tandem gait.
3. Objective Imaging (MRI Date: July 10, 2024): Demonstrated a C6-C7 disc herniation causing moderate spinal cord compression, with T2 signal change within the cord consistent with early myelomalacia (active spinal cord injury).
4. Conservative Treatment Completed:
   - Physical Therapy: Completed 4 weeks (12 sessions). Dr. Chen halted physical therapy immediately upon confirming myelomalacia on the July 10, 2024 MRI, documenting in writing that further physical therapy was contraindicated due to the risk of irreversible neurological deterioration.
   - Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 3-month trial of Gabapentin 300mg TID (resulting in partial pain relief but no improvement in weakness) and a 2-month trial of Naproxen 500mg BID (inadequate response), satisfying the plan's requirement for two agents under Section 2.2(b).
   - Interventional Treatment: Received a cervical epidural steroid injection on September 15, 2024, which provided minimal pain improvement and no change in motor deficits, satisfying Section 2.2(c).

III. PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024:
1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): The member satisfies Criterion 2.1(b) (Cervical myelopathy) based on clinical findings of gait disturbance, bilateral upper extremity weakness, hyperreflexia, and positive Hoffman signs. The member also satisfies Criterion 2.1(c) (Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit) based on the 3/5 grip weakness and MRI findings.
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment): The plan's initial denial mechanically applied the 6-week PT requirement. While the member completed only 4 weeks, the treating physician documented that further PT was contraindicated.
3. Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway Exception): This exception reduces the PT requirement to 4 weeks and waives the interventional requirement if contraindicated. The member completed 4 weeks of PT, meeting this modified requirement. The plan's initial denial rejected this exception solely because the administrative billing code used on PT referrals was "cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy" (ICD-10 M47.812). However, the clinical narrative notes consistently describe cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia. Under clinical and legal standards, the objective clinical and imaging findings govern over administrative billing artifacts.

IV. REGULATORY OVERRIDES AND LEGAL ANALYSIS
The plan's mechanical application of its internal physical therapy criteria is unlawful under California law:
1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1) & (a)(3): These provisions state that a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where:
   - "(1) The member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member..." Dr. Chen explicitly documented that further PT was contraindicated after the MRI revealed myelomalacia.
   - "(3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The MRI of July 10, 2024, provides objective evidence of moderate cord compression and early myelomalacia, which is a structural spinal cord injury that physical therapy cannot resolve.
   - CIC § 10169.5(c) explicitly prohibits applying minimum PT duration requirements mechanically without reference to neurological urgency.
2. DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Section II.2): Mandates that plans shall not require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as cord signal change or myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed or is contraindicated. Section II.1 classifies cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia as an urgent condition with potential for irreversible harm.
3. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b): Establishes that the standard for medical necessity does not require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria. Where plan criteria are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice, the clinical standard governs.

V. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND GENERALLY ACCEPTED STANDARDS
Generally accepted clinical standards strongly support immediate surgical decompression and reject mandatory conservative therapy for this presentation:
1. AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update): Classifies patients with moderate-to-severe myelopathy and myelomalacia (cord signal change) under Tier 1 (Strong Indication), where surgical decompression is recommended as the primary treatment.
2. AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021): States, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
3. NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020): Recommends surgical decompression as the primary treatment for moderate-to-severe myelopathy, noting that conservative treatment has limited efficacy and that myelomalacia on MRI is the strongest predictor of incomplete recovery if surgery is delayed.
4. Cochrane Review (2021): Confirms that physical therapy does not produce meaningful functional improvement in patients with moderate-to-severe myelopathy or objective neurological deficits.

VI. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS (HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY)
A conflict exists between the plan's internal criteria (which mechanically require 6 weeks of PT and rely on administrative billing codes) and California law/clinical standards (which waive PT for structural neurological compromise and prioritize clinical narrative/imaging over billing codes).
Under the established hierarchy of authority:
   California State Law (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) 
   > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine, NASS) 
   > Plan's Internal Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)

State law and clinical standards strictly prohibit the plan from delaying surgical decompression for a patient with active spinal cord injury (myelomalacia) to complete an arbitrary physical therapy duration. The administrative billing code (M47.812) is an artifact that cannot override the objective clinical reality of bilateral grip weakness, hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman signs, and MRI-confirmed cord compression with myelomalacia.

VII. DETERMINATION AND AUTHORIZATION
Based on the foregoing analysis, the initial prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED.
