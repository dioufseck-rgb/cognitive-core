I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The California Appellate Review Division has completed its review of the first-level appeal filed on behalf of Jessica Turner regarding HealthFirst Health Plan’s denial of prior authorization for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT code 22551) at Stanford Medical Center. 

After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), and the California regulatory framework, the initial prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED IN FULL. 

This determination is based on two independent, legally binding grounds:
1. Substantive Compliance and Statutory Override (Physical Therapy): Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5, a health plan cannot mechanically enforce a minimum physical therapy duration (such as the plan's 6-week requirement) when the treating physician documents that the member has reached a functional plateau. The clinical record establishes that Ms. Turner completed 5 weeks of physical therapy (15 sessions) and reached a documented functional plateau, legally satisfying and excusing the physical therapy prerequisite.
2. Procedural Defect and Statutory Deemed Approval: The plan's initial denial, issued verbally on October 15, 2024, failed to provide written clinical criteria or specific clinical reasons, violating California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a). Furthermore, the plan's internal documentation contained a critical data integrity failure, leaking an unpopulated template placeholder ('10/12/2024') instead of documenting the actual peer-to-peer date of October 15, 2024. Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a defective denial notice must be treated as if no denial was issued. Because the plan failed to issue a compliant written decision within the standard 5-day decision window, the request is legally DEEMED APPROVED under CHSC § 1367.01 and California Code of Regulations (CCR) Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1. A remedy of 'REMAND' is legally inconsistent with this statutory framework, as a defective notice legally results in an outright Deemed Approval.

II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND RECORD REVIEW

Ms. Jessica Turner is a 39-year-old female with a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy at C6-C7. The clinical record contains the following objective evidence:
- Imaging: A cervical spine MRI dated September 1, 2024, demonstrating a C6-C7 disc herniation, right C7 nerve root compression, and moderate foraminal stenosis.
- Neurological Examination: A physical examination dated September 5, 2024, documenting objective neurological deficits, including right C7 dermatomal numbness and tingling, a diminished right tricep reflex, and diminished grip strength of 4+/5.
- Conservative Treatment:
  * Physical Therapy: Completed 5 weeks of supervised physical therapy (total of 15 sessions). The treating physician, Dr. Robert Chen, MD, formally declared in the appeal letter that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms.
  * Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 3-month trial of Meloxicam (15mg daily) resulting in only partial relief, and a 2-week trial of a Prednisone taper resulting in only temporary improvement.
  * Interventional Treatment: None performed.

III. CONFLICT RESOLUTION & STATUTORY OVERRIDE OF PHYSICAL THERAPY REQUIREMENTS

HealthFirst Health Plan denied the requested ACDF procedure under its internal clinical coverage criteria, CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a), which mandates a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy as a prerequisite to surgical authorization. 

Under the California regulatory framework, a strict hierarchy of authority applies: California State Law > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Plan Internal Criteria. Where a conflict exists between a plan's internal criteria and state law, state law overrides the plan's criteria.

1. Statutory Override of PT Duration (CIC § 10169.5):
   - CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) explicitly prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where "the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
   - CIC § 10169.5(c) further mandates that "a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau."
   - Dr. Robert Chen, MD, provided a written declaration in the appeal letter stating that Ms. Turner reached a functional plateau after 5 weeks (15 sessions) of physical therapy. Under CIC § 10169.5(b), a "functional plateau" is defined as a documented failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement, as determined by the treating physician.
   - Therefore, the plan's mechanical application of its 6-week physical therapy requirement to deny coverage is a direct violation of California law. The 5-week course of physical therapy, combined with the documented functional plateau, legally satisfies and excuses the physical therapy prerequisite.

2. Clinical Standards for Radiculopathy:
   - Under generally accepted clinical standards (NASS 2020 Guidelines; AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023), surgical decompression (ACDF) is a reasonable and indicated treatment (Tier 2 Indication) for patients with cervical radiculopathy who present with persistent dermatomal pain, objective neurological deficits (diminished reflexes and grip weakness), and correlating MRI findings of nerve root compression who have failed conservative management.
   - Ms. Turner's clinical presentation meets these clinical standards. Forcing an additional week of physical therapy on a patient who has already reached a documented functional plateau and exhibits objective neurological deficits is clinically inappropriate and legally prohibited.

IV. PROCEDURAL DEFECTS AND STATUTORY DEEMED APPROVAL

In addition to the substantive clinical merits, the plan's prior authorization denial is procedurally invalid under California law, requiring an outright overturn rather than a remand.

1. Verbal-Only Denial and Lack of Written Criteria (CHSC § 1374.31(a)):
   - CHSC § 1374.31(a) mandates that when a plan issues a denial based on medical necessity, it must provide a written notice of denial that includes the specific clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the denial.
   - HealthFirst Health Plan failed to issue a compliant written denial notice. The plan's denial was communicated "verbally only" during a peer-to-peer call on October 15, 2024, and the plan failed to provide the member or the provider with the specific written criteria applied.

2. Template Placeholder Leak and Data Integrity Failure:
   - The plan's internal denial documentation contains a critical quality control and data integrity failure. The denial text states: "Plan medical director spoke with requesting physician on 10/12/2024."
   - The use of an unpopulated template placeholder ('10/12/2024') instead of the actual date of the peer-to-peer call (October 15, 2024) demonstrates a lack of individualized clinical review and invalidates the credibility of the plan's documentation. To ensure data integrity, this record is corrected to reflect that the peer-to-peer call occurred on October 15, 2024, and the plan's failure to populate this field is noted as an additional procedural defect.

3. Legal Remedy: Overturn vs. Remand (CHSC § 1374.31(b) & CHSC § 1367.01):
   - Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason is legally defective and "shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."
   - Because a defective notice is legally treated as if no denial was issued, the plan has failed to issue a timely decision. Under CHSC § 1367.01 and CCR Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1 (Part IV.3), if a plan fails to issue a decision within the standard 5-business-day window, the request is legally "DEEMED APPROVED."
   - A remedy of "REMAND" to allow the plan to reissue a compliant written denial is legally inconsistent with the California regulatory framework. Remanding would ignore the statutory penalty of a defective notice. Because the plan's standard decision window has expired and the notice was defective, the request must be treated as a Deemed Approval, resulting in an outright OVERTURN of the denial.

V. CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Based on the statutory override of the physical therapy duration requirement under California Insurance Code § 10169.5, and the statutory Deemed Approval resulting from a procedurally defective denial under California Health & Safety Code § 1374.31(b) and § 1367.01, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED.

HealthFirst Health Plan is hereby ORDERED to immediately authorize and cover the requested Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF), C6-C7, one level (CPT 22551) for member Jessica Turner, to be performed by Dr. Robert Chen, MD, at Stanford Medical Center.
