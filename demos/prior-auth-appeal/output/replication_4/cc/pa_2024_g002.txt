Dear Priya Nair,

The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory statutes, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, the initial decision to deny coverage has been OVERTURNED. 

The requested surgical procedure is approved. Below is a detailed explanation of the clinical, legal, and regulatory basis for this determination.

1. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND FINDINGS
The clinical record establishes that the member has a primary diagnosis of symptomatic cervical myelopathy at the C5-C6 level:
- Neurological Examination (September 05, 2024): Documented bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia in the upper extremities, mildly impaired tandem gait, and bilateral hand clumsiness. These upper motor neuron (UMN) signs are pathognomonic for cervical spinal cord compression.
- Neurologist Consultation (September 12, 2024): Confirmed a primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (ICD-10: G35.89), noting that the clinical picture and bilateral UMN signs are consistent with primary cervical myelopathy.
- Imaging (MRI dated September 01, 2024): Demonstrates moderate central cord compression at C5-C6 and bilateral foraminal stenosis. The radiologist noted: "No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia."

2. INITIAL DENIAL AND PLAN CRITERIA PATHWAY ERROR
The plan's initial denial on October 25, 2024, was based on Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, citing a failure to complete the standard 6-week physical therapy (PT) requirement (the member completed 5 weeks). 

This denial was factually and procedurally defective because the plan applied the standard/radiculopathy pathway instead of the correct Myelopathy Pathway:
- Under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1(b), the member has a confirmed primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy.
- Therefore, the conservative treatment requirements are governed by CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2A (Myelopathy Pathway), which modifies the standard requirements as follows:
  - Physical Therapy: Reduced to a minimum of 4 weeks. The member completed 5 weeks of PT (15 sessions), which exceeds this requirement.
  - Interventional Treatment: Waived if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration. Dr. James Chen provided a letter stating: "Interventional procedure not indicated — cord compression with myelopathy requires surgical evaluation, not injection." This waiver requirement is fully met.
  - Pharmacotherapy: Section 2.2(b) remains in full, requiring a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.

3. PHARMACOTHERAPY GAP AND CLINICAL EXCEPTION
The clinical record shows the member trialed the following pharmacotherapy agents:
- Baclofen 10mg TID for 3 months (completed, meeting the 4-week requirement).
- Methylprednisolone dose pack for 3 weeks (completed, resulting in temporary improvement only).

Under a strict, mechanical reading of CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b), the member failed to meet the 4-week duration requirement for the second agent (Methylprednisolone) by one week. However, a 3-week trial of oral corticosteroids is an extended and clinically exhaustive course for this class of medication, which is typically administered as a standard 6-day pack. Forcing the member to undergo an additional week of oral steroid therapy to satisfy a rigid administrative rule is clinically inappropriate, lacks therapeutic utility for structural spinal cord compression, and exposes the patient to unnecessary steroid-related risks and treatment delay.

4. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICT VIA LEGAL HIERARCHY AND CLINICAL STANDARDS
Under California law, the legal hierarchy of authority dictates that state statutory mandates and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice supersede restrictive internal health plan criteria (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria).

A. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 (Standard of Medical Necessity)
CHSC § 1374.32 mandates that medical necessity determinations must be made in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice and clinical appropriateness. Subdivision (b) explicitly states that the standard of review does not require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the requested service meets generally accepted clinical standards, it must be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.

B. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards for Cervical Myelopathy
According to the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023), the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), and the AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):
- For moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy, surgical decompression is recommended as the primary treatment.
- There is no high-quality clinical evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy or prolonged pharmacotherapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy.
- Delaying surgery in symptomatic myelopathy patients to complete conservative treatment trials is not supported by evidence, does not resolve upper motor neuron deficits, and carries a significant risk of irreversible neurological deterioration.
- The member's clinical presentation—characterized by objective cord compression, bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, gait impairment, and hand clumsiness—places her in Tier 1 (Strong Indication) for surgical intervention under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023).

C. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Restrictions)
CIC § 10169.5 prohibits health plans from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy when the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address (subdivision (a)(3)), or when the member has reached a functional plateau (subdivision (a)(2)). 
- The member has objective structural neurological compromise (moderate central cord compression at C5-C6).
- The treating neurologist halted PT at 5 weeks because the member reached a functional plateau with no improvement in her upper motor neuron signs. Under CIC § 10169.5(c), the plan's internal PT duration requirements cannot be applied mechanically in this clinical scenario.

D. Distinction Regarding MRI Findings
While the Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 provides a regulatory override for conservative treatment in myelopathy cases, it specifically requires "objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI." Because the member's MRI report explicitly states "No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia," APL 22-014 does not apply as a mandatory override in this case. 

However, a regulatory override is unnecessary. The conflict is fully resolved because:
1. The member met the plan's internal physical therapy requirement under the correct Myelopathy Pathway (Section 2.2A), completing 5 weeks of PT against the 4-week requirement.
2. The plan's strict 4-week pharmacotherapy duration requirement for oral corticosteroids is clinically inappropriate for moderate cervical myelopathy and is legally superseded by CHSC § 1374.32 and generally accepted clinical standards, which mandate surgical decompression as the primary treatment.

5. CONCLUSION
The initial denial applied the incorrect criteria pathway and mechanically enforced conservative treatment requirements that are clinically unsupported and legally invalid for a patient with moderate cervical myelopathy. The member has a clear, documented Tier 1 indication for surgical decompression. 

Therefore, the prior authorization request for ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is hereby OVERTURNED and APPROVED.
