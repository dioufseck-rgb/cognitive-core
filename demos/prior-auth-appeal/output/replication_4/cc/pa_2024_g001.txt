Dear Mr. Reyes and Dr. Okonkwo,

The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the first-level appeal submitted on behalf of Marco Reyes regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C6-C7 level (CPT 22551) to be performed at UCSF Medical Center. 

After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, applicable California state regulations, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has determined that the original prior authorization denial issued on November 1, 2024, was based on a factual error and must be OVERTURNED. The requested procedure is APPROVED.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The initial denial issued by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, on November 1, 2024, asserted that the member did not meet the conservative treatment requirements under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. Specifically, the denial claimed that the member completed only 3 weeks of physical therapy (PT) and that a functional plateau was not documented. 

A review of the complete clinical record submitted with the original prior authorization request reveals that this factual claim is incorrect. The member completed a total of 9 weeks of supervised physical therapy across two providers (3 weeks at Valley Physical Therapy and 6 weeks at Capital Physical Therapy). Furthermore, the discharge summary from Capital Physical Therapy explicitly documents that a functional plateau was reached. Because the member has completed 9 weeks of physical therapy (exceeding the plan's 6-week minimum), achieved a documented functional plateau, completed the required pharmacotherapy trials, and undergone an interventional procedure, all clinical and regulatory criteria for authorization are fully satisfied.

II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND HISTORY
Mr. Reyes is a 49-year-old male with a history of persistent, right-sided C6-C7 cervical radiculopathy. 
- Clinical Symptoms & Exam (August 25, 2024): Neurological examination reveals right C7 dermatomal numbness, a diminished right tricep reflex, and right-sided grip strength of 4/5. No upper motor neuron (UMN) signs or bilateral findings are present.
- Diagnostic Imaging (August 20, 2024): An MRI of the cervical spine demonstrates a large C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate right C7 nerve root compression and confirmed right foraminal stenosis. There is no cord contact or myelomalacia.
- Conservative Treatment History:
  1. Physical Therapy (Provider 1): Valley Physical Therapy from June 1, 2024, to June 21, 2024 (3 weeks, 9 sessions). Outcome: Partial improvement, but symptoms persisted.
  2. Physical Therapy (Provider 2): Capital Physical Therapy from July 8, 2024, to August 16, 2024 (6 weeks, 18 sessions). Outcome: Plateau reached. The discharge summary explicitly states: "Maximum therapeutic benefit achieved. No further improvement expected from continued PT."
  3. Pharmacotherapy: Mr. Reyes completed a 4-month trial of Gabapentin (600mg TID) with only partial pain relief and persistent radicular symptoms, and a 3-month trial of Naproxen (500mg BID) which was inadequate.
  4. Interventional Treatment: Mr. Reyes underwent a right C7 cervical epidural steroid injection on September 15, 2024, which provided 35% temporary relief before symptoms returned to baseline within 3 weeks.

III. PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2, authorization for cervical spinal surgery (CPT 22551) is approved when all of the following criteria are satisfied:

1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): SATISFIED. Mr. Reyes has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (Section 2.1(a)), characterized by right-sided C7 nerve root compression with correlating radicular symptoms (pain, numbness, and 4/5 grip strength weakness in the C7 dermatomal distribution). This is supported by a cervical MRI performed on August 20, 2024 (within the required 6-month window) showing a large C6-C7 disc herniation.
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): SATISFIED.
   - (a) Physical Therapy: Mr. Reyes completed 9 weeks of supervised physical therapy directed at his cervical condition (3 weeks at Valley PT and 6 weeks at Capital PT), which exceeds the plan's 6-week minimum requirement. A functional plateau was explicitly documented in the Capital PT discharge summary.
   - (b) Pharmacotherapy: Mr. Reyes completed trials of two qualifying agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each: Gabapentin (neuropathic agent, 4 months) and Naproxen (NSAID, 3 months).
   - (c) Interventional Treatment: Mr. Reyes completed a right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 15, 2024, satisfying the requirement for at least one interventional treatment.
3. Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement): SATISFIED. The MRI dated August 20, 2024, is current (within 6 months of the request and appeal) and demonstrates structural pathology (large C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate right C7 nerve root compression) consistent with the patient's right-sided C7 radicular symptoms.
4. Section 2.4 (Physician Documentation): SATISFIED. Dr. Sarah Okonkwo, MD, provided the necessary clinical documentation, including an attestation of completed conservative treatment, clinical rationale for surgery, description of functional limitations, and discussion of surgical risks and alternatives.

IV. REGULATORY COMPLIANCE AND STATE LAW ANALYSIS
Because Mr. Reyes is a California member, this appeal is governed by California insurance laws and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) regulations. The initial denial violates several key provisions:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):
   Under § 10169.5(a)(2), a health plan cannot condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician or therapist documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms. Furthermore, § 10169.5(c) prohibits plans from mechanically applying a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau. 
   The initial denial mechanically applied a 6-week PT requirement and ignored the Capital PT discharge records documenting a functional plateau. This mechanical application directly violates California law.

2. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b) (Procedural Defect):
   Under § 1374.31(b), a denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reason for the denial. A denial based on an incorrect factual premise (asserting only 3 weeks of PT were completed when 9 weeks were documented in records submitted with the original request) is procedurally defective.

V. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND GUIDELINES ANALYSIS
Generally accepted standards of medical practice strongly support surgical intervention in this case:
- North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020) and the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023) classify Mr. Reyes' presentation as a Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) for surgical decompression. Surgery is indicated for patients with cervical radiculopathy who have persistent, correlating neurological deficits (such as Mr. Reyes' right C7 sensory loss and 4/5 grip strength weakness) and have failed to respond to an adequate course of conservative management (typically 6 to 12 weeks).
- Mr. Reyes' 9-week course of physical therapy, combined with 4 months of neuropathic medication and a cervical epidural steroid injection, constitutes an exhaustive and failed course of conservative care. Delaying necessary surgical decompression in the face of persistent motor weakness (4/5 grip strength) and a documented functional plateau is clinically inappropriate and increases the risk of permanent neurological deficit.

VI. DISPOSITION AND AUTHORIZATION DETAILS
Based on the foregoing clinical, regulatory, and plan criteria reviews, the initial denial is OVERTURNED.
