The Appeals and Grievances Department at HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its formal review of the appeal submitted on your behalf by Dr. Karen Davis regarding the prior authorization denial for a single-level anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551).

APPEAL DISPOSITION: UPHOLD DENIAL
After a comprehensive review of your clinical records, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has determined that the prior authorization denial must be UPHELD. The requested surgical procedure is not medically necessary under your plan's coverage criteria, California law, or national clinical guidelines.

CLINICAL SUMMARY
You are a 54-year-old male employed as a construction worker operating heavy equipment. You presented with a history of axial neck pain, rated at 5/10. An MRI of your cervical spine dated September 15, 2024, demonstrated severe central canal stenosis at the C5-C6 level with a canal diameter of 6 mm (normal is greater than 13 mm). The MRI showed spinal cord contact but no compression, no T2 signal change, and no evidence of myelomalacia.

A comprehensive neurological examination performed on September 20, 2024, was completely normal. There was no evidence of cervical radiculopathy (no dermatomal pain, numbness, or weakness) and no signs of cervical myelopathy (normal gait, no bilateral upper extremity weakness, normal reflexes, and a negative Hoffman sign). Your symptoms consist solely of axial neck pain.

Your conservative treatment history includes:
1. Physical Therapy: 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy, which resulted in modest improvement in your axial neck pain (reducing from 7/10 to 5/10).
2. Pharmacotherapy: Naproxen 500 mg twice daily for 3 months (providing moderate pain relief) and Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks (providing sleep improvement only).
3. Interventional Treatment: A C5-C6 epidural steroid injection performed on October 10, 2024, which provided 60% pain relief lasting 6 weeks.

Your surgeon, Dr. Karen Davis, requested a prophylactic ACDF at C5-C6, arguing that your critically narrow canal diameter (6 mm) puts you at high risk for acute spinal cord injury from minor trauma, particularly given your occupation as a construction worker.

I. ANALYSIS OF PLAN CLINICAL COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery — Cervical Procedures (Document: CC-SPINE-2024), several requirements must be met for authorization of an ACDF (CPT 22551):

1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): This section requires a confirmed diagnosis of either (a) cervical radiculopathy with nerve root compression and radicular symptoms, (b) cervical myelopathy with spinal cord compression and myelopathic symptoms, or (c) cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit. Your clinical record documents a completely normal neurological examination on September 20, 2024, with no radiculopathy, no myelopathy, and no objective neurological deficits. Therefore, you do not meet the diagnosis requirement of Section 2.1.
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): You have completed the conservative treatment requirements, including 8 weeks of physical therapy (exceeding the 6-week minimum in Section 2.2(a)), two qualifying medications for at least 4 weeks (Section 2.2(b)), and one cervical epidural steroid injection (Section 2.2(c)). However, meeting the conservative treatment requirements does not override the necessity of meeting the diagnosis requirement under Section 2.1.
3. Section 3.1 (Exclusions): This section explicitly states that authorization will be denied when "Surgery is requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy." Because your clinical presentation consists solely of axial neck pain (5/10) without any documented radiculopathy or myelopathy, the requested procedure is excluded from coverage under Section 3.1.

II. ANALYSIS OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK
We evaluated your request under the California regulatory standards to ensure compliance with state law:

1. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 (Standard of Review for Medical Necessity): Under California law, "medically necessary" services are those that a physician exercising prudent clinical judgment would provide to prevent, evaluate, diagnose, or treat an illness, injury, disease, or its symptoms in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice. As detailed in Section III below, prophylactic surgery for asymptomatic severe stenosis (or stenosis presenting with axial pain only) is not supported by generally accepted standards of medical practice.
2. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Prior Authorization for Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions): This regulation mandates that plans waive conservative treatment requirements and expedite reviews for urgent neurological conditions, specifically cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI. Your MRI on September 15, 2024, explicitly noted "no T2 signal change" and "no myelomalacia," and your clinical exam showed no myelopathy. Therefore, your condition does not qualify for the urgent exceptions or conservative treatment waivers under APL 22-014.
3. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite): This statute prevents plans from mechanically requiring physical therapy when it is contraindicated or when a functional plateau has been reached. While you completed 8 weeks of physical therapy, this statute is not violated because the denial is based on the lack of a qualifying clinical diagnosis and the absence of surgical indications, not on a failure to complete physical therapy.

III. ANALYSIS OF CLINICAL EVIDENCE BASE (GENERALLY ACCEPTED STANDARDS OF MEDICAL PRACTICE)
We reviewed your case against guidelines from the American Association of Neurological Surgeons / Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS, 2023), the North American Spine Society (NASS, 2020), and the AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):

1. Surgical Indications (AANS/CNS 2023): Under these guidelines, surgical decompression (such as ACDF) is classified into three tiers of clinical indication:
   - Tier 1 (Strong Indication): Moderate to severe myelopathy with cord compression, progressive neurological deficits, or myelomalacia on MRI.
   - Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication): Mild myelopathy or persistent radiculopathy failing conservative treatment.
   - Tier 3 (Surgery NOT Indicated): Axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy, or imaging findings without correlating symptoms.
   Because you have a completely normal neurological exam and your symptoms are limited to axial neck pain, your presentation falls under Tier 3 ("Surgery NOT Indicated").
2. Prophylactic Surgery for Asymptomatic Stenosis: Dr. Davis's appeal basis rests on the prophylactic benefit of surgery to prevent spinal cord injury from potential trauma due to your occupation. However, clinical evidence (including the Cochrane Review, 2021) does not support prophylactic decompression for patients with cervical stenosis who lack clinical signs of myelopathy or radiculopathy, even in the presence of a narrow canal (6 mm) and cord contact. The natural history of asymptomatic cervical stenosis is relatively benign, and the risk of catastrophic spinal cord injury from minor trauma is statistically very low. Consequently, the immediate risks of major spinal surgery (including C5-C6 ACDF, which carries risks of dysphagia, adjacent segment disease, nerve injury, and pseudarthrosis) outweigh the theoretical, unproven benefit of prophylactic decompression. Generally accepted standards of medical practice recommend close clinical monitoring and activity modification rather than prophylactic surgery.

IV. CROSS-SOURCE CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), generally accepted standards of medical practice and state regulatory mandates supersede restrictive internal plan criteria in the event of a conflict. However, in this case, there is no conflict between the sources. HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 (which excludes surgery for axial pain under Section 3.1), California regulatory standards (including DMHC APL 22-014, which limits conservative treatment exceptions to active myelopathy with myelomalacia), and generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023 and NASS 2020, which classify prophylactic surgery for asymptomatic stenosis as Tier 3 'Surgery NOT Indicated') are in complete alignment. All three authorities independently and consistently support the denial of the requested prophylactic ACDF.

CONCLUSION
While we understand and appreciate Dr. Davis's proactive concern regarding your occupation as a construction worker, the requested prophylactic ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) does not meet the definition of medical necessity under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32, or generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020). Therefore, the initial denial is upheld. We recommend that you continue to be monitored closely by your physician for any developing neurological symptoms (such as numbness, weakness, or gait changes) and discuss appropriate activity modifications to minimize the risk of cervical trauma.
