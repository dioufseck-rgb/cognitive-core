HealthFirst has completed its review of the appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). After a thorough review of the clinical records, plan criteria, California regulatory standards, and generally accepted clinical guidelines, the decision to deny coverage for this procedure has been UPHELD.

### 1. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND REQUEST SUMMARY
The clinical records indicate that you are a 54-year-old construction worker who operates heavy equipment. An MRI of your cervical spine dated September 15, 2024, demonstrated severe central canal stenosis at C5-C6 with a canal diameter of 6mm (normal is greater than 13mm) and spinal cord contact. However, the MRI showed no spinal cord compression, no T2 signal change, and no myelomalacia (spinal cord injury).

A neurological examination performed on September 20, 2024, was completely normal, showing no signs of radiculopathy (nerve root compression) or myelopathy (spinal cord compression), and no upper motor neuron signs (such as a positive Hoffman sign or hyperreflexia). Your primary clinical symptom is axial neck pain, rated at 5/10, which has partially improved with conservative treatments, including 8 weeks of physical therapy, pharmacotherapy (Naproxen and Cyclobenzaprine), and a C5-C6 epidural steroid injection on October 10, 2024, which provided 60% pain relief lasting 6 weeks.

Your treating surgeon, Dr. Karen Davis, MD, requested this surgery as a "prophylactic decompression" to prevent catastrophic spinal cord injury. Dr. Davis noted that given your occupation in heavy construction, you are at an elevated risk of cervical trauma, and a single traumatic event could cause acute spinal cord injury due to the severely reduced 6mm canal diameter.

### 2. CLARIFICATION OF THE SURGICAL INDICATION
We explicitly acknowledge that this surgical request is not "solely for axial neck pain" (which would trigger Exclusion 3.1 of the HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 clinical coverage criteria). Rather, the request is for prophylactic decompression to prevent a potential future spinal cord injury in the setting of severe, asymptomatic cervical stenosis (6mm canal) and a high-risk occupation. The initial denial's characterization of the request as being solely for axial neck pain is corrected.

### 3. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK AND MEDICAL NECESSITY DEFINITION
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(a), "medically necessary" health care services include those provided by a physician, exercising prudent clinical judgment, for the purpose of "preventing, evaluating, diagnosing, or treating an illness, injury, disease, or its symptoms." Therefore, under California law, a prophylactic or preventive surgical argument is a legally valid basis for establishing medical necessity, and a plan cannot deny a service simply because it is preventive.

However, CHSC § 1374.32(a) further mandates that any such preventive service must be:
(1) "In accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice;" and
(2) "Clinically appropriate, in terms of type, frequency, extent, site, and duration, and considered effective for the patient's illness, injury, or disease."

Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard is not bound by the plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) if those criteria are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. Therefore, we evaluate this prophylactic request directly against generally accepted clinical standards.

### 4. EVALUATION AGAINST GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS
To determine whether prophylactic ACDF is medically necessary under California law, we reference the generally accepted standards of medical practice, specifically the Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine of the American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) 2023 Guidelines and the North American Spine Society (NASS) 2020 Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines.

Under these clinical standards, surgical intervention (such as ACDF) is categorized into three tiers of clinical indications:
*   **Tier 1 — Strong Indication (Surgery Recommended):** Indicated for moderate-to-severe myelopathy with objective cord compression, progressive neurological deficits, or myelomalacia (T2 cord signal change) on MRI.
*   **Tier 2 — Reasonable Indication (Surgery Reasonable Option):** Indicated for mild myelopathy with stable symptoms after conservative trials, or persistent radiculopathy after 6–12 weeks of conservative treatment.
*   **Tier 3 — Surgery Not Indicated:** Surgery is not supported for axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy, or for imaging findings (such as stenosis or cord contact) in the absence of correlating clinical symptoms or neurological deficits.

Generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020) do not support prophylactic surgical decompression for asymptomatic or non-deficit cervical stenosis, even when the canal is severely narrowed (6mm) and there is spinal cord contact, and even in patients with high-risk occupations (such as heavy construction). The clinical evidence demonstrates that:
1.  **Risk-Benefit Ratio:** The risk-benefit ratio of a major spinal surgery (ACDF) does not favor intervention in a patient with a completely normal neurological examination. The risk of surgical complications (including dysphagia, adjacent segment disease, and nerve injury) outweighs the statistical risk of catastrophic spinal cord injury from future trauma.
2.  **Lack of Active Pathology:** In the absence of myelomalacia (T2 signal change on MRI) or objective upper motor neuron signs (e.g., hyperreflexia, Hoffman sign, gait disturbance), there is no active spinal cord injury or progressive myelopathy to arrest.
3.  **Standard of Care:** The standard of care for patients with severe asymptomatic stenosis is close clinical monitoring, activity modification (such as counseling on avoiding high-impact trauma or modifying occupational duties), and surgical intervention only upon the development of early myelopathic or radicular symptoms.

Because prophylactic surgical decompression in an asymptomatic patient with a normal neurological exam does not align with generally accepted standards of medical practice, it does not satisfy CHSC § 1374.32(a)(1).

### 5. HEALTHFIRST CLINICAL COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
While California law dictates that generally accepted clinical standards govern, we also note that the request does not satisfy the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):
*   **Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement):** Requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with radicular symptoms, cervical myelopathy with myelopathic symptoms, or cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit. Your clinical presentation does not meet any of these diagnoses.
*   **Section 3.2 (Exclusions):** While the surgery is not excluded under Section 3.1 (as it is requested for prophylactic decompression rather than solely for axial pain), it remains non-covered because the core diagnostic requirements of Section 2.1 are not met.

### 6. CONCLUSION AND DISPOSITION
The prior authorization request for a prophylactic ACDF at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is **UPHELD** (denied). Although California law recognizes preventive services within the definition of medical necessity, such services must align with generally accepted standards of medical practice. Because major spine societies (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020) do not support prophylactic surgical decompression for asymptomatic severe cervical stenosis with cord contact in the absence of neurological deficits, the request is not medically necessary. 

We recommend that you continue to be monitored closely by your physician for any developing neurological symptoms (such as hand clumsiness, gait instability, or radiating pain) and discuss occupational activity modifications to minimize the risk of cervical trauma.
