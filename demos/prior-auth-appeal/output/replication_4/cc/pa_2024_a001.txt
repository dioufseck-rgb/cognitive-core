The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California statutory and regulatory requirements, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has determined that the prior authorization denial issued on November 1, 2024, must be OVERTURNED. The requested surgical procedure is APPROVED.

I. CLINICAL BACKGROUND AND PRESENTATION
Ms. Diana Torres is a 61-year-old female who presented to Dr. James Kim on August 10, 2024, with a 4-month history of progressive bilateral hand clumsiness and gait instability. A comprehensive neurological examination performed on that date documented significant upper motor neuron signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia in both the upper and lower extremities, bilateral positive Babinski signs, and gait ataxia.

A cervical spine MRI performed on August 15, 2024, revealed severe central cord compression at the C5-C6 level with associated T2 hyperintensity within the spinal cord, confirming a diagnosis of cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia. The radiologist noted on the MRI report: "No prior imaging for comparison."

Following the initial diagnosis, conservative management was initiated. Ms. Torres participated in supervised physical therapy at Valley PT from September 1, 2024, to September 14, 2024, completing 6 sessions over 2 weeks. However, physical therapy was discontinued by Dr. Kim after 2 weeks because it caused a documented exacerbation of her neurological symptoms. In addition, Ms. Torres underwent a 2-week trial of an oral methylprednisolone taper, which provided only temporary, mild improvement with no sustained clinical benefit. On October 15, 2024, Dr. Kim issued a formal clinical declaration stating that further physical therapy is strictly contraindicated due to the presence of myelomalacia and the high risk of irreversible spinal cord injury, and that surgical decompression is urgently indicated.

II. THE PLAN'S DENIAL BASIS
On November 1, 2024, HealthFirst Utilization Management denied the prior authorization request under Clinical Coverage Criteria Document CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. The denial, reviewed by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, was based on the following grounds:
1. Physical Therapy Deficit: The member completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy. Under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a), the standard pathway requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. Under the modified "Myelopathy Pathway" (Exception 2.2(a)), the physical therapy requirement is reduced but still requires a minimum of 4 weeks.
2. Pharmacotherapy Deficit: The member trialed only one agent (methylprednisolone) for 2 weeks. CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b) requires a trial of at least two specified pharmacotherapy agents (NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each.

III. CLINICAL AND REGULATORY ANALYSIS
To resolve this appeal, HealthFirst must evaluate the conflict between the plan's internal coverage criteria and the governing clinical and legal standards. Under California law, specifically California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, clinical standards of medical necessity override more restrictive plan criteria.

1. Acknowledgment of Plan Criteria Non-Compliance
It is factually established that Ms. Torres did not satisfy the literal conservative treatment requirements set forth in HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. She completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy (failing both the 6-week standard and 4-week myelopathy exception thresholds) and trialed only one pharmacotherapy agent for 2 weeks (failing the requirement to trial two agents for 4 weeks each).

2. Acknowledgment of Gaps in Specific Statutory and Regulatory Exceptions
On appeal, the provider argued that the plan's conservative treatment requirements are legally waived. However, a strict legal analysis reveals that the specific statutory and regulatory exceptions cited do not fully resolve the clinical deficits:
- California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) prohibits an insurer from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise. While this statute legally waives the physical therapy requirement due to the documented myelomalacia, it strictly governs physical therapy and does not apply to or waive the plan's independent pharmacotherapy requirements under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b).
- DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 Section II.2 allows for a waiver of conservative treatment when objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology on serial imaging. Because Ms. Torres's MRI report from August 15, 2024, explicitly states "No prior imaging for comparison," there is no serial imaging available to demonstrate progressive pathology. Consequently, the specific conservative treatment exception under APL 22-014 Section II.2 cannot be legally invoked.

3. Resolution via Governing Clinical Standards and California Medical Necessity Law
Because the specific statutory waivers do not fully excuse the pharmacotherapy deficit, the request must be evaluated under the broader clinical standard of medical necessity as mandated by California law.

Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the standard for medical necessity does not require consistency with a plan's internal coverage criteria. If a service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, the plan must approve the service regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. Furthermore, DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3 mandates that plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice, and where a conflict exists, the clinical standard governs.

Generally accepted clinical standards for the management of cervical myelopathy are established by the American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine (2023 Update), the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), and AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021). These clinical standards dictate the following:
- Primary Surgical Indication: For patients presenting with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy and objective evidence of spinal cord compression with myelomalacia (T2 cord signal hyperintensity), immediate surgical decompression is recommended as the primary, first-line treatment (AANS/CNS 2023 Tier 1 Recommendation; NASS 2020 Grade B Evidence).
- Conservative Treatment Contraindicated: There is no clinical evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy or extended pharmacotherapy prior to surgical intervention in patients with moderate-to-severe myelopathy. The AO Spine Guidelines (2021) explicitly state: "Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
- Risk of Irreversible Harm: Myelomalacia represents an established, irreversible structural injury to the spinal cord. Clinical studies demonstrate that each month of delay in surgical decompression for patients with moderate-to-severe myelopathy and cord signal changes is directly associated with a decreased likelihood of neurological recovery and a high risk of permanent, irreversible neurological deficits (AANS/CNS 2023; DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.1).

Applying these clinical standards to Ms. Torres's case:
- Her clinical presentation is highly progressive, characterized by a 4-month history of worsening hand clumsiness and gait instability, accompanied by profound upper motor neuron signs (Hoffman, Babinski, hyperreflexia, and ataxia).
- Her MRI demonstrates severe central cord compression with myelomalacia, representing an active, vulnerable structural injury.
- Requiring Ms. Torres to undergo an additional 2 weeks of physical therapy (which has already caused documented symptom exacerbation) and a 4-week trial of a second pharmacotherapy agent is not only clinically unsupported but is actively contraindicated and dangerous. It exposes the patient to an unacceptable risk of permanent, irreversible spinal cord damage.

IV. CONCLUSION AND DISPOSITION
A direct conflict exists between the conservative treatment requirements of HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 and generally accepted standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine). Pursuant to California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32(b) and DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 Section II.3, the clinical standard must govern over the plan's more restrictive criteria.

Because immediate surgical decompression is the medically necessary and clinically indicated standard of care for progressive moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia, the conservative physical therapy and pharmacotherapy requirements are clinically overridden.

Accordingly, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED, and the request for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is APPROVED.
