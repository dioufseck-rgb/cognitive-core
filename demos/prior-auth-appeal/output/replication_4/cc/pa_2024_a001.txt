DISPOSITION: OVERTURN THE DENIAL IN FULL

Dear Diana Torres and Dr. James Kim,

This letter serves as the formal appeal determination regarding the prior authorization request for a one-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, the California regulatory framework, and the established clinical evidence base, the initial denial is OVERTURNED. Immediate surgical intervention is authorized.

I. ESTABLISHED FINDINGS (NOT SUBJECT TO RE-EXAMINATION)
As established in prior reviews of this case, the following facts are uncontested and legally binding:
1. Clinical Presentation: The member presents with progressive primary cervical myelopathy at C5-C6 with objective structural neurological compromise, specifically severe central cord compression and T2 hyperintensity within the cord (myelomalacia) on MRI (dated 08/15/2024), accompanied by objective upper motor neuron signs (bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, positive Babinski, and gait ataxia).
2. Plan Criteria Failure: The plan's initial denial mechanically applied standard 6-week physical therapy (PT) and multi-agent pharmacotherapy requirements under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. In doing so, the plan failed to apply its own internal "Myelopathy Pathway" exception (Section 2.2, Exception 2.2(A)), which reduces the PT requirement to 4 weeks and waives the interventional treatment requirement.
3. California Regulatory Framework: Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3), a health insurer is prohibited from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Furthermore, Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 mandates that plans shall not require conservative treatment when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents urgency.

II. CLINICAL EVIDENCE ANALYSIS: PT CONTRAINDICATION AND SURGICAL URGENCY
Pursuant to the governing clinical review directives, this determination focuses on whether the clinical evidence base supports the treating physician's declaration (dated 10/15/2024) that physical therapy is contraindicated and that immediate surgery is indicated for cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia.

Based on a rigorous evaluation of the Clinical Reference Summary and the peer-reviewed guidelines cited therein, the clinical evidence base strongly supports Dr. Kim's declaration.

1. Clinical Consensus on Physical Therapy in the Presence of Myelomalacia
The clinical evidence base establishes that physical therapy is not only ineffective but clinically contraindicated for patients with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy and objective cord signal changes (myelomalacia):
- Category Error of Conservative Therapy: As detailed in Section 1.1 of the Clinical Reference Summary, the evidence base supporting conservative treatment (such as physical therapy) is primarily derived from studies of cervical radiculopathy (nerve root compression), not myelopathy (spinal cord compression). Applying radiculopathy-derived conservative treatment timelines to myelopathy is a clinical category error.
- Lack of Functional Benefit: The Cochrane Review (Nikolaidis et al., 2021) cited in Section 2.1 demonstrates that for moderate-to-severe myelopathy, physical therapy does not produce meaningful functional improvement.
- Risk of Neurological Deterioration: The AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guideline (2021) explicitly states: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." (Section 2.1).
- Documented Treatment Exacerbation: In this specific case, the member attempted physical therapy (Valley PT, 09/01/2024 to 09/14/2024) but experienced documented symptom exacerbation, leading to a written physician order to discontinue after 2 weeks (6 sessions). This clinical course directly aligns with the evidence that mechanical manipulation or exercise in the presence of severe cord compression can worsen spinal cord injury.

2. Clinical Consensus on Conservative Treatment Duration
There is no clinical consensus or evidence supporting a mandatory minimum duration of conservative treatment for patients with myelomalacia:
- Absence of Evidence: As noted in Section 2.2, no randomized controlled trials support a defined minimum duration of conservative treatment before surgery for cervical myelopathy.
- Predictor of Permanent Deficit: Preoperative myelomalacia (T2 hyperintensity) is the strongest predictor of incomplete neurological recovery postoperatively (Section 2.3). Delaying surgery to meet arbitrary payer timelines (such as 4 or 6 weeks of PT) directly increases the risk of permanent, irreversible neurological deficits. Prospective studies show that each month of delay in surgical treatment for moderate-to-severe myelopathy is associated with a decreased likelihood of neurological recovery (Section 2.3).

3. Clinical Indications for Immediate Surgery (ACDF CPT 22551)
Under the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023 Update) outlined in Section 3.1, the member's clinical presentation meets "Tier 1 — Strong Indication" for immediate surgical decompression:
- The member has moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI (Section 3.1, Tier 1(a)).
- The member has documented myelomalacia on MRI (Section 3.1, Tier 1(c)).
- The member has progressive neurological deficits (hand clumsiness, gait instability, ataxia) that failed to respond to conservative measures and were actively exacerbated by physical therapy (Section 3.1, Tier 1(b)).

III. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS
Where a conflict exists between the plan's internal coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) and generally accepted standards of medical practice, the clinical standard and state law govern. 
1. Under DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3, plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice.
2. Under California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(3), the plan's physical therapy requirement is legally unenforceable due to the objective structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) documented on the 08/15/2024 MRI.
3. Under the clinical standards of the AANS/CNS (2023) and AO Spine (2021), immediate surgical decompression is the standard of care, and further conservative treatment is contraindicated.

IV. CONCLUSION
The treating physician's declaration that physical therapy is contraindicated and that immediate surgery is indicated is fully supported by the clinical evidence base. The plan's insistence on the completion of conservative treatment in the presence of progressive myelopathy and myelomalacia is clinically unsound, violates California law, and poses an imminent threat of irreversible neurological harm.

The prior authorization denial for ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is hereby OVERTURNED. Immediate surgical intervention is approved.
