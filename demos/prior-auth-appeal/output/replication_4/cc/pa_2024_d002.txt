Dear Mr. Green and Dr. Kim,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of the first-level internal appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT code 22551). 

Based on a comprehensive clinical-merits-first review of the medical record, the initial denial issued on November 08, 2024, is OVERTURNED. The clinical evidence demonstrates that the member meets the substantive medical necessity criteria for the requested surgical intervention under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024. 

However, please note that final release of the surgical authorization is administratively conditioned upon the requesting provider submitting the outstanding formal documentation required under Section 2.4 of CC-SPINE-2024, as detailed below.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I. CLINICAL MERITS AND PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2, an ACDF procedure is considered medically necessary when all clinical, imaging, and conservative treatment criteria are satisfied. The member's clinical presentation is classified as primary cervical radiculopathy, which is subject to the standard conservative treatment pathway.

1. DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT (Section 2.1):
   - Criteria: Confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (nerve root compression with radicular pain, numbness, or weakness in a dermatomal distribution), supported by an MRI within 6 months.
   - Clinical Findings: The clinical record documents a presenting diagnosis of C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy with functional impairment. A neurological examination on September 25, 2024, revealed objective neurological deficits, including left C6 dermatomal numbness, a diminished left bicep reflex, and left bicep motor weakness graded at 4/5.
   - Imaging: An MRI of the cervical spine dated September 20, 2024 (within the required 6-month window), demonstrated a large disc herniation at C5-C6 causing moderate left C6 nerve root compression, with no spinal cord contact. This correlates directly with the member's left-sided C6 radicular symptoms. Section 2.1 is MET.

2. CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT (Section 2.2):
   - Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): Requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy directed at the cervical spine. The member completed 7 weeks (total of 21 sessions) of supervised physical therapy. PT notes document that the member reached a functional plateau at week 6, with persistent radicular symptoms. This satisfies both the plan's 6-week requirement and California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2) regarding functional plateaus. Section 2.2(a) is MET.
   - Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): Requires a trial of at least two specified agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The member completed a 4-month trial of Gabapentin 600mg TID (neuropathic agent) resulting in only partial relief, and a 3-month trial of Naproxen 500mg BID (NSAID) which provided inadequate relief. Section 2.2(b) is MET.
   - Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): Requires at least one interventional treatment. The member underwent a left C6 cervical epidural steroid injection on October 28, 2024, which provided 40% temporary relief before symptoms returned to baseline within 3 weeks. Section 2.2(c) is MET.

3. IMAGING REQUIREMENT (Section 2.3):
   - Criteria: Current imaging within 6 months demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms and proposed surgical level.
   - Findings: The September 20, 2024 MRI showing moderate left C6 nerve root compression due to a large C5-C6 disc herniation directly correlates with the left C6 dermatomal numbness and bicep weakness. Section 2.3 is MET.

4. PHYSICIAN DOCUMENTATION AND ADMINISTRATIVE CONDITIONS (Section 2.4):
   - Criteria: Section 2.4 requires the treating physician to provide: (a) an attestation that conservative treatment has been completed, (b) clinical rationale for surgical intervention, (c) a description of functional limitations, and (d) a statement of surgical risks and alternatives considered.
   - Findings: Dr. Sarah Kim's detailed clinical notes satisfy the clinical substance of Section 2.4(b) and (c) by outlining the clinical rationale, objective neurological deficits, and functional limitations. However, the clinical record currently lacks a formal, signed (a) Attestation of conservative treatment completion and (d) Statement of surgical risks and alternatives considered.
   - Resolution: Because the clinical history itself proves that conservative treatment was completed and failed, the denial is clinically overturned. However, to ensure full compliance with utilization management safety policies, Dr. Kim must submit the outstanding formal Section 2.4(a) attestation and Section 2.4(d) risk/alternative statement to the Appeals Department. Final surgical authorization and the release of the authorization number to Cedars-Sinai Medical Center are conditioned upon receipt of these two documents.

II. REGULATORY AND PROCEDURAL DEFECTS REVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

In the appeal, the member noted that the initial denial notice dated November 08, 2024, was procedurally defective because it referenced an attached clinical criteria document that was never provided, and failed to include the mandatory Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights notice.

1. STATUTORY EVALUATION OF CHSC § 1374.31(b):
   - Under California Health & Safety Code § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is deemed defective. 
   - However, the statute explicitly dictates that a defective notice "shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines." It does not mandate or authorize an automatic substantive approval of the requested medical service, nor does it serve as an independent legal ground to overturn a denial.
   - Establishing a precedent where clerical omissions (such as a failure to attach a PDF criteria document) result in automatic substantive approvals of invasive surgeries would violate the plan's fiduciary duty to ensure clinical safety and medical necessity.

2. CORRECTIVE ACTION:
   - The plan acknowledges these procedural omissions as serious regulatory defects. This matter has been formally referred to the HealthFirst Regulatory Compliance Department for internal audit and corrective action to ensure future notices comply with CHSC § 1374.31(a).
   - To be clear, these procedural defects did not serve as the basis for this overturn. The decision to overturn the denial is justified solely on the clinical merits of the patient's case, as documented in Section I of this letter.
