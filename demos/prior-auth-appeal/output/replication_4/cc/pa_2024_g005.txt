FINAL APPEAL DETERMINATION:
The prior authorization denial for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED. The requested surgical procedure is approved.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
On November 8, 2024, HealthFirst Utilization Management issued a denial for a requested C6-C7 ACDF (CPT 22551) based on two grounds: (1) an alleged physical therapy (PT) shortfall of 5 weeks completed versus the 6 weeks required under plan criteria, and (2) poorly controlled type 2 diabetes (HbA1c 8.2%) presenting perioperative and wound healing risks. 

Upon comprehensive clinical and regulatory review of the appeal, both denial grounds are determined to be invalid. First, the PT shortfall is factually incorrect; the clinical record documents that the member completed 7 weeks of supervised PT (21 sessions) from July 1, 2024, through August 16, 2024, with a documented functional plateau. Second, diabetes control is not an authorization criterion under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024. Under California law and clinical standards, perioperative glycemic optimization is a surgical planning responsibility managed by the clinical team, not a prior authorization barrier.

Furthermore, while the member trialed two neuropathic agents (Pregabalin and Duloxetine) which technically fall under a single category in Section 2.2(b) of CC-SPINE-2024, strict adherence to this checklist to require a second class of medication (such as NSAIDs or oral corticosteroids) is clinically contraindicated due to the member's active diabetes and violates California's medical necessity standard under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32. Accordingly, all medical necessity requirements are satisfied, and the denial is overturned.

II. DETAILED ANALYSIS OF DENIAL GROUNDS

1. Physical Therapy Requirement (Section 2.2(a) & CIC § 10169.5)
The initial denial asserted that the member completed only 5 weeks of physical therapy, failing the 6-week minimum requirement under Section 2.2(a) of CC-SPINE-2024. This assertion is factually incorrect.
- Evidence: The physical therapy records demonstrate continuous, supervised treatment from July 1, 2024, through August 16, 2024, totaling 7 weeks and 21 sessions.
- Clinical Outcome: At discharge, the physical therapist documented a functional plateau, noting "no further functional improvement expected."
- Regulatory Standard: Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2), a health plan cannot condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy if the member has completed a course of therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a "functional plateau" without adequate resolution of symptoms. Additionally, CIC § 10169.5(c) prohibits the mechanical application of a minimum duration requirement without reference to a documented functional plateau. Because the member completed 7 weeks of PT (exceeding the 6-week requirement) and reached a documented functional plateau, the physical therapy requirement is fully satisfied.

2. Diabetes Control and Perioperative Risk (CHSC § 1374.32)
The initial denial cited the member's poorly controlled type 2 diabetes (HbA1c 8.2%) as a ground for denial due to perioperative and wound healing risks. This ground is invalid under both plan criteria and California law.
- Plan Criteria: HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 does not condition ACDF authorization on HbA1c levels or metabolic control.
- Clinical Management: The clinical record shows that both the treating surgeon (Dr. Diallo) and the endocrinologist (Dr. Patel) are actively managing the member's diabetes. Dr. Diallo's note states: "Diabetes management will be optimized preoperatively. This is standard perioperative protocol. Diabetes control is a surgical planning consideration, not a PA criterion." Dr. Patel's note from October 20, 2024, recommends optimization prior to elective surgery.
- Regulatory Standard: Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, medical necessity is defined based on "generally accepted standards of medical practice" and "prudent clinical judgment." Generally accepted standards dictate that perioperative risk management, including preoperative glycemic optimization, is the sole responsibility of the surgical and medical teams. Using a clinical comorbidity that is actively being managed as a prior authorization barrier to deny coverage for a structurally indicated procedure is a non-criterion denial and violates CHSC § 1374.32.

III. CLINICAL MEDICAL NECESSITY & PHARMACOTHERAPY ANALYSIS

1. Pharmacotherapy Requirement (Section 2.2(b) & CHSC § 1374.32)
Section 2.2(b) of CC-SPINE-2024 requires a trial of at least two of the following categories of medications for a minimum of 4 weeks each: NSAIDs/COX-2 inhibitors, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids.
- Member's Trial: The member completed a 5-month trial of Pregabalin 150mg BID (partial relief) and a 3-month trial of Duloxetine 60mg (moderate benefit). Both medications are classified as "Neuropathic agents," representing a single category under the plan's internal criteria.
- Clinical and Regulatory Reconciliation: Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the standard for medical necessity does not require strict consistency with a plan's internal coverage criteria if those criteria are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. 
- Clinically, trialing two distinct, high-potency neuropathic agents (Pregabalin, a calcium channel alpha-2-delta ligand, and Duloxetine, a selective serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor) for a combined 8 months represents an intensive and highly appropriate conservative pharmacotherapy course for cervical radiculopathy.
- Furthermore, requiring a trial of alternative classes is clinically contraindicated in this patient:
  - NSAIDs: Contraindicated or highly restricted due to the member's 12-year history of type 2 diabetes, which carries a high risk of diabetic nephropathy and cardiovascular complications.
  - Oral Corticosteroids: Strongly contraindicated due to the member's current HbA1c of 8.2%. Systemic corticosteroids cause severe glycemic disruption and would dangerously elevate blood glucose levels, exacerbating the very perioperative risks cited in the initial denial.
- Therefore, forcing a trial of a second medication class would violate prudent clinical judgment and the clinical standard of care. Under CHSC § 1374.32, the member's pharmacotherapy course is determined to be clinically appropriate, effective, and medically necessary.

2. Diagnosis, Imaging, and Neurological Deficit (Sections 2.1 & 2.3)
The member's clinical presentation fully satisfies the diagnostic and imaging criteria under CC-SPINE-2024:
- Diagnosis (Section 2.1(a)): Confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy C6-C7 with functional impairment.
- Imaging (Section 2.3): A cervical MRI dated September 20, 2024 (within the 6-month requirement) demonstrates a large C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate left C7 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis.
- Neurological Deficit (Section 2.1(c)): A neurological examination on September 25, 2024, documents objective neurological deficits correlating precisely with the C7 nerve root compression: left C7 dermatomal numbness, diminished left tricep reflex, left tricep weakness (4/5), and diminished left grip strength (4+/5). The member is working reduced hours due to severe pain and hand weakness.

3. Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c))
The member completed a left C7 epidural steroid injection on October 10, 2024, which provided 45% temporary relief before symptoms returned to baseline in 4 weeks. This satisfies the interventional treatment requirement under Section 2.2(c).

4. Physician Documentation (Section 2.4)
Dr. Diallo has provided the required clinical documentation, including an attestation of conservative treatment completion, clinical rationale for surgery, description of functional limitations (reduced working hours, hand weakness), and a statement of surgical risks and alternatives.

IV. CONCLUSION & ORDER
The clinical record demonstrates that Gerald Obi has a severe, structurally correlated cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits that have failed a comprehensive and clinically appropriate course of conservative management, including 7 weeks of physical therapy, 8 months of dual neuropathic pharmacotherapy, and a cervical epidural steroid injection. 

The plan's initial denial was based on a factual error regarding physical therapy duration and an invalid, non-criterion ground regarding diabetes control. Under California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32 and California Insurance Code § 10169.5, the requested single-level ACDF at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is medically necessary and clinically appropriate.

The initial denial is hereby OVERTURNED. Prior authorization for CPT 22551 is APPROVED for a period of 90 days from the date of this determination (valid through February 20, 2025), per Section 3.5 of CC-SPINE-2024. Preoperative glycemic optimization remains the responsibility of the surgical and endocrinology teams prior to the scheduled procedure.
