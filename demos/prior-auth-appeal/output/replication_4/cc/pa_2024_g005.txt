Dear Mr. Obi and Dr. Diallo,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its clinical and regulatory review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory statutes, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, the initial decision to deny coverage has been OVERTURNED. 

Surgical authorization is hereby APPROVED. Below is the detailed clinical and legal rationale for this determination.

---

### I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY OF DETERMINATION

The initial denial issued on November 8, 2024, cited two grounds: (1) a physical therapy (PT) shortfall of 5 weeks completed versus the 6 weeks required under plan criteria, and (2) poorly controlled type 2 diabetes (HbA1c 8.2%) presenting elevated perioperative and wound healing risks. 

Upon appeal review, both denial grounds are determined to be invalid:
1. **Physical Therapy Shortfall (Factually Incorrect):** The clinical record demonstrates that the member completed 7 weeks of supervised physical therapy (21 sessions) from July 1, 2024, through August 16, 2024, with a documented functional plateau. This exceeds the plan's 6-week requirement.
2. **Diabetes Control (Inappropriate Prior Authorization Criterion):** While the patient's elevated HbA1c (8.2%) and the associated wound healing risks are clinically significant, metabolic optimization is a standard perioperative risk management issue for the surgical and medical teams to address preoperatively. It is not a valid prior authorization criterion under CC-SPINE-2024 or generally accepted clinical standards.
3. **Pharmacotherapy Trial (Technical Shortfall Acknowledged but Waived):** Under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b), the member's trial of Pregabalin and Duloxetine technically represents only one medication category (neuropathic agents) rather than two distinct categories. However, this technicality cannot be used to sustain the denial because: (a) the plan failed to cite a pharmacotherapy shortfall in its initial denial notice, waiving this ground under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a); and (b) forcing additional medication trials in a patient with progressive, objective neurological deficits violates generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice under CHSC § 1374.32.

---

The clinical record establishes the following:
*   **Diagnosis:** Cervical radiculopathy C6-C7 with functional impairment.
*   **Objective Imaging:** An MRI dated September 20, 2024, reveals a large C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate left C7 nerve root compression and confirmed foraminal stenosis. No cord contact or myelomalacia is present.
*   **Neurological Deficits:** A physical examination on September 25, 2024, documented objective neurological deficits correlating with the imaging: left C7 dermatomal numbness, a diminished left tricep reflex, left tricep weakness (graded 4/5), and left grip weakness (graded 4+/5). The member is working reduced hours due to severe pain and hand weakness.
*   **Conservative Treatment History:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** Completed 7 weeks (21 sessions) of supervised PT from July 1, 2024, through August 16, 2024. The discharge note documented a functional plateau: "no further functional improvement expected."
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Completed a 5-month trial of Pregabalin 150mg BID (partial pain relief) and a 3-month trial of Duloxetine 60mg (moderate neuropathic benefit).
    *   **Interventional Treatment:** Received a left C7 epidural steroid injection on October 10, 2024, which provided 45% temporary relief before symptoms returned to baseline within 4 weeks.
*   **Comorbidities:** Type 2 diabetes for 12 years. Recent HbA1c was 8.2% on October 15, 2024. The patient's endocrinologist, Dr. Patel, noted on October 20, 2024, that the HbA1c is suboptimally controlled and recommended optimization prior to elective surgery due to elevated wound healing risks. The treating surgeon, Dr. Diallo, documented that glycemic management will be optimized preoperatively as part of standard perioperative protocol.

### III. DETAILED ANALYSIS AND REGULATORY COMPLIANCE

#### 1. Physical Therapy Requirement (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a) & CIC § 10169.5)
The plan's initial denial asserted that the member completed only 5 weeks of PT. This claim is factually incorrect. The submitted physical therapy records explicitly document continuous, supervised treatment from July 1, 2024, through August 16, 2024, representing exactly 7 weeks and 21 sessions. 

Furthermore, California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2) mandates that a health plan shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy if the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a "functional plateau" without adequate resolution of symptoms. The physical therapy discharge summary explicitly documented a functional plateau ("no further functional improvement expected"). Mechanical application of a duration requirement to deny coverage under these circumstances violates CIC § 10169.5. Therefore, the physical therapy requirement is fully satisfied.

#### 2. Diabetes Control and Perioperative Risk (CHSC § 1374.32(a))
The plan's initial denial cited the member's poorly controlled type 2 diabetes (HbA1c 8.2%) and elevated wound healing risks as a basis for denial. However, HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria document, CC-SPINE-2024, contains no provisions or criteria conditioning surgical authorization for ACDF on glycemic control or specific HbA1c thresholds.

Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(a), medical necessity is defined as health care services that are "clinically appropriate" and "in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice." Generally accepted clinical standards (including guidelines from the American Association of Neurological Surgeons [AANS], Congress of Neurological Surgeons [CNS], and the North American Spine Society [NASS]) dictate that perioperative risk factors, such as glycemic control, are clinical management issues to be addressed and optimized by the surgical and medical teams preoperatively. They do not constitute valid prior authorization criteria to deny or delay medically necessary spinal surgery. 

While Dr. Patel (endocrinologist) appropriately recommended optimization prior to elective surgery, this is a clinical safety recommendation for the surgical team, not a contractual barrier to coverage. Dr. Diallo has explicitly documented that glycemic management will be optimized preoperatively in accordance with standard protocols. Denying prior authorization based on a standard perioperative risk factor that is actively being managed is clinically inappropriate and violates the medical necessity standard set forth in CHSC § 1374.32(a).

#### 3. Pharmacotherapy Trial (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b) & CHSC § 1374.31(a))
Under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b), a member must complete a trial of at least two of the following medication categories for a minimum of 4 weeks each: NSAIDs/COX-2 inhibitors, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids. The member trialed Pregabalin (5 months) and Duloxetine (3 months). Because both medications are classified as neuropathic agents under Section 2.2(b), the member technically trialed only one of the listed categories.

However, this technical shortfall cannot be used to uphold the denial on appeal for two independent reasons:

*   **Procedural Waiver under California Law:** Under CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2), a health plan's denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the denial. The initial denial notice dated November 8, 2024, did not cite a pharmacotherapy shortfall as a ground for denial. Under California regulatory standards, a health plan cannot raise new, uncited criteria on appeal to sustain a denial that was originally based on different, invalid grounds. Doing so constitutes a procedural defect under CHSC § 1374.31(b).
*   **Clinical Standard of Care (CHSC § 1374.32(b)):** Under California's Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard, a service must be approved if it is clinically appropriate and in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice, even if it does not meet the plan's internal criteria. The member has a large C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate left C7 nerve root compression and objective, progressive neurological deficits (tricep weakness 4/5, diminished reflex, grip weakness 4+/5) causing significant functional impairment. The member has already failed 7 weeks of physical therapy, failed two strong neuropathic agents, and failed an epidural steroid injection. Forcing a patient with documented motor weakness to undergo further conservative medication trials (such as NSAIDs or corticosteroids) is clinically inappropriate, medically unnecessary, and poses a risk of progressive neurological deterioration. Generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS Tier 2 indications) support surgical decompression under these clinical circumstances.

---

### IV. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION

Because the physical therapy shortfall ground is factually incorrect, the diabetes control ground is not a valid prior authorization criterion, and the technical pharmacotherapy shortfall is procedurally waived and clinically superseded by the standard of care, the initial denial is contractually and legally unsustainable. 

The requested procedure is medically necessary and is hereby approved:

*   **Approved Procedure:** Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF), C6-C7, single level (CPT 22551)
*   **Facility:** Stanford Medical Center
