Dear Ms. Kamau and Dr. Osei,

The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its independent clinical review of the appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial decision to deny coverage has been OVERTURNED. The requested surgical procedure is APPROVED.

I. PROCEDURAL REVIEW AND DEFECT OF INITIAL DENIAL
The initial denial issued on November 20, 2024, was based on the member's prior authorization history, specifically a denial in May 2024 for a three-level cervical fusion. The initial reviewer (Dr. Alan Foster, MD) asserted that the member's cervical pathology had not materially changed and that the prior denial precluded reconsideration.

This denial basis is procedurally defective and clinically invalid under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024:
1. Unauthorized Denial Basis: CC-SPINE-2024 does not authorize the use of prior authorization history as a criterion or exclusion for denying current requests. Each prior authorization request must be evaluated independently on its own merits based on the member's current clinical status.
2. Different Clinical Request: The May 2024 request was for a highly invasive three-level fusion (C4-C5, C5-C6, C6-C7), which was correctly denied because pathology was limited to a single level. The current request is for a single-level ACDF at C5-C6 only. Notably, the May 2024 denial documentation explicitly stated that a single-level C5-C6 request would have been approved at that time.
3. Defective Denial Notice: Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), a denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the denial. By relying on prior PA history rather than evaluating the current clinical record against CC-SPINE-2024, the plan issued a defective denial.

II. CLINICAL RECORD AND CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT ANALYSIS
A review of the current clinical record demonstrates that the member's clinical status has worsened significantly since May 2024, and she has completed an extensive course of conservative therapy.

1. Physical Therapy (PT):
The member completed a new, supervised 8-week course of physical therapy (24 sessions) completed July-September 2024 (resolving any prior documentation discrepancies regarding the completion date). The treating physical therapist and physician explicitly documented a functional plateau: "no further improvement expected, neurological deficit persistent and worsening." This satisfies the 6-week requirement under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a). Furthermore, under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2), a health plan cannot condition surgical approval on continued physical therapy once a functional plateau has been documented.

2. Interventional Treatment:
The member completed a right C6 cervical epidural steroid injection on November 5, 2024, which provided 50% temporary relief for 2 weeks before symptoms returned to baseline. This satisfies the interventional requirement under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(c).

3. Pharmacotherapy and Reconciliation of the Duration Gap:
Under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b), members must complete a trial of at least two pharmacotherapy agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
- Agent 1: Gabapentin 600mg TID was trialed for 6 months, providing only partial pain relief with persistent deficits. This satisfies the 4-week minimum.
- Agent 2: Methylprednisolone dose pack x2 (2 courses) was administered. A standard methylprednisolone dose pack is a 6-day course; two courses total 12 days of therapy.

Acknowledge of Gap: Under the strict, literal terms of CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b), the 12-day duration of the oral corticosteroid trial fails to meet the 4-week (28-day) minimum requirement for a second agent.

Clinical Standard Override: While the strict terms of the plan's internal criteria were not met for the second agent's duration, requiring the member to undergo an additional or extended conservative pharmacotherapy trial (such as another 4-week course of oral steroids or NSAIDs) is clinically contraindicated and violates generally accepted standards of medical practice.
- Progressive Neurological Deficit: The neurological exam on October 20, 2024, documented right C6 dermatomal numbness, an absent right bicep reflex, and right bicep motor weakness of 3+/5. This represents an interval worsening of her motor deficit.
- Regulatory Framework: Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 and DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, where a conflict exists between restrictive plan criteria and generally accepted standards of medical practice, the clinical standard governs. APL 22-014, Section II.2, mandates that plans shall not require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when serial imaging demonstrates progressive pathology and the member exhibits progressive neurological deficits, as continued delay risks irreversible neurological harm.
- Clinical Guidelines: According to the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update) and NASS Guidelines (2020), a progressive motor deficit (such as 3+/5 bicep weakness) in the presence of correlating nerve root compression is a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) for surgical decompression. These guidelines state that delaying surgery to complete conservative trials in the face of progressive motor weakness is clinically contraindicated due to the high risk of permanent, irreversible nerve damage.
- Statutory Protection: Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), conservative treatment prerequisites are waived when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that conservative therapy cannot address.

Therefore, the 4-week duration requirement for the second pharmacotherapy agent is overridden by the clinical standard, as further conservative treatment is clinically contraindicated.

III. IMAGING AND CLINICAL CORRELATION
CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.3 requires current imaging (within 6 months) demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms.
- Current MRI (October 15, 2024) reveals a large C5-C6 disc herniation with moderate-to-severe right C6 nerve root compression and new foraminal stenosis. This represents a documented interval increase in herniation size compared to the prior MRI from April 20, 2024.
- This structural pathology correlates directly with the member's clinical presentation of right C6 dermatomal numbness, absent right bicep reflex, and 3+/5 right bicep weakness.

IV. CONCLUSION
The member meets the diagnostic criteria for cervical radiculopathy under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1(a). While the second pharmacotherapy agent did not meet the strict 4-week duration requirement of Section 2.2(b), requiring further conservative treatment in the presence of a progressive, severe motor deficit (3+/5 bicep weakness) is clinically contraindicated under AANS/CNS guidelines, DMHC APL 22-014, and CIC § 10169.5.

Accordingly, the initial denial is OVERTURNED, and the request for a single-level ACDF at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is APPROVED.
