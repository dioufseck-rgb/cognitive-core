FINAL APPEAL DETERMINATION: OVERTURN

Dear Mr. Reed and Dr. Liu,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its clinical and regulatory review of the first-level appeal submitted on November 5, 2024, regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551).

After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California statutory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial prior authorization denial issued on October 20, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED. The requested procedure is APPROVED.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The initial denial was based on two primary findings: (1) that physical therapy (PT) had not been completed to a functional plateau, and (2) that imaging showed only mild cord contact without measurable compression or myelopathy findings.

Upon appeal review, both bases for denial are found to be clinically and legally invalid:
1. Factual Error Regarding Conservative Treatment: The clinical record demonstrates that the member completed 7 weeks (21 sessions) of supervised physical therapy. The PT discharge summary explicitly documents that a functional plateau was reached, with no improvement over the final 3 sessions. Denying coverage on this basis represents a clear factual error and violates California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5.
2. Clinical Category Misapplication: The member's primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, not cervical myelopathy. The plan's initial reviewer applied myelopathy-specific imaging thresholds (requiring cord compression or myelopathy findings) to a radiculopathy case. Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1(a), the member meets the diagnostic criteria for cervical radiculopathy based on objective C7 nerve root compression, dermatomal sensory deficits, and diminished reflexes correlating with C6-C7 pathology.

II. CLINICAL HISTORY AND TIMELINE
- August 1, 2024: Cervical Spine MRI demonstrates a C6-C7 disc herniation with mild cord contact and mild right C7 foraminal stenosis.
- August 5, 2024: Neurological examination by Dr. Karen Liu, MD, reveals right C7 dermatomal distribution numbness, a diminished right tricep reflex, and rest pain rated at 7/10. No upper motor neuron signs or bilateral findings are present, confirming a primary diagnosis of unilateral C7 cervical radiculopathy.
- June to October 2024 (Pharmacotherapy): The member completed a 4-month trial of Pregabalin (150mg BID) with only partial pain relief and persistent radicular symptoms, and a 3-month trial of Meloxicam (15mg daily) with inadequate response.
- August to September 2024 (Physical Therapy): The member completed 7 weeks (21 sessions) of supervised physical therapy directed at the cervical spine. The PT discharge summary explicitly notes that a functional plateau was reached, with no clinical improvement observed over the final 3 sessions, and radicular symptoms remaining unchanged.
- September 20, 2024 (Interventional Treatment): The member underwent a right C7 cervical epidural steroid injection, which provided 30% temporary pain relief lasting 3 weeks before symptoms returned to baseline.

III. ANALYSIS OF PLAN CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
The requested ACDF C6-C7 (CPT 22551) fully satisfies all medical necessity criteria outlined in HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024:
1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy under Section 2.1(a), defined as nerve root compression with radicular symptoms (documented right C7 dermatomal numbness, diminished right tricep reflex, and 7/10 pain). The MRI dated August 1, 2024 (within 6 months) demonstrates structural pathology (C6-C7 disc herniation and right C7 foraminal stenosis) consistent with these symptoms.
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement):
   - (a) Physical Therapy: The member completed 7 weeks of supervised PT (exceeding the 6-week minimum) with documented functional outcomes showing a plateau.
   - (b) Pharmacotherapy: The member completed trials of two distinct classes of medications for at least 4 weeks each (Pregabalin for 4 months, Meloxicam for 3 months).
   - (c) Interventional Treatment: The member underwent a right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 20, 2024.
3. Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement): The MRI dated August 1, 2024, demonstrates C6-C7 disc herniation and right C7 foraminal stenosis, which directly correlates with the right C7 radiculopathy symptoms and the proposed surgical level.
4. Section 2.4 (Physician Documentation): Dr. Karen Liu, MD, provided the required clinical rationale, description of functional limitations, and statement of surgical risks.

IV. ANALYSIS OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK
The initial denial violates several key provisions of California law, which govern this appeal:
1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite): Under subdivision (a)(2), a health plan cannot condition approval of a surgical procedure on physical therapy completion where the member has completed a course of therapy and the treating provider documents a functional plateau. The plan's denial on the basis that "PT [was] not yet completed to functional plateau" directly contradicts the physical therapist's explicit discharge summary and violates this statute.
2. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 (Standard of Review): Under subdivision (b), the standard for medical necessity is based on generally accepted standards of medical practice and clinical appropriateness, not solely on restrictive internal plan criteria.
3. CHSC § 1374.31(b) (Defective Denial Notice): The initial denial notice relied on a factual error (stating PT was not completed to a plateau) and applied incorrect clinical criteria (demanding myelopathy findings for a radiculopathy diagnosis). This renders the initial denial procedurally defective.

V. ANALYSIS OF CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARDS
Generally accepted clinical standards strongly support surgical intervention for this patient:
1. AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update) & NASS Guidelines (2020): Under Section 3.1 of the Clinical Reference Summary, the member meets Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) criteria for ACDF. Surgery is recommended for patients with cervical radiculopathy who have persistent symptoms and objective neurological deficits (diminished reflex, dermatomal numbness) after an adequate conservative trial of 6 to 12 weeks.
2. Functional Plateau Definition: Under clinical standards (Clinical Reference Summary Section 5), a functional plateau is defined as a failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement over 3 consecutive sessions. The member's PT discharge summary explicitly documents this plateau.

VI. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS
The initial reviewer, Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, committed a category match error by evaluating this case under myelopathy criteria (noting "no measurable compression" and "no myelopathy findings on exam") while ignoring the clear, documented evidence of cervical radiculopathy.

To the extent that any conflict exists between the plan's restrictive interpretation of "mild cord contact" and generally accepted clinical standards, California law (CHSC § 1374.32(b) and DMHC APL 22-014 Part II.3) dictates that generally accepted clinical standards govern. Because the member has objective C7 nerve root compression (foraminal stenosis) correlating with C7 dermatomal numbness and reflex loss, and has exhausted all conservative options, ACDF is clinically indicated and medically necessary.

VII. CONCLUSION & AUTHORIZATION DETAILS
The prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED.
