Dear Mr. Reed,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of the first-level appeal submitted on your behalf by Dr. Karen Liu, MD, regarding the prior authorization denial for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551). 

After a comprehensive clinical and regulatory review of the medical records, plan coverage criteria, California state regulations, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has determined that the initial denial was issued in error. Accordingly, the denial is OVERTURNED, and the requested surgical procedure is APPROVED.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND HISTORY
Mr. Thomas Reed is a 52-year-old male with a history of persistent right-sided C6-C7 cervical radiculopathy. 
- Clinical Symptoms: Severe, persistent right C7 dermatomal distribution numbness and pain rated at 7/10 at rest.
- Physical Examination (August 5, 2024): Documented right C7 dermatomal numbness and a diminished right tricep reflex. No bilateral findings, upper motor neuron signs, or clinical evidence of myelopathy were present.
- Diagnostic Imaging (MRI, August 1, 2024): Demonstrated a C6-C7 disc herniation with mild spinal cord contact without measurable compression, no cord signal change, no myelomalacia, and mild right C7 foraminal stenosis.
- Conservative Treatment History:
  1. Physical Therapy: Completed 7 weeks (21 sessions) of supervised physical therapy. The physical therapy discharge summary explicitly documents that a functional plateau was reached, noting no improvement over the final 3 sessions, with radicular symptoms remaining unchanged.
  2. Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 4-month trial of Pregabalin (150mg BID) yielding only partial pain relief with persistent radicular symptoms, and a 3-month trial of Meloxicam (15mg) which was clinically inadequate.
  3. Interventional Treatment: Received a right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 20, 2024, which provided 30% temporary pain relief lasting 3 weeks before symptoms returned to baseline.

II. INITIAL DENIAL RATIONALE
The prior authorization request was initially denied on October 20, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, citing reason code MED-003. The denial stated that the member did not meet criteria for ACDF because:
1. Imaging showed C6-C7 disc herniation with mild cord contact but no measurable compression.
2. There were no myelopathy findings on exam.
3. Physical therapy had not yet been completed to a functional plateau.

III. ANALYSIS OF PLAN CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
A review of the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery (Document: CC-SPINE-2024) demonstrates that the clinical record fully satisfies all requirements for authorization under the cervical radiculopathy pathway:

1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy under Section 2.1(a), defined as nerve root compression with radicular symptoms (pain, numbness, weakness in dermatomal distribution), and cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit under Section 2.1(c). The clinical record documents right C7 dermatomal numbness, a diminished right tricep reflex, and a correlating C6-C7 disc herniation with right C7 foraminal stenosis on MRI.
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement):
   - Section 2.2(a) (Physical Therapy): Requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. The member completed 7 weeks (21 sessions), satisfying this requirement.
   - Section 2.2(b) (Pharmacotherapy): Requires a trial of at least two listed agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The member completed a 4-month trial of Pregabalin (neuropathic agent) and a 3-month trial of Meloxicam (NSAID), satisfying this requirement.
   - Section 2.2(c) (Interventional Treatment): Requires at least one cervical epidural steroid injection. The member received a right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 20, 2024, satisfying this requirement.
3. Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement): Requires current imaging (within 6 months) demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms. The MRI dated August 1, 2024, is within 6 months and demonstrates a C6-C7 disc herniation and right C7 foraminal stenosis, which directly correlates with the member's right C7 radicular symptoms and diminished tricep reflex.

The initial denial's assertion that physical therapy was not completed to a functional plateau is a factual error directly contradicted by the physical therapy discharge summary, which explicitly states a functional plateau was reached. Furthermore, the denial's citation of "no measurable compression" and "no myelopathy findings" represents a clinical category mismatch. The member was submitted under the radiculopathy pathway (Section 2.1(a)), which does not require spinal cord compression or myelopathic findings.

IV. ANALYSIS OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY COMPLIANCE
The initial denial violates several key provisions of the California regulatory framework:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):
   - Under § 10169.5(a)(2), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms.
   - Under § 10169.5(c), a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau.
   The plan's mechanical denial based on physical therapy completion, despite explicit documentation of a functional plateau after 7 weeks and 21 sessions, directly violates this statute.

2. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b) (Defective Denial Notice):
   A denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is defective. By misapplying myelopathy criteria to a radiculopathy presentation and committing a clear factual error regarding physical therapy completion, the initial denial notice was procedurally defective.

3. California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32 (Standard of Review for IMR):
   Under § 1374.32(b), the Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard does not require that the service be consistent with the plan's internal coverage criteria if the service is medically necessary under generally accepted standards of medical practice. Under § 1374.32(c), internal criteria cannot be the sole or determinative basis for a denial when clinical standards support the procedure.

V. ANALYSIS OF CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND STANDARDS
Generally accepted clinical standards strongly support surgical intervention for this patient:

1. AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023) & NASS Clinical Guidelines (2020):
   These guidelines classify single-level ACDF for cervical radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after an adequate conservative trial (6–12 weeks) and correlating imaging findings as a Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) procedure. The member's 7-week physical therapy course, multi-month pharmacotherapy trials, and epidural steroid injection constitute an exhaustive conservative trial.
2. Definition of Functional Plateau:
   Per clinical standards (Section 5 of the Clinical Reference Summary), a functional plateau is defined as less than 10% improvement in validated functional outcome measures over 3 consecutive therapy sessions. The physical therapy discharge summary explicitly documents that the patient did not improve over the final 3 sessions, meeting the clinical definition of a functional plateau.
3. Symptom-Imaging Correlation:
   The clinical standard supports surgery when there is clear correlation between clinical deficits and imaging findings. The member's right C7 dermatomal numbness and diminished right tricep reflex correlate perfectly with the C6-C7 disc herniation and right C7 foraminal stenosis documented on the August 1, 2024 MRI. The "mild cord contact" noted in the denial is a borderline imaging finding that does not preclude surgery for radiculopathy, as the primary pathology is foraminal stenosis and nerve root compression, not spinal cord compression.

VI. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS AND DISPOSITION
Applying the mandatory hierarchy of authority (California State Law > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Plan Criteria):

1. State Law Override: Under CIC § 10169.5, the plan is legally prohibited from denying the procedure based on physical therapy completion because a functional plateau was explicitly documented.
2. Clinical Standard Override: Under CHSC § 1374.32 and DMHC regulations, generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023) govern. These standards support ACDF for persistent radiculopathy with correlating MRI findings after conservative treatment failure, overriding any restrictive or misapplied plan criteria regarding cord compression.
3. Plan Criteria Compliance: Even under the plan's own internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), the member fully satisfies all requirements for the radiculopathy pathway. The initial denial was based on a factual error regarding physical therapy and an incorrect clinical pathway application (applying myelopathy requirements to a radiculopathy case).

CONCLUSION
Because the member has documented C6-C7 cervical radiculopathy with correlating clinical and MRI findings, has exhausted all conservative treatment requirements (including 7 weeks of physical therapy to a documented functional plateau, dual-agent pharmacotherapy, and an epidural steroid injection), and meets all applicable clinical, regulatory, and plan criteria, the initial prior authorization denial is unsubstantiated.

The prior authorization denial for ACDF C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is hereby OVERTURNED. The procedure is approved.
