HealthFirst has completed its first-level internal appeal review of the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) at UCLA Medical Center. 

FINAL DETERMINATION: THE PRIOR AUTHORIZATION DENIAL IS UPHELD.

After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory statutes, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has determined that the requested surgical procedure is not medically necessary at this time. Below is the detailed clinical and legal rationale for this determination.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND RECORD SUMMARY
Ms. Stephanie Oduya is a 44-year-old female with a presenting diagnosis of right-sided C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy. 
- Imaging: A cervical spine MRI dated September 10, 2024, demonstrated a mild-to-moderate disc herniation at C5-C6 with right C6 nerve root contact but without measurable compression. The radiologist noted no spinal cord contact, no spinal cord compression, no T2 signal change, no myelomalacia, and mild right foraminal narrowing.
- Neurological Examination: A physical examination performed on September 15, 2024, documented right shoulder and neck pain rated at 6/10, and mild right C6 dermatomal tingling. However, the exam revealed normal (2+ bilateral and symmetric) bicep reflexes, full (5/5 bilateral) grip strength, no motor weakness, and no upper motor neuron (UMN) signs. Functionally, the patient is working full-time with no documented activity limitations.
- Conservative Treatment: The patient completed 4 weeks of supervised physical therapy (12 sessions total) with mild improvement in pain and no neurological deficits to track. Dr. Walsh elected to halt physical therapy to seek surgical authorization. Pharmacotherapy consisted of a single agent, Naproxen 500mg BID, for 8 weeks, which provided moderate pain relief. No interventional treatments (such as epidural steroid injections) have been attempted.

II. PLAN CLINICAL COVERAGE CRITERIA ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2, a cervical surgical procedure must satisfy ALL of the following criteria to be authorized:

1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1): The member must have a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with nerve root compression and radicular symptoms, cervical myelopathy, or cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit. 
   - Finding: Non-compliant. While the patient has radicular symptoms (pain and mild tingling), the MRI dated September 10, 2024, explicitly states there is "no measurable compression" of the nerve root and "no cord compression." Furthermore, the neurological exam dated September 15, 2024, confirms there is "no weakness" and no objective neurological deficit.
2. Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2): The member must complete a documented course of conservative treatment consisting of:
   - (a) Physical Therapy: Minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. (Non-compliant: The patient completed only 4 weeks of physical therapy before Dr. Walsh halted treatment).
   - (b) Pharmacotherapy: Trial of at least two specified agents (NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each. (Non-compliant: The patient trialed only one agent, Naproxen, for 8 weeks).
   - (c) Interventional Treatment: At least one cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point injection series. (Non-compliant: The patient has had no interventional treatments).
3. Myelopathy Pathway Exception (Section 2.2(A)): This exception modifies conservative treatment requirements for patients with confirmed cervical myelopathy.
   - Finding: Not applicable. The patient's primary diagnosis is radiculopathy, and there is no clinical or imaging evidence of myelopathy (no cord compression, no myelomalacia, and no myelopathic symptoms such as gait disturbance or hyperreflexia).

Because the clinical record fails to satisfy the diagnosis, physical therapy, pharmacotherapy, and interventional treatment requirements, the request does not meet the plan's internal criteria for medical necessity.

III. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY COMPLIANCE ANALYSIS
HealthFirst evaluated whether California statutory protections or regulatory mandates override the plan's internal criteria in this case:

1. Physical Therapy as a Prerequisite (California Insurance Code [CIC] § 10169.5):
   Under CIC § 10169.5(a), a plan cannot condition surgical approval on physical therapy if: (1) PT is documented as contraindicated or unlikely to benefit; (2) the patient reached a documented "functional plateau" without symptom resolution; or (3) there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address.
   - Finding: None of these statutory exceptions are met. Dr. Walsh did not document that PT was contraindicated. The physical therapy summary notes "mild improvement in pain" rather than a functional plateau, and the therapy was halted prematurely by the provider. Finally, there is no objective structural neurological compromise (no cord compression, no nerve root compression, and no motor weakness) that would preclude physical therapy.
2. Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions (DMHC All-Plan Letter [APL] 22-014):
   APL 22-014 mandates that plans waive conservative treatment requirements for time-sensitive neurological conditions where delay risks irreversible harm, specifically citing the presence of myelomalacia or progressive cord compression on MRI.
   - Finding: Not applicable. The MRI dated September 10, 2024, explicitly states "no cord compression" and "no myelomalacia." There is no objective evidence of a time-sensitive, progressive neurological condition that would warrant an emergency waiver under this regulatory guidance.

IV. GENERALLY ACCEPTED STANDARDS OF MEDICAL PRACTICE
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, clinical reviews must align with generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersede internal plan criteria if a conflict exists. HealthFirst reviewed the request against the Joint Guidelines of the American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS) and the Congress of Neurological Surgeons (CNS) (2023 Update):

- Tier 1 (Strong Indication for Surgery): Reserved for moderate-to-severe myelopathy with cord compression, progressive neurological deficits, or myelomalacia on MRI. (The patient does not meet any Tier 1 criteria).
- Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication for Surgery): Includes radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after an adequate 6-to-12-week trial of conservative treatment, or significant functional impairment. (The patient does not meet Tier 2 criteria, as she has only trialed 4 weeks of PT, has no motor deficits, and is working full-time with no activity limitations).
- Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated): Applies to mild radiculopathy that has not yet completed an adequate trial of conservative treatment, or imaging findings without correlating clinical deficits.
   - Finding: The patient's clinical presentation falls squarely under Tier 3. Performing an invasive spinal fusion (ACDF) at this stage, without completing standard conservative care and in the absence of objective neurological deficits, is inconsistent with AANS/CNS guidelines.

V. RESOLUTION OF CLINICAL CONFLICT
HealthFirst acknowledges the strong disagreement between the treating surgeon, Dr. Patrick Walsh, MD (25 years of experience, over 2,000 ACDFs performed), and the plan's independent reviewer, Dr. Maria Rivera, MD (board-certified neurosurgeon, 15 years of practice).

Dr. Walsh appeals on the basis of his clinical judgment, direct physical examination, and a warning that the patient will suffer "permanent disability" without immediate surgery. However, under objective clinical review, Dr. Walsh's assertions are directly contradicted by the documented clinical evidence:
1. The claim of imminent "permanent disability" is unsupported by the neurological exam dated September 15, 2024, which shows normal reflexes, 5/5 grip strength, no weakness, and full functional capacity (working full-time with no limitations).
2. The claim of "significant cervical pathology" requiring immediate decompression is refuted by the objective MRI report dated September 10, 2024, which shows only mild-to-moderate herniation, no cord compression, and no measurable nerve root compression.

While a treating physician's clinical opinion is highly valued, California regulatory standards (CHSC § 1374.32) and clinical guidelines require that medical necessity determinations be based on objective clinical evidence. Dr. Walsh's professional experience and emotional appeal cannot override the objective imaging and physical examination findings, which demonstrate that the patient is clinically stable and has not exhausted safe, non-operative treatment pathways.

VI. CONCLUSION
Because the patient has not completed the required conservative treatment pathway (6 weeks of PT, two pharmacotherapy agents, and one interventional procedure), and because there is no objective clinical or imaging evidence of neurological compromise or myelopathy to justify bypassing these requirements, the requested ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is determined to be not medically necessary. The denial is UPHELD.
