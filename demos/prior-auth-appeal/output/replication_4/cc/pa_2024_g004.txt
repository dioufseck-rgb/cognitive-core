Dear Ms. Oduya,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of the appeal submitted on your behalf by Dr. Patrick Walsh regarding the denial of prior authorization for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551). After a thorough, independent clinical review of all submitted medical records, plan coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical guidelines, HealthFirst has determined that the original denial of prior authorization must be UPHELD. The requested surgical procedure is not medically necessary at this time.

Below is a detailed explanation of the clinical findings, plan criteria, regulatory provisions, and clinical evidence used to reach this determination.

1. CLINICAL HISTORY AND FINDINGS
The clinical records submitted for review demonstrate the following:
- Presenting Diagnosis: Cervical radiculopathy at C5-C6, right-sided.
- Imaging: A cervical spine MRI dated September 10, 2024, revealed a mild-to-moderate disc herniation at C5-C6 with right C6 nerve root contact without measurable compression. There was no spinal cord contact, no spinal cord compression, no T2 signal change, and no evidence of myelomalacia.
- Neurological Examination: A clinical examination dated September 15, 2024, documented right shoulder and neck pain rated 6/10, and mild right C6 dermatomal tingling. However, the examination revealed no objective neurological deficits: bilateral bicep reflexes were normal and symmetric (2+), bilateral grip strength was full (5/5), there was no motor weakness, and there were no upper motor neuron (UMN) signs. Your functional assessment indicated that you are working full-time with no activity limitations.
- Conservative Treatment:
  - Physical Therapy: You completed 4 weeks of supervised physical therapy (total of 12 sessions). The physical therapy summary noted mild improvement in pain and "no neurological deficit to track." Dr. Walsh elected to halt physical therapy to seek surgical authorization.
  - Pharmacotherapy: You trialed one medication, Naproxen 500mg twice daily (BID), for 8 weeks, which provided moderate pain relief.
  - Interventional Treatment: No interventional treatments, such as cervical epidural steroid injections, medial branch blocks, or trigger point injections, have been performed.

2. ANALYSIS OF PLAN COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery — Cervical Procedures (Document: CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2), surgical authorization for CPT 22551 requires that ALL medical necessity criteria be satisfied. The requested procedure fails to meet these criteria as detailed below:
- Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): While you have symptoms of right-sided C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy, your MRI dated September 10, 2024, shows nerve root contact "without measurable compression" and no spinal cord compression.
- Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): You have not completed the required conservative treatment pathway:
  - Section 2.2(a) requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. Your records document only 4 weeks of physical therapy.
  - Section 2.2(b) requires a trial of at least two specified pharmacotherapy agents (such as NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each. Your records document a trial of only one agent (Naproxen) for 8 weeks.
  - Section 2.2(c) requires at least one interventional treatment (cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point injection series). No interventional treatments have been performed.
- Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway): This exception, which reduces the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks and waives the interventional treatment requirement, does not apply. Under Section 2.2, this exception applies only when cervical myelopathy is the primary diagnosis. Your primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, and your MRI explicitly confirms there is no spinal cord compression, no cord contact, and no myelomalacia.
- Section 3.2 (Exclusions): This section mandates denial of authorization when conservative treatment requirements have not been met and the myelopathy exception does not apply. Because you have completed only 4 weeks of physical therapy, trialed only one medication, and had no interventional treatments, your request must be denied under Section 3.2.

3. ANALYSIS OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK
We evaluated your request against the California regulatory framework to ensure compliance with state mandates:
- California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite): Under this statute, a health plan cannot condition surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy if specific exceptions are met. However, none of these exceptions apply to your case:
  1. Under § 10169.5(a)(1), there is no written documentation from your treating physician that physical therapy is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit you.
  2. Under § 10169.5(a)(2), there is no documentation that you reached a "functional plateau" without adequate resolution of symptoms. In fact, your physical therapy records from September 2024 show "mild improvement in pain," and therapy was halted prematurely by Dr. Walsh solely to seek surgical authorization.
  3. Under § 10169.5(a)(3), your condition does not involve objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Your MRI shows no spinal cord compression and no measurable nerve root compression, and your neurological exam shows no motor weakness or deficits.
  Therefore, the plan's requirement to complete the remaining 2 weeks of physical therapy is legally valid and does not violate CIC § 10169.5.
- Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014: This guidance waives conservative treatment requirements for urgent neurological conditions, specifically identifying "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI" as a condition risking irreversible harm. Because your MRI dated September 10, 2024, shows no cord compression and no myelomalacia, and your clinical exam shows no myelopathic symptoms, your condition does not qualify for an urgent waiver under APL 22-014.
- California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 (Standard of Review for IMR): This statute requires that medical necessity be determined in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice, which override restrictive internal plan criteria. As analyzed below, the requested surgery does not meet generally accepted clinical standards.

4. ANALYSIS OF CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND GENERALLY ACCEPTED STANDARDS
Generally accepted standards of medical practice, including the American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Joint Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), do not support surgical intervention for your clinical presentation at this time:
- Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), surgical indications are classified into Tiers. Your presentation falls under Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated) or, at best, Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication).
- Tier 1 (Strong Indication) requires moderate-to-severe myelopathy with objective cord compression, progressive neurological deficits, or myelomalacia on MRI. Your clinical record documents none of these findings.
- Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) for cervical radiculopathy requires persistent symptoms after an adequate conservative trial of 6 to 12 weeks. Because you have only completed 4 weeks of physical therapy, trialed only one medication, and have no objective neurological deficits (such as motor weakness) and no functional limitations (working full-time), a surgical intervention is premature and not supported by clinical evidence.
- The NASS Guidelines (2020) and AO Spine International Guidelines (2021) state that conservative management is highly effective for mild cervical radiculopathy without neurological deficits, and surgical decompression should be reserved for patients who fail a comprehensive 6-to-12-week conservative treatment trial.

5. RESOLUTION OF PHYSICIAN CONFLICT
In his appeal letter dated October 18, 2024, Dr. Patrick Walsh strongly advocated for immediate surgery, citing his 25 years of spine surgery experience, having performed over 2,000 ACDFs, and stating that you will suffer "permanent disability" without immediate authorization. He argued that his clinical judgment as the treating surgeon who examined you directly must take precedence over a remote reviewer.

While HealthFirst respects Dr. Walsh's extensive clinical experience and the value of direct patient examination, we must resolve this conflict by independently evaluating the objective clinical evidence against established medical standards:
- Dr. Walsh's assertion of an urgent risk of "permanent disability" is directly contradicted by the objective clinical findings in your record. Your MRI dated September 10, 2024, shows only mild-to-moderate disc herniation with no spinal cord compression, no cord contact, and no myelomalacia.
- Your neurological examination dated September 15, 2024, confirms that you have normal reflexes (2+), full grip strength (5/5), no motor weakness, and no upper motor neuron signs. Furthermore, you are working full-time with no activity limitations.
- Under generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS, 2023), there is no clinical evidence of neurological urgency or imminent risk of permanent disability in the absence of spinal cord compression, myelomalacia, or progressive motor deficits.
- Therefore, Dr. Walsh's clinical opinion is not supported by the objective clinical record, and the independent review by Dr. Maria Rivera, MD (a board-certified neurosurgeon), which concluded that you do not meet medical necessity criteria for surgery at this time, is clinically correct and consistent with the evidence.

6. CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS
In summary, the prior authorization denial is UPHELD because:
1. You have not completed the required conservative treatment pathway under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 (completed 4 of 6 weeks of physical therapy, trialed 1 of 2 required medications, and had no interventional treatments).
2. You do not meet any regulatory exceptions for physical therapy under CIC § 10169.5 or urgent neurological waivers under DMHC APL 22-014.
3. Surgical intervention is not indicated under generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines, 2023) for mild radiculopathy without a completed conservative trial and in the absence of objective neurological deficits.

To establish medical necessity for this procedure in the future, it is recommended that you:
- Complete the remaining 2 weeks of supervised physical therapy (to reach the 6-week minimum).
- Trial a second class of pharmacotherapy (such as a muscle relaxant or neuropathic agent) for at least 4 weeks, if clinically appropriate.
- Consider an interventional treatment, such as a cervical epidural steroid injection, if symptoms persist.
- Undergo a repeat clinical evaluation if you develop new or worsening neurological symptoms, such as motor weakness or gait disturbances.

7. NOTICE OF RIGHT TO INDEPENDENT MEDICAL REVIEW (IMR)
If you disagree with this appeal decision, you have the right to request an Independent Medical Review (IMR) from the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) or the California Department of Insurance, depending on your plan's regulator.

Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.30, you are entitled to an IMR if your health care service has been denied, modified, or delayed by the plan based on medical necessity.
- The IMR process is free to you and is conducted by independent medical specialists who are not affiliated with HealthFirst.
- Under CHSC § 1374.32, the independent reviewer will determine whether the requested service is medically necessary based on generally accepted standards of medical practice, and is not bound by the plan's internal coverage criteria.
- You may apply for an IMR by submitting an application to the DMHC. You must typically exhaust the plan's internal appeal process before requesting an IMR, which you have now done by completing this first-level appeal.
- For urgent cases involving an imminent and serious threat to your health, you may request an expedited IMR immediately without completing the internal appeal process (CHSC § 1374.30(c)). However, as noted above, your current clinical records do not indicate an urgent neurological condition.

To apply for an IMR, please complete the IMR Application Form enclosed with this letter or apply online at the DMHC website (www.dmhc.ca.gov). You may also contact the DMHC Help Center at 1-888-466-2219 for assistance.
