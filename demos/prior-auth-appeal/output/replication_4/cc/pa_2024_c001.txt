I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The prior authorization denial for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED. 

The initial denial was based on a mechanical application of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, citing that the member failed to complete the standard 6-week physical therapy (PT) requirement (completing 5 weeks) and that the "Myelopathy Exception" did not apply due to the absence of imaging-confirmed cord compression or signal change on MRI. 

Upon comprehensive clinical and regulatory review, this determination finds that:
1. The member has established clinical cervical myelopathy, supported by five objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs on examination and correlating MRI findings of a C5-C6 disc herniation with mild cord contact.
2. Under CC-SPINE-2024 Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway), the PT requirement is reduced to 4 weeks. The member's completion of 5 weeks of PT (15 sessions) fully satisfies this requirement.
3. While the member did not complete the plan's internal pharmacotherapy requirement under Section 2.2(b) (completing only a 10-day Methylprednisolone trial instead of the required 4 weeks), California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 mandates that medical necessity determinations be governed by generally accepted standards of medical practice rather than restrictive internal plan criteria.
4. Under generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine), a 4-week trial of oral corticosteroids is clinically contraindicated due to systemic toxicity risks, and conservative pharmacotherapy is ineffective for established clinical myelopathy. Delaying surgery to complete an unsafe and ineffective drug trial violates clinical standards and risks irreversible neurological deterioration.
5. While Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 does not apply as a mandatory waiver in this case due to the absence of measurable cord compression or myelomalacia on MRI, the denial is overturned independently under CHSC § 1374.32 based on clinical standards.

Accordingly, the requested surgical intervention is medically necessary and is hereby approved.

III. ANALYSIS OF PLAN CRITERIA AND FACTUAL GAPS
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024:
- Section 2.1(b) requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, defined as "spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms (gait disturbance, bilateral upper extremity weakness, hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign, bowel/bladder dysfunction)."
- Section 2.2(a) requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy.
- Section 2.2(b) requires a trial of at least two pharmacotherapy agents (NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
- Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway) modifies these requirements for members with confirmed cervical myelopathy: the PT requirement is reduced to 4 weeks, the interventional treatment requirement (2.2(c)) is waived, but the "Pharmacotherapy requirement (2.2(b)) remains in full."

Factual Compliance Assessment:
1. Physical Therapy: The member completed 5 weeks of PT. Under the standard pathway, this is non-compliant (6 weeks required). Under the Myelopathy Pathway (Exception 2.2(A)), this is fully compliant (4 weeks required).
2. Pharmacotherapy: The member completed a 2-month trial of Baclofen (compliant). However, the member's trial of Methylprednisolone was only 10 days. Under a strict, mechanical reading of Section 2.2(b), the member is non-compliant because the second agent was not trialed for the minimum 4-week duration, and Exception 2.2(A) explicitly states that the pharmacotherapy requirement remains in full.

IV. LEGAL AND CLINICAL JUSTIFICATION FOR OVERTURN
While the member technically failed to meet the plan's internal 4-week duration requirement for the second pharmacotherapy agent under Section 2.2(b), the plan's denial must be overturned because the internal criteria are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice, in violation of California law.

1. Statutory Override of Restrictive Plan Criteria (CHSC § 1374.32)
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(a), a health plan's medical necessity determinations must be made in accordance with "generally accepted standards of medical practice" and must be "clinically appropriate, in terms of type, frequency, extent, site, and duration."
Crucially, CHSC § 1374.32(b) explicitly states: "The IMR standard does NOT require that the service be consistent with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the service meets the standard in subdivision (a), the reviewer SHALL recommend approval regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive."
Therefore, the member's technical non-compliance with the plan's internal 4-week steroid trial requirement is not a valid ground for denial if that requirement is clinically inappropriate and inconsistent with generally accepted medical standards.

2. Clinical Inappropriateness and Safety Risks of a 4-Week Steroid Trial
The plan's requirement of a 4-week trial of oral corticosteroids (Section 2.2(b)) is clinically inappropriate and highly unsafe. According to standard pharmacological and clinical practice, oral corticosteroids (such as Methylprednisolone) are prescribed as short-term tapers (typically 5 to 10 days) to treat acute inflammatory flares. Prolonged continuous use of oral steroids for 4 weeks is clinically contraindicated due to severe systemic risks, including:
- Adrenal suppression and hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis dysfunction.
- Avascular necrosis of the femoral head.
- Severe hyperglycemia, hypertension, and immunosuppression.
Requiring a patient to undergo an unsafe, clinically contraindicated 4-week course of oral steroids to satisfy an administrative coverage criterion violates the clinical appropriateness standard of CHSC § 1374.32(a)(2).

3. Inefficacy of Pharmacotherapy and Risk of Neurological Delay in Myelopathy
Generally accepted clinical guidelines from major spine societies, including the AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine (2023), the North American Spine Society (NASS, 2020), and AO Spine International (2021), establish that cervical myelopathy is a structural, mechanical compression of the spinal cord.
- Inefficacy: Pharmacotherapy (including muscle relaxants and steroids) has no clinical efficacy in reversing mechanical spinal cord compression or resolving objective upper motor neuron deficits (Clinical Reference Summary Section 1.1 & 5).
- Risk of Delay: Delaying surgical decompression to complete ineffective conservative treatments poses a severe risk of irreversible spinal cord injury. Prospective clinical studies demonstrate that each month of delay in surgical treatment for established myelopathy is associated with a decreased likelihood of neurological recovery (Clinical Reference Summary Section 2.3).
- Clinical Guidelines: The AO Spine Clinical Practice Guideline (2021) states: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy [or conservative treatment] prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients... is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." (Clinical Reference Summary Section 2.1).
Therefore, requiring a 4-week trial of an ineffective medication in a patient with established, progressive neurological signs is clinically contraindicated and inconsistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice.

4. Establishment of Clinical Myelopathy Notwithstanding MRI Findings
The plan's initial reviewer asserted that upper motor neuron signs are not conclusive for myelopathy in the absence of imaging-confirmed cord compression. This assertion is clinically incorrect.
According to the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), cervical myelopathy is primarily a clinical diagnosis established by the presence of objective upper motor neuron signs on physical examination. "Imaging-negative myelopathy" is a well-recognized clinical entity where clinical signs of spinal cord dysfunction precede gross structural changes visible on standard MRI sequences (Clinical Reference Summary Section 1.1).
Frances Kim exhibits five distinct, objective UMN signs (bilateral Hoffman, hyperreflexia, sustained ankle clonus, positive finger escape, and wide-based gait) with correlating structural pathology (C5-C6 disc herniation with mild cord contact). This clinical presentation establishes a definitive diagnosis of cervical myelopathy under generally accepted medical standards, satisfying CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1(b) and triggering the Myelopathy Pathway.

5. Explicit Clarification on DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014
We explicitly acknowledge that DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 does not apply as a mandatory waiver in this specific case. APL 22-014 Section II.1 and II.2 restrict its mandatory conservative treatment waivers to cases of "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI." Because Frances Kim's MRI explicitly noted "no measurable cord compression" and "no myelomalacia," the regulatory mandate of APL 22-014 is not triggered.
However, the plan's internal criteria remain fully subject to CHSC § 1374.32. The denial is overturned based on the statutory standard of CHSC § 1374.32 and generally accepted standards of medical practice, which operate independently of APL 22-014.

V. CONCLUSION AND APPROVAL
The requested Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is medically necessary under California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32 and generally accepted standards of medical practice.

The member has established clinical cervical myelopathy with objective neurological deficits. The physical therapy requirement has been met (5 weeks completed, exceeding the 4-week myelopathy pathway requirement). The plan's 4-week oral steroid trial requirement is clinically contraindicated, unsafe, and ineffective for this structural condition. Delaying surgery to complete this trial violates clinical standards and risks permanent neurological harm.

The prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED, and the procedure is APPROVED.
