The HealthFirst Medical Appeals Department has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory statutes, and generally accepted clinical guidelines, HealthFirst has determined that the initial prior authorization denial must be UPHELD.

I. CLINICAL SUMMARY
Ms. Kim is a 58-year-old female presenting with clinical signs of suspected cervical myelopathy. A neurological examination dated September 5, 2024, documented multiple upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including bilateral positive Hoffman sign, bilateral hyperreflexia of the upper extremities, sustained ankle clonus (3 beats) bilaterally, a positive finger escape sign, and a mildly wide-based gait. 

A cervical spine MRI performed on September 1, 2024, demonstrated a C5-C6 disc herniation with mild cord contact. However, the radiologist's report explicitly noted: "No measurable cord compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia. NOTE: Cord contact is present but not quantifiable as compression on standard sequences."

Conservative treatment completed to date includes:
1. Physical Therapy: 5 weeks (15 sessions) under a myelopathy protocol, which was halted by the treating physician citing "myelopathy concern."
2. Pharmacotherapy: A 2-month trial of Baclofen 5mg TID (muscle relaxant) with minimal spasticity relief, and a 10-day trial of a Methylprednisolone taper (oral corticosteroid) with no improvement.
3. Interventional Treatment: None documented (null).

II. PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2, surgical authorization for CPT 22551 requires the satisfaction of specific diagnostic and conservative treatment criteria. The clinical record fails to meet these contractually binding requirements:

1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1(b)): Cervical myelopathy is defined under plan criteria as "spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms," requiring supporting MRI documentation demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms. Ms. Kim's MRI explicitly states there is "no measurable cord compression" and only "mild cord contact," failing to satisfy the diagnostic imaging threshold.

2. Pharmacotherapy Requirement (Section 2.2(b)): The plan requires a documented trial of at least two specified agents (such as NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each. This requirement remains in full force under the Myelopathy Pathway Exception (Section 2.2(A)). While Ms. Kim completed a compliant 2-month trial of Baclofen, her second agent, Methylprednisolone, was only administered as a 10-day taper. This 10-day trial fails to meet the contractually required 4-week minimum duration.

3. Interventional Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2(c)): The plan requires at least one interventional treatment (e.g., cervical epidural steroid injection). Ms. Kim has had no interventional treatments. Under the Myelopathy Pathway Exception (Section 2.2(A)), this requirement is waived only "if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration." No such documentation or clinical rationale exists in the submitted medical record.

4. Physical Therapy Requirement (Section 2.2(a)): The standard pathway requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. Ms. Kim completed 5 weeks. Because she does not meet the criteria for the Myelopathy Pathway Exception (due to the lack of imaging-confirmed cord compression), the standard 6-week requirement applies, and her 5-week trial is insufficient.

III. REGULATORY COMPLIANCE AND APPLICABILITY
We evaluated whether California regulatory provisions supersede the plan's internal criteria to permit an override, and concluded they do not apply to Ms. Kim's clinical profile:

1. DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014: Section II.1 limits its mandate for urgent processing and conservative treatment waivers specifically to "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI." Because Ms. Kim's MRI explicitly states there is "no measurable cord compression" and "no myelomalacia," APL 22-014 does not apply. The plan cannot waive conservative treatment requirements under this regulatory letter.

2. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5: Under Section 10169.5(a)(3), a patient is exempt from physical therapy prerequisites only when there is "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Ms. Kim's MRI shows "no measurable cord compression" and "mild cord contact only," which does not constitute objective structural compromise. Furthermore, the treating physician halted physical therapy at 5 weeks citing "myelopathy concern" without documenting any actual clinical deterioration or objective contraindication. Therefore, the statutory exemption under CIC § 10169.5 does not apply, and the 6-week physical therapy requirement is legally enforceable.

IV. CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARDS
According to the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023) and NASS Guidelines (2020), surgical decompression is strongly recommended as a primary treatment (Tier 1) for moderate-to-severe myelopathy with objective cord compression or myelomalacia on MRI. For patients with mild, stable, or imaging-negative myelopathy, conservative management with close monitoring is the standard of care. Because Ms. Kim has mild, imaging-negative symptoms and has not completed the required conservative trials (specifically the 4-week duration for two pharmacotherapy agents and the interventional treatment trial), upholding the denial is consistent with both plan criteria and generally accepted clinical standards.

V. CONCLUSION AND DISPOSITION
Because the clinical record fails to satisfy the plan's diagnostic and conservative treatment criteria under CC-SPINE-2024, and because no statutory or regulatory exemptions (APL 22-014 or CIC § 10169.5) apply to the patient's imaging profile, the prior authorization denial for ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is UPHELD.
