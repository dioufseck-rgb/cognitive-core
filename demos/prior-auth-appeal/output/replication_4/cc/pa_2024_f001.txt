The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the first-level clinical appeal submitted on November 16, 2024, regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) at UCLA Medical Center. 

After a comprehensive review of the complete clinical record, including newly submitted physical therapy records, plan coverage criteria, California regulatory statutes, and generally accepted clinical practice guidelines, HealthFirst has determined that the initial denial issued on November 2, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, must be OVERTURNED. The requested surgical procedure is hereby APPROVED.

I. CLINICAL BACKGROUND AND HISTORY
Ms. Dorothy James is a 51-year-old female with a history of bilateral cervical radiculopathy at C6-C7. 
- Clinical Presentation & Neurological Exam: A neurological examination dated September 5, 2024, documented bilateral C7 dermatomal symptoms, including bilateral tricep weakness (graded 4/5), diminished bilateral tricep reflexes, and bilateral finger tingling.
- Diagnostic Imaging: A cervical spine MRI dated September 1, 2024, demonstrated a large C6-C7 disc herniation, bilateral C7 nerve root compression, bilateral foraminal stenosis, and mild spinal cord contact without compression.
- Conservative Treatment History:
  1. Physical Therapy (Provider 1): Valley Physical Therapy from June 1, 2024, to June 28, 2024 (4 weeks, 12 sessions). Records show moderate initial improvement followed by a documented functional plateau.
  2. Physical Therapy (Provider 2): Mountain Physical Therapy from July 15, 2024, to August 9, 2024 (4 weeks, 12 sessions). Records submitted with this appeal confirm no improvement in neurological symptoms and a documented functional plateau at discharge.
  3. Pharmacotherapy: Trial of Pregabalin 150mg BID for 3 months (neuropathic agent, resulting in partial relief) and Naproxen 500mg BID for 2 months (NSAID, resulting in inadequate relief).
  4. Interventional Treatment: Bilateral C7 epidural steroid injection performed on September 20, 2024, which provided temporary relief (50% on the left, 30% on the right) before symptoms returned to baseline.

II. ANALYSIS OF COMPLIANCE WITH PLAN COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery — Cervical Procedures (Document: CC-SPINE-2024), authorization for CPT 22551 is approved when all criteria in Section 2 are satisfied:
1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with nerve root compression and radicular symptoms (bilateral tricep weakness, diminished reflexes, tingling). This is supported by a cervical MRI performed within 6 months (September 1, 2024) demonstrating structural pathology (large C6-C7 disc herniation and bilateral C7 nerve root compression) consistent with symptoms. This criterion is MET.
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement):
   - (a) Physical Therapy: The plan requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. While the initial review only verified 4 weeks of therapy from Valley Physical Therapy, the newly submitted records from Mountain Physical Therapy document an additional 4 weeks (July 15, 2024, to August 9, 2024). This establishes a total of 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy directed at the cervical spine, exceeding the 6-week plan requirement. The 17-day gap between the two therapy courses (June 28 to July 15) does not violate plan criteria, as CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a) specifies cumulative duration rather than continuous, uninterrupted treatment. This criterion is MET.
   - (b) Pharmacotherapy: The member completed trials of Pregabalin (neuropathic agent) for 3 months and Naproxen (NSAID) for 2 months. Both trials exceed the plan's requirement of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. This criterion is MET.
   - (c) Interventional Treatment: The member underwent a bilateral C7 epidural steroid injection on September 20, 2024, satisfying the requirement for at least one interventional treatment. This criterion is MET.
3. Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement): The MRI dated September 1, 2024, is within 6 months of the request and demonstrates C6-C7 pathology correlating with bilateral C7 radiculopathy. This criterion is MET.
4. Section 2.4 (Physician Documentation): Dr. Paul Adams provided the necessary clinical documentation, including conservative treatment outcomes, surgical rationale, functional limitations, and risk discussion. This criterion is MET.

Because all clinical coverage criteria under CC-SPINE-2024 are fully satisfied upon review of the complete record, the surgical request is approved under the plan's internal guidelines.

III. ANALYSIS OF COMPLIANCE WITH CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK
Even if the plan's internal criteria were interpreted to require continuous or single-provider physical therapy, California law overrides such restrictive interpretations:
1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite): Under § 10169.5(a)(2), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician or physical therapist documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms. Furthermore, § 10169.5(c) mandates that a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau. 
   Here, Valley Physical Therapy records (received during initial review) explicitly documented a "moderate improvement then plateau" after 4 weeks of treatment. Mechanically denying the request for failing to meet a 6-week threshold violated CIC § 10169.5, as the member had already reached a documented functional plateau.
2. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b) (Denial Notice Requirements): The initial denial notice issued on November 2, 2024, failed to address the documented functional plateau in the Valley PT records and instead mechanically cited the lack of a 6-week duration. Under § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason (including why a documented plateau was ignored) is procedurally defective.
3. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard of Review): Under California law, the standard for medical necessity is governed by generally accepted standards of medical practice, not the plan's internal criteria. If a service is clinically appropriate and effective under clinical standards, it must be approved. As detailed below, the requested ACDF meets this standard.

IV. ANALYSIS OF CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARDS
Generally accepted clinical guidelines, including the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020) and the American Association of Neurological Surgeons / Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Joint Section Guidelines (2023), support surgical intervention for this patient:
1. Surgical Indications: Under AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Section 3.1, the patient presents with a Tier 2 surgical indication: cervical radiculopathy with persistent dermatomal symptoms (bilateral tricep weakness 4/5, diminished reflexes, tingling) and correlating MRI evidence of nerve root compression that has failed to respond to conservative management.
2. Conservative Treatment and Functional Plateau: Clinical evidence demonstrates that extending physical therapy beyond a documented functional plateau does not yield therapeutic benefit and may delay necessary care. The clinical standard (Clinical Reference Summary Section 5) defines a functional plateau as a documented failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement. Both Valley PT and Mountain PT documented that the patient reached a functional plateau. Under these clinical standards, requiring additional physical therapy is clinically inappropriate and inconsistent with evidence-based spine care.

V. CONCLUSION AND DISPOSITION
Based on the clinical records now in evidence, the patient has completed 8 weeks of physical therapy across two providers, trialed two appropriate pharmacotherapy agents, and undergone an interventional epidural steroid injection, all of which failed to resolve her objective neurological deficits. The clinical presentation fully satisfies HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, California Insurance Code § 10169.5, and generally accepted clinical standards.

Therefore, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED.
