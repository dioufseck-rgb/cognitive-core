Dear Mr. Clark and Dr. Nguyen,

The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the prior authorization appeal for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551). Based on a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical documentation, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory statutes, and generally accepted clinical practice guidelines, the initial decision to deny coverage is OVERTURNED. The requested surgical procedure is approved.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & DISPOSITION RATIONALE

The initial denial issued on October 10, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, was based on reason code MED-003, citing "conflicting physical therapy documentation" between Riverside Physical Therapy (6 weeks) and Coastal Physical Therapy (4 weeks). This denial was factually and legally flawed for two primary reasons:
1. The plan's sole stated reason for denial is factually incorrect. Riverside Physical Therapy records, which were received and verified by the plan prior to the denial, documented a complete 6-week course of physical therapy (April 1, 2024, to May 10, 2024; 18 sessions) with a documented functional plateau. Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a), this 6-week course independently and fully satisfies the plan's physical therapy requirement. The clerical discrepancy regarding the duration of the separate Coastal Physical Therapy course was entirely irrelevant to whether the medical necessity criteria for physical therapy were met.
2. Complete records for Coastal Physical Therapy have now been submitted on appeal, documenting an additional 6-week course (June 1, 2024, to July 12, 2024; 18 sessions) with a documented functional plateau. In total, the patient has completed 12 weeks of supervised physical therapy, far exceeding the plan's requirements.

Regarding the plan's pharmacotherapy criteria (Section 2.2(b)), a strict reading of CC-SPINE-2024 reveals that the patient's medication trials did not technically satisfy the internal criteria. Section 2.2(b) requires a trial of at least two distinct categories of medications from the specified list (NSAIDs, Muscle relaxants, Neuropathic agents, Oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The patient trialed Gabapentin (600mg TID for 5 months) and Duloxetine (60mg daily for 3 months). Because both medications belong to the single category of "Neuropathic agents," the patient did not trial medications from two distinct categories.

However, under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 and California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5, this technical deficit under the plan's internal criteria cannot be used to deny coverage. The patient presents with objective structural neurological compromise, including a large C5-C6 disc herniation with severe right C6 nerve root compression, 4/5 right bicep weakness, and an absent right bicep reflex. Under California law, the clinical standard of care governs and overrides internal plan criteria when the plan's criteria are more restrictive. Generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS and NASS guidelines) dictate that requiring further conservative medication trials in the face of persistent, objective motor deficits and reflex loss is clinically inappropriate, medically contraindicated, and risks permanent neurological damage. Therefore, the clinical standard of care is met, and the denial must be overturned.

II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION & EVIDENCE REVIEW

The clinical record establishes the following objective findings:
- Presenting Diagnosis: Cervical radiculopathy C5-C6 with functional impairment.
- Objective Imaging: An MRI of the cervical spine dated August 15, 2024, demonstrates a large C5-C6 disc herniation causing severe right C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis. This satisfies the imaging requirement under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.3.
- Neurological Examination: A physical examination dated August 20, 2024, documents objective neurological deficits, including right C6 dermatomal numbness, right bicep weakness graded at 4/5, an absent right bicep reflex, and a positive right Spurling's sign.
- Conservative Treatment History:
  1. Physical Therapy (Provider 1): Riverside Physical Therapy from April 1, 2024, to May 10, 2024 (6 weeks, 18 sessions). Records show a documented functional plateau with persistent symptoms.
  2. Physical Therapy (Provider 2): Coastal Physical Therapy from June 1, 2024, to July 12, 2024 (6 weeks, 18 sessions). Complete records submitted on appeal document no improvement in neurological deficits and a functional plateau (maximum therapeutic benefit reached).
  3. Pharmacotherapy: Gabapentin 600mg TID for 5 months (partial relief) and Duloxetine 60mg daily for 3 months (partial neuropathic benefit).
  4. Interventional Treatment: Right C6 epidural steroid injection on August 28, 2024, which provided 60% temporary relief but symptoms returned to baseline within 3 weeks. This satisfies CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(c).

III. COMPLIANCE ANALYSIS: PLAN CRITERIA VS. CALIFORNIA LAW

1. Physical Therapy Requirement (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a) vs. CIC § 10169.5)
The plan's initial denial cited "conflicting physical therapy documentation" because the initial submission for Coastal PT only showed 4 weeks of records, while the member claimed 10 weeks total. However, the plan already possessed complete, verified records from Riverside PT documenting 6 weeks (18 sessions) of supervised therapy with a documented plateau. 
Under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a), a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy is required. Riverside PT alone fully and independently satisfied this requirement. Denying the authorization based on a clerical discrepancy in a secondary, unnecessary PT course violates California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(c), which prohibits plans from mechanically applying physical therapy duration requirements without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau. Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), a plan cannot condition surgical approval on further physical therapy once a functional plateau has been documented by the treating therapist or physician. Both Riverside PT and Coastal PT documented a clear functional plateau.

2. Pharmacotherapy Requirement (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b) vs. CHSC § 1374.32)
Under a strict reading of CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b), the patient did not meet the plan's internal pharmacotherapy criteria because both trialed medications (Gabapentin and Duloxetine) fall under the single category of "Neuropathic agents." 
However, California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) explicitly states that the standard for medical necessity does not require consistency with a plan's internal coverage criteria. If a service is medically necessary under generally accepted standards of medical practice, the reviewer must approve the service regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. 
Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) prohibits plans from conditioning surgical approval on conservative treatment where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that conservative therapy cannot address. The patient's 4/5 bicep weakness and absent bicep reflex constitute objective structural neurological compromise. Forcing the patient to undergo an additional 4-week trial of a second class of medication (such as NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, or oral corticosteroids) while they suffer from active, severe nerve root compression and motor weakness is clinically inappropriate and legally non-compliant.

IV. CLINICAL EVIDENCE & STANDARD OF CARE ANALYSIS

According to generally accepted standards of medical practice, including the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020) and the American Association of Neurological Surgeons / Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Joint Section Guidelines (2023):
- Surgical decompression via ACDF (CPT 22551) is strongly indicated (Tier 2 - Reasonable Indication) for patients with cervical radiculopathy who present with persistent radicular pain, objective neurological deficits (such as 4/5 motor weakness and reflex loss), and corresponding structural pathology on MRI (large C5-C6 disc herniation with severe nerve root compression) that has failed to respond to 6 to 12 weeks of conservative management.
- The patient has completed 12 weeks of supervised physical therapy, failed two neuropathic medications, and failed a targeted cervical epidural steroid injection. 
- Clinical evidence demonstrates that delaying surgical decompression in patients with objective motor weakness (4/5 bicep strength) and reflex loss to perform further conservative medication trials is associated with a significantly higher risk of permanent, irreversible neurological deficit (AANS/CNS Guidelines 2023). 
- Therefore, the requested ACDF C5-C6 is medically necessary, clinically appropriate, and represents the standard of care for this patient's clinical presentation.

The prior authorization denial for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C5-C6, one level (CPT 22551) at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center, requested by Dr. Sarah Nguyen, MD, is OVERTURNED.
