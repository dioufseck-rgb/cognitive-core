The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a multi-level anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) surgery. 

Based on a comprehensive review of the clinical record, plan coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has issued a PARTIAL OVERTURN of the initial denial dated November 25, 2024.

The specific authorization and coding determinations are as follows:
1. APPROVED: CPT 22551 (Anterior cervical discectomy and fusion, single interspace) for the C4-C5 level.
2. APPROVED: CPT 22552 (Anterior cervical discectomy and fusion, each additional interspace) for the C5-C6 level.
3. DENIED: Surgical fusion at the C3-C4 level (which would have been billed under an additional unit of CPT 22552) is UPHELD as not medically necessary.

Below is the detailed clinical, regulatory, and coding rationale for each level.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION & SUMMARY OF EVIDENCE
The clinical record demonstrates that Ms. Catherine Mills is a 53-year-old female presenting with multi-level cervical spondylosis with myeloradiculopathy (Clinical Record).
- MRI of the Cervical Spine (2024-10-01):
  * C3-C4: Mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing.
  * C4-C5: Large disc herniation, severe cord compression, and T2 signal change (myelomalacia).
  * C5-C6: Moderate disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression, and foraminal stenosis.
- Neurological Examination (2024-10-05):
  * Myelopathic signs: Bilateral hyperreflexia, bilateral Hoffman sign, and a wide-based gait.
  * Motor deficits: Bilateral deltoid weakness (4/5, C5 level) and left bicep weakness (4-/5, C6 level).
  * Reflexes: Diminished left bicep reflex.
  * C3-C4 level: Neck pain with C3-C4 palpation, but no specific radicular pattern or objective neurological deficit.
- Conservative Treatment History:
  * Physical Therapy: Completed 9 weeks (27 sessions) of supervised physical therapy. The physical therapist documented that myelopathic symptoms remained stable but not improved, C5-C6 radicular symptoms persisted, and the C3-C4 contribution remained unclear (PT Summary).
  * Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 3-month trial of Baclofen 10mg TID (minimal spasticity relief) and a 4-month trial of Pregabalin 300mg BID (partial pain relief) (Pharmacotherapy Record).
  * Interventional Therapy: Received a cervical epidural steroid injection on 2024-10-20, which resulted in "modest improvement in radicular symptoms" but "no change in myelopathy signs" (Interventional Record).

II. LEVEL-BY-LEVEL ANALYSIS & DETERMINATION

1. C4-C5 LEVEL — OVERTURNED (APPROVED FOR CPT 22551)
- Plan Criteria Alignment: Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria Section 2.1(b), the member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, supported by objective MRI findings of severe cord compression and myelomalacia (Section 2.3) and clinical findings of bilateral hyperreflexia, bilateral Hoffman sign, and a wide-based gait. Under Section 2.2 Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway), the physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks (satisfied by 9 weeks) and the interventional requirement is waived if documented as a risk. 
- Regulatory Framework: Under California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter APL 22-014, cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI is classified as a time-sensitive neurological condition with potential for irreversible harm. Standard conservative treatment requirements cannot be mechanically applied to delay surgery. Furthermore, under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3), a health plan cannot condition approval of a surgical procedure on physical therapy where there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (severe cord compression and myelomalacia) that physical therapy cannot address.
- Clinical Standards: Under the NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020) and the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), the presence of moderate-to-severe myelopathy with myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a Tier 1 - Strong Indication for surgical decompression. Delaying surgery in this population is associated with a high risk of permanent neurological deficit.
- Coding Correction: To prevent administrative billing errors and claims rejection, the primary level is authorized under CPT 22551 (Anterior cervical discectomy and fusion; single interspace).

2. C5-C6 LEVEL — OVERTURNED (APPROVED FOR CPT 22552)
- Plan Criteria Alignment: The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy and cervical disc herniation with an objective neurological deficit under Sections 2.1(a) and 2.1(c), supported by MRI findings of moderate left C6 nerve root compression and clinical findings of left bicep weakness (4-/5) and a diminished left bicep reflex.
- Resolution of Conservative Treatment Tension (Addressing Epidural Response):
  * HealthFirst acknowledges that the clinical record notes "modest improvement in radicular symptoms" following the epidural steroid injection on 2024-10-20. Under a strict, mechanical reading of Plan Criteria Section 2.2, it could be argued that conservative treatment was successful and surgery is premature.
  * However, the clinical record explicitly documents that despite this modest temporary improvement, the member's C5-C6 radicular symptoms persist (PT Summary: "C5-C6 radicular symptoms persist") and she continues to exhibit an objective, persistent neurological deficit (left bicep weakness 4-/5 and diminished left bicep reflex).
  * Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), a plan cannot condition surgical approval on physical therapy where the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a "functional plateau" without adequate resolution of symptoms. Under CIC § 10169.5(b), a functional plateau is defined as a documented failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement. Because the member completed 9 weeks (27 sessions) of physical therapy and still has persistent radicular symptoms and objective motor weakness, she has reached a functional plateau.
  * Additionally, under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (C6 nerve root compression with motor weakness) that physical therapy cannot address.
  * Under generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023, Tier 2), surgical decompression is indicated for radiculopathy with persistent symptoms and objective neurological deficits after 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment. The "modest improvement" from the epidural did not result in "adequate resolution of symptoms" or reversal of the objective neurological deficit.
- Coding Correction: Because CPT 22551 is a primary code and can only be billed once per operative session, the C5-C6 level is authorized under CPT 22552 (Anterior cervical discectomy and fusion; each additional interspace) as an add-on code to CPT 22551.

3. C3-C4 LEVEL — UPHELD (DENIED)
- Plan Criteria Alignment: Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria Section 3.1, surgical authorization is excluded when requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy. Under Section 3.3, authorization is excluded when imaging does not demonstrate pathology at the proposed level consistent with the clinical presentation.
- Clinical Findings: The MRI shows only a mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing at C3-C4. The neurological exam notes "unclear - neck pain with C3-C4 palpation but no specific radicular pattern." The physical therapy summary notes "C3-C4 contribution unclear." There is no objective evidence of radiculopathy or myelopathy originating from the C3-C4 level.
- Clinical Standards: Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), this presentation falls under Tier 3 - Surgery Not Indicated (imaging findings without correlating symptoms, or axial neck pain without radiculopathy/myelopathy).
- Conclusion: The denial of surgical fusion at the C3-C4 level is UPHELD.

IV. NOTICE OF CALIFORNIA INDEPENDENT MEDICAL REVIEW (IMR) RIGHTS
Because a portion of your requested service was denied (the C3-C4 level), you have the right to appeal this decision through the California Independent Medical Review (IMR) process.

Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.30:
- You may submit an application for an Independent Medical Review (IMR) to the Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) if you believe the decision to deny the C3-C4 level was incorrect.
- The IMR is an independent review conducted by non-affiliated medical specialists who will determine whether the denied service is medically necessary under generally accepted standards of medical practice (CHSC § 1374.32).
- The DMHC will make a decision within 30 days of receiving your completed application (or within 3 days for urgent cases involving an imminent threat to your health).
- If the IMR reviewer decides in your favor, HealthFirst is legally required to authorize the service.

To apply for an IMR, please complete the IMR Application Form enclosed with this letter or apply online at www.dmhc.ca.gov. You may also contact the DMHC Help Center at 1-888-466-2219 for assistance.
