### I. LEVEL-BY-LEVEL CLINICAL & REGULATORY ANALYSIS

#### 1. C4-C5 Level: OVERTURNED (APPROVED)
*   **Clinical Presentation & Imaging:** The MRI dated October 1, 2024, demonstrates a large disc herniation at C4-C5 causing severe spinal cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia). The neurological exam dated October 5, 2024, confirms objective myelopathy findings, including bilateral hyperreflexia, bilateral Hoffman's sign, a wide-based gait, and bilateral deltoid weakness (4/5) at the C5 level.
*   **Plan Criteria Alignment:** This level satisfies HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(b) (Cervical Myelopathy) and Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement). Under Section 2.2, Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway), the conservative treatment requirements are modified because myelopathy is the primary diagnosis. The physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks (the patient completed 9 weeks), and the interventional treatment requirement is waived if it poses a risk of neurological deterioration.
*   **Regulatory Framework Override:** Under California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI is classified as an urgent, time-sensitive neurological condition with potential for irreversible harm. APL 22-014, Section II.2, mandates that plans shall not require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgery in these cases. Furthermore, California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) prohibits conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (such as myelomalacia) that physical therapy cannot address.
*   **Clinical Standards:** Under the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023 Update) and NASS Guidelines (2020), moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia represents a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) for surgical decompression. Delaying surgery in this setting is clinically contraindicated due to the high risk of permanent neurological deficit.
*   **Conclusion:** The initial denial of the C4-C5 level is overturned. Surgical intervention at this level is medically necessary and mandated by California regulatory standards.

#### 2. C5-C6 Level: OVERTURNED (APPROVED)
*   **Clinical Presentation & Imaging:** The MRI dated October 1, 2024, demonstrates a moderate disc herniation at C5-C6 with moderate left C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis. The neurological exam dated October 5, 2024, documents left bicep weakness (4-/5) and a diminished left bicep reflex, which correlates directly with a C6 radiculopathy.
*   **Plan Criteria Alignment:** This level satisfies HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(a) (Cervical Radiculopathy) and Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement). The patient has also fully satisfied the conservative treatment requirements under Section 2.2:
    *   *Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)):* Completed 9 weeks (27 sessions) of supervised physical therapy, exceeding the 6-week minimum.
    *   *Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)):* Completed trials of Baclofen 10mg TID (muscle relaxant) for 3 months and Pregabalin 300mg BID (neuropathic agent) for 4 months, satisfying the requirement for two agents for at least 4 weeks each.
    *   *Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)):* Received a cervical epidural steroid injection on October 20, 2024, satisfying the interventional requirement.
*   **Regulatory Framework Alignment:** Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), a health plan cannot condition surgical approval on further physical therapy once a patient has completed a course of therapy and the treating physician documents that the patient has reached a "functional plateau" without adequate resolution of symptoms. The physical therapy summary notes that while the patient completed 9 weeks of PT, her C5-C6 radicular symptoms persisted, representing a clinical functional plateau.
*   **Clinical Standards:** Under the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023 Update), cervical radiculopathy with persistent objective neurological deficits (weakness and reflex changes) that has failed to respond to a comprehensive 9-week conservative trial represents a Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) for surgical decompression.
*   **Conclusion:** The initial denial of the C5-C6 level is overturned. The patient has met all plan criteria, regulatory requirements, and clinical standards for surgical intervention at this level.

#### 3. C3-C4 Level: UPHELD (DENIED)
*   **Clinical Presentation & Imaging:** The MRI dated October 1, 2024, shows only a mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing at C3-C4. The neurological exam dated October 5, 2024, notes that the clinical contribution of this level is "unclear," documenting neck pain with C3-C4 palpation but "no specific radicular pattern." The physical therapy summary also notes that the C3-C4 clinical contribution is unclear.
*   **Plan Criteria Alignment:** This level fails to meet HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024. Under Section 2.1, there is no documented cervical radiculopathy or myelopathy correlating to the C3-C4 level. Under Section 2.3, the imaging findings (mild disc bulge, minimal narrowing) are not consistent with the proposed surgical level's symptoms. Furthermore, Section 3.1 explicitly excludes surgical authorization when requested solely for axial neck pain (such as pain on palpation) without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy.
*   **Regulatory Framework Alignment:** No regulatory overrides or exceptions apply to the C3-C4 level. The protections of DMHC APL 22-014 and CIC § 10169.5 do not extend to spinal levels that lack objective structural compromise or correlating neurological deficits.
*   **Clinical Standards:** Under the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023 Update) and NASS Guidelines (2020), surgical intervention for mild imaging findings without correlating radicular or myelopathic symptoms is classified as Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated). Surgical fusion at an asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic level is not supported by clinical evidence and increases the risk of adjacent segment disease.
*   **Conclusion:** The initial denial of the C3-C4 level is upheld. There is insufficient clinical evidence to establish medical necessity for surgical intervention at this level.

### II. PROCEDURAL DEFECT NOTE
Pursuant to California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a), when a health plan issues a denial based on medical necessity, it must provide a written notice that includes the specific criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the denial. The initial denial notice issued on November 25, 2024, failed to cite the specific sections of the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) applied to each level, rendering the initial notice procedurally defective under California law. This defect has been corrected in this appeal determination through the detailed level-by-level analysis provided above.

### III. NOTICE OF INDEPENDENT MEDICAL REVIEW (IMR) RIGHTS

Because a portion of your prior authorization request (the C3-C4 level) has been upheld (denied) on appeal, you have the right to request an Independent Medical Review (IMR) through the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC).

**What is an Independent Medical Review?**
An IMR is an evaluation of your case by independent, board-certified specialist physicians who are not associated with HealthFirst. If the IMR reviewer decides that the C3-C4 surgical level is medically necessary, HealthFirst is legally required to approve the service. The IMR process is free to you.

**How to Apply for an IMR:**
1.  **Submit an Application:** You may apply for an IMR by completing the Independent Medical Review/Complaint Form envelope provided with this letter or by applying online at the DMHC website (www.dmhc.ca.gov).
2.  **Timeframe:** You must submit your application for an IMR within 180 days of receiving this appeal determination letter.
3.  **Expedited IMR:** If your treating physician determines that your condition poses an imminent and serious threat to your health (such as severe pain or risk of irreversible neurological damage), you may request an expedited IMR, which is decided within 3 days of the DMHC receiving your application.

For questions regarding your IMR rights, you may contact the DMHC Help Center at 1-888-466-2219 or TDD at 1-877-688-9891.
