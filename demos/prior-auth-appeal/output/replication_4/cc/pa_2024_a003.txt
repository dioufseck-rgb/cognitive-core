The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria, California regulatory mandates, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, the initial denial issued on October 15, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, is hereby OVERTURNED. 

Prior authorization for CPT 22551 is APPROVED for performance at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center by Dr. Michael Torres, MD.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The initial denial was based on a mechanical application of standard conservative treatment requirements, citing that the member completed only 5 of the required 6 weeks of physical therapy and had no record of a cervical epidural steroid injection. This determination was erroneous and is overturned on three independent grounds:
1. Plan Criteria Compliance: The member meets the criteria for the "Myelopathy Pathway" under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2 Exception 2.2(A), which reduces the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks (satisfied by the member's 5 weeks) and waives the interventional requirement due to documented medical contraindications.
2. California Regulatory Mandates: Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter APL 22-014, health plans are prohibited from conditioning surgical approval on conservative treatment (including physical therapy and injections) for patients with progressive cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia, as doing so risks irreversible neurological harm.
3. Generally Accepted Standards of Medical Practice: Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), clinical standards of medical practice supersede restrictive internal plan criteria. Major spine societies (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) classify progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia as a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) surgical emergency where conservative treatment is not recommended and delay is clinically contraindicated.

II. CLINICAL FINDINGS & EVIDENCE REVIEWED
The review was based on the following clinical documentation enclosed in the appeal record:
- MRI Report (Cedars-Sinai Medical Center, dated 2024-08-20): Performed using a pacemaker-compatible protocol, demonstrating severe central cord compression at C4-C5 with confirmed T2 signal change (myelomalacia), indicating progressive spinal cord injury.
- Neurological Examination (dated 2024-08-25): Documented bilateral lower extremity spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor skills in bilateral hands, and a positive Lhermitte sign.
- Physical Therapy Records: Documented 5 weeks (15 sessions) of supervised physical therapy. Therapy was discontinued due to pacemaker precautions limiting specific modalities and a clinical determination by the treating physician that further therapy was failing to address, and potentially exacerbating, the member's progressive neurological decline.
- Anesthesiology Consultation Letter (Dr. Rivera, dated 2024-09-10): Documented that a fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injection is strictly contraindicated due to the patient's permanent pacemaker (Boston Scientific, implanted 2019).
- Pharmacotherapy Records: Documented trials of Baclofen 10mg TID for 3 months (partial spasticity relief) and two 10-day courses of oral Methylprednisolone taper (temporary improvement only).

III. DETAILED ANALYSIS & JUSTIFICATION FOR OVERTURN

1. Compliance with HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)
The initial reviewer applied the standard conservative treatment criteria under Section 2.2, which requires 6 weeks of physical therapy and at least one interventional treatment (such as an epidural steroid injection). However, the reviewer failed to apply the mandatory "Myelopathy Pathway" exception:
- Section 2.1(b) (Diagnosis): The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, supported by objective clinical findings (gait/spasticity, hyperreflexia, positive Lhermitte sign) and imaging demonstrating severe C4-C5 cord compression.
- Section 2.2 Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway): For members with confirmed cervical myelopathy as the primary diagnosis, the physical therapy requirement is reduced to a minimum of 4 weeks. The member completed 5 weeks of physical therapy, fully satisfying this modified requirement.
- Section 2.2 Exception 2.2(A) (Interventional Waiver): The interventional treatment requirement (Section 2.2(c)) is waived if the treating physician documents that such treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration or is contraindicated. Dr. Torres and Dr. Rivera (Anesthesiology) documented that a cervical epidural steroid injection is medically contraindicated due to the member's permanent pacemaker. 
Therefore, the member fully satisfied the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria when the correct clinical pathway was applied.

2. Compliance with California Regulatory Framework
Even if the plan's internal criteria did not contain a myelopathy exception, the initial denial directly violates California insurance law:
- California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1) & (3): A health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the treating physician documents that physical therapy is unlikely to benefit the member, or where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (such as myelomalacia) that physical therapy cannot address. Under § 10169.5(c), mechanical application of a minimum-week physical therapy requirement is strictly prohibited.
- DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Section II.2): For time-sensitive neurological conditions where treatment delay risks irreversible harm, plans shall not require completion of conservative treatment when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (confirmed myelomalacia on the 2024-08-20 MRI) and the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression.
- CHSC § 1374.31(b): The plan's initial denial notice was procedurally defective as it failed to apply the correct clinical criteria (the Myelopathy Pathway) and ignored documented medical contraindications, applying standard criteria mechanically.

3. Alignment with Generally Accepted Standards of Medical Practice
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), the standard for determining medical necessity must align with generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersede restrictive internal plan guidelines:
- AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) & AO Spine Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021): These guidelines establish that moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia (T2 signal change) constitutes a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) for surgical decompression. 
- Clinical Evidence on PT Futility: The Cochrane Review (2021) and NASS Guidelines (2020) demonstrate that physical therapy has no proven efficacy in arresting neurological progression in patients with moderate-to-severe myelopathy. Delaying surgical decompression to complete arbitrary conservative trials is associated with a significantly decreased likelihood of neurological recovery and a high risk of permanent, irreversible spinal cord damage.

IV. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS
In accordance with California regulatory standards, where a conflict exists between a health plan's internal coverage criteria, generally accepted clinical standards, and state law, the hierarchy of authority dictates that California State Law and Generally Accepted Clinical Standards govern the determination. 
In this case, the plan's initial denial relied on a mechanical application of standard criteria that is explicitly prohibited by CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014, and is clinically contraindicated under AANS/CNS and AO Spine guidelines. Accordingly, the conflict is resolved in favor of state law and clinical standards, requiring an immediate overturn of the denial.

The prior authorization denial for CPT 22551 is OVERTURNED.
