Dear Ms. Osei and Dr. Torres,

The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the appeal submitted on October 28, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 (CPT 22551). 

After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory statutes, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has determined that the initial denial was incorrect. The prior authorization denial is hereby OVERTURNED, and the requested surgical procedure is APPROVED.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The initial denial issued on October 15, 2024, cited a failure to complete standard conservative treatment requirements under Section 2.2 of the plan's clinical criteria, specifically noting that only 5 of the required 6 weeks of physical therapy had been completed and that no cervical epidural steroid injection was on record. 

This appeal determination overturns that decision based on three independent, legally binding, and clinically sound grounds:
1. Compliance with Plan Criteria: The member qualifies for "EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY" of the plan's own clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024). Under this exception, the physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks (the member completed 5 weeks), and the interventional treatment requirement is waived due to a documented medical contraindication (permanent pacemaker).
2. California Regulatory Mandates: Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, health plans are legally prohibited from conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy or interventional trials when a member has a documented medical contraindication, has reached a functional plateau, or presents with objective structural neurological compromise (such as myelomalacia) that conservative treatment cannot address.
3. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards: Major national spine societies, including the American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS), Congress of Neurological Surgeons (CNS), and the North American Spine Society (NASS), establish that progressive cervical myelopathy with confirmed myelomalacia is a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) surgical emergency. Delaying surgery to complete conservative therapy in this clinical scenario is medically contraindicated and poses a severe risk of irreversible spinal cord injury.

II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND HISTORY
Ms. Patricia Osei is a 55-year-old female with a history of a permanent pacemaker (implanted in 2019, Boston Scientific), Type 2 diabetes, and hypertension. She presents with progressive cervical myelopathy at C4-C5. 

A neurological examination performed by Dr. Michael Torres on August 25, 2024, documented significant objective neurological deficits, including:
- Bilateral lower extremity spasticity and hyperreflexia.
- Impaired fine motor skills in bilateral hands.
- A positive Lhermitte's sign (indicating active spinal cord pathology).

A cervical spine MRI performed on August 20, 2024, utilizing a pacemaker-compatible protocol, demonstrated:
- Severe central cord compression at the C4-C5 level.
- Confirmed T2-weighted signal changes within the spinal cord, diagnostic of myelomalacia, indicating progressive, active spinal cord injury.

Ms. Osei's conservative treatment history includes:
- Physical Therapy: Completed 5 weeks (15 sessions) of supervised physical therapy. Therapy was discontinued due to pacemaker precautions limiting specific modalities and a clinical determination by her physician that further therapy was failing to address her progressive neurological decline.
- Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 3-month trial of Baclofen 10mg TID (resulting in only partial spasticity relief) and two separate 10-day courses of a methylprednisolone taper (providing only temporary, transient improvement). This satisfies the plan's requirement for a trial of at least two qualifying agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
- Interventional Treatment: Not performed because it is strictly medically contraindicated. An anesthesiology consultation performed by Dr. Rivera on September 10, 2024, formally concluded that a fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injection is contraindicated due to the patient's permanent pacemaker. This written contraindication was documented in the clinical chart.

III. DETAILED ANALYSIS AND FINDINGS

1. COMPLIANCE WITH HEALTHFIRST CLINICAL COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
The initial reviewer, Dr. Alan Foster, MD, applied the standard conservative treatment criteria under Section 2.2 mechanically, failing to apply the plan's explicit myelopathy provisions.
- Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): Ms. Osei has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy under Section 2.1(b), supported by objective clinical findings (gait/spasticity, bilateral hand weakness, hyperreflexia, positive Lhermitte's sign) and a current MRI (within 6 months) showing severe cord compression.
- Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Exception): Because cervical myelopathy is the primary diagnosis, "EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY" is triggered. 
  - Under Exception 2.2(A), the physical therapy requirement is reduced from 6 weeks to a minimum of 4 weeks. Ms. Osei completed 5 weeks of physical therapy, fully satisfying this requirement.
  - Under Exception 2.2(A), the interventional treatment requirement (Section 2.2(c)) is waived if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration or is otherwise contraindicated. Dr. Torres and Dr. Rivera (Anesthesiology) documented that a cervical epidural steroid injection is contraindicated due to her permanent pacemaker.
  - The pharmacotherapy requirement under Section 2.2(b) was fully met via trials of Baclofen and oral corticosteroids (methylprednisolone).
Therefore, Ms. Osei fully satisfied the plan's internal medical necessity criteria. The initial denial under Section 2.2 was a misapplication of the plan's own policy.

2. COMPLIANCE WITH CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK
Even if the plan's internal criteria did not contain a myelopathy exception, the initial denial is legally unenforceable under California law:
- California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1) & (3): A health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member, or where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (such as myelomalacia) that physical therapy cannot address. Ms. Osei has severe structural cord compression and myelomalacia, which physical therapy cannot anatomically resolve.
- CIC § 10169.5(c): This statute explicitly mandates that a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or has neurological urgency. The initial reviewer's mechanical denial based on "5 weeks completed, 6 required" directly violates this statutory prohibition.
- DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014: This regulatory directive establishes that cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a time-sensitive neurological condition with potential for irreversible harm. APL 22-014 dictates that plans shall not require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (confirmed on Ms. Osei's 2024-08-20 MRI) and the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression.
- California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b): Under the California Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard, "medically necessary" services must be approved if they are in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice, regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.

3. ALIGNMENT WITH GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARDS
Under CHSC § 1374.32 and DMHC APL 22-014, generally accepted clinical standards govern over restrictive plan criteria.
- AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023): Surgical decompression (ACDF) is a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) recommendation for patients presenting with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy, objective cord compression, and myelomalacia on MRI.
- North American Spine Society (NASS) Guidelines (2020): Conservative treatment is only appropriate as an initial trial for mild, stable myelopathy. For progressive myelopathy with objective neurological deficits, surgical decompression is the recommended primary treatment.
- AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021): There is no high-quality clinical evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy or interventional injections prior to surgery for patients with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery to complete arbitrary conservative trials is associated with progressive, irreversible spinal cord injury and significantly worse long-term neurological outcomes.
- Cochrane Review (2021): Confirms that physical therapy does not produce meaningful functional improvement in patients with moderate-to-severe myelopathy. Applying radiculopathy-derived conservative treatment timelines (such as 6 weeks of PT or epidural injections) to a patient with spinal cord compression (myelopathy) is a clinical category error.

IV. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION
Based on the plan's internal "EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY," the statutory protections of CIC § 10169.5, the regulatory mandates of DMHC APL 22-014, and the overwhelming clinical evidence establishing the urgency of surgical decompression for progressive myelomalacia, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED.
