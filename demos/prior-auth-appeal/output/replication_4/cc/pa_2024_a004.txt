The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the first-level internal appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's internal coverage criteria, California regulatory statutes, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has determined that the initial denial issued on November 18, 2024, is OVERTURNED. Full prior authorization is hereby APPROVED for the requested surgical procedure.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND RECORD SUMMARY
The clinical record demonstrates that Mr. Robert Chang, a 38-year-old male, presented to the Emergency Department on November 10, 2024, with an acute onset of rapidly progressive neurological deficits over the preceding 72 hours. The patient had no prior history of cervical spine disease. 

Upon presentation, a comprehensive neurological examination revealed:
- Bilateral upper extremity (arm) weakness graded at 2/5.
- Bilateral lower extremity (leg) weakness graded at 3/5.
- Generalized hyperreflexia in all extremities.
- Bilateral positive Babinski signs.
- New-onset urinary urgency.
These clinical findings are diagnostic of an acute incomplete spinal cord syndrome (cervical myelopathy).

An emergency cervical spine MRI performed on November 10, 2024, revealed:
- Severe central cord compression at the C5-C6 level.
- Extensive T2-weighted hyperintensity throughout the spinal cord at the level of compression, consistent with active myelomalacia and acute spinal cord injury.

Neurosurgery was emergently consulted. The patient was admitted to the hospital and initiated on the standard acute spinal cord injury protocol, receiving intravenous (IV) methylprednisolone for 24 hours. Dr. Elena Marchetti, MD, documented that the patient has an acute incomplete cervical cord syndrome with extensive myelomalacia, representing a surgical emergency. Dr. Marchetti explicitly declared that physical therapy is absolutely contraindicated, as any mechanical manipulation or delay in surgical decompression risks converting the incomplete cord syndrome into complete, permanent quadriplegia.

II. PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery — Cervical Procedures (Document: CC-SPINE-2024), Section 2.1(b) requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, supported by an MRI demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms. Mr. Chang's clinical presentation and emergency MRI of November 10, 2024, fully satisfy these diagnostic and imaging requirements.

However, Section 2.2 of CC-SPINE-2024 requires a documented course of conservative treatment prior to surgical authorization, consisting of:
(a) Physical therapy: minimum 6 weeks (reduced to 4 weeks under the Myelopathy Pathway Exception 2.2(A)).
(b) Pharmacotherapy: trial of at least two agents (NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
(c) One interventional treatment: cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point injection series (waived under Exception 2.2(A) if documented to pose a risk of neurological deterioration).

As established, Mr. Chang has completed 0 weeks of physical therapy, 0 weeks of outpatient pharmacotherapy, and has had no interventional injections. Consequently, the patient does not meet the plan's internal prospective criteria under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 or Exception 2.2(A), as the pharmacotherapy requirement (Section 2.2(b)) remains contractually required in full under the internal policy.

III. LEGAL AND REGULATORY RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS
To resolve the conflict between the plan's internal criteria and the clinical reality of this acute surgical emergency, HealthFirst applies the hierarchy of authority mandated by California law, which places state statutory and regulatory requirements above internal plan guidelines.

1. Resolution of the Physical Therapy Requirement (California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(3))
Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3), a health insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... [t]he condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
The emergency MRI dated November 10, 2024, provides objective, indisputable evidence of severe structural neurological compromise (severe C5-C6 central cord compression and extensive myelomalacia). Furthermore, Dr. Marchetti documented in writing that physical therapy is absolutely contraindicated and would cause immediate, irreversible harm. 
Therefore, pursuant to CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), the plan's internal requirement for physical therapy (Section 2.2(a)) is legally waived and cannot be applied to deny this request.

2. Resolution of the Pharmacotherapy and Interventional Treatment Requirements (CHSC § 1374.32 and DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3)
HealthFirst acknowledges that the physical therapy waiver under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) does not extend to the plan's pharmacotherapy or interventional treatment requirements. HealthFirst also acknowledges that the regulatory safe harbor under DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 Section II.2 is not directly triggered, as that section requires that "conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression" (subdivision b), whereas this patient has an acute onset (72 hours) with no prior conservative treatment attempted.

However, the conflict regarding the non-PT requirements (pharmacotherapy and injections) is resolved under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 and DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3:
- DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3 explicitly mandates: "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for conditions identified in this guidance. Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs." Cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia is specifically identified in Section II.1 of the APL as a time-sensitive neurological condition with potential for irreversible harm.
- CHSC § 1374.32(a) defines "medically necessary" services as those that a provider, exercising prudent clinical judgment, would provide in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice and that are clinically appropriate in terms of type, extent, and duration. Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the standard for medical necessity does not require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria if those criteria are more restrictive than generally accepted standards.

As detailed in Section IV below, generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice strongly contraindicate conservative treatment (including outpatient pharmacotherapy and interventional injections) for acute, progressive cervical myelopathy with active myelomalacia. Because the plan's internal requirements for a 4-week trial of two pharmacotherapy agents (Section 2.2(b)) and interventional injections (Section 2.2(c)) directly conflict with generally accepted clinical standards, DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3 and CHSC § 1374.32 dictate that the clinical standard must govern. The plan's internal non-PT conservative treatment requirements are therefore waived.

3. Clarification of Emergency Prior Authorization (CHSC § 1371.4)
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1371.4, emergency services and care do not require prior authorization, and plans are prohibited from requiring prior authorization for emergency services. 
Because the provider submitted a pre-service prior authorization request, the plan is technically required to review the request prospectively. However, in performing this prospective review, the plan cannot mechanically apply its elective criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) to an acute, emergency presentation. Doing so would violate CHSC § 1374.32 and DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3 by demanding that the provider perform medically contraindicated and dangerous conservative trials on an unstable patient. The plan must evaluate the pre-service request under the clinical standards applicable to the patient's acute presentation, which dictate immediate surgical decompression.

IV. GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS OF MEDICAL PRACTICE
The clinical standard of care for cervical myelopathy is established by major national and international spine societies, including the American Association of Neurological Surgeons / Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine (2023 Update), the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), and the AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021).

1. Surgical Indications for Myelopathy with Myelomalacia
Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), surgical decompression is a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) and the recommended primary treatment for patients with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy, objective cord compression, and myelomalacia (cord signal change) on MRI. 
The AO Spine Guidelines (2021) state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy [or other conservative treatments] prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."

2. Contraindication of Conservative Treatment in Acute Myelopathy
For a patient presenting with acute, rapidly progressive incomplete spinal cord syndrome (2/5 arm strength, 3/5 leg strength, hyperreflexia, Babinski, and urinary urgency) and active myelomalacia, conservative treatment is clinically contraindicated:
- Pharmacotherapy: A 4-week trial of NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral steroids (as required by CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b)) has no therapeutic efficacy in reversing mechanical spinal cord compression. Forcing a minimum 8-week delay to trial two agents would guarantee irreversible spinal cord injury and permanent neurological deficits.
- Interventional Injections: Cervical epidural steroid injections or medial branch blocks (Section 2.2(c)) are highly contraindicated in the presence of severe central cord compression and active myelomalacia, as the volume of the injectate can acutely increase intrathecal pressure, risking immediate and complete spinal cord injury (paralysis).

3. Risk of Delay
Prospective clinical evidence demonstrates that preoperative myelomalacia is the strongest predictor of incomplete neurological recovery, and each month of delay in surgical decompression for moderate-to-severe myelopathy is associated with a statistically significant decrease in the likelihood of neurological recovery. In an acute, progressive setting, delaying surgery to satisfy elective conservative treatment criteria is inconsistent with the standard of care and constitutes a severe threat to patient safety.

V. FINAL DETERMINATION
The clinical record establishes that Mr. Robert Chang has an acute, progressive incomplete spinal cord syndrome with severe C5-C6 central cord compression and active myelomalacia. 
- The physical therapy requirement is legally waived under California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(3).
- The pharmacotherapy and interventional treatment requirements are waived under DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3 and CHSC § 1374.32, as they directly conflict with generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021), which mandate immediate surgical decompression and contraindicate conservative treatment trials in this acute setting.
- While pre-service prior authorization is reviewed prospectively, the plan is legally bound to apply clinical standards that prioritize patient safety and prevent irreversible neurological harm.

Accordingly, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED, and the request for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is APPROVED.
