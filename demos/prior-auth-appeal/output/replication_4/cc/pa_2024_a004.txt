Dear Mr. Chang and Dr. Marchetti,

HealthFirst Health Plan's Appeal Determination Services has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an emergency Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's internal coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards of care, the initial denial has been OVERTURNED. The requested emergency surgical procedure is approved.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND RECORD SUMMARY
The clinical record establishes that the member, Robert Chang, a 38-year-old male, presented to the emergency department on November 10, 2024, with an acute onset of rapidly progressive neurological deficits over the preceding 72 hours. 

The neurological examination documented on November 10, 2024, revealed severe deficits consistent with an incomplete spinal cord syndrome:
- Bilateral upper extremity (arm) weakness graded at 2/5;
- Bilateral lower extremity (leg) weakness graded at 3/5;
- Diffuse hyperreflexia in all extremities and bilateral positive Babinski signs;
- Acute urinary urgency.

An emergency cervical spine MRI performed on November 10, 2024, demonstrated severe central cord compression at the C5-C6 level accompanied by extensive T2-weighted intramedullary hyperintensity throughout the spinal cord. The radiologist and consulting neurosurgeon interpreted these findings as extensive myelomalacia, indicating acute, active structural spinal cord injury. 

The member was emergently admitted, and the standard acute spinal cord injury protocol was initiated, consisting of a 24-hour inpatient course of intravenous (IV) methylprednisolone. Dr. Elena Marchetti, MD (Neurosurgery), submitted an emergency prior authorization request for a single-level ACDF at C5-C6 (CPT 22551), declaring that physical therapy was absolutely contraindicated and that any delay in surgical decompression posed an imminent risk of permanent, complete quadriplegia. 

The clinical record confirms that the member has completed zero (0) weeks of physical therapy and has not undergone any outpatient trials of oral pharmacotherapy (such as NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, or neuropathic agents) prior to this presentation.

II. INITIAL DENIAL AND PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS
On November 18, 2024, HealthFirst Health Plan denied the request under clinical coverage criteria document CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. The denial cited the member's failure to complete the required conservative treatment course, specifically noting that 0 weeks of physical therapy, 0 weeks of pharmacotherapy, and 0 interventional procedures had been documented.

Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024:
- Section 2.2 requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy, a minimum 4-week trial of at least two pharmacotherapy agents (Section 2.2(b)), and at least one interventional treatment (Section 2.2(c)).
- Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway Exception) modifies these requirements for members with a primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy by reducing the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks and waiving the interventional treatment requirement (Section 2.2(c)) if documented as a risk. However, Section 2.2(A) explicitly states that the pharmacotherapy requirement under Section 2.2(b) (minimum 4-week trial of two agents) "remains in full."

Because the member has completed 0 weeks of physical therapy and has not completed a 4-week trial of two outpatient pharmacotherapy agents, the clinical record does not satisfy the literal requirements of CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 or the modified Myelopathy Pathway Exception under Section 2.2(A).

III. ACKNOWLEDGEMENT OF REGULATORY LIMITATIONS AND GAPS
In evaluating this appeal, we must explicitly address and acknowledge the limitations of certain regulatory provisions cited in the appeal basis:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5:
We acknowledge that the statutory waiver under CIC § 10169.5 is strictly limited to physical therapy. While CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) prohibits a health plan from conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise, this statute does not independently waive or excuse the plan's separate, independent pharmacotherapy requirements under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b). Therefore, CIC § 10169.5 alone is insufficient to support a complete overturn of the denial, as the member's failure to attempt the 4-week pharmacotherapy trial remains an unresolved barrier under the plan's literal criteria.

2. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 Section II.2:
We further acknowledge that the specific regulatory waiver for conservative treatment under DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.2 does not apply to the facts of this case. Section II.2 permits a waiver of conservative treatment only when: (a) progressive pathology is demonstrated on "serial imaging" (such as progressive cord signal changes across multiple scans), and (b) the treating physician documents that conservative treatment "has been attempted and has failed." In this case, there is only a single emergency MRI (no serial imaging exists), and conservative treatment was never attempted (0 weeks of physical therapy or outpatient pharmacotherapy). Consequently, the factual conditions required to trigger the Section II.2 waiver are not met.

IV. LEGAL AND CLINICAL BASIS FOR OVERTURN
Because the literal plan criteria are not met, and the specific waivers under CIC § 10169.5 and APL 22-014 Section II.2 are factually or legally inapplicable to the entire conservative treatment bundle, the determination must resolve the fundamental conflict between the plan's restrictive criteria and the clinical standard of care. 

This overturn is grounded on California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 and DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 Section II.3, which establish that generally accepted clinical standards of care govern over restrictive internal plan criteria.

1. Statutory Standard of Medical Necessity (CHSC § 1374.32):
Under CHSC § 1374.32(a), "medically necessary" services are defined as those that a physician, exercising prudent clinical judgment, would provide to a patient to treat an illness or injury in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice and that are clinically appropriate in terms of type, extent, and duration. Crucially, CHSC § 1374.32(b) mandates that the standard of review for medical necessity does NOT require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the requested service meets generally accepted clinical standards, the plan's internal criteria are superseded, and the service must be approved.

2. Regulatory Primacy of Clinical Standards (DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3):
DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3 explicitly addresses time-sensitive neurological conditions, stating: "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for conditions identified in this guidance. Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs." Cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI is specifically classified under APL 22-014 Section II.1 as a time-sensitive condition with high potential for irreversible harm.

3. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards of Care:
The clinical standard of care for cervical myelopathy is established by the AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine Guidelines (2023 Update), the AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021), and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020). 

- Tier 1 Surgical Indications: Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), immediate surgical decompression is a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) for patients presenting with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy, objective cord compression, and myelomalacia (cord signal change) on MRI, particularly in the setting of acute or rapidly progressive neurological deficits.
- Contraindication of Conservative Treatment: The AO Spine International Guidelines (2021) state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
- Clinical Harm of Delay: Clinical evidence demonstrates that myelomalacia (T2 hyperintensity) represents active spinal cord injury. Requiring a patient with acute 2/5 upper extremity weakness, 3/5 lower extremity weakness, and urinary urgency to undergo physical therapy is not only clinically ineffective but is highly dangerous, as mechanical manipulation of an unstable, severely compressed cervical cord risks converting an incomplete spinal cord injury into a complete, permanent spinal cord injury (quadriplegia).
- Inappropriateness of Pharmacotherapy Trials: Similarly, requiring a 4-week trial of outpatient oral pharmacotherapy (such as NSAIDs or neuropathic agents) under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b) is clinically inappropriate and violates the standard of care. Oral medications cannot mechanically decompress a severely compromised spinal cord. Delaying necessary surgical decompression for 4 to 8 weeks to complete a pharmacotherapy trial is associated with a high rate of irreversible neurological deficit and permanent paralysis (AANS/CNS Guidelines 2023).

V. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICT
A direct conflict exists between HealthFirst's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2), which mechanically require physical therapy and a 4-week pharmacotherapy trial, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, which mandate immediate surgical decompression and contraindicate conservative treatment for acute myelopathy with myelomalacia.

Pursuant to CHSC § 1374.32(b) and DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3, the clinical standard of care governs and supersedes the plan's restrictive internal criteria. Because the requested emergency ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is highly indicated under Tier 1 of the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) and is necessary to prevent permanent, irreversible neurological harm, it is determined to be medically necessary.

VI. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION
The prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED. Emergency Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is APPROVED.
