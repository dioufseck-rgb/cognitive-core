## I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This formal appeal review evaluates the medical necessity of a C5-C6 ACDF for a 58-year-old female member exhibiting profound, objective clinical signs of cervical myelopathy. 

The health plan’s initial denial on October 30, 2024, was based on a rigid application of standard conservative care guidelines, specifically citing the member's completion of only five (5) weeks of physical therapy (PT) out of a standard six (6) week requirement, and dismissing clinical upper motor neuron (UMN) signs because of "mild cord contact" rather than "measurable compression" on MRI sequences.

Following a comprehensive review of the clinical record, relevant California statutes, and generally accepted clinical guidelines, this reviewer finds the plan's denial to be clinically inappropriate and legally non-compliant. Under the **Governing Hierarchy of Authority**, California state law and national clinical standards override the plan's restrictive internal criteria. 

The member has five distinct, objective UMN signs establishing a primary diagnosis of clinical cervical myelopathy. Consequently, she qualifies for the plan's internal **Myelopathy Exception Pathway (Section 2.2(A))**, which requires only four (4) weeks of PT. Because she completed five (5) weeks of supervised PT before her neurosurgeon safely halted treatment due to neurological risks, she has fully satisfied the applicable clinical requirements.

## II. STEP-BY-STEP EVALUATION ANALYSIS

### Step 1: Procedural Adequacy & Disclosure Review (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
*   **Evaluation:** The initial denial letter dated October 30, 2024, issued by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, was analyzed. The letter cited the plan's clinical criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), detailed the clinical findings used (mild cord contact, lack of signal change, and 5 out of 6 weeks of PT completed), and provided statutory Independent Medical Review (IMR) disclosures.
*   **Finding:** The denial notice is **procedurally adequate** under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b). The review must proceed to evaluate the clinical merits of the case.

### Step 2: Clinical Diagnosis & Pathology Verification (Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy)
*   **Evaluation:** The clinical record and physical examination dated September 5, 2024, were contrasted against the C-spine MRI findings dated September 1, 2024.
*   **Clinical Examination Findings:** 
    1. Bilateral positive Hoffman's sign.
    2. Bilateral upper extremity hyperreflexia.
    3. Sustained clonus at bilateral ankles (3 beats).
    4. Positive finger escape sign.
    5. Mildly wide-based gait.
*   **Imaging Findings:** MRI of the cervical spine reveals a C5-C6 disc herniation with "mild cord contact." No measurable cord compression, T2 signal change, or myelomalacia are quantified.
*   **Resolution & Finding:** Clinical myelopathy is a diagnosis primarily established by a constellation of objective UMN signs on clinical examination. While imaging findings of cord compression or myelomalacia support the diagnosis, their absence on standard, non-weight-bearing MRI sequences does not invalidate a clinical presentation of myelopathy. According to the joint guidelines of the **AANS/CNS (2023)**, "imaging-negative myelopathy is a recognized clinical entity where clinical signs of cord dysfunction precede measurable MRI changes." 
    
    The presence of five separate, objective, bilateral UMN signs (Hoffman's, hyperreflexia, sustained ankle clonus, finger escape, and gait instability) in the context of documented C5-C6 disc herniation with cord contact establishes a primary clinical diagnosis of **cervical myelopathy**. 

### Step 3: Application of Plan Criteria and Internal Consistency (CC-SPINE-2024)
*   **Evaluation:** The plan’s medical policy **CC-SPINE-2024** was reviewed. Section 2.2 outline standard conservative treatments (6 weeks of PT, 2 pharmacotherapy trials, and an interventional procedure). However, **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY** modifies these requirements for members with a primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy:
    1. The physical therapy requirement is reduced to a **minimum of 4 weeks**.
    2. The interventional treatment requirement (Section 2.2(c)) is **waived** if the treating physician documents a risk of neurological deterioration.
*   **Finding:** The member completed **5 weeks of supervised physical therapy** (15 total sessions), which exceeds the modified 4-week requirement under Exception 2.2(A). Furthermore, the physical therapy notes record that the treating neurosurgeon, Dr. Adams, actively halted PT at week 5 due to "myelopathy concern" (neurological signs remained unchanged and further manipulation posed a deterioration risk). This clinical action satisfies the waiver for interventional procedures. 
    
    The plan's initial reviewer applied the standard 6-week radiculopathy pathway to a patient with myelopathy, which represents an **internal misapplication of the plan's own clinical coverage criteria**. The member met the requirements of the Myelopathy Pathway exception.

### Step 4: Statutory and Regulatory Override Assessment (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)
*   **Evaluation:** The plan’s physical therapy requirement must be assessed against California statutory protections.
*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5:** This statute mandates that a health plan shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where:
    *   *(a)(1)* The treating physician has documented in writing that PT is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member given the severity of the condition; OR
    *   *(a)(3)* The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address.
*   **Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:** Directs that plans shall not require a full course of conservative therapy when a delay risks irreversible neurological harm and surgery is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines.
*   **Finding:** Dr. Adams halted the member's physical therapy at 5 weeks due to active myelopathic signs and safety concerns, documenting that continued PT was contraindicated. Additionally, the MRI confirmed physical "cord contact" at the C5-C6 level, representing structural compromise that PT cannot anatomically resolve. Under **CIC § 10169.5(c)**, the health plan is legally prohibited from mechanically enforcing the remaining one week of its PT duration requirement. The statutory protections override the plan's internal criteria.

### Step 5: Generally Accepted Clinical Standards Evaluation (AANS/CNS & AO Spine)
*   **Evaluation:** The clinical presentation was compared to national guidelines from the American Association of Neurological Surgeons / Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS, 2023) and AO Spine (2021).
*   **Finding:** **AO Spine (2021) Guidelines** explicitly state: *"There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."* 
    
    Applying a conservative PT trial (which is validated for radiculopathy) to established cervical myelopathy is a clinical category error. Under **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**, clinical standards of medical practice dictate that surgical decompression is the appropriate primary treatment for patients with objective UMN signs and structural cord contact. These clinical standards supersede any more restrictive internal criteria of the plan.

## III. DETERMINATION RATIONALE & CITATIONS

The denial of CPT code 22551 is overturned based on three independent, compounding factors:

1.  **Compliance with Plan's Internal Exception Criteria:** The clinical record establishes a primary diagnosis of clinical cervical myelopathy based on multiple bilateral UMN signs. Under HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(A), the PT requirement is reduced to 4 weeks and interventional procedures are waived. The member completed 5 weeks of PT (15 sessions), satisfying the plan's internal myelopathy exception pathway.
2.  **California Statutory Protections (CIC § 10169.5):** The member’s treating neurosurgeon documented safety concerns and halted PT at 5 weeks due to risk of neurological injury. Under California law, a health plan cannot mechanically enforce a PT duration minimum when the treating physician has documented a clinical contraindication or safety risk.
3.  **National Clinical Consensus (AANS/CNS & AO Spine):** For patients presenting with clinical myelopathy (clonus, hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman's, and gait instability), there is no medical evidence supporting a mandatory trial of physical therapy. Delaying decompression surgery in this clinical state risks permanent, irreversible spinal cord damage. Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), medical necessity must be determined in accordance with these clinical guidelines, overriding the plan's internal policies.

The requested Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 is medically necessary, clinically appropriate, and legally authorized.
