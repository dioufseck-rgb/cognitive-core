### PART I: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY OF THE DETERMINATION

The member is a 52-year-old male with a history of persistent, severe right C7 cervical radiculopathy. The initial denial issued on October 20, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang asserted that the member did not meet clinical criteria because "PT [was] not yet completed to a functional plateau" and imaging did not demonstrate measurable spinal cord compression. 

A comprehensive appellate review of the full clinical record, physical therapy discharge summaries, and pharmacotherapy history reveals that the initial denial was factually incorrect. The member has fully completed **7 weeks (21 sessions) of supervised physical therapy**, with a documented, explicit **functional plateau** (<10% improvement over the final 3 sessions, with symptoms persisting unchanged). Under **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2)**, a health plan is legally prohibited from requiring further physical therapy once a functional plateau is reached and documented. Furthermore, the member has completed a multi-class pharmacological trial (4 months of Pregabalin and 3 months of Meloxicam) and an interventional cervical epidural steroid injection, thereby fully satisfying the standard conservative treatment requirements under **HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Policy CC-SPINE-2024**.

As the member has objective evidence of C6-C7 disc herniation and corresponding right C7 nerve root compression with correlated neurological deficits (diminished right triceps reflex, dermatomal numbness), surgical intervention is indicated under both internal plan criteria and national clinical standards (NASS 2020, AANS/CNS 2023).

### PART II: NUMBERED EVALUATION PLAN EXECUTION

#### Step 1: Procedural Adequacy and Defect Review (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
*   **Evaluation:** The initial denial letter dated October 20, 2024, was evaluated for procedural compliance under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b). 
*   **Findings:** The denial notice was procedurally adequate. It specified the applied policy code (MED-003 / CC-SPINE-2024), detailed the clinical rationale (absence of cord compression, lack of myelopathic findings, and alleged incomplete PT), and enclosed instructions regarding Independent Medical Review (IMR) and appeal rights. 
*   **Conclusion:** There are no fatal procedural defects under CHSC § 1374.31(b) that would warrant an automatic procedural overturn. *Review proceeds to Step 2.*

#### Step 2: Diagnostic Classification and Verification (Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy)
*   **Evaluation:** Clinical and imaging records were reviewed to classify the spinal pathology.
*   **Findings:**
    *   *Imaging:* Cervical MRI dated August 1, 2024, demonstrates a C6-C7 disc herniation with mild cord contact but **no measurable cord compression, no cord signal changes, and no myelomalacia**. It notes mild right C7 foraminal stenosis.
    *   *Clinical Exam:* Neurological examination dated August 5, 2024, reveals a right C7 dermatomal distribution numbness and a diminished right triceps reflex. Crucially, there are **no bilateral findings, no upper motor neuron (UMN) signs** (such as Hoffman's sign, hyperreflexia, clonus, or gait disturbance), and no bowel/bladder dysfunction.
*   **Diagnostic Conclusion:** The member does *not* present with cervical myelopathy. The clinical presentation and imaging are classic for **Cervical Radiculopathy (right C7)**. 
*   **Path Determination:** Per the evaluation plan, because the condition is classified as cervical radiculopathy without myelopathy or myelomalacia, we bypass Step 3 (Statutory Myelopathy/Myelomalacia PT waivers under APL 22-014) and Step 4 (Clinical Standards for Myelopathy), and proceed directly to **Step 5** (Plan Criteria and Radiculopathy Conservative Therapy compliance).

#### Step 5: Plan Criteria Compliance and Exception Review (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)
*   **Evaluation:** The clinical record was assessed against the standard conservative treatment criteria set forth in **HealthFirst Policy CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2**.

    1.  **Section 2.1 — Diagnosis Requirement:**
        *   *Criteria:* Confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (nerve root compression with radicular pain, numbness, or weakness in a dermatomal distribution) supported by an MRI within 6 months.
        *   *Member Status:* **MET.** MRI (08/01/2024) shows C6-C7 disc herniation and right C7 foraminal stenosis. Clinical exam (08/05/2024) confirms correlating right C7 sensory deficit (numbness) and right triceps reflex diminution, with radicular pain rated 7/10.
    2.  **Section 2.2(a) — Physical Therapy Requirement:**
        *   *Criteria:* Minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy with documented functional outcomes.
        *   *Member Status:* **MET and exceeded.** The clinical record demonstrates the member completed **7 weeks (21 sessions)** of supervised PT. The physical therapy discharge summary explicitly states that a **functional plateau was reached**, noting that the patient failed to improve over the final 3 consecutive sessions, with radicular symptoms persisting unchanged.
    3.  **Section 2.2(b) — Pharmacotherapy Requirement:**
        *   *Criteria:* Trial of at least two covered agents (NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
        *   *Member Status:* **MET.** The member underwent a **4-month trial of Pregabalin 150mg BID** (neuropathic agent) which yielded only partial relief, and a **3-month trial of Meloxicam 15mg daily** (NSAID) which was inadequate. Both trials exceeded the 4-week requirement.
    4.  **Section 2.2(c) — Interventional Treatment Requirement:**
        *   *Criteria:* Minimum of 1 cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point series.
        *   *Member Status:* **MET.** The member underwent a right C7 cervical epidural steroid injection on September 20, 2024. This resulted in a brief 30% improvement lasting only 3 weeks before symptoms returned to baseline.

*   **State Law Override Integration (CIC § 10169.5):** 
    Under California Insurance Code Section 10169.5(a)(2), a health plan is legally prohibited from conditioning the approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy if "the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." Section 10169.5(b) defines a functional plateau as a documented failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement. The therapist's documentation of no improvement over the final 3 sessions meets this statutory definition. The plan’s mechanical application of "PT not completed to functional plateau" is legally invalid and factually contradicted by the clinical record.
*   **Conclusion:** The member has satisfied all standard medical necessity criteria under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2. The initial denial was factually incorrect.

### PART III: RATIONALE AND CLINICAL STANDARD COMPARISON

The clinical standard of care for persistent, severe cervical radiculopathy refractory to comprehensive conservative management strongly supports surgical decompression (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020). 

According to the **North American Spine Society (NASS) 2020 Evidence-Based Guidelines**, surgical intervention (ACDF) is a Tier 2 "reasonable and clinically indicated" option for patients with cervical radiculopathy who present with:
1.  Objective radiographic evidence of nerve root compression (demonstrated on Thomas Reed's MRI as C6-C7 herniation and right C7 foraminal stenosis);
2.  Correlating dermatomal neurological deficits (demonstrated on clinical exam as right C7 numbness and diminished triceps reflex);
3.  Failure of an exhaustive 6-to-12 week course of conservative management.

The member's conservative trial lasted over 4 months and included 7 weeks (21 sessions) of PT, two therapeutic classes of medication, and a targeted epidural steroid injection. Forcing the member to undergo additional physical therapy when an authoritative physical therapy discharge summary has documented a functional plateau is not supported by any high-quality clinical evidence. Under **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**, the standard of review for medical necessity must align with generally accepted standards of medical practice. Clinical consensus dictates that continuing physical therapy past a documented functional plateau in the setting of persistent, severe (7/10 pain) nerve compression and reflex deficit is medically futile and serves only to delay necessary definitive care.

Because the member meets all plan criteria, and because the plan's initial denial is legally overridden by **CIC § 10169.5**, the denial must be overturned.
