### FORMAL DETERMINATION
Pursuant to California health insurance regulations, generally accepted standards of spinal care, and the clinical record of the member, the initial decision to deny coverage for CPT 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) is hereby **OVERTURNED**. 

HealthFirst Health Plan is directed to **APPROVE** the prior authorization request for the requested surgical procedure immediately. The plan’s internal clinical coverage criteria requiring 6 weeks of physical therapy are legally and clinically superseded by California statutory overrides and generally accepted medical standards.

---

### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY OF DECISION
This appeal concerns the denial of a single-level anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7. HealthFirst denied the procedure because the member completed only 4 weeks of physical therapy (PT) instead of the plan-mandated 6 weeks, and because the treating physician utilized an administrative billing code for "cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy" (ICD-10 M47.812) on PT referrals rather than "myelopathy," thereby disqualifying the member from the plan’s internal "Myelopathy Exception" (Section 2.2A).

A formal clinical and regulatory review reveals that:
1. **Clinical Reality vs. Billing Code:** The member exhibits objective, severe upper motor neuron (UMN) signs (positive bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, gait impairment, 3/5 bilateral grip weakness) and an MRI demonstrating moderate spinal cord compression with T2 signal changes consistent with early myelomalacia. Clinical standards dictate this is a clear-cut case of cervical spondylotic myelopathy.
2. **Statutory Override:** Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1) & (3) and DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, health plans are strictly prohibited from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy when there is objective structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) or when the treating physician documents that further PT is contraindicated.
3. **Clinical Standard Override:** Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), the health plan’s restrictive internal criteria are bypassed in favor of generally accepted medical practices (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021), which classify moderate-to-severe myelopathy with myelomalacia as a **Tier 1 Strong Surgical Indication** where conservative therapy is not supported and delays risk irreversible neurological damage.

### DETAILED STEP-BY-STEP EVALUATION

#### STEP 1: Procedural Adequacy and Defect Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
* **Objective:** Assess the initial denial notice dated November 5, 2024, issued by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, for statutory compliance.
* **Findings:** The denial notice specified the exact clinical criteria applied (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2 PT requirements), cited the specific clinical reasons for denial (failure to complete 6 weeks of PT; myelopathy not listed as primary diagnosis on billing/referral forms), and included mandatory California Independent Medical Review (IMR) disclosure language.
* **Conclusion:** The denial notice is procedurally adequate. The review proceeds to Step 2.

#### STEP 2: Clinical Diagnostic Reconciliation (Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Reality)
* **Objective:** Analyze the medical record to determine if the member exhibits objective clinical signs of cervical myelopathy and/or myelomalacia, irrespective of the primary billing diagnosis code utilized.
* **Analysis of Clinical Record:**
  * **Neurological Examination (2024-07-01):** Clinically confirms profound upper motor neuron (UMN) deficits, including bilateral grip weakness (3/5), positive bilateral Hoffman sign, bilateral upper extremity hyperreflexia, and an impaired tandem gait. These findings are diagnostic of cervical myelopathy, not simple radiculopathy.
  * **Radiographic Imaging (MRI, 2024-07-10):** Confirms a C6-C7 disc herniation causing moderate spinal cord compression and, crucially, T2-signal intensity changes within the spinal cord consistent with *early myelomalacia*.
  * **Billing Discrepancy Reconciliation:** The insurer's reliance on the ICD-10 billing code M47.812 (spondylosis with radiculopathy) to deny the "Myelopathy Exception" ignores the explicit, consistent narrative clinical documentation of cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia. An administrative billing code is an artifact of referral and billing systems; it does not vacate the objective clinical findings of spinal cord injury documented by the treating surgeon.
* **Conclusion:** The clinical reality of moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with active myelomalacia is established. The review proceeds to Step 3.

#### STEP 3: Statutory PT Exception and Waiver Evaluation (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)
* **Objective:** Determine if California statutory provisions override the plan's 6-week physical therapy requirement.
* **Statutory Analysis:**
  * **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) (Structural Neurological Compromise):** A health plan cannot condition surgical approval on physical therapy completion when the condition involves "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The member’s MRI demonstrates moderate spinal cord compression with active myelomalacia. Physical therapy cannot remediate mechanical spinal cord compression or reverse spinal cord parenchymal signal changes.
  * **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) (Contraindication):** The treating surgeon halted physical therapy after 4 weeks (12 sessions) because MRI findings confirmed myelomalacia, documenting in writing that further PT was clinically contraindicated. Under California law, a plan must waive PT requirements under such written physician documentation.
  * **DMHC APL 22-014 (Progressive Neurological Urgency):** This mandate states that health plans *shall not* require full conservative treatment for time-sensitive neurological conditions where objective imaging shows progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia/cord signal change) and the physician documents urgency or failure to progress. Myelomalacia is explicitly protected under this directive as a condition exempt from mechanical conservative treatment trials.
* **Conclusion:** The plan's rigid requirement for 6 weeks of PT is legally unenforceable under California law. The 4 weeks of completed therapy, halted due to safety concerns and structural compromise, satisfies and exhausts all legally permissible conservative treatment demands. The review proceeds to Step 4.

#### STEP 4: Generally Accepted Clinical Standards Evaluation (CHSC § 1374.32(b))
* **Objective:** Apply the clinical standards of the joint AANS/CNS (2023), NASS (2020), and AO Spine (2021) guidelines to evaluate the medical necessity of CPT 22551.
* **Clinical Standard Analysis:**
  * **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023):** Moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective spinal cord compression and myelomalacia constitutes a **Tier 1 Strong Surgical Indication**. Conservative therapy is not indicated as primary treatment for this presentation.
  * **AO Spine Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):** There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical decompression in patients with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery to complete arbitrary PT blocks risks irreversible, permanent upper motor neuron deficits.
  * **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard Override):** Under California law, when an insurer's internal criteria (such as HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024) are more restrictive than generally accepted medical standards, the clinical standards govern. The plan's denial based on an administrative "primary diagnosis" requirement and an arbitrary 6-week PT threshold constitutes a clinical category error (applying a radiculopathy treatment timeline to an urgent myelopathic condition).
* **Conclusion:** Decompression and fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 is medically necessary, clinically urgent, and highly indicated under all generally accepted medical guidelines.
