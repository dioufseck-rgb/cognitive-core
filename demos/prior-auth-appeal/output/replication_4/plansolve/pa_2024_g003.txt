### SECTION 1: EVALUATION STEPS

#### Step 1: Procedural Defect Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
A review of the initial denial letter dated November 5, 2024, written by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, was performed to assess procedural compliance with California notice standards.
*   **Finding:** **No Procedural Defect.** The denial notice explicitly cited the clinical coverage criteria applied (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024) and outlined the precise clinical reasons for the denial: failure to complete 6 weeks of physical therapy (3 weeks completed), failure to trial at least two pharmacotherapy agents (one agent completed), and the absence of an interventional procedure. The notice also included mandatory disclosures regarding the member’s right to an Independent Medical Review (IMR) under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.30. The initial denial is procedurally adequate.

#### Step 2: Clinical Diagnosis & Pathology Classification
The clinical records and imaging reports were analyzed to classify the member's spinal pathology.
*   **Finding:** **Mild, Stable Cervical Myelopathy.** The clinical examination dated October 5, 2024, reveals mild upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs and mild bilateral hyperreflexia. Crucially, the member’s tandem gait is fully intact, hand grip strength is 4+/5 bilaterally, there is no frank motor weakness, and there are no dermatomal deficits. Activities of daily living (ADLs) are fully intact and independent. The calculated modified Japanese Orthopaedic Association (mJOA) score is 15/18, which is classified as *mild myelopathy* (score 15–17).
*   **Imaging Analysis:** The cervical MRI dated October 1, 2024, reveals moderate central cord compression at C5-C6 with cord contact. Critically, the radiologist's report explicitly notes: **"No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia."**
*   **Classification Conclusion:** While the member has a primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (not simple radiculopathy), it is classified objectively as **mild and stable** (symptom duration of 6 months without progression, mJOA 15, and an absence of myelomalacia or spinal cord signal changes).

#### Step 3: Application of California Statutory Overrides (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)
We evaluated whether California statutory protections override the plan's conservative therapy requirements.
*   **CIC § 10169.5 Analysis:** Under California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(3), a plan cannot condition surgery on physical therapy if there is "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." In spine care, this is defined as progressive spinal cord injury, typically manifested as myelomalacia (T2 cord signal changes) with corresponding functional deterioration. Because Mr. Achebe's MRI explicitly confirms **no myelomalacia** and **no T2 signal changes**, and his neurological exam demonstrates stable, mild symptoms, there is no objective structural compromise of a severity that legally waives the physical therapy requirement under this provision. Furthermore, the member did not reach a "functional plateau" under § 10169.5(a)(2); rather, physical therapy was prematurely halted at 3 weeks by the requesting neurologist.
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 Analysis:** DMHC APL 22-014 prohibits plans from requiring conservative therapy only when: (a) objective imaging demonstrates *progressive* pathology (e.g., myelomalacia, progressive herniation on serial imaging), AND (b) the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression, AND (c) surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. Mr. Achebe’s clinical file lacks serial imaging demonstrating progression, his MRI is negative for myelomalacia, and clinical documentation confirms his symptoms have been stable for 6 months. Therefore, the statutory overrides under DMHC APL 22-014 do not apply to this case.

#### Step 4: Application of Plan Criteria & Myelopathy Exception (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2A)
We assessed whether the plan's utilization management correctly applied its own clinical guidelines, specifically the modified pathway for myelopathy.
*   **Finding:** Because the member's primary diagnosis is cervical myelopathy, **Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway)** is active. Under this modified pathway:
    1.  The physical therapy requirement is reduced from 6 weeks to a **minimum of 4 weeks**.
    2.  The interventional treatment requirement (Section 2.2(c)) is waived if the physician documents a risk of deterioration (which is clinically reasonable in myelopathy).
    3.  The pharmacotherapy requirement (Section 2.2(b)) remains fully active: a trial of at least **two** agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
*   **Compliance Gap:** 
    *   *Physical Therapy:* The member completed only 3 weeks (9 sessions) of the required 4-week modified minimum. Dr. Osei halted therapy prematurely at 3 weeks.
    *   *Pharmacotherapy:* The member tried only **one** agent (Baclofen 5mg BID for 6 weeks). The plan requires a trial of at least two agents (e.g., an NSAID, gabapentinoid, or oral steroid) for 4 weeks each.
*   **Conclusion:** The member fails to meet the plan’s modified Myelopathy Exception criteria (Section 2.2A) due to incomplete physical therapy (3 weeks completed vs. 4 weeks required) and insufficient pharmacotherapy (1 agent trialed vs. 2 agents required).

#### Step 5: Clinical Standard of Care Evaluation (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021)
Pursuant to CHSC § 1374.32(b), we evaluated whether generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice mandate surgical intervention in this case, which would override the plan's internal criteria.
*   **Guidelines Analysis:** 
    *   **AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines:** Surgical decompression is designated as a **Tier 1 (Strong Indication)** for *moderate-to-severe* cervical myelopathy or in the presence of *myelomalacia* (T2 signal changes) because delay risks irreversible spinal cord damage. However, for *mild, stable* cervical myelopathy (mJOA 15–17, stable symptoms, no myelomalacia), surgery is classified under **Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication)** and is indicated *only after an adequate trial of conservative management has failed*.
    *   **AO Spine 2021 Guidelines:** For mild degenerative cervical myelopathy, clinicians may offer a trial of structured conservative treatment. If the patient does not improve or demonstrates clinical progression, surgical intervention is recommended. 
*   **Reconciliation of Medical Opinion:** Dr. Osei's letter of support dated November 1, 2024, states that "further delay risks irreversible neurological deterioration" and characterizes the cord compression as "severe and progressive." However, this assertion is directly contradicted by the objective evidence in the clinical record:
    1.  The MRI report explicitly states there is **no myelomalacia** and **no T2 signal change**, meaning there is no active, irreversible tissue damage (existing cord injury) present.
    2.  The physical examination shows a stable, mild presentation (mJOA of 15, fully intact gait, independent ADLs).
    3.  The clinical records document that the patient's symptoms have been entirely **stable for 6 months**.
*   **Conclusion:** Under generally accepted clinical standards, immediate surgical decompression is not clinically mandated for mild, stable cervical myelopathy without myelomalacia. A conservative trial remains the appropriate first-line standard of care. Prematurely halting physical therapy at 3 weeks and failing to utilize a second pharmacological agent is inconsistent with conservative trial standards for this clinical tier.

### SECTION 2: DISPOSITION DECISION

Based on the clinical evidence and legal framework:
1.  **Procedural Adequacy:** The initial denial was procedurally valid (Step 1).
2.  **Clinical Classification:** The patient has mild, stable cervical myelopathy without myelomalacia (Step 2).
3.  **Statutory Overrides:** California legal protections (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014) do not apply due to the lack of objective structural compromise (myelomalacia) or documented disease progression (Step 3).
4.  **Plan Criteria:** The member has not completed the modified conservative requirements under the plan's Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2A), lacking 1 additional week of physical therapy and a trial of a second pharmacotherapy agent (Step 4).
5.  **Clinical Standards:** Generally accepted standards (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021) do not mandate immediate surgery for mild, stable myelopathy in the absence of myelomalacia, supporting conservative therapy as the primary standard (Step 5).

Therefore, the health plan's initial denial of prior authorization for **CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6)** is **UPHELD**.

### RECOMMENDATIONS TO THE MEMBER AND TREATING PROVIDER

To satisfy clinical coverage criteria and secure future authorization for the requested surgical procedure, the following clinical steps must be completed:
1.  **Complete the Physical Therapy Requirement:** Complete at least one additional week of supervised physical therapy to satisfy the 4-week modified minimum requirement under HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(A). If the member achieves a documented "functional plateau" (less than 10% improvement over 3 sessions) during this time, the therapist must document this in the daily notes.
2.  **Complete the Pharmacotherapy Requirement:** Trial a second approved pharmacological agent (such as an NSAID, gabapentin, pregabalin, or a short course of oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks, in addition to the completed 6-week trial of Baclofen.
3.  **Document Progression if Applicable:** If the member experiences any objective neurological decline (e.g., new gait instability, worsening hand grip, or new dermatomal deficits) or if repeat imaging demonstrates new myelomalacia or progressive cord compromise, the treating neurologist should submit these findings immediately to trigger an urgent statutory override under DMHC APL 22-014.
