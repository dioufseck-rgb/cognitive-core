### II. FORMAL DETERMINATION

#### **DISPOSITION: UPHOLD THE DENIAL OF COVERAGE**

Following a rigorous, multi-step clinical and regulatory review of the complete administrative and medical record, the prior authorization denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) is hereby **UPHELD**. 

The clinical record demonstrates that the member has mild-to-moderate right-sided C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy without objective spinal cord compression, myelomalacia, or motor weakness. Under California law, generally accepted clinical standards, and the applicable HealthFirst clinical criteria, this clinical presentation requires the completion of a standard, structured conservative treatment pathway prior to surgical intervention. The member has not completed the required conservative trial, and no statutory or clinical exceptions apply to waive these requirements.

### IV. STEP-BY-STEP EVALUATION & FINDINGS

#### **Step 1: Procedural Adequacy and Regulatory Compliance Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))**
*   **Mandate:** Ensure the initial denial notice dated October 15, 2024, specifies the exact clinical criteria applied, provides specific clinical reasons for the denial, and includes mandated Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights.

This notice specified that the request was reviewed by Dr. Maria Rivera (a board-certified neurosurgeon, 15 years practice) who conducted a peer-to-peer review. The clinical reason was clearly detailed: "imaging shows mild-moderate disc herniation with no cord compression and no neurological deficit on examination. Conservative treatment pathway not completed." State-mandated IMR disclosures and appeal instructions were attached.
*   **Finding:** **POSITIVE FINDING.** The denial notice is procedurally adequate, compliant with CHSC § 1374.31(b), and is legally valid. The appeal must proceed to substantive clinical review.

#### **Step 2: Diagnostic and Pathology Classification (Radiculopathy vs. Myelopathy)**
*   **Mandate:** Analyze the medical record, imaging, and clinical examinations to determine if the primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, cervical myelopathy, or axial neck pain. 
*   **Evaluation:**
    *   **Imaging Analysis (MRI dated 2024-09-10):** Demonstrates "C5-C6 mild to moderate disc herniation. Right C6 nerve root contact without measurable compression." Crucially, the radiologist notes: *"No cord contact. No cord compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia. Mild right foraminal narrowing."*
    *   **Clinical Examination (dated 2024-09-15):** The physical and neurological exam shows right shoulder and neck pain (6/10) with mild right C6 dermatomal tingling. However, bicep reflexes are 2+ bilaterally and symmetric. Grip strength is 5/5 bilaterally, with no motor weakness. There are **no** upper motor neuron (UMN) signs (e.g., negative Hoffman sign, no hyperreflexia, no clonus, no gait disturbance). Functional assessment notes that the member is working full-time with no activity limitations.
*   **Finding:** **NEGATIVE FINDING.** The member's condition is classified as **mild, unilateral C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy**. There is absolutely no objective evidence of cervical myelopathy (no spinal cord compression, no cord contact, no UMN signs, and no myelomalacia). Standard conservative treatment requirements remain fully applicable.

#### **Step 3: Evaluation of State Law Exceptions to Conservative Treatment (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)**
*   **Mandate:** Determine if the clinical presentation qualifies for a statutory waiver of the plan's conservative treatment requirements under California law.
*   **Evaluation of Statutory Exceptions:**
    1.  *CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) [Physician Contraindication]:* Did the treating physician document in writing that physical therapy (PT) is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member? *No.* Dr. Walsh's letter of October 18, 2024, asserts his clinical superiority and experience but does not provide any clinical contraindication to PT. He simply "elected to halt PT" to seek surgical authorization.
    2.  *CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) [Functional Plateau]:* Did the member reach a documented functional plateau? *No.* The PT notes of the 4-week trial indicate "mild improvement in pain" with "no neurological deficit to track." There is no documentation from the physical therapist or physician establishing that a functional plateau (<10% improvement over 3 sessions or maximum benefit) was reached.
    3.  *CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) [Objective Structural Neurological Compromise]:* Is there objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address? *No.* As established by the MRI on September 10, 2024, there is no cord compression, no cord contact, and no myelomalacia. The right C6 nerve root has "contact without measurable compression," which is highly amenable to physical and pharmacological management.
    4.  *DMHC APL 22-014 [Progressive Pathology Exception]:* Does imaging show progressive, time-sensitive pathology (e.g., myelomalacia or progressive cord signal changes) such that delaying surgery poses an imminent risk of irreversible harm? *No.* The MRI explicitly confirms the absence of myelomalacia, cord contact, or cord signal changes.
*   **Finding:** **NEGATIVE FINDING.** No statutory exceptions under California law apply to this case. The plan’s requirements for conservative therapy remain legally enforceable.

#### **Step 4: Clinical Standard Medical Necessity Evaluation (AANS/CNS 2023 & AO Spine 2021)**
*   **Mandate:** Assess if the requested surgery meets generally accepted standards of medical practice under CHSC § 1374.32(a) and (b), independent of internal plan criteria.
*   **Clinical Standard Analysis:**
    *   According to the **American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS) and the Congress of Neurological Surgeons (CNS) Joint Guidelines (2023 Update)**, surgical intervention for cervical radiculopathy is classified as a **Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication)** or **Tier 3 (Not Indicated)** option depending on the duration and failure of conservative care. 
    *   Tier 2 requires "radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after **6 to 12 weeks** of conservative treatment." 
    *   The **AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021)** and the **Cochrane Review (Nikolaidis et al., 2021)** emphasize that for mild-to-moderate radiculopathy without motor deficit, a minimum of 6 weeks of structured conservative care is the standard of care, as 70% to 80% of these cases resolve without surgical intervention. Surgery is not indicated as a primary first-line treatment for mild sensory radiculopathy.
*   **Finding:** **NEGATIVE FINDING.** Because the member has only completed 4 weeks of physical therapy and has no motor deficit (grip strength is 5/5, no weakness), proceeding to an invasive ACDF does not meet generally accepted clinical standards at this time (constituting Tier 3 / Surgery Not Indicated under AANS/CNS 2023 guidelines).

### V. CLINICAL RESOLUTION OF APPEAL ARGUMENTS

The appeal basis submitted by Dr. Walsh has been carefully reviewed and is clinically addressed as follows:

1.  **Dr. Walsh's Argument:** *The treating surgeon has 25 years of spine surgery experience and has performed over 2,000 ACDFs; therefore, his clinical judgment should take precedence over a remote plan reviewer.*
    *   **Clinical Evaluation:** While Dr. Walsh’s extensive surgical experience is highly acknowledged, clinical decisions under California law (CHSC § 1374.32) must be based on objective, evidence-based clinical guidelines. Prudent clinical judgment is defined by adherence to peer-reviewed, generally accepted standards of medical practice. Guidelines from the AANS/CNS and AO Spine clearly state that surgery for mild sensory radiculopathy without motor deficits should be reserved for those who have failed a comprehensive 6-to-12-week course of conservative management. Dr. Walsh's preference to operate after only 4 weeks of PT, and without attempting a second pharmacological class or interventional therapies, departs from these clinical standards.
2.  **Dr. Walsh's Argument:** *The patient will suffer "permanent disability" without immediate authorization.*
    *   **Clinical Evaluation:** This assertion is not supported by the objective clinical evidence. The MRI demonstrates only "mild to moderate disc herniation" and "nerve root contact without measurable compression." There is no spinal cord compression, no myelomalacia, and no T2 signal change, meaning there is no pre-existing or threatened spinal cord injury. On clinical examination, the patient's grip strength is 5/5, motor function is entirely intact, reflexes are normal, and there are no upper motor neuron signs. The patient is currently working full-time with no activity limitations. In the absence of motor weakness, progressive neurological deficits, or cord compression, there is no clinical data suggesting a risk of "permanent disability" from completing the remaining 2 weeks of physical therapy and exploring conservative options.
