## I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The member is a 44-year-old female with a presenting diagnosis of right-sided C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy. Her treating surgeon, Dr. Patrick Walsh, has requested prior authorization for a single-level anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). The plan initially denied the request on October 15, 2024, citing incomplete conservative treatment and a lack of objective neurological deficits or spinal cord compromise.

Dr. Walsh appealed the decision, asserting that his clinical judgment as an examining surgeon of 25 years should take precedence over a remote reviewer, and warning of "permanent disability" if the surgery is delayed. 

A comprehensive review of the clinical record, including a cervical MRI dated September 10, 2024, and a physical examination dated September 15, 2024, was performed. The evaluation demonstrates that:
1. The member has **mild, stable cervical radiculopathy** with normal motor strength, normal symmetric reflexes, and no functional limitations.
2. There is **no objective evidence of spinal cord compression, myelomalacia, or structural neurological compromise** on imaging.
3. The member has **not completed the standard conservative treatment trials** mandated by the plan and recommended by national clinical guidelines.
4. No California statutory exemptions or generally accepted clinical standards of care apply to override the conservative management requirements in this case.

Accordingly, the initial denial is upheld. The clinical details and regulatory analyses supporting this determination are outlined below.

## II. STEP-BY-STEP EVALUATION & DETERMINATION ANALYSIS

Consistent with California clinical review guidelines, this appeal has been evaluated using the mandatory **Hierarchy of Authority**:

$$\text{California State Law \& Regulations} > \text{Generally Accepted Clinical Standards} > \text{Plan Coverage Criteria}$$

### Step 1: Procedural Defect Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), a health plan's denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied, provide a clear and detailed clinical reason for the denial tied to the member's clinical presentation, and include notice of Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights. A notice failing these requirements is legally defective, requiring an immediate remand.

*   **Evaluation:** The initial denial notice issued by Dr. Maria Rivera on October 15, 2024, explicitly cited the relevant clinical coverage guidelines (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024), detailed the specific clinical findings from the member's medical record (lack of spinal cord compression on imaging, absence of objective neurological deficits, and failure to complete conservative therapy), and provided standard notice of California IMR rights.
*   **Finding:** **Procedurally Adequate.** No procedural defects are present. The appeal proceeds to clinical evaluation.

### Step 2: Diagnosis Verification & Clinical Classification
Proper clinical classification of the cervical pathology (Cervical Myelopathy versus Cervical Radiculopathy) is required, as these diagnoses carry different risks, treatment algorithms, and regulatory protections.

*   **Imaging Review (MRI, 2024-09-10):** Demonstrates a C5-C6 mild-to-moderate disc herniation with right C6 nerve root contact *without measurable compression*. Crucially, the radiologist notes: **"No cord contact. No cord compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia. Mild right foraminal narrowing."**
*   **Neurological Exam Review (2024-09-15):** Bilateral grip strength is **5/5** (no weakness). Bicep reflexes are **2+ bilateral and symmetric** (normal). Right C6 dermatomal tingling is characterized as "mild." There are **no upper motor neuron (UMN) signs** (no Hoffman sign, hyperreflexia, clonus, or gait instability).
*   **Functional Status:** The member continues to work full-time with **no activity limitations**.
*   **Finding:** **Radiculopathy/Axial Pain Only (Negative for Myelopathy).** There is no objective evidence of cervical myelopathy (no spinal cord compression, no T2 cord signal changes/myelomalacia, and no UMN findings). The member’s clinical presentation is classified as stable, mild right-sided cervical radiculopathy.

### Step 3: Plan Criteria Application & Myelopathy Exception Review (Section 2.2A)
The request was evaluated against HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria `CC-SPINE-2024`:

1.  **Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement):** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy, satisfying Criterion 2.1(a).
2.  **Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement):**
    *   *(a) Physical Therapy:* Requires a minimum of **6 weeks** of supervised PT. The member has completed only **4 weeks (12 sessions)**. Dr. Walsh elected to prematurely halt PT to seek surgical authorization.
    *   *(b) Pharmacotherapy:* Requires a trial of at least **two** agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The clinical record documents only one agent (**Naproxen 500mg BID for 8 weeks**), which yielded moderate pain relief. No second agent (e.g., muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents like gabapentin, or oral corticosteroids) was trialed.
    *   *(c) Interventional Treatment:* Requires at least **one** interventional procedure (cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point injection series). **None** have been performed or scheduled.
3.  **Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Exception):** This exception reduces the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks and waives the interventional requirement *only* when cervical myelopathy is the primary diagnosis. As established in Step 2, the member has no myelopathic findings.
4.  **Finding:** **Plan Criteria Not Met (Negative Finding).** The member failed to meet the standard conservative treatment requirements of Section 2.2, and does not qualify for the Section 2.2(A) Myelopathy Exception.

### Step 4: State Law / Regulatory Override Analysis (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)
We must determine whether California statutory provisions legally override the plan’s conservative treatment mandates:

*   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) Analysis:** Insurers cannot mandate PT if the condition involves "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." On review, the member's MRI shows *no* cord compression, *no* myelomalacia, and *no* measurable nerve root compression (only mild right foraminal narrowing and root contact). Therefore, this statutory waiver is inapplicable.
*   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) & (2) Analysis:** Dr. Walsh did not document that PT was clinically contraindicated or likely to cause harm. Rather, the physical therapy progress note indicates the member was experiencing "mild improvement in pain" with "no neurological deficit to track." The therapy was halted by the surgeon, not because of a documented clinical "functional plateau" or contraindication, but to pursue surgery.
*   **DMHC APL 22-014 Analysis:** This directive prohibits plans from requiring conservative therapy where objective imaging demonstrates *progressive pathology* (e.g., progressive herniation, myelomalacia, or cord signal changes on serial imaging) and the treating physician documents urgency. The member has a single MRI showing stable, mild-to-moderate findings with *no* myelomalacia or progressive spinal cord injury. No clinical urgency exists.
*   **Finding:** **No Statutory Override (Negative Finding).** The plan's conservative treatment criteria remain legally enforceable under California law.

### Step 5: Clinical Standard of Care Evaluation (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021, & NASS 2020)
Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the standard for clinical review is generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersede plan criteria:

*   **AANS/CNS 2023 Joint Guidelines:** Single-level ACDF is classified as a **Tier 1 (Strong Indication)** surgical procedure for moderate-to-severe myelopathy or progressive neurological deficits, and a **Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication)** procedure for radiculopathy *only after an adequate 6-to-12-week trial of conservative treatment*.
*   **NASS 2020 Guidelines:** For mild, stable radiculopathy without objective motor weakness, a minimum of **6 to 12 weeks of structured conservative management** (including physical therapy, pharmacotherapy, and/or interventional injections) is strongly recommended as the initial standard of care. Surgical intervention is not indicated as a first-line treatment for stable radiculopathy in the absence of a motor deficit or failure of an exhaustive conservative trial.
*   **AO Spine 2021 Guidelines:** While the guidelines support immediate decompression in cases of existing spinal cord injury (myelomalacia) due to the risk of irreversible progression, they affirm that patients with stable radicular pain and normal neurological examinations are safe to undergo, and benefit from, extended conservative management.
*   **Evaluation of Dr. Walsh's Arguments:** Dr. Walsh asserts that his clinical judgment must take precedence, and that the patient risks "permanent disability" without immediate surgery. However, this assertion is directly contradicted by his own documented physical examination from September 15, 2024:
    1.  The member has perfect **5/5 motor strength** and intact symmetric reflexes.
    2.  She has no spinal cord compression, no cord contact, and no myelomalacia on MRI.
    3.  She is fully functional, working full-time without activity limitations.
    There is no objective clinical data supporting a risk of "permanent disability" or establishing acute neurological urgency. Prudent clinical practice and national spine guidelines dictate that a full trial of conservative therapy must be exhausted before proceeding to an elective cervical fusion.
*   **Finding:** **Clinical Standard Supports Conservative Trial (Negative Finding).** Generally accepted clinical standards do not support surgical decompression at this time.

## IV. CONCLUSION

In accordance with the California clinical review framework and the established Hierarchy of Authority:

1.  **Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):** The member does not meet the necessary conservative treatment criteria for an elective cervical fusion, having completed only 4 of the required 6 weeks of physical therapy, trialing only one of the required two pharmacotherapy agents, and having had no interventional spinal treatments.
2.  **California State Law:** No statutory exceptions (CIC § 10169.5 or DMHC APL 22-014) apply to waive conservative management. The member has no spinal cord compression, no myelomalacia, and no progressive or urgent neurological pathology that would make conservative management clinically inappropriate or unsafe.
3.  **Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine):** Generally accepted standards of medical practice strongly support completing a full 6-to-12-week course of conservative management for stable, non-deficit radiculopathy. The treating physician's warning of "permanent disability" is unsupported by his own documented physical exam, which demonstrates normal motor function, normal reflexes, and a patient who remains fully active and working full-time.

The member is a strong candidate to complete her conservative treatment pathway. This should include completing the remaining 2 weeks of structured physical therapy, trialing an additional class of pharmacotherapy (such as a neuropathic agent or muscle relaxant), and considering a cervical epidural steroid injection if radicular pain persists. Should she fail to experience adequate functional improvement after exhausting these measures, a re-request for surgical authorization with updated clinical records would be medically appropriate.

At this time, surgical intervention is not medically necessary. The plan's decision to deny prior authorization for CPT code 22551 is **UPHELD**.
