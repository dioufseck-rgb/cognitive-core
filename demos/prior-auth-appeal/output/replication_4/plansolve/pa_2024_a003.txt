### **CASE SUMMARY & CLINICAL PROGRESSION**

On October 15, 2024, the plan’s initial reviewer, Dr. Alan Foster, MD, denied prior authorization for a single-level ACDF (C4-C5) under Denial Reason Code MED-003, citing a failure to complete standard conservative treatment under Section 2.2 of the HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 guidelines. Specifically, the denial noted that the member completed only 5 of 6 required physical therapy weeks and lacked a documented cervical epidural steroid injection on record.

The member appeals this determination. The clinical record demonstrates that the member has a primary diagnosis of **progressive cervical myelopathy C4-C5 with myelomalacia** (confirmed on MRI), accompanied by severe comorbidities including a permanent pacemaker (implanted in 2019). The clinical record confirms that physical therapy was halted after 5 weeks (15 sessions) due to pacemaker-related modality limits and neurological progression. Furthermore, a cervical epidural steroid injection was explicitly contraindicated by an anesthesiology consultation due to the permanent pacemaker and structural risks.

Applying the governing hierarchy of authority (State Law > Clinical Standards > Plan Criteria), the initial denial must be overturned on three independent grounds: (1) statutory override under California Law, (2) failure of the plan to apply its own internal myelopathy exception, and (3) clinical standard deviations.

---

### **EVALUATION STEP-BY-STEP ANALYSIS**

#### **Step 1: Procedural Defect and Regulatory Compliance Check**
Under **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b)**, a denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reason for the denial. 
*   **Finding:** The denial notice issued on October 15, 2024, cited CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 and identified the specific missing elements (6 weeks of PT, lack of an injection). While the clinical reasoning itself was substantively incorrect (as detailed below), the notice was procedurally structured, allowing the evaluation to proceed to Step 2.

#### **Step 2: Diagnostic Classification and Severity Analysis**
A critical clinical distinction must be made between cervical radiculopathy (nerve root compression) and cervical myelopathy (spinal cord compression). 
*   **Clinical Exam Audit:** The neurological examination dated August 25, 2024, documents profound **upper motor neuron (UMN) signs**: bilateral lower extremity spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor skills in both hands, and a positive Lhermitte sign.
*   **Imaging Audit:** The MRI (dated August 20, 2024, performed via a pacemaker-compatible protocol) confirms **severe central cord compression at C4-C5 with T2 signal change (myelomalacia)**.
*   **Classification:** The member's primary pathology is classified as **severe, progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia**. There is objective spinal cord injury, which is a fundamentally different diagnostic category than simple radiculopathy.

#### **Step 3: Statutory Alignment Check (California Law Overrides)**
Under California law, the plan's conservative treatment rules are voided by two statutory provisions:
1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3):** Insurers shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Severe central cord compression and myelomalacia (T2 signal change) constitute severe, objective structural compromise. Physical therapy cannot mechanically decompress the spinal cord or repair myelomalacia. Thus, applying a 6-week physical therapy requirement violates CIC § 10169.5.
2.  **Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:** This mandate explicitly states that plans **shall not** require a full course of conservative treatment where objective imaging shows progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia) and the physician documents conservative treatment failure. The member completed 5 weeks (15 sessions) of PT, which was suspended because the treating physician and physical therapist determined it was not addressing, and potentially exacerbating, the patient's progressive neurological decline.
3.  **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1):** The treating neurosurgeon and anesthesiologist (Dr. Rivera, 2024-09-10) documented that a fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injection was **contraindicated** due to her permanent pacemaker and the risk of neurological deterioration in the presence of severe cord compression. Under § 10169.5(a)(1), conservative measures cannot be mandated if documented as contraindicated.

#### **Step 4: Plan Criteria Application and Exception Check**
The initial reviewer failed to apply the plan's own internal guidelines correctly, specifically **CC-SPINE-2024 Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway)**.
*   Under the **Myelopathy Exception**:
    *   The physical therapy requirement is reduced from 6 weeks to a **4-week minimum**. The member completed **5 weeks** (15 sessions), satisfying this criterion.
    *   The interventional treatment requirement (epidural injection) is **waived** when the physician documents that interventional procedures pose a risk of neurological deterioration. The neurosurgeon documented this risk, supported by an anesthesiology consult on September 10, 2024, citing the pacemaker and the physical threat of needle placement near an already compromised, myelomalacic spinal cord.
    *   The pharmacotherapy requirement (2 agents) was met: the patient tried Baclofen 10mg TID for 3 months (muscle relaxant) and two 10-day courses of oral Methylprednisolone (oral corticosteroid) with only temporary/partial relief.
*   **Finding:** The plan committed a clinical category error by applying standard Section 2.2 criteria (demanding 6 weeks of PT and an injection) instead of Exception 2.2(A), despite the member’s clear primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy.

#### **Step 5: Clinical Standards and Evidence-Based Necessity Evaluation**
Pursuant to **CHSC § 1374.32(b)** (the Independent Medical Review standard), a health service must be approved if it is in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice, regardless of whether it meets a plan's more restrictive internal guidelines.
*   **AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines:** Recommend immediate surgical decompression for moderate-to-severe myelopathy with objective spinal cord compression and myelomalacia (**Tier 1 — Strong Surgical Indication**). Delaying surgical decompression to satisfy an arbitrary conservative treatment timeline (such as a 6-week PT rule) is clinically contraindicated because myelomalacia represents active spinal cord injury; further delay increases the risk of permanent, irreversible neurological deficit (quadriparesis, neurogenic bowel/bladder).
*   **AO Spine 2021 Clinical Practice Guidelines:** Conclude there is no high-quality evidence supporting mandatory physical therapy trials for patients with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy. Forcing a physical therapy trial in this cohort is a clinical deviation that risks patient safety.

### **DETERMINATION DECISION PATHWAY**
1.  **Diagnostic Audit:** Progressive Cervical Myelopathy with Myelomalacia is confirmed (Step 2).
2.  **Statutory Review:** Under **CIC § 10169.5** and **DMHC APL 22-014**, the 6-week physical therapy and epidural injection requirements are legally waived due to structural neurological compromise and documented contraindications (Step 3).
3.  **Plan Review:** Even within the plan's policy, the member meets **Exception 2.2(A)** (Myelopathy Pathway) because they completed >4 weeks of PT (5 weeks documented) and had the injection waived (Step 4).
4.  **Clinical Standards:** The surgery is highly indicated as a **Tier 1 Surgical Indication** under AANS/CNS guidelines (Step 5).
