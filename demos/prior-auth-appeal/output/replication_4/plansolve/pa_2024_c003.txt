### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

HealthFirst has completed its clinical and regulatory review of the first-level appeal submitted on behalf of member Helen Brooks for a one-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 (CPT 22551). 

The initial denial, dated November 20, 2024, was based on the assertion that the member did not meet the standard conservative treatment pathway because an interventional procedure (such as an epidural steroid injection) had not been performed. Additionally, the plan’s initial reviewer classified the MRI T2-weighted signal change as a "minimal artifact" below the threshold of clinical significance, thereby refusing to apply the plan's internal Myelopathy Exception.

Upon comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, independent radiologic assessments from UCSF Medical Center, and the application of California statutory overrides, **the initial denial is OVERTURNED**. 

The clinical evidence confirms that Ms. Brooks suffers from early cervical myelopathy with objective spinal cord compression and reproducible myelomalacia (T2 hyperintensity) at C4-C5, corroborated by objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs on neurological examination. Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, conservative requirements (specifically interventional procedures) are legally waived in cases of structural neurological compromise. Furthermore, the member met all requirements of the plan's internal Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A), rendering the initial denial clinically and procedurally erroneous.

### EVALUATION AND FINDINGS

#### Step 1: Procedural Adequacy & Defect Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
The initial denial notice issued on November 20, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, was procedurally adequate. It specified the criteria applied (the standard conservative pathway under CC-SPINE-2024), provided a clear clinical rationale (asserting that T2 signal changes represented artifact and citing the lack of an interventional block), and included standard notices regarding Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights under California law. 

*Finding:* Because the denial was procedurally valid, a procedural remand is not triggered. The evaluation proceeds to substantive clinical and regulatory review.

#### Step 2: Diagnostic Verification & Disease Classification (Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy)
The clinical record was reviewed to classify the member's pathology. Under generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023), cervical myelopathy requires objective spinal cord compression on imaging combined with objective clinical upper motor neuron (UMN) signs and/or gait disturbances.
*   **Neurological Exam (dated October 5, 2024):** Confirms bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia of the upper extremities, and subtle balance impairment on tandem gait. 

*Finding:* These are classic upper motor neuron (UMN) findings. The clinical presentation is diagnostic of cervical myelopathy as the primary disease entity. Classification as simple cervical radiculopathy is clinically incorrect.

#### Step 3: Objective Imaging Assessment (Cord Compression & Myelomalacia Verification)
The plan's initial denial relied heavily on an internal plan radiologist review (dated November 5, 2024) asserting that the T2 signal change on the October 1, 2024, MRI was "equivocal," "minimal," and "represents artifact." 

However, a formal second read was performed on October 15, 2024, by Radiologist B at the treating academic medical center (UCSF). This expert interpretation confirmed:
*   C4-C5 cord compression with early myelomalacia.
*   The T2 hyperintensity is **reproducible on multiple sequences** and represents early spinal cord signal change (myelomalacia), not an artifact.
*   The findings are highly consistent with the member's clinical presentation of early cervical myelopathy.

*Finding:* Under clinical standards, a reproducible T2 hyperintensity on multiple MRI sequences cannot be summarily dismissed as an artifact, especially when it correlates perfectly with bilateral UMN signs (Hoffman's, hyperreflexia). Objective spinal cord compression with early myelomalacia is confirmed.

#### Step 4: Application of California Statutory Overrides (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)
Because the member resides in California and is covered under a California-regulated health plan, state statutory mandates supersede more restrictive internal plan policies:
1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3):** Prohibits a health plan from conditioning the approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy or other conservative trials where *"the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."* Objective spinal cord compression with myelomalacia constitutes severe structural neurological compromise.
2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:** Mandates that plans *shall not* require a full course of conservative treatment (including interventional blocks) as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (specifically citing myelomalacia/cord signal change) and the treating physician documents a clinical contraindication or risk of delay.

*Finding:* California law prohibits the health plan from denying this surgical request based on a missing interventional procedure, as doing so would violate both CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014.

#### Step 5: Clinical Standard Review (AANS/CNS 2023 & AO Spine 2021)
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), medical necessity must be evaluated using generally accepted clinical standards rather than a plan's restrictive internal criteria:
*   **AANS/CNS Guidelines (2023):** Moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI is classified as a **Tier 1 (Strong) Surgical Indication**. Delaying surgical decompression risks irreversible spinal cord damage and permanent neurological deficits.
*   **AO Spine Guidelines (2021):** Confirms there is no high-quality evidence supporting mandatory physical therapy or interventional blocks prior to surgery for patients with cervical myelopathy. In fact, AO Spine guidelines explicitly state that delaying surgery in patients with myelomalacia to complete physical therapy or interventional trials is clinically contraindicated and increases the risk of permanent neurological decline.

*Finding:* Surgical decompression via ACDF C4-C5 is the standard of care and is medically necessary under generally accepted clinical guidelines.

#### Step 6: Standard Radiculopathy Pathway Evaluation
*Finding:* Not applicable. The primary diagnosis is established as cervical myelopathy (Step 2), not radiculopathy.

#### Step 7: Plan Criteria Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A) Evaluation
Even if evaluated strictly under the plan's internal guidelines (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024), the initial denial was issued in error because the member met all requirements of **Exception 2.2(A) — Myelopathy Pathway**:
1.  **Primary Diagnosis:** Cervical myelopathy is confirmed as the primary clinical diagnosis.
2.  **Physical Therapy:** The plan’s exception requires a minimum of 4 weeks. The member completed **8 weeks (24 sessions)** of supervised PT at UCSF with documented functional plateau (no improvement in UMN signs).
3.  **Pharmacotherapy:** The member completed more than 4 weeks of two agents: Gabapentin 400mg TID (3 months) and Cyclobenzaprine (6 weeks).
4.  **Interventional Waiver:** Section 2.2(A) waives the interventional block if the physician documents that the procedure poses a risk of neurological deterioration. Dr. James Park documented that epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated in patients with significant mechanical spinal cord compression and myelomalacia due to the risk of acute mass effect and cord injury.

*Finding:* The member met 100% of the plan's internal criteria for the Myelopathy Exception. The initial reviewer's failure to apply this exception was a clinical misapplication of the plan's own policy.

### RESOLUTION OF CLINICAL CONFLICT (HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY)

In resolving the conflict between the initial plan reviewer (Dr. Alan Foster) and the treating neurosurgeon (Dr. James Park/UCSF Medical Center), we apply the strict regulatory hierarchy mandated by the State of California:

$$\text{California Law \& Regulatory Mandates (APL 22-014 / CIC \S\ 10169.5)} > \text{Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023)} > \text{Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)}$$

1.  **California Law (Highest Authority):** Legally waives conservative and interventional prerequisites for patients with objective structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) under CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014.
2.  **Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (Intermediate Authority):** Recommends prompt surgical decompression (Tier 1 indication) and warns that requiring interventional steroid blocks or further conservative delay in the presence of myelomalacia is medically inappropriate and poses a severe clinical risk.
3.  **Plan Criteria (Lowest Authority):** Even under the plan’s own criteria, the member fully satisfied the clinical requirements of the Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2(A)). 

The initial reviewer's categorization of the MRI findings as "minimal artifact" is directly refuted by reproducible, multi-sequence imaging and matching clinical signs. Denying surgical coverage under these circumstances violates California state insurance regulations and contradicts established clinical practice guidelines.
