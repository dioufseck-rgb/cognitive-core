### **FINAL DISPOSITION: OVERTURN IN FULL**

The initial determination by HealthFirst to deny coverage for the requested C6-C7 Anterior Cervical Decompression and Fusion (ACDF) is **OVERTURNED**. HealthFirst is directed to authorize CPT code 22551 immediately.

---

### **EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

The member is a 63-year-old male presenting with a mixed cervical myelopathy and radiculopathy at the C6-C7 level. A cervical MRI dated September 1, 2024, reveals moderate spinal cord compression at C6-C7 accompanied by T2 signal change, indicative of **myelomalacia** (active spinal cord injury). Physical examination confirms upper motor neuron (UMN) signs including bilateral hyperreflexia, a positive right Hoffman's sign, and a mildly wide-based gait, alongside right-sided radicular deficits (4/5 right triceps weakness and finger numbness). 

HealthFirst denied the procedure on October 5, 2024, on the grounds that the myelopathy exception (which waives or reduces conservative care requirements) was inapplicable because the member's symptoms are primarily right-sided, making radiculopathy the "primary" diagnosis. 

This review finds the initial denial to be clinically unsound, factually incorrect under the plan’s own criteria, and in direct violation of California statutory law and national clinical standards. Specifically:
1. **Plan Criteria Violation:** The member fully satisfied the standard 6-week conservative treatment pathway under CC-SPINE-2024. The plan’s reviewer committed an analytical error by ignoring standard pathway completion and focusing solely on the myelopathy exception.
2. **Statutory Overrides:** Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, the presence of myelomalacia on MRI represents a progressive structural neurological compromise that legally waives standard conservative treatment requirements.
3. **Clinical Standard Override:** Under AANS/CNS (2023) and AO Spine (2021) guidelines, moderate-to-severe myelopathy with myelomalacia is a Tier 1 strong surgical indication. Delaying surgery for physical therapy in this population is clinically contraindicated and poses a severe risk of permanent neurological deficit.

Applying the governing **Hierarchy of Authority** (State Law $>$ Clinical Standards $>$ Plan Criteria), the initial denial must be overturned.

### **DETAILED EVALUATION FINDINGS**

#### **Step 1: Procedural Adequacy Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))**
* **Finding:** The initial denial notice issued on October 5, 2024, specified the clinical criteria applied (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024) and detailed the clinical rationale (asserting that radiculopathy was the primary diagnosis and that the myelopathy exception requirements were not met). Because the notice specified the criteria and reasons for the decision, it is **procedurally adequate** under California Health & Safety Code § 1374.31(b). The appeal cannot be overturned on procedural technicalities alone and must be decided on its clinical and statutory merits.

#### **Step 2: Diagnostic and Pathology Verification**
* **Finding:** Review of the submitted objective clinical evidence confirms the presence of severe cervical spine pathology. 
  * **Imaging (MRI dated September 1, 2024):** Demonstrates moderate spinal cord compression at C6-C7 and, critically, **T2-weighted hyperintense signal change within the cord**, confirming **myelomalacia**. It also demonstrates prominent right C7 foraminal stenosis.
  * **Neurological Examination (dated September 5, 2024):** Documents bilateral hyperreflexia (right greater than left), a positive right Hoffman's sign, and a mildly wide-based gait (objective UMN signs of spinal cord compression). It also documents right triceps weakness (4/5) and right finger numbness (LMN radicular signs).
  * **Conclusion:** The pathology is objectively verified as a mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy with MRI-proven myelomalacia.

#### **Step 3: California Statutory Override Evaluation (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)**
* **Finding:** California law strictly prohibits health plans from enforcing conservative therapy rules in the presence of structural spinal cord damage.
  * **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3):** Mandates that a health insurer **shall not** condition surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy where the member's condition involves "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Cord compression and myelomalacia constitute severe structural compromise that physical therapy cannot reverse or treat.
  * **DMHC APL 22-014:** Explicitly establishes that plans **shall not** require a full course of conservative treatment when (a) objective imaging shows progressive pathology (specifically citing *myelomalacia* on MRI as supporting this waiver), (b) the treating physician documents conservative failure, and (c) surgery is clinically indicated. 
  * **Application:** Because Mr. Santos has objective MRI-proven myelomalacia and cord compression, California state law **overrides** any conservative therapy prerequisites. Delaying surgery under these conditions is a violation of state regulatory standards.

#### **Step 4: Clinical Standard Review (CHSC § 1374.32(b), AANS/CNS & AO Spine)**
* **Finding:** Under California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32(b), the standard of medical necessity is governed by generally accepted standards of medical practice, not a plan’s restrictive internal criteria.
  * **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023):** Classify moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI as a **Tier 1 Strong Surgical Indication**. Immediate surgical decompression is recommended to arrest progression and prevent permanent spinal cord injury.
  * **AO Spine Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):** State that there is **no scientific evidence** to support a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgery in patients with cervical myelopathy and cord signal changes. The guidelines warn that delaying surgery to complete an arbitrary physical therapy trial is clinically unsupported and "may result in irreversible neurological deterioration."
  * **Application:** Clinically, the member has an active, permanent spinal cord injury (myelomalacia) and objective myelopathic signs (hyperreflexia, Hoffman's sign, gait deficit). Forcing further conservative care is clinically contraindicated. The surgical request meets national standards of medical necessity, overriding the plan's "primary diagnosis" restriction.

#### **Step 5: Plan Criteria Compliance Check (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)**
* **Finding:** Even if state law and clinical standards did not override the plan’s guidelines, the initial reviewer committed a **factual and clinical error** in applying the plan's internal criteria:
  * **The Denial Error:** The initial reviewer denied the request under "Exception 2.2(A)" (the Myelopathy Exception) because the reviewer determined the "primary" diagnosis was radiculopathy due to the right-sided predominance of the member's pain.
  * **Standard Pathway Satisfied:** The reviewer completely failed to evaluate whether the member satisfied the **standard conservative treatment requirements (Section 2.2)**. The clinical record demonstrates that the member fully completed the standard pathway:
    * **Section 2.2(a) (Physical Therapy):** Completed 6 weeks (18 sessions) of physical therapy (myelopathy protocol), reaching a documented functional plateau with no improvement.
    * **Section 2.2(b) (Pharmacotherapy):** Trialed Pregabalin (neuropathic agent) for 3 months and Naproxen (NSAID) for 2 months, representing two distinct classes for $>$ 4 weeks each.
    * **Section 2.2(c) (Interventional):** Completed 1 right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 28, 2024, with only temporary (3-week) relief.
  * **Conclusion under Plan Criteria:** Because the member completed 6 weeks of PT, 2 medications, and 1 injection, the member **satisfied the full standard conservative pathway under Section 2.2**. The debate over whether myelopathy or radiculopathy is the "primary" diagnosis is irrelevant, as the member met the most stringent criteria of the policy. Denial under plan guidelines was factually incorrect.

### **SUMMARY OF DISPOSITION DETERMINATION**

Pursuant to the **Hierarchy of Authority**:
1. **State Law (Highest Authority):** Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) and DMHC APL 22-014, the plan is legally prohibited from requiring physical therapy or conservative trials for a patient with MRI-proven myelomalacia and cord compression. **(Mandates OVERTURN)**
2. **Clinical Standards:** Under AANS/CNS 2023 and AO Spine 2021, the member’s presentation is a Tier 1 strong surgical indication. Delaying surgery represents a severe patient safety risk. **(Mandates OVERTURN)**
3. **Plan Criteria (Lowest Authority):** The member completed the standard conservative trial (Section 2.2) of 6 weeks of PT, 2 medications, and an epidural injection, rendering the plan's denial factually incorrect. **(Mandates OVERTURN)**

Consequently, the denial of anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 is **OVERTURNED** in its entirety.
