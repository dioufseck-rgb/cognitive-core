## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This formal appeal review evaluates the medical necessity of a requested two-level anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C5–C6 and C6–C7. The health plan's initial review approved the clinical pathology at C5–C6 but denied the entire two-level procedure because the C6–C7 level did not meet clinical coverage criteria. 

Applying the mandatory **California Hierarchy of Authority** ($\text{State Law \& Regulations} > \text{Generally Accepted Clinical Practice Standards} > \text{HealthFirst Plan Criteria}$), this review finds that:
1. The C5–C6 level fully satisfies the plan's medical necessity criteria, statutory standards, and clinical practice guidelines due to severe, symptomatic, and conservative-treatment-refractory C6 radiculopathy.
2. The C6–C7 level does not exhibit objective radiographic nerve root or spinal cord compression, lacks correlating clinical or dermatomal symptoms, and fails to satisfy plan criteria, statutory override provisions, or generally accepted clinical practice guidelines. 

Consequently, the health plan's initial denial is **OVERTURNED IN PART** to authorize the single-level C5–C6 ACDF (CPT 22551) and **UPHELD IN PART** to deny the additional C6–C7 level (CPT 22552).

## DETAILED EVALUATION FINDINGS

### STEP 1: PROCEDURAL ADEQUACY ASSESSMENT (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), a plan’s denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied, the specific clinical reason for the denial, and notice of Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights. A failure to provide this specific medical rationale renders the notice legally defective, resulting in an automatic procedural overturn.

*   **Finding:** The initial denial notice issued by Dr. Patricia Huang on 2024-11-01 is **procedurally adequate and legally valid**.
*   **Rationale:** The denial notice clearly articulated that while the C5–C6 level demonstrated pathology consistent with surgical criteria, the C6–C7 level demonstrated "mild changes only" on imaging. The plan explained the clinical rationale—that a two-level fusion cannot be approved when one of the requested levels fails to meet medical necessity criteria. Because the notice provided a clear, level-specific clinical explanation and cited the standard surgical criteria used by the plan, it satisfies the transparency requirements of CHSC § 1374.31(b). 
*   **Procedural Action:** Because no procedural defect exists, this appeal will be determined based on its clinical and statutory merits.

### STEP 2: DIAGNOSIS & CLINICAL PATHOLOGY CLASSIFICATION
Generally accepted spine care standards (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021) require a fundamental distinction between **cervical radiculopathy** (nerve root compression) and **cervical myelopathy** (spinal cord compression), as they dictate separate clinical pathways.

*   **C5–C6 Level Pathology: Confirmed Severe Cervical Radiculopathy (Right C6)**
    *   *Imaging:* MRI dated 2024-09-15 demonstrates a large disc herniation, moderate-to-severe right C6 nerve root compression, and foraminal stenosis.
    *   *Neurological Exam:* Clinical examination on 2024-09-20 reveals objective focal deficits correlating precisely with the imaging: right bicep weakness (3+/5), an absent right bicep reflex, and right C6 dermatomal numbness and tingling.
    *   *Classification:* This level presents objective, severe, and correlating **cervical radiculopathy**.

*   **C6–C7 Level Pathology: Mild, Asymptomatic Degenerative Disc Disease**
    *   *Imaging:* MRI dated 2024-09-15 shows only a small disc bulge and mild foraminal narrowing. Critically, there is **no nerve root compression identifiable and no spinal cord contact**.
    *   *Neurological Exam:* Clinical examination on 2024-09-20 reveals a slightly diminished right tricep reflex but **no definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms**. The physical therapy discharge note at week 8 explicitly documents that C7 symptoms were "minimal and possibly resolving."
    *   *Classification:* This level represents mild degenerative changes without correlating radiculopathy.

*   **Myelopathy Evaluation (Both Levels): Negative**
    *   There is no MRI evidence of spinal cord compression, cord contact, or T2 signal changes (myelomalacia) at any cervical level.
    *   Clinical examination is entirely negative for upper motor neuron signs (no hyperreflexia, no positive Hoffman sign, no clonus, no gait instability, and no bilateral upper extremity symptoms).
    *   *Classification:* The member **does not have cervical myelopathy**.

### STEP 4: STATUTORY OVERRIDE ANALYSIS (CA INSURANCE CODE & DMHC APL)
Because California state law supersedes health plan internal policies, we evaluated whether California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 or Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 mandates an override of the plan’s conservative treatment or multi-level criteria.

*   **CIC § 10169.5 Analysis:** Under subdivision (a)(3), a plan cannot condition surgical approval on physical therapy if there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address (such as spinal cord compression or myelomalacia). Because the member's condition is purely radicular and does not involve cord compression or myelomalacia, this statutory waiver does not apply to the C6–C7 level. (The C5–C6 level has already fully completed PT, rendering a waiver unnecessary).
*   **DMHC APL 22-014 Analysis:** This regulatory letter prohibits plans from requiring conservative therapy where objective imaging shows progressive pathology (e.g., myelomalacia or progressive cord signal changes) and delaying surgery risks irreversible neurological harm. Because the member has no cord compression or myelomalacia, this regulatory waiver does not apply.
*   **Conclusion:** There are no California statutory or regulatory provisions that override the health plan's requirements for the C6–C7 level.

---

### STEP 5: CLINICAL PRACTICE STANDARDS OVERRIDE ANALYSIS
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) (the standard applied during Independent Medical Review), a health plan's internal criteria cannot be the sole basis for a denial if the requested service is medically necessary under generally accepted clinical practice standards.

*   **C5–C6 Level (CPT 22551) Clinical Standard:** The American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS 2023) and the North American Spine Society (NASS 2020) clinical guidelines support surgical decompression and fusion (Tier 2 indication) for patients with persistent, severe cervical radiculopathy who exhibit objective motor deficits (3+/5 bicep weakness) and have failed a comprehensive course of conservative therapy (PT, neuropathic medications, and epidural injections). Clinical standards strongly support surgical intervention at C5–C6.
*   **C6–C7 Level (CPT 22552) Clinical Standard:** The treating physician’s appeal argues that the adjacent C6–C7 level should be fused "simultaneously to prevent accelerated degeneration." 
    *   However, generally accepted standards of medical practice (AO Spine 2021, NASS 2020) **strongly advise against prophylactic fusion of adjacent levels** that lack correlating clinical symptoms or objective radiographic compression. 
    *   Extending an ACDF to an asymptomatic, non-compressed adjacent level (C6–C7) does not prevent adjacent segment disease. On the contrary, clinical literature demonstrates that expanding a fusion to an unnecessary level increases overall surgical morbidity, reduces cervical range of motion, and shifts the biomechanical stress further down the spine (to C7–T1), potentially accelerating degeneration at that next junction.
    *   Because there is no radiographic nerve root compression and no correlating clinical symptoms at C6–C7, performing a fusion at this level is inconsistent with prudent clinical judgment and generally accepted standards of medical practice.
*   **Conclusion:** Generally accepted clinical standards align with the plan's criteria. They support surgical fusion at C5–C6 but do not support fusion at C6–C7.

## RESOLUTION SUMMARY

1.  **CPT 22551 (ACDF, single level, C5–C6): OVERTURNED (APPROVED).** The member has severe, symptomatic, and objectively verified C6 radiculopathy with significant motor weakness (3+/5). He has completed 8 weeks of physical therapy, 4 months of neuropathic medication, and a cervical epidural steroid injection with only temporary relief. Surgical decompression and fusion at this level are medically necessary under both HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) and generally accepted clinical practice standards (AANS/CNS 2023).
2.  **CPT 22552 (ACDF, additional level, C6–C7): UPHELD (DENIED).** The C6–C7 level exhibits only mild degenerative changes on MRI, with no identifiable nerve root compression and no spinal cord contact. The member has no correlating C7 dermatomal symptoms, and C7-specific conservative therapy was never indicated or attempted. Prophylactic fusion to prevent adjacent segment disease is not supported by peer-reviewed clinical guidelines (AO Spine 2021) and exposes the member to unnecessary surgical risks. 

Accordingly, the plan is directed to authorize a single-level anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C5–C6 (CPT 22551) and deny authorization for the additional C6–C7 level (CPT 22552).
