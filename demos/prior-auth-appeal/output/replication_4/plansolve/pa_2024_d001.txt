### I. FORMAL DISPOSITION

The clinical denial of prior authorization for CPT Code 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) is hereby **UPHELD**.

Under generally accepted clinical standards (CHSC § 1374.32(b)) and the governing Hierarchy of Authority, the requested ACDF is not medically necessary at this time because the member has a stable, non-emergent cervical radiculopathy and has not completed an adequate trial of conservative management.

### III. STEP-BY-STEP EVALUATION

#### STEP 1: Procedural Adequacy & Compliance Review
*   **Authority:** California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b).
*   **Analysis:** Review of the initial denial notice dated October 5, 2024, issued by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, reveals major omissions:
    1.  The notice contains only a generic, boilerplate reference to "plan criteria" without citing the specific clinical policy name, section number, or exact criteria applied.
    2.  The notice fails to include the mandatory, state-regulated Independent Medical Review (IMR) notification.
*   **Finding (Negative):** The denial notice is **defective** under CHSC § 1374.31(b). A denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or fails to include the mandatory state-regulated IMR notice constitutes a fatal procedural defect. Under state law, this must be treated as an administrative failure and documented as a primary basis of appeal. However, because performing unnecessary spinal fusion risks patient harm, this administrative defect does not bypass a rigorous review of clinical necessity. The review must proceed to Step 2.

#### STEP 2: Diagnostic Classification and Severity Assessment
*   **Authority:** Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023, AO Spine Clinical Practice Guidelines 2021); HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1.
*   **Analysis:** 
    *   *Imaging:* The MRI dated August 15, 2024, demonstrates "C6-C7 disc herniation, moderate right C7 nerve root compression." Crucially, the radiologist notes: **"No cord contact. No myelomalacia."**
    *   *Neurological Exam:* Dr. Paul Lee's physical exam on August 20, 2024, documents "Right C7 dermatomal numbness, tricep weakness 4/5 right, tricep reflex diminished." These are unilateral, lower motor neuron findings matching a C7 nerve root distribution. There are no bilateral upper extremity deficits, no upper motor neuron signs (e.g., hyperreflexia, clonus, positive Hoffman sign), and no gait instability.
*   **Finding (Negative):** The clinical record does not support a diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (which requires objective spinal cord compression, upper motor neuron signs, and/or cord signal changes/myelomalacia). Instead, the clinical presentation and objective imaging are classified as **Cervical Radiculopathy** under Section 2.1(a). The standard Radiculopathy clinical pathway must be applied.

#### STEP 3: Application of Plan-Specific Criteria and Internal Exceptions
*   **Authority:** HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2.
*   **Analysis:** For a primary diagnosis of Cervical Radiculopathy, the member must satisfy the standard conservative treatment requirements under Section 2.2:
    1.  *Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)):* Requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy with documented functional outcomes. *The member has completed only 4 weeks (12 sessions).* While the member self-reported a functional plateau, the physical therapy discharge summary does not support this language or document a clinical plateau.
    2.  *Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)):* Requires a trial of at least two classes of agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. *The member has trialed only one agent* (Ibuprofen 800mg TID for 6 weeks, providing partial relief).
    3.  *Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)):* Requires at least one interventional procedure (e.g., cervical epidural steroid injection). *The member has completed zero interventional procedures.*
    4.  *Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway):* This exception, which reduces the PT requirement to 4 weeks and waives the interventional treatment requirement, does *not* apply because the member's primary diagnosis is radiculopathy without cord compression or myelopathy.
*   **Finding (Negative):** The member has not completed the standard conservative treatment trials required under HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2.

#### STEP 4: Statutory and Regulatory Override Assessment
*   **Authority:** California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5; DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014.
*   **Analysis:** We must evaluate whether state mandates override the plan's conservative therapy requirements:
    *   *CIC § 10169.5(a)(1):* The treating physician has not documented in writing that PT is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member.
    *   *CIC § 10169.5(a)(2):* There is no provider-documented "functional plateau" (defined as a documented failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement after a course of therapy, as determined by the therapist or physician).
    *   *CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) & DMHC APL 22-014:* These provisions prohibit plans from requiring conservative therapy completion when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address, or progressive pathology. Under APL 22-014, the presence of **myelomalacia** or severe cord compression on MRI specifically supports a waiver of conservative trials. However, the member's MRI explicitly rules out cord contact and myelomalacia, demonstrating stable, moderate nerve root compression.
*   **Finding (Negative):** The statutory and regulatory overrides under CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014 do not apply. The plan's conservative therapy requirements are legally valid in this clinical scenario.

#### STEP 5: Clinical Standards Conformity Review
*   **Authority:** CHSC § 1374.32(b); AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023); AO Spine Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021).
*   **Analysis:** Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the standard of medical necessity must align with generally accepted standards of medical practice, which override plan-specific policies if those policies are more restrictive.
    *   According to the **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023)**, a 1-level ACDF for cervical radiculopathy is a Tier 2 "reasonable option" only after a failed course of 6 to 12 weeks of conservative therapy. Proceeding directly to major spinal fusion after only 4 weeks of physical therapy and a single NSAID trial, with no interventional therapy trials and no progressive neurological deficit, does *not* meet generally accepted standards of medical practice.
    *   **AO Spine (2021)** and **NASS (2020)** guidelines support an initial 6-to-12-week trial of conservative therapies for stable radiculopathy. Because 70% to 80% of cervical radiculopathy cases resolve non-operatively, exposing a patient to the risks of a cervical fusion (such as adjacent segment disease, pseudarthrosis, and dysphagia) without completing an adequate conservative trial is clinically inappropriate.
*   **Finding (Negative):** The requested procedure does not meet generally accepted clinical standards of medical necessity at this time.

### IV. CLINICAL RATIONALE & DISCUSSION

This case represents a clinical scenario where the member has a clear, objectively confirmed diagnosis of **Cervical Radiculopathy** at the C6-C7 level. The member's symptoms (unilateral C7 dermatomal numbness, 4/5 tricep weakness, and diminished tricep reflex) correlate with the August 15, 2024 MRI findings of moderate right C7 nerve root compression. 

However, there is no clinical or radiographic evidence of **Cervical Myelopathy**. The spinal cord itself is entirely free of compression ("no cord contact"), and there is no spinal cord injury or signal change ("no myelomalacia"). 

For stable, non-emergent cervical radiculopathy, both HealthFirst plan criteria and national neurosurgical standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) require an adequate trial of conservative management prior to electing a major surgical fusion. This trial is designed to prevent unnecessary surgery, as the majority of radiculopathies resolve with non-operative care. 

The member has only completed **4 weeks of physical therapy**, trialed only **one medication class** (Ibuprofen), and has had **no interventional trials** (such as a cervical epidural steroid injection). While the member self-reported a "functional plateau," this is not documented in the physical therapy discharge summary or by the treating neurosurgeon, and does not meet the legal definition of a functional plateau under CIC § 10169.5(b). 

Because there is no progressive neurological deficit, cord compression, or myelomalacia, there is no medical justification to bypass the remaining conservative therapy trials. Therefore, the request for CPT 22551 is clinically premature and is not medically necessary.

3.  **Interventional Consultation:** Undergo at least one cervical epidural steroid injection (or provide documented clinical justification from Dr. Lee explaining why an injection poses an unacceptable risk of neurological deterioration).

If the member's symptoms fail to improve after these steps, or if she experiences a documented progressive motor deficit (e.g., worsening tricep weakness below 4/5), a re-submitted request for ACDF should be approved immediately.
