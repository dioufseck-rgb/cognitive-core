### I. FORMAL DETERMINATION
Pursuant to the California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.30 et seq., the Clinical Appeals Division has completed its evaluation of the first-level prior authorization appeal for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). 

Following a rigorous, step-by-step review of the clinical record, objective imaging, plan criteria, California statutory frameworks, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, this reviewer finds that the requested prophylactic procedure is **not medically necessary** for the management of the member's condition. 

Accordingly, the Health Plan's initial denial of coverage dated October 25, 2024, is **UPHELD**.

---

### II. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The member is a 54-year-old male employed as a construction worker (heavy equipment operator) who presents with axial neck pain (5/10) and severe central canal stenosis at C5-C6. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) dated September 15, 2024, demonstrates a critically narrowed cervical canal diameter of 6mm with spinal cord contact, but explicitly shows **no spinal cord compression, no T2 signal changes, and no myelomalacia**. 

A comprehensive physical and neurological examination on September 20, 2024, was completely normal, demonstrating no radicular symptoms, no upper motor neuron (UMN) signs (e.g., negative Hoffman's sign, normal reflexes), and no myelopathic gait or functional disturbances. 

The requesting surgeon, Dr. Karen Davis, MD, seeks authorization for a single-level ACDF on a prophylactic basis, citing the member's high-risk occupation and the risk of acute spinal cord injury in the event of minor cervical trauma. The health plan denied the request under criteria document **CC-SPINE-2024 (Reason Code: MED-003)**, stating that surgical intervention is not indicated for axial neck pain in the absence of documented radiculopathy or myelopathy.

Applying the governing hierarchy of authority ($$\text{California State Law \& Regulations} > \text{Generally Accepted Clinical Standards} > \text{HealthFirst Plan Criteria}$$), this review concludes that because the member lacks objective neurological deficits, radiculopathy, or myelopathy on physical exam, and exhibits no cord compression or myelomalacia on MRI, standard clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021) classify this scenario as a **Tier 3 indication (Surgery Not Indicated)**. Prophylactic surgical decompression for asymptomatic or axial-pain-only stenosis is not supported by clinical evidence or statutory overrides, necessitating an uphold of the health plan's decision.

### III. STEP-BY-STEP APPEAL EVALUATION

#### Step 1: Procedural Defect Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
Under California Health and Safety Code **§ 1374.31(b)**, a health plan's medical necessity denial must specify the exact clinical criteria applied and detail the specific clinical reasons for the denial. A notice failing these requirements is procedurally defective.
*   **Review Findings:** The initial denial notice issued on October 25, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, cited the specific clinical policy utilized (**CC-SPINE-2024**) and provided detailed clinical reasons: "Cervical stenosis documented but no neurological deficit on examination. Member has axial neck pain only. No radiculopathy, no myelopathy documented. Surgical intervention not indicated for axial pain without neurological deficit."
*   **Procedural Determination:** **Positive Finding.** The denial notice met all statutory procedural standards. No procedural defects are present. The review proceeds to Step 2.

#### Step 2: Clinical & Imaging Record Verification (Fact-Finding)
An audit of the submitted clinical documentation was conducted to verify the surgeon's claims of progressive pathology, structural neurological compromise, or myelopathic symptoms.
*   **Neurological Examination (September 20, 2024):** Completely normal. There is no evidence of cervical radiculopathy (no dermatomal sensory loss, motor weakness, or reflex changes). There are no upper motor neuron (UMN) signs of cervical myelopathy (Hoffman's sign is negative, reflexes are normal, gait is normal, and there is no upper/lower extremity weakness or incoordination). The clinical presentation is restricted to axial neck pain (rated 5/10).
*   **Objective Imaging (MRI, September 15, 2024):** Shows C5-C6 severe central canal stenosis with a canal diameter of 6mm (normal >13mm) and spinal cord contact. However, the radiologist notes: *"Cord contact without compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia."*
*   **Conservative Treatments Attempted:** The member completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of physical therapy, resulting in modest pain improvement (7/10 to 5/10). Pharmacotherapy included Naproxen 500mg BID for 3 months (moderate relief) and Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks. A C5-C6 epidural steroid injection on October 10, 2024, provided 60% pain relief for 6 weeks.
*   **Fact-Finding Determination:** **Negative Finding.** The clinical and imaging records confirm the member has axial neck pain only, with no documented radiculopathy or myelopathy on physical exam, and no structural cord compression or myelomalacia on MRI. The proposed surgery is strictly prophylactic. The review proceeds to Step 3.

#### Step 3: Plan Criteria Compliance Assessment (CC-SPINE-2024)
The clinical records were evaluated against HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria document **CC-SPINE-2024**.
*   **Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement):** Requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (2.1(a)), cervical myelopathy (2.1(b)), or cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit (2.1(c)). The member meets none of these criteria due to his normal neurological exam and lack of radicular or myelopathic findings.
*   **Section 3.1 (Exclusions):** Explicitly states: *"Authorization will be DENIED when... Surgery is requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy."*
*   **Section 2.2 Exception (Myelopathy Pathway):** The "Myelopathy Pathway" exception (which waives interventional blocks and reduces PT requirements) is inapplicable because the primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy is absent.
*   **Plan Compliance Determination:** **Positive Finding.** The health plan correctly applied its internal coverage guidelines. Under CC-SPINE-2024, Section 3.1, the request was appropriately denied. The review proceeds to Step 4 to evaluate state statutory overrides.

#### Step 4: Statutory Override Analysis (California Law)
We must assess whether California law overrides the plan's conservative therapy or diagnosis criteria in this case.
*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3):** This statute prohibits a plan from conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy completion if there is *"objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."* In spine care, this is defined as cord compression accompanied by myelomalacia (T2 signal changes). The member's MRI explicitly notes "no T2 signal change" and "no myelomalacia." Thus, this statutory protection is not triggered.
*   **Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter APL 22-014:** This letter prevents plans from enforcing exhaustive conservative treatment for time-sensitive, progressive neurological conditions where objective imaging shows progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia) and the physician documents urgency. Because the member's MRI is negative for myelomalacia or progressive cord injury, APL 22-014 does not apply.
*   **Statutory Override Determination:** **Negative Finding.** The member does not meet the legal threshold for structural neurological compromise or progressive pathology. Standard conservative and diagnostic requirements remain legally enforceable under California law. The review proceeds to Step 5.

#### Step 5: Generally Accepted Clinical Standards Review (AANS/CNS & AO Spine)
Under **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**, the standard for Independent Medical Review is "medical necessity" as defined by generally accepted clinical standards, which supersede restrictive plan criteria.
*   **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023):** State that surgical decompression (ACDF) is strongly recommended (**Tier 1 — Strong Indication**) for moderate-to-severe myelopathy with objective cord compression/myelomalacia on MRI, or progressive neurological deficit. Conversely, surgical decompression is **not indicated (Tier 3)** for axial neck pain in the absence of radiculopathy or clinical myelopathy, even when imaging reveals severe stenosis.
*   **AO Spine International Guidelines (2021):** Emphasize that while surgery is highly effective for established, symptomatic myelopathy, there is no high-quality clinical evidence to support prophylactic decompressive surgery (ACDF) for asymptomatic patients or those with axial pain alone, regardless of occupation or radiographic canal diameter (e.g., 6mm). The clinical risks of ACDF (e.g., dysphagia, adjacent segment disease, pseudarthrosis, infection, or nerve injury) outweigh the statistically low risk of acute spinal cord injury from hypothetical trauma.
*   **Clinical Standards Determination:** **Negative Finding.** The requested prophylactic ACDF for axial neck pain and asymptomatic stenosis is classified as a **Tier 3 (Not Indicated)** procedure. It does not meet the generally accepted standards of medical practice.

### IV. DISPOSITION DECISION
Following the **Numbered Appeal Evaluation Plan Guidelines**:
1.  **Step 1:** The denial was procedurally adequate (**Positive**).
2.  **Step 2:** Clinical and imaging records confirm the member has axial neck pain only, with no objective neurological deficits, radiculopathy, or myelopathy on examination (**Negative**).
3.  **Step 3:** The plan correctly applied its CC-SPINE-2024 criteria, identifying that the diagnostic requirements were not met (**Positive**).
4.  **Step 4:** No structural neurological compromise (cord compression or myelomalacia) is present on MRI to trigger California statutory overrides under **CIC § 10169.5** or **DMHC APL 22-014** (**Negative**).
5.  **Step 5:** The requested prophylactic ACDF is classified as **Tier 3 (Not Indicated)** under AANS/CNS 2023 and AO Spine 2021 clinical standards (**Negative**).

Under the **Disposition Decision Tree**, because clinical records confirm axial neck pain only with no objective deficits (Step 2 negative), no structural neurological compromise is present to trigger state statutory overrides (Step 4 negative), and the procedure is classified as Tier 3/Not Indicated under clinical guidelines (Step 5 negative), **the health plan's denial must be upheld.**

**DETERMINATION:**
The Health Plan's denial of prior authorization for single-level ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) at Stanford Medical Center is **UPHELD** in its entirety. The requested surgical intervention is not medically necessary.
