### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY OF DISPUTE

The initial request for single-level ACDF (C6-C7) was denied by the health plan on November 2, 2024, under Denial Reason Code **MED-003**. The initial reviewer, Dr. Alan Foster, MD, cited an incomplete conservative treatment trial, noting that while the plan verified 4 weeks of physical therapy from Valley Physical Therapy, the additional 4 weeks claimed by the member at Mountain Physical Therapy could not be verified because those records were missing from the initial submission. 

On appeal, the member’s representative submitted the complete clinical records from Mountain Physical Therapy, establishing a total of 8 weeks of completed physical therapy across two providers. The appellant asserts that these records were submitted with the initial request but may have been omitted during plan intake. 

Upon de novo review of the complete clinical file, including the newly submitted Mountain Physical Therapy records, the member has fully met the standard conservative therapy requirements of the plan's clinical criteria. Furthermore, California statutory protections and generally accepted clinical guidelines independently mandate the approval of this procedure due to the patient's objective, progressive neurological deficits.

#### Step 1: Procedural Adequacy and Defect Check
*   **Source Authority:** California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b)
*   **Finding:** The initial denial notice issued on November 2, 2024, failed to cite the precise clinical coverage criteria applied (e.g., HealthFirst Clinical Policy CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2). It merely referenced a generic "physical therapy requirement." Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the exact clinical criteria applied is procedurally **defective** and can be treated as if no denial was issued for the purposes of appeal timelines. While this procedural defect is sufficient to support a procedural **OVERTURN**, we proceed to a full clinical merits review to ensure a robust determination.

#### Step 2: Diagnostic Classification and Pathology Verification
*   **Source Authority:** AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines; AO Spine 2021 Guidelines; Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1)
*   **Finding:** The member's MRI dated September 1, 2024, demonstrates a large C6-C7 disc herniation with bilateral C7 nerve root compression but notes "mild cord contact *without* compression" and no cord signal changes (myelomalacia). Clinical examinations show bilateral C7 dermatomal sensory deficits and tricep weakness but **no** upper motor neuron signs (e.g., hyperreflexia, Hoffman sign, clonus, or gait disturbance). 
*   **Classification:** The pathology is classified as **Bilateral C6-C7 Cervical Radiculopathy**. The plan’s standard radiculopathy pathway applies; the myelopathy exception under Section 2.2(A) is inapplicable.

#### Step 3: Application of Plan Criteria (Standard Radiculopathy Pathway)
*   **Source Authority:** Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2)
*   **Finding:** The plan's clinical criteria under Section 2.2 require three elements of conservative care for cervical radiculopathy:
    1.  *Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)):* Minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy.
        *   *Review:* With the submission of the Mountain PT records, the plan now has documented evidence of **8 weeks** (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy (4 weeks at Valley PT and 4 weeks at Mountain PT). **(MET)**
    2.  *Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)):* Trial of at least two covered agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
        *   *Review:* The member trialed Pregabalin 150mg BID for 3 months (neuropathic agent) and Naproxen 500mg BID for 2 months (NSAID). **(MET)**
    3.  *Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)):* Minimum of one cervical epidural steroid injection.
        *   *Review:* The member underwent a bilateral C7 epidural steroid injection on September 20, 2024. **(MET)**
*   **Conclusion:** With the clinical records provided on appeal, the member has satisfied **100% of the plan's medical necessity criteria** under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2.

#### Step 4: Statutory Physical Therapy Exemptions
*   **Source Authority:** California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5
*   **Finding:** Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), a health plan is legally prohibited from conditioning the approval of a surgical procedure on additional physical therapy if the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician/therapist documents that the member has reached a **functional plateau** without adequate resolution of symptoms. 
*   **Application:** The Mountain PT discharge record explicitly documents that the member reached a clinical and functional plateau as of August 9, 2024, with persistent bilateral 4/5 tricep weakness and dermatomal sensory deficits. The plan cannot legally demand further physical therapy or delay authorization based on physical therapy requirements. **(SUPPORTIVE OF OVERTURN)**

#### Step 5: Clinical Standard Override and Urgent Pathology Review
*   **Source Authority:** CHSC § 1374.32(b); DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014; NASS 2020 Clinical Guidelines; AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines
*   **Finding:** Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the standard of medical necessity must align with generally accepted standards of medical practice, which override restrictive internal plan criteria. 
*   **Application:** Both NASS (2020) and the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023) state that surgical decompression (such as ACDF) is a **Tier 1 (Strongly Indicated)** treatment for patients with cervical radiculopathy who present with objective, progressive neurological deficits (such as bilateral 4/5 motor weakness) and fail to respond to 6–12 weeks of structured conservative care. Continued delay of surgical decompression in the presence of motor weakness (tricep 4/5) risks permanent, irreversible axonal damage and long-term motor deficits.

### HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY RESOLUTION

1.  **State Law (CIC § 10169.5 & CHSC § 1374.31(b)):** Prohibits the plan from denying surgical authorization based on physical therapy requirements once a functional plateau is documented. It also deems the initial denial notice defective due to a lack of criteria citation.
2.  **Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS & NASS):** Mandate surgical decompression for patients with objective motor deficits (4/5 weakness) who have completed 8 weeks of failed conservative therapy, overriding any further plan-imposed administrative delays.
3.  **Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):** Fully satisfied upon receipt and verification of the Mountain PT records (completing 8 weeks of PT, 3 months of Pregabalin, 2 months of Naproxen, and 1 epidural injection).

There is no conflict of authority; all tiers of the hierarchy independently and concordantly support the immediate approval of the requested surgical intervention.
