**EXPERT APPEALS REVIEW FINDINGS AND DETERMINATION**

A comprehensive review of the submitted prior authorization appeal, member's clinical record, HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory framework (CIC, CHSC, DMHC APL 22-014), and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine Guidelines) has been conducted. The review prioritizes sources in the following hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.

**1. Clinical Presentation Analysis:**
The member, Marcus Webb, presents with objective clinical findings highly consistent with cervical myelopathy. The neurological exam dated 2024-07-01 documents bilateral grip weakness (3/5), positive bilateral Hoffman sign, bilateral upper extremity hyperreflexia, and impaired tandem gait. These are classic upper motor neuron signs and symptoms indicative of spinal cord compression.
Further, the MRI from 2024-07-10 confirms C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate cord compression and, critically, demonstrates **T2 signal change within the cord consistent with early myelomalacia**. Myelomalacia signifies existing spinal cord injury.

While the treating physician's ICD-10 billing code on PT referrals was M47.812 (cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy), the accompanying clinical notes consistently describe cervical myelopathy and the presence of myelomalacia. The physician explicitly clarifies this as an administrative billing discrepancy, stating that cervical myelopathy is the primary clinical concern. Based on the totality of the clinical evidence, the primary diagnosis is indeed **Cervical Spondylotic Myelopathy with Myelomalacia**.

**2. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

*   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** The member meets criterion 2.1(b) for Cervical Myelopathy (spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms) and 2.1(c) for Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit. MRI within 6 months supports this. **MET.**
*   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Member completed Gabapentin (3 months) and Naproxen (2 months), exceeding the 4-week minimum for two agents. **MET.**
    *   **Interventional:** Member completed a cervical epidural injection. **MET.**
    *   **Physical Therapy (PT):** Member completed 4 weeks of PT. The standard pathway requires a minimum of 6 weeks. This was the basis for the initial denial.
*   **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY:** This exception reduces the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks minimum and waives interventional treatment if risk is documented, *when myelopathy is the PRIMARY diagnosis*.
    *   The initial denial stated the member did not qualify for this exception because 'cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy' was listed as the primary diagnosis. This constitutes a **misapplication of HealthFirst's own criteria.** The clinical record clearly substantiates cervical myelopathy as the primary diagnosis, explicitly clarifying the billing code as an administrative artifact that does not reflect the clinical reality.
    *   If the myelopathy exception is correctly applied based on the clear clinical documentation of myelopathy, the member has completed the required **4 weeks of physical therapy**. Therefore, the PT requirement under the plan's own myelopathy exception **IS MET**.
*   **2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:** MRI confirms C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate cord compression and myelomalacia, consistent with symptoms. **MET.**
*   **2.4 PHYSICIAN DOCUMENTATION:** Documentation provided supports the request, with a clear rationale for surgery given the myelopathy and myelomalacia. **MET.**

**Conclusion on Plan Criteria Application:** The plan's initial denial incorrectly applied its own myelopathy exception by prioritizing a billing code over extensive clinical documentation. When correctly applied, the member meets HealthFirst's criteria, including the reduced 4-week PT requirement under the myelopathy exception.

**3. Application of California Regulatory Framework (State Law):**

*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This California Insurance Code explicitly states that a health insurer cannot condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where:
    *   (a)(1) The treating physician documents PT as contraindicated or unlikely to benefit. The treating physician halted PT after myelomalacia was confirmed and stated further PT was contraindicated. **This condition is met.**
    *   (a)(3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. The presence of myelomalacia (T2 cord signal change) with moderate cord compression constitutes objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. **This condition is met.**
    *   Therefore, per CIC § 10169.5, the plan's 6-week PT requirement **cannot be applied** to deny this authorization.

*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions):** This regulatory guidance mandates specific actions for cases involving myelopathy, especially with myelomalacia:
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** APL 22-014 states that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm, and plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent. Marcus Webb's case precisely fits this description.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** This section mandates that plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when:
        *   (a) Objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (myelomalacia). **MET.**
        *   (b) Treating physician documents conservative treatment has failed. **MET.**
        *   (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines. **MET** (as detailed below).
    *   The APL specifically identifies **myelomalacia on MRI** as a finding that supports the waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements.
    *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** This section states that plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice, and where a conflict exists, the clinical standard governs.
    *   **Conclusion:** DMHC APL 22-014 directly mandates the waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements for Marcus Webb's presentation, rendering the plan's 6-week PT requirement for myelopathy invalid.

*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This section specifies that the Independent Medical Review standard (and by extension, the standard for internal appeals in California) requires approval if the service is medically necessary according to generally accepted standards of medical practice, *regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive*.

**4. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

*   **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines consistently emphasize the fundamental difference in treatment algorithms. Myelopathy (spinal cord compression) carries a greater risk of irreversible neurological damage than radiculopathy (nerve root compression).
*   **Myelomalacia Significance:** All major spine societies (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) recognize myelomalacia (T2 signal change) as a strong indicator of existing spinal cord injury, a predictor of poorer outcomes if surgery is delayed, and a **strong indication for surgical intervention** that may **contraindicate extended conservative treatment trials.**
*   **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** The AO Spine Guidelines (2021) explicitly state, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." The 6-week PT requirement is largely derived from radiculopathy evidence and is a "category error" when applied to myelopathy.
*   **Surgical Indications:** Marcus Webb's condition—moderate cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression, myelomalacia on MRI, and objective neurological deficits—meets the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION** for surgery.
*   **Risk of Delay:** Clinical evidence confirms that delaying surgery for moderate-severe myelopathy, especially with myelomalacia, significantly increases the risk of permanent neurological deficit and reduced recovery.

**5. Conflict Resolution and Disposition:**

The initial denial was based on the member not meeting the 6-week PT requirement and a misinterpretation of the "primary diagnosis" for the myelopathy exception in HealthFirst's own criteria.

Applying the hierarchy of authority (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):

1.  **State Law Override:** Both CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014 directly and unequivocally mandate the waiver of standard conservative treatment (including a minimum PT duration) in cases like Marcus Webb's due to the presence of myelomalacia and the physician's documentation of PT being contraindicated or unlikely to benefit. These state laws supersede HealthFirst's internal criteria.
2.  **Clinical Standard Override:** Generally accepted clinical guidelines from leading spine societies (AANS/CNS, AO Spine, NASS) do not support a mandatory 6-week PT trial for moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy, especially when myelomalacia is present, and caution against delaying surgery. These clinical standards confirm the medical necessity of the requested ACDF and contradict the plan's specific conservative treatment requirements for this condition.
3.  **Plan Criteria Misapplication:** Even applying HealthFirst's own criteria correctly, the myelopathy exception (2.2A) should have been granted, reducing the PT requirement to 4 weeks, which the member completed.

Given the clear documentation of cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia, the objective neurological deficits, the physician's assessment that further PT is contraindicated, and the explicit mandates of California state law and generally accepted clinical guidelines, the denial of prior authorization for ACDF C6-C7 is inappropriate. The service is medically necessary and cannot be denied based on the conservative treatment requirements cited.

**DISPOSITION:** **OVERTURN**

**WARRANT:** The denial of prior authorization for ACDF C6-C7 is hereby OVERTURNED. The member's clinical presentation of cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and objective neurological deficits warrants surgical intervention as a Tier 1 strong indication under generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021). The plan's requirement for 6 weeks of physical therapy is directly overridden by California Insurance Code § 10169.5, which waives PT requirements when a physician documents contraindication or when objective structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) is present. Furthermore, DMHC All-Plan Letter 22-014 specifically mandates the waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements for myelopathy with myelomalacia due to the risk of irreversible harm. The plan's initial denial also incorrectly applied its own myelopathy exception by relying on an administrative billing code over clear clinical documentation. The requested procedure is medically necessary and consistent with state law and generally accepted standards of medical practice for this time-sensitive neurological condition.
