**Warrant:**

This appeal concerns a prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 for Mr. Marcus Webb, aged 47. The initial denial cited failure to meet the standard 6-week physical therapy requirement and determined that the myelopathy exception did not apply due to "cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy" being listed as the primary diagnosis.

Upon thorough review of the submitted clinical record, the appeal basis, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory framework, and generally accepted clinical standards, the denial is hereby **OVERTURNED**. Our determination is based on the following findings, adhering to the hierarchy of State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria:

1.  **Clinical Diagnosis and Presentation:**
    *   The clinical record consistently documents signs and symptoms highly indicative of cervical myelopathy, not merely radiculopathy. Mr. Webb's initial presentation included bilateral hand weakness (3/5 grip), coordination problems, positive Hoffman sign bilaterally, bilateral hyperreflexia, and impaired tandem gait. These are classic upper motor neuron (UMN) signs and symptoms associated with cervical myelopathy.
    *   Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) dated 2024-07-10 confirmed C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate cord compression and, critically, "T2 signal change within cord consistent with early myelomalacia." Myelomalacia signifies existing spinal cord injury and is a strong indicator of myelopathy.
    *   The treating physician's narrative notes consistently describe cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia, clarifying that the ICD-10 billing code (M47.812 for radiculopathy) was an "administrative artifact" for billing purposes and does not accurately reflect the primary clinical diagnosis.
    *   **Conclusion:** Based on objective neurological findings and imaging, Mr. Webb's primary clinical diagnosis is cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia.

2.  **Application of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
    *   **Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1):** Mr. Webb clearly meets criterion 2.1(b) for Cervical Myelopathy and 2.1(c) for Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit.
    *   **Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2) and Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2(A)):**
        *   The plan's initial denial incorrectly stated that Mr. Webb did not qualify for the myelopathy exception because myelopathy was not the "PRIMARY diagnosis" based on a billing code. This rigid interpretation contradicts the clear clinical evidence presented.
        *   HealthFirst's own myelopathy exception (Section 2.2(A)) reduces the physical therapy requirement to a **4-week minimum** for confirmed cervical myelopathy. Mr. Webb completed 4 weeks (12 sessions) of physical therapy.
        *   The pharmacotherapy (Gabapentin for 3 months, Naproxen for 2 months) and interventional treatment (cervical epidural) requirements were also met.
        *   **Conclusion:** By applying the plan's own myelopathy exception correctly based on the clinical diagnosis, Mr. Webb *did meet* the conservative treatment requirements.

3.  **California Regulatory Requirements:**
    *   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This state law explicitly prohibits a health plan from conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy completion where:
        *   (a)(1) The treating physician documents PT is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit. The treating physician halted PT after myelomalacia was confirmed and stated further PT was contraindicated.
        *   (a)(3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. Myelomalacia with cord compression undeniably constitutes such evidence.
        *   **Conclusion:** The plan's requirement for additional PT is overridden by state law due to documented myelomalacia and the treating physician's professional judgment.
    *   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This All-Plan Letter mandates that plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when:
        *   (a) Objective imaging shows progressive pathology (cord signal change/myelomalacia), which is present in this case.
        *   (b) The treating physician documents conservative treatment failure, which the record supports (no improvement in weakness, PT halted).
        *   (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines, which is also met (see below).
        *   The APL specifically identifies myelomalacia as a finding that supports waiver of standard conservative requirements. Furthermore, it states plans SHALL treat cases with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia as urgent, and SHALL NOT apply internal criteria more restrictive than generally accepted standards.
        *   **Conclusion:** State regulatory guidance directly supports the waiver of extended conservative treatment, including further PT, for Mr. Webb's condition.

4.  **Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:**
    *   **Diagnosis & Urgency:** Clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021) distinguish myelopathy from radiculopathy and identify myelomalacia (T2 signal change) as evidence of existing spinal cord injury, predicting poorer outcomes if surgery is delayed and constituting a strong indication for surgical intervention. This contradicts the plan's implicit treatment of the condition as less urgent based on a billing code.
    *   **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy:** Major spine societies (NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021, Cochrane Review 2021) state there is *no high-quality evidence* supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgery for moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery for such patients to complete a PT trial is not supported and may result in neurological deterioration. The plan's 6-week PT requirement for myelopathy is a "category error" unsupported by evidence.
    *   **Surgical Indication:** Mr. Webb's presentation (moderate myelopathy, UMN signs, objective deficits, myelomalacia on MRI, cord compression) meets TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION for surgery per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023).
    *   **Conclusion:** The clinical consensus unequivocally supports surgical intervention without further mandatory conservative treatment for Mr. Webb's presentation, and delaying surgery may lead to irreversible harm.

**Conflict Resolution:**
The plan's denial rested on a misinterpretation of Mr. Webb's primary diagnosis (confounding a billing code with clinical reality) and a rigid application of PT duration requirements that are neither clinically appropriate for myelopathy with myelomalacia nor permissible under California law. State law (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) explicitly override the plan's more restrictive criteria and its misapplication of the myelopathy exception.

**Decision:**
For the reasons stated above, including the clear clinical evidence of cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia, the fulfillment of HealthFirst's *modified* conservative treatment requirements for myelopathy, and the overriding authority of California state law and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial denial is **OVERTURNED**. Authorization for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C6-C7, one level) for Marcus Webb is approved.
