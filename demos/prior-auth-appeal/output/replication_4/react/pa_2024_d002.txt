**WARRANT FOR DISPOSITION**

The denial of prior authorization for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) is overturned due to significant procedural defects in the initial denial notice, the member's demonstrated fulfillment of the plan's own clinical criteria for cervical radiculopathy, adherence to California state law regarding conservative treatment, and alignment with generally accepted clinical standards for surgical intervention.

**I. Procedural Defects in Initial Denial Notice (State Law Violation)**

The initial denial notice dated 2024-11-08 was procedurally defective, rendering it non-compliant with California state law.
1.  **Failure to Provide Specific Criteria:** The denial notice referenced an "attached criteria document" but no such document was attached, as confirmed by the member and provider. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a)(2) mandates that a denial notice must "Include in the denial notice: the specific criteria applied, the specific clinical reason for the denial." Furthermore, CHSC § 1374.31(b) explicitly states, "A denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is DEFECTIVE and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."
2.  **Failure to Include IMR Notice:** The denial notice also failed to include notice of the member's Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights, which is a mandatory requirement under CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2).

These procedural defects alone are sufficient grounds to overturn the denial, as the member and provider were unable to adequately understand or address the basis of the denial without the referenced criteria.

**II. Member Meets HealthFirst's Own Clinical Criteria (Plan Criteria Compliance)**

Even if the procedural defects were disregarded, a review of the clinical record against HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 for cervical spinal surgery demonstrates that the member, Michael Green, meets all applicable criteria for cervical radiculopathy.

1.  **Diagnosis Requirement (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(a)):** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy C5-C6 with functional impairment. The MRI dated 2024-09-20 shows a "C5-C6 large disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression," and the neurological exam on 2024-09-25 documented "Left C6 dermatomal numbness, bicep reflex diminished left, bicep weakness 4/5 left." These findings are consistent with cervical radiculopathy.
2.  **Conservative Treatment Requirement (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2):**
    *   **(a) Physical Therapy:** The member completed 7 weeks of supervised physical therapy (21 sessions), exceeding the plan's "minimum 6 weeks" requirement. The PT notes specifically document a "plateau at week 6" with persistent radicular symptoms.
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** The member completed trials of two pharmacotherapy agents for adequate durations: Gabapentin 600mg TID for 4 months ("Partial relief") and Naproxen 500mg BID for 3 months ("Inadequate"). Both trials exceeded the "minimum 4 weeks each."
    *   **(c) Interventional Treatment:** The member underwent a Left C6 epidural on 2024-10-28, which provided "40% relief, returned to baseline in 3 weeks." This meets the requirement for "one interventional treatment."
    *   **Myelopathy Exception:** The myelopathy exception (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A)) does not apply as the primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, not myelopathy (no cord compression or UMN signs were documented).
3.  **Imaging Requirement (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.3):** The MRI dated 2024-09-20 (within 6 months of the request) demonstrates "C5-C6 large disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression," which is consistent with the member's symptoms and the proposed surgical level.

Therefore, the plan's denial reason, "Member does not meet criteria for requested procedure under plan policy," is factually incorrect based on the provided clinical information and the plan's own published criteria for radiculopathy.

**III. Adherence to California State Law Regarding Physical Therapy (State Law Override)**

California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2) states that a health insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... [t]he member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."

In this case, the member completed 7 weeks of physical therapy, and the PT notes explicitly documented a "plateau at week 6" with persistent radicular symptoms. This meets the conditions outlined in CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), legally preventing HealthFirst from requiring additional physical therapy as a prerequisite for surgery.

**IV. Alignment with Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (Clinical Standard Compliance)**

The requested ACDF is medically necessary and consistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice for cervical radiculopathy.

1.  **Radiculopathy vs. Myelopathy:** The clinical record clearly indicates cervical radiculopathy, distinguished from myelopathy by unilateral symptoms, objective C6 dermatomal findings, and absence of cord contact or upper motor neuron signs on MRI (AANS/CNS 2023, Section 1.1).
2.  **Adequate Conservative Treatment:** The member has undergone a significant course of conservative treatment (7 weeks of PT with documented plateau, two pharmacotherapy agents for extended durations, and an interventional injection with only temporary relief). This course of treatment, with persistent symptoms, satisfies the standard for proceeding to surgical evaluation for radiculopathy (AANS/CNS 2023, Section 3.1, TIER 2(b); NASS 2020, Grade B Evidence).
3.  **Functional Plateau:** The documented functional plateau in physical therapy (less than 10% improvement or maximum benefit reached) aligns with the clinical standard definition, further supporting that continued PT is unlikely to provide additional benefit (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 5).
4.  **Surgical Indication:** Persistent radicular symptoms and objective neurological deficits (weakness, diminished reflex) after an adequate and failed conservative treatment trial is a "Reasonable Indication" for surgical intervention (AANS/CNS 2023, Section 3.1, TIER 2(b)).

**V. Conclusion**

The initial denial was fundamentally flawed due to procedural defects and misapplication of the plan's own criteria. Furthermore, the member's clinical presentation and documented conservative treatment unequivocally meet both state law requirements (CIC § 10169.5) and generally accepted clinical standards for ACDF for cervical radiculopathy. Based on the hierarchy of authority (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), the request for ACDF C5-C6 is medically necessary.
