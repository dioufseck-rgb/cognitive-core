**APPEAL DETERMINATION: OVERTURN**

**CASE ID:** PA-2024-D002
**MEMBER:** Michael Green, M-445577
**PROCEDURE:** ACDF C5-C6, one level (CPT 22551)
**REVIEW DATE:** 2024-11-15

**SUMMARY OF DENIAL:**
The initial prior authorization request for ACDF C5-C6 for Michael Green was denied on 2024-11-08 with reason code MED-003, stating "Member does not meet criteria for requested procedure under plan policy." The denial notice referenced an attached criteria document, but no document was provided, nor was an Independent Medical Review (IMR) notice included.

**APPEAL BASIS:**
The appeal cites procedural defects (no criteria document attached, IMR notice missing) and the member's inability to evaluate the denial without the applied criteria.

**CLINICAL PRESENTATION REVIEW:**
Mr. Green presents with cervical radiculopathy at C5-C6 with functional impairment.
*   **Imaging (MRI):** C5-C6 large disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression. Critically, there is *no cord contact* and no mention of myelomalacia.
*   **Neurological Exam:** Left C6 dermatomal numbness, diminished left bicep reflex, and 4/5 left bicep weakness. These are classic radicular findings.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 7 weeks (21 sessions) completed, with documentation of a functional plateau at week 6, and persistent radicular symptoms.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Gabapentin 600mg TID for 4 months (partial relief) and Naproxen 500mg BID for 3 months (inadequate).
    *   **Interventional:** Left C6 epidural steroid injection with 40% relief, which returned to baseline in 3 weeks.

**KEY FINDINGS AND APPLICATION OF HIERARCHY:**

1.  **Procedural Adequacy of Denial (State Law – CHSC § 1374.31(b)):**
    *   The denial notice is procedurally defective. It failed to include the specific clinical criteria applied (by not attaching the referenced criteria document) and did not include the required IMR notice.
    *   **CHSC § 1374.31(b) states:** "A denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is DEFECTIVE and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."
    *   **Conclusion:** This procedural defect alone is grounds for overturning the denial.

2.  **Application of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
    *   **Diagnosis (2.1a/c):** Mr. Green has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (2.1a) with nerve root compression and symptoms, and a cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit (2.1c). MRI supports structural pathology. **CRITERIA MET.**
    *   **Conservative Treatment (2.2):**
        *   **Physical Therapy (2.2a):** Requires "minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy with documented functional outcomes." Mr. Green completed 7 weeks of PT, and notes document a functional plateau at week 6. **CRITERIA MET.**
        *   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2b):** Requires "at least two... for minimum 4 weeks each." Mr. Green had Gabapentin for 4 months and Naproxen for 3 months. **CRITERIA MET.**
        *   **Interventional (2.2c):** Requires "one of the following." Mr. Green received a Left C6 epidural. **CRITERIA MET.**
    *   **Imaging Requirement (2.3):** Current MRI (2024-09-20) demonstrates pathology consistent with symptoms. **CRITERIA MET.**
    *   **Myelopathy Exception (2.2(A)):** This exception does *not* apply. Mr. Green's clinical presentation is clearly cervical radiculopathy, not myelopathy. The plan's criteria explicitly state this exception applies "only when myelopathy is the PRIMARY diagnosis."
    *   **Conclusion:** Contrary to the denial reason, Mr. Green appears to meet *all* of HealthFirst's stated clinical coverage criteria for CPT 22551 for cervical radiculopathy. The plan's denial based on "Member does not meet criteria" is unsubstantiated by its own policy when properly applied to the clinical facts.

3.  **Application of State Law – Physical Therapy Requirement (CIC § 10169.5):**
    *   Even if the plan were to imply the 7 weeks of PT was insufficient, **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) states:** "A health insurer... shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
    *   The clinical record explicitly states: "PT notes document plateau at week 6. Radicular symptoms persist."
    *   **Conclusion:** State law dictates that the plan cannot require further PT once a functional plateau has been reached, which is documented here. The PT requirement has been satisfied per state law.

4.  **Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020):**
    *   The clinical documentation confirms cervical radiculopathy. Conservative treatment for radiculopathy typically involves 6-12 weeks of non-operative management.
    *   **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), Section 3.1, TIER 2 (Reasonable Indication):** "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care."
    *   Mr. Green has completed 7 weeks of PT, 4 months of pharmacotherapy, and an interventional procedure, with persistent radicular symptoms (numbness, weakness 4/5) and documented plateau. This aligns with the clinical standard for surgical intervention for persistent cervical radiculopathy after failed conservative management.
    *   **DMHC APL 22-014 and Myelopathy Clinical Criteria:** These guidelines and clinical standards concerning myelopathy, myelomalacia, and urgent treatment *do not apply* to this case, as Mr. Green has radiculopathy without evidence of spinal cord compression or myelomalacia. The distinction between myelopathy and radiculopathy is critical, and the evidence base for conservative treatment differs significantly.
    *   **Conclusion:** The requested procedure is consistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice for cervical radiculopathy failing conservative treatment.

**CONFLICT RESOLUTION & DISPOSITION:**

The plan's denial is fatally flawed due to multiple reasons:
1.  **Procedural Defects:** The denial notice failed to provide the specific criteria applied and the IMR notice, violating CHSC § 1374.31(b). This alone is sufficient grounds for overturn. (State Law > Plan Criteria).
2.  **Incorrect Application of Plan Criteria:** Upon review, the member demonstrably meets all of HealthFirst's published criteria for ACDF for cervical radiculopathy, including the specified conservative treatment requirements (7 weeks PT, two pharmacotherapy agents, one interventional procedure). The plan's stated reason for denial ("Member does not meet criteria") is not supported by the facts and its own policy.
3.  **State Law Override on PT:** Even if the plan were to have an unstated or more stringent interpretation of its PT requirement, state law (CIC § 10169.5) explicitly prohibits denial based on PT duration when a functional plateau has been reached, which is documented in Mr. Green's case. (State Law > Plan Criteria).
4.  **Clinical Standards:** The service is consistent with generally accepted clinical standards for radiculopathy requiring surgery after a trial of conservative care. (Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria).

Therefore, based on the three-source hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), the denial by HealthFirst is not supported.

**DETERMINATION:**
**OVERTURN** the denial.

**WARRANT:**
The denial is OVERTURNED due to significant procedural defects, the member meeting all applicable plan criteria, and the denial being inconsistent with California state law regarding physical therapy requirements and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice. The denial notice was defective per CHSC § 1374.31(b) for failing to provide the specific criteria applied and the IMR notice. The member's clinical record demonstrates that all criteria outlined in HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 for radiculopathy, including the 6-week minimum for physical therapy (7 weeks completed with documented plateau), two pharmacotherapy agents (Gabapentin 4 months, Naproxen 3 months), and one interventional procedure (C6 epidural), have been met. Furthermore, California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2) prohibits conditioning approval on further physical therapy once a functional plateau has been documented, which is the case here. Finally, the proposed surgery is consistent with TIER 2 indications of the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) for radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after conservative treatment. The myelopathy-specific exceptions and regulations are not applicable to this case, as Mr. Green's presentation is cervical radiculopathy.
