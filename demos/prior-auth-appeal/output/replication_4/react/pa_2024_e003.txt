**APPEAL DISPOSITION: OVERTURN**

---

**WARRANT AND RATIONALE:**

Upon review of the submitted clinical documentation, HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), relevant California regulatory frameworks, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial denial for CPT 22551 for Mr. George Harris is hereby **OVERTURNED**.

The denial was based on a misapplication of the plan's own stated criteria and is procedurally defective under state law.

**1. Misapplication of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
The initial denial letter (dated 2024-10-30, reason code MED-003) explicitly states that revision criteria for ACDF include "documentation of hardware failure, **pseudarthrosis**, or new pathology at previously treated level." The denial then states, "Documentation submitted does not confirm hardware failure."

*   The clinical record includes a CT scan from 2024-09-15 with findings: "CT without artifact limitation: C5-C6 — incomplete bridging bone formation. Lucency around hardware suggesting **pseudarthrosis**. Findings consistent with failed fusion/pseudarthrosis. Hardware appears intact without fracture."
*   The plan's own stated criteria provide for *pseudarthrosis* as a valid indication for revision surgery. The denial failed to address the clear documentation of pseudarthrosis, instead focusing solely on the absence of hardware failure (one of *three* alternative criteria). This constitutes a misapplication of HealthFirst's own Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) which lists "pseudarthrosis" as an acceptable reason for revision.

**2. Procedural Defect under California Law:**
California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b) stipulates that a denial notice is defective if it "fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial." In this case, the denial specified the criteria for revision surgery, which included pseudarthrosis, but then failed to apply those criteria to the submitted documentation that clearly established pseudarthrosis. By ignoring the documented pseudarthrosis, the denial failed to provide a specific clinical reason for the denial *in light of the submitted evidence*, rendering it procedurally defective.

**3. Adherence to HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
Even if the pseudarthrosis was not considered, the member otherwise met the plan's medical necessity criteria for cervical spinal surgery for radiculopathy:

*   **2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** The neurological exam (2024-09-20) confirms "Return of right C6 symptoms: bicep weakness 4/5 right, bicep reflex diminished right, C6 dermatomal numbness," consistent with cervical radiculopathy (2.1a). The CT scan provides supporting documentation.
*   **2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:**
    *   **(a) Physical Therapy:** Member completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of PT, exceeding the 6-week minimum. The treating physician noted "No improvement — treating physician notes PT cannot address structural hardware/fusion failure."
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** Member completed a 4-month trial of Naproxen and a 3-month trial of Gabapentin, meeting the requirement of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
    *   **(c) Interventional Treatment:** One epidural was performed (2024-10-10), meeting the requirement.
    *   The "Myelopathy Exception" (2.2A) is not applicable here as the primary diagnosis is radiculopathy, not myelopathy. However, the conservative treatment requirements were fully met under the standard pathway.
*   **2.3 Imaging Requirement:** The CT scan (2024-09-15) demonstrates "Findings consistent with failed fusion/pseudarthrosis," which is structural pathology consistent with symptoms and the proposed surgical level.

**4. Compliance with California Insurance Code (CIC § 10169.5) and Clinical Standards:**
Even if the conservative treatment was deemed insufficient by the plan (which it was not, as per the above), California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a) states that a plan cannot condition approval on physical therapy where: (1) the physician documents PT unlikely to benefit, (2) the member reached a functional plateau, OR (3) there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address.
*   The treating physician documented that PT "cannot address structural hardware/fusion failure," (matching CIC 10169.5(a)(1) & (3)).
*   The 8 weeks of PT resulted in "No improvement" in symptoms and objective neurological findings (weakness, diminished reflex), indicating a functional plateau despite partial pain relief from pharmacotherapy and minimal improvement from the epidural. This also aligns with the clinical standard definition of functional plateau (Source 3, Section 5) where neurological symptoms are not expected to respond to PT when a structural failure is present.

Under generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023, Section 3.1), surgery is a "Reasonable Indication" (Tier 2) for "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care." Mr. Harris's condition with documented pseudarthrosis, recurrent and worsened C6 radiculopathy, objective weakness, and failed conservative management clearly meets these standards.

**CONCLUSION:**
The denial was based on a flawed application of the plan's own stated criteria. The clinical documentation clearly demonstrates pseudarthrosis, which is an accepted indication for revision surgery according to HealthFirst's own denial reasoning. Furthermore, the member has met all conservative treatment requirements, and the underlying structural issue is appropriately addressed by surgical intervention under generally accepted clinical guidelines and state law.

Therefore, the prior authorization for CPT 22551 for Mr. George Harris is medically necessary and should be approved.
