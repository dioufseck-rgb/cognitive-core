**DISPOSITION: OVERTURNED**

**WARRANT:**

This appeal determination is based on a comprehensive review of the provided case information, the HealthFirst PPO Gold plan criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), relevant California state laws and regulations, and generally accepted clinical standards. The review adheres to the established hierarchy of authority: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.

**1. Procedural Adequacy of Denial Notice (State Law Review):**
The appeal basis highlights significant procedural defects in the initial denial notice.
*   **CHSC 1374.31(b) mandates that a denial notice SHALL:**
    *   Specify the exact clinical criteria applied.
    *   Include the specific clinical reason for the denial.
    *   Provide notice of the member's right to Independent Medical Review (IMR).
*   **Case Findings:** The initial denial notice explicitly states "Generic reference to plan criteria — no section number, no specific criterion" and "imr_notice_included: false."
*   **Conclusion:** The denial notice is procedurally defective, as it fails to meet the clear requirements of California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b). A denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is to be treated as if no denial was issued. This procedural defect alone is sufficient grounds to **OVERTURN** the denial.

**2. Clinical Presentation and Applicability of Myelopathy Exceptions:**
Before addressing the substantive denial reason, it is critical to accurately classify the member's condition.
*   **Clinical Record Findings:**
    *   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Cervical radiculopathy C6-C7.
    *   **Imaging (MRI):** C6-C7 disc herniation, moderate right C7 nerve root compression. **Crucially, there is no cord contact and no myelomalacia documented.**
    *   **Neurological Exam:** Right C7 dermatomal numbness, tricep weakness 4/5 right, tricep reflex diminished. These are classic signs of radiculopathy, not myelopathy (which would typically involve upper motor neuron signs like hyperreflexia, Hoffman's sign, gait disturbance, and often bilateral symptoms).
*   **Domain Knowledge and Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021):** Clearly distinguishes radiculopathy from myelopathy. Myelopathy involves spinal cord compression, often with upper motor neuron signs and potentially myelomalacia (cord signal change). Radiculopathy involves nerve root compression.
*   **Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A) Exception):** The myelopathy pathway for reduced conservative treatment (4 weeks PT, waived interventional) explicitly states it "applies only when myelopathy is the PRIMARY diagnosis."
*   **State Law (DMHC APL 22-014):** This All-Plan Letter, which allows for waiver of conservative treatment, is specifically for "time-sensitive neurological conditions" like myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia.
*   **Conclusion:** Based on the clinical evidence (no cord contact, no myelomalacia, and neurological findings consistent with nerve root compression rather than spinal cord compression), the member's primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy. Therefore, neither the plan's myelopathy exception nor the state law provisions regarding urgent myelopathy with myelomalacia apply to this case.

**3. Substantive Review of Conservative Treatment (Plan Criteria & Clinical Standard):**
The plan's denial was based on incomplete conservative treatment. We assess this against plan criteria and clinical standards for *radiculopathy*.
*   **HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2):**
    *   (a) **Physical therapy:** Requires a minimum of 6 weeks. Member completed 4 weeks (12 sessions). **CRITERION NOT MET.**
    *   (b) **Pharmacotherapy:** Requires a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. Member tried Ibuprofen 800mg TID for 6 weeks (one agent). **CRITERION NOT MET.**
    *   (c) **Interventional treatment:** Requires one interventional treatment. Member had none. **CRITERION NOT MET.**
*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):**
    *   This law prohibits conditioning surgery on PT completion if: (1) PT is contraindicated/unlikely to benefit, OR (2) member reached a functional plateau, OR (3) there's objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address.
    *   **Case Findings:** There is no documentation that PT is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit. While the member claims a functional plateau, the PT discharge note does not use that language, and the structured fields do not provide sufficient detail to definitively meet the clinical definition of a functional plateau (<10% improvement over 3 sessions or therapist documentation of maximum benefit). As established, there is no objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (like myelomalacia or cord contact) that PT cannot address.
    *   **Conclusion:** The available documentation does not conclusively meet the criteria in CIC § 10169.5 to waive the plan's PT duration requirement.
*   **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020):** For cervical radiculopathy, surgery is typically indicated for "persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" (Tier 2). The evidence base for conservative treatment is strong for radiculopathy. Generally accepted standards for managing radiculopathy include a trial of physical therapy (often 6-12 weeks), multiple pharmacotherapy agents, and often interventional procedures (e.g., epidural steroid injections).
*   **Conclusion:** The member's conservative treatment regimen (4 weeks of PT, one pharmacotherapy agent, no interventional procedures) is incomplete when compared to both HealthFirst's plan criteria for radiculopathy and generally accepted clinical standards for the management of cervical radiculopathy. The plan's criteria for conservative treatment in radiculopathy cases (6 weeks PT, 2 pharmacotherapies, 1 interventional) are consistent with, and not more restrictive than, generally accepted standards of medical practice.

**Summary and Final Determination:**

The initial denial by HealthFirst Health Plan (PA-2024-D001) is procedurally defective because it violates California Health & Safety Code § 1374.31(b) by failing to specify the clinical criteria applied and not including the Independent Medical Review notice. State law is the highest authority in the review hierarchy. Therefore, the denial cannot be upheld.

However, it is also important to note for future consideration that a substantive review of the clinical record against both the plan's criteria and generally accepted clinical standards for cervical radiculopathy reveals that the member's conservative treatment regimen is incomplete. The member's condition is consistent with cervical radiculopathy, not myelopathy, and therefore the specific state law and plan exceptions related to myelopathy or myelomalacia do not apply.

Given the mandatory nature of CHSC § 1374.31(b), the denial must be **OVERTURNED** due to its procedural inadequacy. HealthFirst Health Plan is advised to process the prior authorization request in a manner compliant with CHSC § 1374.31(b) if further review or a new denial is deemed necessary.
