**APPLICATION OF CRITERIA AND REGULATORY GUIDANCE:**

**1. Application of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
*   **Section 2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** Met. Member has confirmed cervical radiculopathy with objective imaging (C6-C7 disc herniation, C7 nerve root compression) consistent with symptoms.
*   **Section 2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:**
    *   The original denial claimed the member completed only 3 weeks of PT and functional plateau was not documented. This is factually incorrect based on the submitted clinical record.
    *   **(a) Physical therapy:** Plan requires "minimum 6 weeks... with documented functional outcomes." The member completed **9 weeks** of PT, exceeding the 6-week minimum. Furthermore, functional plateau was explicitly documented by the treating physical therapist ("Maximum therapeutic benefit achieved. No further improvement expected from continued PT"), meeting the plan's definition of a functional plateau (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 NOTE, and Clinical Standard Section 5).
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** Plan requires "trial of at least two... for minimum 4 weeks each." Member completed Gabapentin for 4 months and Naproxen for 3 months. Met.
    *   **(c) Interventional treatment:** Plan requires "one of the following." Member completed a right C7 epidural. Met.
    *   **Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A):** This exception is not applicable as the member's primary diagnosis is radiculopathy, not myelopathy (confirmed by lack of cord contact, myelomalacia, and UMN signs). The standard conservative treatment pathway applies here.
*   **Section 2.3 Imaging Requirement:** Met. Current MRI (within 6 months) demonstrates pathology at C6-C7 consistent with symptoms.
*   **Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** Based on a correct review of the submitted documentation, the member **meets all applicable criteria** outlined in HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, directly contradicting the original denial reason. The denial was based on a factual error in assessing the submitted clinical information.

**2. Application of California Regulatory Framework:**
*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute prohibits conditioning approval on PT completion where "(2) The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
    *   In this case, the member completed 9 weeks of PT and a functional plateau was explicitly documented. Even if the plan's minimum of 6 weeks had not been met (which it was), the plan could not require further PT per this state law once a plateau is documented.
    *   Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(c) states that a plan's minimum weeks of PT "shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau." The original denial mechanically applied the 6-week minimum and incorrectly stated a plateau was not documented.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (Standard of Review for IMR):** Although this is an internal appeal, the principle that services meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice SHALL be approved regardless of a plan's more restrictive internal criteria is relevant. In this case, the plan's criteria, when *correctly applied*, were met.
*   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This APL primarily addresses cases of myelopathy, particularly with myelomalacia or progressive pathology, to waive or modify conservative treatment requirements due to urgency. As the member has radiculopathy without myelomalacia or cord contact, the *direct* waiver provisions of this APL do not apply. However, the general principle of not applying internal criteria more restrictively than generally accepted standards (DMHC APL 22-014, II.3) supports the overarching review process.

**3. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
*   **Distinction:** Clinical guidelines (Clinical Reference Summary Section 1.1) clearly differentiate radiculopathy from myelopathy, emphasizing different treatment algorithms. This case is radiculopathy.
*   **Conservative Treatment for Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, TIER 2) consider surgery a "reasonable indication" for "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx." The member has undergone 9 weeks of PT, 4 months of pharmacotherapy, and an epidural injection, with persistent radicular symptoms. This aligns with the clinical standard for considering surgical intervention.
*   **Functional Plateau:** The clinical standard definition (Clinical Reference Summary Section 5) includes "therapist documentation that patient has reached maximum therapeutic benefit," which was explicitly provided in Capital PT's discharge summary.

**CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND DETERMINATION:**

The denial was based on a factual error regarding the member's completion of conservative physical therapy and the documentation of a functional plateau.
1.  **Factual Inaccuracy:** The original denial stated 3 weeks of PT and no documented functional plateau. The clinical record clearly shows 9 weeks of PT completed (exceeding the plan's 6-week minimum) and explicit documentation of a functional plateau by the treating physical therapist. Both records were submitted with the initial request.
2.  **Plan Criteria:** When the factual inaccuracies are corrected, the member fully meets the conservative treatment requirements (Section 2.2) of HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, as 9 weeks of PT with a documented plateau, two pharmacotherapy agents for adequate duration, and one interventional procedure were completed.
3.  **State Law Override:** Even if the 6-week minimum PT was not met, California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2) explicitly prohibits conditioning approval on further PT once a functional plateau is reached and documented. This state law reinforces that the plan's denial reason, even if factually accurate (which it is not), would be invalid under California law.
4.  **Clinical Standard Alignment:** The member's completed course of conservative treatment and persistent symptoms align with generally accepted clinical standards for considering surgical intervention for cervical radiculopathy.

Therefore, the original denial is inconsistent with the submitted clinical documentation, the plan's own criteria (when correctly applied), and California state law.

**DISPOSITION:** **OVERTURNED**

**WARRANT:**
The prior authorization denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) for Marco Reyes is **OVERTURNED**. The denial was issued based on demonstrably false factual claims concerning the member's completion of conservative treatment, specifically physical therapy duration and documentation of a functional plateau.

The clinical record unequivocally shows:
1.  The member completed a total of **9 weeks of physical therapy** across two providers, which exceeds the HealthFirst Plan's CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a) requirement for a "minimum 6 weeks." (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a); Clinical Record)
2.  A **functional plateau was explicitly documented** by Capital Physical Therapy, stating "Maximum therapeutic benefit achieved. No further improvement expected from continued PT." This satisfies both HealthFirst Plan's criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 NOTE) and the clinical standard definition for functional plateau (Clinical Reference Summary Section 5).
3.  Both sets of physical therapy records were submitted with the original prior authorization request, indicating a failure by the original reviewer to accurately process the submitted documentation. (Appeal Basis)
4.  The member also met the plan's requirements for pharmacotherapy (Gabapentin for 4 months, Naproxen for 3 months) and interventional treatment (right C7 epidural). (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b), (c); Clinical Record)
5.  California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2) explicitly prohibits conditioning surgical approval on further physical therapy once a functional plateau has been documented by the treating provider, further invalidating the original denial's premise. (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2))

All of the HealthFirst Plan's medical necessity criteria for conservative treatment, as outlined in CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, have been met by the member. The original denial was based on a factual error in the review of submitted clinical documentation. The requested service is medically necessary per plan criteria, state law, and generally accepted clinical standards for persistent cervical radiculopathy after an adequate and failed conservative trial.
