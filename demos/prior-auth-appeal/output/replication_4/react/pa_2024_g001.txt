**I. BACKGROUND OF DENIAL AND APPEAL BASIS**

The initial prior authorization request for an ACDF at C6-C7 for Mr. Marco Reyes was denied by HealthFirst Utilization Management. The denial cited HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2, claiming the member completed only 3 weeks of physical therapy (PT) and lacked documentation of a functional plateau, thereby failing to meet the minimum 6-week PT requirement for the standard pathway.

Mr. Reyes' appeal asserts that the denial is based on factual errors. The appeal documentation states that Mr. Reyes completed a total of 9 weeks of physical therapy across two providers (3 weeks at Valley PT and 6 weeks at Capital PT), with both providers' records submitted with the original request. Furthermore, the appeal highlights that the Capital PT discharge summary explicitly documents a functional plateau. The appeal also attests that pharmacotherapy and an interventional injection were completed, satisfying all other conservative treatment criteria.

**III. REVIEWER ANALYSIS AND APPLICATION OF HIERARCHY**

This appeal determination applies the three-source hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.

**A. Evaluation against HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

*   **Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1):** Member has confirmed cervical radiculopathy (2.1a) with supporting MRI and neurological exam. **MET.**
*   **Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2):** This was the sole basis for denial.
    *   **Physical Therapy (2.2a):** The denial stated 3 weeks PT and no documented functional plateau.
        *   **Factual Finding:** Clinical records clearly show **9 weeks of PT** (3 weeks + 6 weeks), which exceeds the plan's **minimum 6-week requirement**.
        *   **Factual Finding:** The Capital PT discharge summary explicitly documents "Maximum therapeutic benefit achieved. No further improvement expected from continued PT," which meets the plan's definition of "functional plateau" (therapist documents maximum benefit reached).
        *   Therefore, the plan's stated reason for denial regarding PT duration and functional plateau is **factually incorrect** and **not supported by the submitted clinical documentation**.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2b):** Member completed trials of Gabapentin (4 months) and Naproxen (3 months). **MET.**
    *   **Interventional Treatment (2.2c):** Member completed one cervical epidural steroid injection. **MET.**
    *   **Myelopathy Exception (2.2A Note):** The member has cervical radiculopathy, not myelopathy (confirmed by MRI showing no cord contact/myelomalacia and neurological exam showing no bilateral/UMN signs). Therefore, the myelopathy exception, which modifies PT to 4 weeks and waives interventional treatment, **does not apply** in this specific case. However, this is irrelevant given that the member met the *standard* conservative treatment criteria.
*   **Imaging Requirement (Section 2.3):** Current MRI within 6 months demonstrates pathology consistent with symptoms. **MET.**
*   **Physician Documentation (Section 2.4):** Presumed met as not cited in denial.

**Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The HealthFirst initial denial was based on an inaccurate assessment of the submitted clinical documentation. Marco Reyes **does meet** all applicable conservative treatment requirements as per HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2, including the 6-week PT minimum and documented functional plateau.

**B. Evaluation against California Regulatory Framework:**

1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:**
    *   This state law prohibits conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where: "(2) The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
    *   **Application:** Marco Reyes completed 9 weeks of PT, and his physical therapist explicitly documented that he reached a functional plateau ("Maximum therapeutic benefit achieved. No further improvement expected from continued PT.") with persistent symptoms.
    *   **Finding:** The denial directly violates CIC § 10169.5 by denying based on a purported unmet PT requirement, despite clear documentation of a functional plateau. The plan's mechanical application of a minimum duration, even if the plateau was met, is explicitly prohibited by this statute.
2.  **Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) APL 22-014 — Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions:**
    *   This APL primarily addresses cases of myelopathy with myelomalacia, providing exceptions to conservative treatment requirements due to urgency.
    *   **Application:** While Marco Reyes does not have myelopathy or myelomalacia (per MRI and neurological exam), the APL's general principle that "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice" remains relevant. The APL also generally cautions against requiring full conservative treatment where surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted guidelines.
3.  **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) — Standard of Review for IMR:**
    *   This statute mandates that services meeting "generally accepted standards of medical practice" shall be approved regardless of the plan's more restrictive internal criteria. While the plan criteria were in fact met here (upon factual correction), this provision underscores that clinical standards take precedence over plan criteria if there were a conflict.

**Conclusion on State Law:** The denial based on PT requirements, when a functional plateau was documented, directly conflicts with and is superseded by California Insurance Code § 10169.5.

**C. Evaluation against Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

1.  **Diagnosis Classification:** The clinical documentation clearly supports a diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy, not myelopathy, as evidenced by unilateral symptoms, lack of UMN signs, and absence of cord contact/myelomalacia on MRI (AANS/CNS Section 1.1). This distinction is critical as treatment algorithms and conservative treatment expectations differ significantly.
2.  **Conservative Treatment for Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines (NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) recommend a trial of conservative treatment for radiculopathy, typically for 6-12 weeks, including physical therapy, pharmacotherapy, and potentially injections. Marco Reyes has completed 9 weeks of PT (with a documented functional plateau), 3-4 months of pharmacotherapy, and one epidural injection.
3.  **Functional Plateau:** The clinical standard defines functional plateau as "therapist documentation that patient has reached maximum therapeutic benefit" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 5), which is explicitly documented in Mr. Reyes' Capital PT discharge summary. For radiculopathy, once conservative treatment has been adequately trialed and a plateau is reached with persistent symptoms, surgical intervention becomes a reasonable next step.
4.  **Surgical Indications for ACDF:** Per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), TIER 2 (Reasonable Indication) includes "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care" (Section 3.1). Mr. Reyes' persistent right C7 radicular symptoms (numbness, diminished reflex, 4/5 grip strength) and failure to achieve adequate resolution after 9 weeks of PT (with plateau) and other conservative measures place him squarely within this indication.

**Conclusion on Clinical Standard:** Marco Reyes has undergone an appropriate and adequate course of conservative treatment for cervical radiculopathy, reaching a functional plateau, and his persistent symptoms and objective neurological deficits meet accepted clinical standards for surgical intervention.

---

**IV. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS AND DISPOSITION**

The initial denial by HealthFirst was based on demonstrably false factual claims regarding the duration of physical therapy completed and the lack of documentation of a functional plateau. The clinical records clearly show 9 weeks of PT and explicit documentation of a functional plateau.

Upon accurate review, Mr. Reyes has fully met HealthFirst's own conservative treatment criteria, including the minimum 6 weeks of PT, documentation of a functional plateau, pharmacotherapy, and interventional treatment.

Furthermore, California State Law (CIC § 10169.5) explicitly prohibits a health plan from denying surgery based on a PT requirement where a functional plateau has been reached. This state law, being a higher authority, supersedes any misapplication or restrictive interpretation of the plan's criteria.

Finally, Mr. Reyes' clinical presentation, conservative treatment trial, and persistent symptoms/deficits align with generally accepted clinical standards for proceeding with ACDF for cervical radiculopathy.

Therefore, applying the hierarchy of authority (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria), the denial is unwarranted.

**V. DETERMINATION**

**DISPOSITION:** OVERTURNED

**WARRANT:** The HealthFirst Utilization Management denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) is hereby **OVERTURNED** for the following reasons:

1.  **Factual Error in Initial Denial:** The denial's stated reason that the member completed "only 3 weeks of physical therapy" and that "Functional plateau not documented" is factually incorrect. The clinical record clearly shows the member completed a total of **9 weeks of physical therapy** across two providers and that a **functional plateau was explicitly documented** by Capital Physical Therapy. Both records were submitted with the original prior authorization request.
2.  **Compliance with Plan Criteria:** Upon accurate review of the clinical documentation, Mr. Marco Reyes **meets all of HealthFirst's conservative treatment requirements** under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, including the minimum 6 weeks of PT with documented functional plateau, trials of two pharmacotherapy agents, and one interventional procedure.
3.  **Violation of State Law:** The denial based on unmet physical therapy requirements, despite clear documentation of a functional plateau, directly violates **California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2) and (c)**, which explicitly prohibits health insurers from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy when a member has reached a functional plateau.
4.  **Adherence to Clinical Standards:** The member's persistent cervical radiculopathy, objective neurological deficits, and failure to respond adequately to a comprehensive 9-week course of conservative treatment (including PT with documented plateau, pharmacotherapy, and injection) meet the generally accepted clinical standards for surgical intervention as outlined by AANS/CNS, NASS, and AO Spine guidelines for radiculopathy (Tier 2 indication).
