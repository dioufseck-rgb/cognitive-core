**Appeal Determination: PA-2024-G003**

**Member:** Bernard Achebe
**Procedure:** ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551)
**Original Denial Date:** 2024-11-05
**Reviewer:** Health Plan Appeals Reviewer

**Disposition:** **OVERTURN**

**Warrant for Disposition:**

The initial denial by HealthFirst Utilization Management cited non-completion of conservative treatment, specifically 3 weeks of physical therapy (instead of 6 required), only one pharmacotherapy agent (instead of two), and no interventional procedure on record. Upon review of the member's clinical record, HealthFirst's own clinical criteria, California state law, and generally accepted clinical standards, the denial is found to be inappropriate and is hereby overturned.

The determination is based on the following findings, applying the hierarchy of State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria:

1.  **Primary Diagnosis and Inappropriate Application of Plan Criteria (Plan Criteria, Clinical Standard, State Law):**
    *   The member has a confirmed primary diagnosis of "Mild cervical myelopathy C5-C6" with objective findings including moderate central cord compression on MRI, bilateral Hoffman signs, and mild hyperreflexia. Myelopathy is distinct from radiculopathy and has different treatment algorithms (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.1).
    *   The initial denial cited conservative treatment requirements (6 weeks PT, 2 pharmacotherapy agents, 1 interventional procedure) that are derived from the plan's *standard pathway* (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2). This standard pathway's physical therapy requirement is specifically derived from radiculopathy evidence and is explicitly stated by clinical guidelines as having "NO evidentiary basis for myelopathy" and being a "category error" when applied to myelopathy patients (Clinical Reference Summary, Sections 1.1, 2.2; AO Spine Guidelines 2021).
    *   Furthermore, the initial denial failed to acknowledge or apply HealthFirst's own **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY**, which reduces the physical therapy requirement to a 4-week minimum and waives the interventional treatment requirement under certain conditions. The failure to apply the plan's own applicable criteria constitutes a procedural defect in the denial process.

2.  **Physical Therapy Requirement Overridden by California State Law (State Law):**
    *   Even if HealthFirst's myelopathy exception (4 weeks PT) were applied, the member only completed 3 weeks. However, California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a) explicitly prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of surgery on completion of physical therapy where:
        *   **(1)** The treating physician (Dr. Osei) documented that PT was halted due to "myelopathy concern" and declared that "further delay is medically contraindicated," implying PT is unlikely to benefit or is contraindicated.
        *   **(3)** The condition involves "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The MRI finding of "moderate central cord compression" coupled with clinical evidence of upper motor neuron signs (bilateral Hoffman signs, mild hyperreflexia) constitutes such objective evidence. Clinical standards affirm that neurological symptoms in myelopathy (weakness, hyperreflexia) are not expected to respond to PT regardless of duration (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 5).
    *   Therefore, the plan's requirement for further physical therapy is legally unenforceable under California law.

3.  **Interventional Procedure Requirement Waived (Plan Criteria, Clinical Standard):**
    *   HealthFirst's own myelopathy exception (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2A) waives the interventional treatment requirement if the treating physician documents that "interventional treatment poses risk of neurological deterioration." Dr. Osei's declaration states, "'Further delay is medically contraindicated. I cannot overstate the urgency — any further delay risks irreversible neurological deterioration. Immediate authorization is required.'" This documentation is sufficient to invoke the plan's waiver for interventional procedures.

4.  **Pharmacotherapy Requirement Review (Clinical Standard, State Law):**
    *   The plan's myelopathy exception does not modify the pharmacotherapy requirement of "at least two agents for minimum 4 weeks each." The member only completed a 6-week trial of Baclofen, which provided "mild spasticity improvement."
    *   However, generally accepted clinical standards for myelopathy highlight the "LIMITED efficacy for established myelopathy" from conservative treatment (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.1). While mild myelopathy (mJOA 15/18) *may* be considered for initial conservative management (NASS 2020), there is no evidence base establishing a mandatory minimum number of pharmacotherapy agents as a prerequisite for surgery for myelopathy.
    *   CHSC § 1374.32(b) dictates that an Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard of "medically necessary" is based on "generally accepted standards of medical practice" and that services meeting this standard "SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." Requiring a second pharmacotherapy agent, given the limited efficacy of conservative care for myelopathy and the presence of objective cord compression and UMN signs, is considered a more restrictive criterion than generally accepted standards.

5.  **Overall Medical Necessity and Risk of Delay (Clinical Standard, State Law):**
    *   While the member's myelopathy is classified as mild (mJOA 15) and the record does not objectively demonstrate *progression* or *myelomalacia* (which would indicate strong urgency per AANS/CNS Tier 1), the condition still involves objective spinal cord compression and upper motor neuron signs, which meet criteria for surgical consideration (AANS/CNS Tier 2: Mild myelopathy with stable symptoms after adequate conservative trial).
    *   The clinical evidence further underscores that "each month of delay in surgical treatment for moderate-severe myelopathy is associated with decreased likelihood of neurological recovery" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.3). While this specific patient is mild, the principle of avoiding unnecessary delay for a condition of spinal cord compression is medically sound.
    *   DMHC APL 22-014 (Section II.3) mandates that plans "shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for conditions identified in this guidance." The plan's rigid conservative treatment requirements for myelopathy contradict generally accepted standards.

**Conclusion:**

The HealthFirst denial, based on unfulfilled conservative treatment requirements, is superseded by California state law regarding physical therapy and is inconsistent with both the plan's own myelopathy exception (for interventional treatment) and generally accepted clinical standards for the management of cervical myelopathy. Therefore, the request for ACDF C5-C6 is medically necessary.

**Action:** The denial dated 2024-11-05 for procedure code 22551 is **OVERTURNED**. Authorization for ACDF C5-C6 is approved.
