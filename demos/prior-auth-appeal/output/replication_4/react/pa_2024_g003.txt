**Subject: Appeal Determination for Prior Authorization Denial of Cervical Spinal Surgery**

This letter serves as the final determination for the appeal of the prior authorization denial dated 2024-11-05 for Mr. Bernard Achebe's requested Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C5-C6. This review has been conducted by the HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals Committee, adhering to the three-source hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.

**I. Background of Denial and Appeal Basis**

The initial request for CPT 22551 was denied by HealthFirst Utilization Management (Dr. Alan Foster) on 2024-11-05 with reason code MED-003, stating: "Request denied pending completion of conservative treatment. Physical therapy: 3 weeks completed, 6 required under standard pathway. Pharmacotherapy: one agent only. No interventional procedure on record."

The appeal filed on behalf of Mr. Achebe asserts surgical urgency and contraindication to further delay, confirmed myelopathy on clinical exam, and failure of conservative treatment to arrest neurological progression.

**II. Clinical Information Review**

*   **Diagnosis:** Mild cervical myelopathy C5-C6.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-10-01):** C5-C6 moderate central cord compression. **Crucially, no T2 signal change or myelomalacia.** Canal diameter 8mm. Cord contact present *without* compression.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-10-05):** Bilateral Hoffman signs present, bilateral hyperreflexia mild. Tandem gait intact. Hand grip strength 4+/5 bilateral. **No frank weakness, no dermatomal deficit.** Activities of daily living (ADLs) fully intact. mJOA score 15/18 (indicating mild myelopathy).
*   **Functional Status:** mJOA 15/18 (mild category). AANS/CNS guidelines note that surgery is appropriate for mild myelopathy, but urgency requires acute progression or myelomalacia, *neither of which is present*. Symptom duration is **6 months, stable.**
*   **Treating Neurologist Declaration (Dr. Osei letter 2024-11-01):** Declares "immediate surgical intervention," "severe and progressive" compression, and risk of "irreversible neurological deterioration."
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   Physical Therapy (PT): 3 weeks (9 sessions) completed. Dr. Osei halted PT due to "myelopathy concern."
    *   Pharmacotherapy: One agent (Baclofen 5mg BID for 6 weeks) provided mild spasticity improvement.
    *   Interventional Procedures: None on record.

**III. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)**

1.  **Diagnosis Requirement (2.1):** Met. Member has confirmed cervical myelopathy (2.1(b)) with supporting MRI.
2.  **Conservative Treatment Requirement (2.2):**
    *   **Physical Therapy (2.2a):** The standard pathway requires 6 weeks. The myelopathy exception (2.2(A)) reduces this to 4 weeks. Member completed 3 weeks. **Not met.**
    *   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2b):** Requires trial of at least two agents. Member completed one agent. **Not met.**
    *   **Interventional Treatment (2.2c):** Requires one interventional treatment. The myelopathy exception (2.2(A)) waives this if the treating physician documents risk of neurological deterioration. Dr. Osei did not document this specific risk. **Not met.**

Based solely on HealthFirst's plan criteria, Mr. Achebe has not met the conservative treatment requirements.

**IV. Application of California Regulatory Framework**

1.  **CIC § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:**
    *   This state law prohibits plans from conditioning surgical approval on PT completion where: "(3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
    *   Mr. Achebe's MRI shows "moderate central cord compression" and his neurological exam reveals "Bilateral Hoffman signs present. Bilateral hyperreflexia mild." These are objective indicators of structural neurological compromise (spinal cord compression and upper motor neuron signs) that physical therapy, by its nature, cannot resolve. PT aims to improve function and manage symptoms, but it cannot decompress the spinal cord or reverse existing neurological signs of cord injury.
    *   **Conclusion:** Per CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), HealthFirst **cannot deny** the request based on the insufficient duration of physical therapy. The PT component of the denial is invalid under state law.

2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 — Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions:**
    *   This APL addresses waivers for conservative treatment when objective imaging shows *progressive pathology* (cord signal change, myelomalacia, or progressive herniation on serial imaging) AND the treating physician documents conservative failure AND surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines.
    *   Mr. Achebe's MRI explicitly states **"No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia."** Furthermore, his symptom duration is "6 months, stable," and the functional assessment notes that "urgency classification requires acute progression or myelomalacia, neither of which is present."
    *   **Conclusion:** The objective clinical evidence does **not** support "progressive pathology" as defined by DMHC APL 22-014. Therefore, the specific conditions for a full waiver of all conservative treatment due to urgency, as outlined in this APL, are **not met** in Mr. Achebe's case. Despite Dr. Osei's strong declaration of urgency and progression, it is contradicted by the objective clinical findings in the record.

3.  **CHSC § 1374.32(b) — Standard of Review for IMR:**
    *   This provision mandates that if a service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice," it shall be approved regardless of the plan's more restrictive internal criteria. This principle guides the application of clinical standards.

**V. Application of Clinical Standard Knowledge (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)**

1.  **Distinction Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** The patient has myelopathy. Clinical standards emphasize that conservative treatment requirements for radiculopathy (e.g., mandatory PT duration) do not apply to myelopathy.
2.  **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** The AO Spine Guidelines (2021) state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence..." This further supports the override of the PT requirement by CIC § 10169.5(a)(3).
3.  **Surgical Indications for Mild Myelopathy:** AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) categorize Mr. Achebe's presentation (mJOA 15/18, stable, no myelomalacia, no frank weakness) as **Tier 2 ("Reasonable Indication")**: "Mild myelopathy with stable symptoms after adequate conservative trial." This indicates that while surgery is a reasonable option, an *adequate conservative trial* is still generally expected for this classification, absent signs of urgency (like myelomalacia or acute progression).
4.  **Myelomalacia:** The absence of myelomalacia is a key finding. Clinical standards identify myelomalacia as a "strong surgical indication" and a "contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials," but its absence in this case means the *highest level of urgency* is not objectively present.

**VI. Conclusion and Disposition**

This review finds that the initial denial for CPT 22551 is **partially flawed** because one of its core reasons for denial (insufficient physical therapy) is explicitly overridden by California state law. However, the other elements of the denial (insufficient pharmacotherapy and lack of interventional procedures) are not universally overridden by state law or clinical standards for this specific patient's presentation.

1.  **Physical Therapy Requirement:** The denial's reliance on insufficient physical therapy (3 weeks completed, 6 or 4 required) is **INVALID**. Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), HealthFirst cannot deny based on PT completion where there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address, which is present in this case (cord compression, Hoffman's sign, hyperreflexia).

2.  **Pharmacotherapy and Interventional Procedures:** For Mr. Achebe's objectively determined *mild and stable* cervical myelopathy (mJOA 15/18, 6 months stable, no myelomalacia), the conditions for a full waiver of all conservative treatment under DMHC APL 22-014 (requiring progressive pathology or myelomalacia) are **not met**. The clinical standards (AANS/CNS Tier 2) indicate that surgery is a "reasonable option" *after an adequate conservative trial*. HealthFirst's requirements for two pharmacotherapy agents and one interventional procedure, in the absence of documented contraindications for the latter, can be considered part of an "adequate conservative trial" for a mild, stable myelopathy patient who does not meet objective criteria for surgical urgency. Therefore, the denial reasons concerning insufficient pharmacotherapy (1/2 agents) and lack of interventional procedures (0/1 procedures, without documented contraindication for waiver) are **not overridden** by the state law or clinical standards for this specific clinical presentation.

3.  **Treating Neurologist's Declaration:** While Dr. Osei's declaration expresses significant urgency, it is directly contradicted by the objective clinical findings in the record (MRI showing no myelomalacia or T2 signal change; neurological exam showing no frank weakness, intact ADLs, and mild myelopathy; and a documented stable symptom duration). Objective evidence must guide the determination.

Given that a significant portion of the denial basis (physical therapy) is invalid, but other components of the conservative treatment requirement are not universally waived by higher authority for this patient's specific mild and stable condition, the most appropriate disposition is to **REMAND** the case to HealthFirst for re-evaluation.

**Disposition: REMAND**

**Warrant:**
The denial is REMANDED to HealthFirst Health Plan. The plan must re-evaluate the prior authorization request by:
1.  Removing the physical therapy requirement as a basis for denial, in accordance with California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(3), as the member exhibits objective structural neurological compromise (cord compression, bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia) that physical therapy cannot address.
2.  Re-assessing the request based on the remaining unmet conservative treatment criteria (pharmacotherapy and interventional procedures), specifically considering whether, for a patient with *mild, stable cervical myelopathy without myelomalacia or objective progression*, the completion of these specific conservative treatment elements is medically necessary under generally accepted clinical standards of practice. The plan must consider if its specific criteria for number of pharmacotherapy agents and interventional procedures are consistent with the "adequate conservative trial" recommended by AANS/CNS for Tier 2 indications, or if they are more restrictive than generally accepted standards for this specific presentation.

HealthFirst is required to provide a new determination within applicable regulatory timeframes, clearly outlining its findings based on this remand.
