## APPEAL DETERMINATION: Prior Authorization for ACDF C6-C7

**Case ID:** PA-2024-B002
**Member:** Thomas Reed (M-667788)
**Procedure:** ACDF C6-C7 (CPT 22551)
**Date of Review:** [Current Date]
**Reviewer:** Expert Health Plan Appeals Reviewer

---

**I. BACKGROUND**

Mr. Thomas Reed, a 52-year-old member, requested prior authorization for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7. The request was initially denied by HealthFirst PPO Gold on 2024-10-20, citing "Member does not meet criteria for ACDF. Imaging shows C6-C7 disc herniation with mild cord contact but no measurable compression. No myelopathy findings on exam. PT not yet completed to functional plateau." Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, conducted the initial denial. The member appealed this decision, asserting that 7 weeks of Physical Therapy (PT) were completed with a documented functional plateau, and the surgeon believes ACDF is indicated for persistent radiculopathy.

---

**II. CLINICAL SUMMARY**

The member's presenting diagnosis is Cervical Radiculopathy C6-C7, persistent after conservative treatment.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-08-01):** Reveals a C6-C7 disc herniation with mild cord contact, *without measurable compression or cord signal change (myelomalacia)*. Mild right C7 foraminal stenosis is noted.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-08-05):** Shows right C7 dermatomal numbness and a diminished right tricep reflex. Critically, there are *no bilateral findings and no upper motor neuron signs*, which excludes a primary diagnosis of myelopathy. Pain is documented as 7/10 at rest.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy (PT):** 7 weeks of supervised PT (21 sessions) were completed. The PT discharge summary explicitly states that a "functional plateau reached — patient has not improved over final 3 sessions. Radicular symptoms persist unchanged."
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Pregabalin 150mg BID for 4 months (partial pain relief, radicular symptoms persist) and Meloxicam 15mg for 3 months (inadequate response) were trialed.
    *   **Interventional:** A right C7 epidural performed on 2024-09-20 resulted in 30% improvement lasting 3 weeks, followed by a return to baseline symptoms.

---

**III. REVIEW OF DENIAL AND APPEAL BASIS AGAINST CRITERIA AND REGULATIONS**

The review proceeds using the hierarchy: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.

**A. Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)**

1.  **Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1):** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of Cervical Radiculopathy C6-C7 (2.1a) with supporting MRI findings. *Met.*
2.  **Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2):**
    *   **Physical Therapy (2.2a):** The plan requires a "minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy with documented functional outcomes." The member completed 7 weeks of PT.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2b):** The plan requires a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The member trialed Pregabalin for 4 months and Meloxicam for 3 months. *Met.*
    *   **Interventional (2.2c):** The plan requires one interventional treatment. The member received a right C7 epidural. *Met.*
    *   **Myelopathy Exception (2.2A):** The denial explicitly states "No myelopathy findings on exam," and the clinical record confirms "No upper motor neuron signs, no bilateral findings, no cord signal change." The primary diagnosis is radiculopathy. Therefore, the myelopathy exception is *not applicable* to this case.
    *   **Functional Plateau:** The plan's criteria define "functional plateau" as "<10% improvement over 3 sessions OR therapist documents maximum benefit reached." The PT discharge summary explicitly states "functional plateau reached — patient has not improved over final 3 sessions." This aligns with the plan's definition.

    *   **Analysis of Plan's Application:** The plan's denial reason states "PT not yet completed to functional plateau." This statement directly contradicts the clinical record which documents completion of 7 weeks of PT and the explicit declaration of a "functional plateau reached" by the physical therapist, a finding that also aligns with HealthFirst's own definition of a functional plateau. Therefore, the plan misapplied its own criteria regarding the completion of conservative treatment.

**B. California Regulatory Framework**

1.  **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):**
    *   This state law prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where "(2) The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms; OR (b) 'functional plateau' means documented failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement after a course of therapy, as determined by the treating physical therapist or physician."
    *   **Analysis:** The member completed 7 weeks of PT, and the PT discharge summary explicitly states a functional plateau was reached due to a lack of improvement over the final 3 sessions, with symptoms persisting. This directly satisfies the conditions of CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) and (b). The plan's denial based on PT "not yet completed to functional plateau" is in direct violation of this state law, as a functional plateau *was* demonstrably reached.

2.  **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):**
    *   This APL addresses conditions risking irreversible harm, particularly myelopathy with myelomalacia. The current case is diagnosed as radiculopathy, and the MRI explicitly notes "no cord signal change. No myelomalacia," and the exam shows "No upper motor neuron signs."
    *   **Analysis:** While this APL's specific myelopathy provisions do not directly apply given the clinical presentation, Section II.3 states that "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice." This general principle is relevant if plan criteria contradict clinical standards.

**C. Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)**

1.  **Diagnostic Classification:** The clinical record clearly identifies the condition as Cervical Radiculopathy, not Myelopathy. The absence of UMN signs, bilateral symptoms, and myelomalacia supports this distinction (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.1).
2.  **Conservative Treatment for Radiculopathy:** For cervical radiculopathy, conservative treatment is typically recommended for 6-12 weeks (NASS Guidelines 2020, AANS/CNS 2023). The member completed 7 weeks of PT, trials of two pharmacotherapy agents for adequate durations, and an interventional procedure that provided only temporary, partial relief.
3.  **Functional Plateau (Clinical Standard Definition, Section 5):** Defined as "<10% improvement in validated functional outcome measure ... over 3 consecutive therapy sessions, OR therapist documentation that patient has reached maximum therapeutic benefit." The PT discharge summary stating no improvement over the final 3 sessions and reaching a functional plateau aligns with this clinical definition.
4.  **Surgical Indications for Radiculopathy:** AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), Section 3.1, identifies "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" as a TIER 2 "Reasonable Indication" for surgery.
5.  **Analysis:** The member has completed a comprehensive and appropriate course of conservative treatment for radiculopathy, lasting over 7 weeks, and has reached a documented functional plateau with persistent symptoms and functional limitations (Pain 7/10). The requested ACDF is consistent with generally accepted clinical standards for persistent cervical radiculopathy that has failed adequate conservative management.

---

**IV. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND CONCLUSION**

The HealthFirst plan denied the prior authorization primarily on the grounds that "PT not yet completed to functional plateau." This assertion is directly contradicted by the clinical record, which explicitly states that 7 weeks of PT were completed and a functional plateau was reached, with no improvement over the final 3 sessions. This finding is consistent with both HealthFirst's own definition of "functional plateau" and the clinical standard's definition.

Furthermore, California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2) and (b) explicitly prohibit a health insurer from denying a surgical procedure on the basis of physical therapy completion when the member has completed a course of PT and reached a functional plateau, as documented by the treating therapist or physician. This state law overrides any contrary interpretation or application of plan criteria.

The member has met all other conservative treatment requirements of the plan (pharmacotherapy and interventional) and continues to experience significant, persistent radicular symptoms. Surgical intervention (ACDF) for radiculopathy that has failed adequate conservative treatment and reached a functional plateau is a generally accepted clinical standard (AANS/CNS TIER 2 indication).

Therefore, the plan's denial is based on an incorrect assessment of the clinical facts relative to its own criteria and is in direct violation of state law.

---

**V. DISPOSITION**

**OVERTURN**

---

**VI. WARRANT**

The denial for ACDF C6-C7 for Mr. Thomas Reed is **OVERTURNED** because the denial's primary stated reason—that "PT not yet completed to functional plateau"—is demonstrably inaccurate based on the provided clinical record and is in direct violation of California state law.

1.  **Failure to Apply Plan's Own Criteria Correctly:** The clinical record shows that 7 weeks of physical therapy were completed, and the PT discharge summary explicitly states a "functional plateau reached" because "patient has not improved over final 3 sessions." This precisely matches HealthFirst's own definition of a functional plateau: "<10% improvement over 3 sessions OR therapist documents maximum benefit reached" (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2). The plan's denial therefore misapplied its own criteria.
2.  **Violation of California State Law:** California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2) and (b) explicitly states that a health insurer cannot deny a surgical procedure based on incomplete physical therapy when "the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." This provision, which is superior in the hierarchy of authority, has been fully met by the member.
3.  **Medical Necessity Under Clinical Standards:** The member's diagnosis is persistent cervical radiculopathy after a comprehensive trial of conservative treatment, including 7 weeks of PT, two pharmacotherapy agents, and an epidural injection, all of which failed to provide adequate, sustained relief. The patient has reached a functional plateau and continues to experience persistent, severe symptoms (pain 7/10, numbness, diminished reflex). This clinical presentation, after an adequate and failed conservative trial, constitutes a "Reasonable Indication" (TIER 2) for ACDF per the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023, Section 3.1).

The evidence unequivocally supports that the member has met all necessary conservative treatment requirements, including reaching a functional plateau in physical therapy as defined by both the plan and state law. The requested ACDF is medically necessary under generally accepted standards of medical practice for persistent radiculopathy refractory to conservative management.
