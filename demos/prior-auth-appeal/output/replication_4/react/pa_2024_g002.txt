## HealthFirst PPO Gold — Prior Authorization Appeal Determination

**Case ID:** PA-2024-G002
**Member:** Priya Nair (M-G002), Age 56
**Plan:** HealthFirst PPO Gold — Group Plan 77441, California
**Procedure:** CPT 22551, Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C5-C6, one level
**Requested by:** Dr. James Chen, MD — Neurosurgery, Cedars-Sinai Medical Center
**Date of Review:** 2024-11-15

---

### INTRODUCTION

This letter serves as the appeal determination for the prior authorization request for an ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) for member Priya Nair, which was initially denied on 2024-10-25. The initial denial cited reason code MED-003, stating, "Member does not meet conservative treatment requirements under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. Physical therapy: 5 weeks completed. Standard pathway requires 6 weeks minimum. Member has not completed the required minimum conservative treatment."

The member's appeal argues that the plan applied the incorrect pathway, asserting that the myelopathy pathway (Section 2.2A) applies, which requires a 4-week PT minimum, and that the member has a confirmed myelopathy diagnosis and has completed 5 weeks of PT, thereby meeting all applicable criteria.

We have reviewed the clinical record, the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), relevant California state laws (California Insurance Code (CIC), California Health & Safety Code (CHSC), Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL)), and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.

---

### BACKGROUND

Priya Nair, a 56-year-old member, presents with cervical myelopathy at C5-C6. This diagnosis is confirmed by neurological exam findings (bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia upper extremities, tandem gait impairment, bilateral hand clumsiness indicating upper motor neuron signs) and MRI imaging (C5-C6 moderate central cord compression). A neurologist consultation on 2024-09-12 explicitly confirmed "cervical myelopathy, primary."

The member underwent a trial of conservative treatment, including 5 weeks (15 sessions) of physical therapy under a myelopathy protocol. The treating physician documented no improvement in upper motor neuron signs and halted PT at 5 weeks due to the myelopathy diagnosis and lack of improvement. Pharmacotherapy included Baclofen for 3 months with minimal spasticity relief and a Methylprednisolone dose pack for 3 weeks with only temporary improvement. Interventional treatment was waived by the treating physician, who documented that it was not indicated due to cord compression with myelopathy requiring surgical evaluation.

The initial denial was based on the standard pathway (Section 2.2) requiring 6 weeks of physical therapy, asserting the member had only completed 5 weeks.

---

### REVIEW AND ANALYSIS

Our review applies the established hierarchy of authority: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.

**1. Application of Plan's Own Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**

*   **Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1):** The clinical record clearly confirms a diagnosis of "Cervical myelopathy C5-C6" supported by objective neurological findings and MRI, meeting Section 2.1(b).
*   **Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2):** The initial denial incorrectly applied the standard pathway (Section 2.2) for conservative treatment.
    *   **Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2A):** This exception explicitly states: "For members with confirmed cervical myelopathy (criterion 2.1(b)), the conservative treatment requirement is MODIFIED as follows: Physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks minimum. Interventional treatment requirement (2.2(c)) is WAIVED if treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses risk of neurological deterioration. Pharmacotherapy requirement (2.2(b)) remains in full." The policy also notes this exception applies "only when myelopathy is the PRIMARY diagnosis."
    *   The clinical record unequivocally confirms cervical myelopathy as the primary diagnosis, stating "Diagnosis: cervical myelopathy, primary."
    *   **Physical Therapy:** The member completed 5 weeks of physical therapy. Under the applicable Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2A), the minimum PT requirement is 4 weeks. The member has exceeded this requirement.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** The member completed trials of Baclofen (3 months) and Methylprednisolone (3 weeks), meeting Section 2.2(b).
    *   **Interventional Treatment:** Dr. Chen documented that interventional treatment was "not indicated — cord compression with myelopathy requires surgical evaluation, not injection," which fulfills the waiver condition under Section 2.2A.
*   **Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The initial denial was based on an incorrect application of HealthFirst's own clinical coverage criteria. The Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2A) is clearly applicable, and under this exception, the member has met all conservative treatment requirements. The factual claim in the denial notice ("Standard pathway (Section 2.2) requires 6 weeks PT") was indeed incorrect for this patient's diagnosis.

**2. California State Law Review:**

*   **CIC § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This state law explicitly prohibits conditioning surgical approval on PT completion where certain conditions are met. The member's case fulfills multiple conditions:
    *   **(a)(1) Physician documentation of PT contraindication/unlikely benefit:** The treating physician halted PT at 5 weeks, documenting "no improvement in UMN signs" and indicating PT was unlikely to benefit further. Dr. Chen also deemed interventional procedures "not indicated" due to the nature of myelopathy.
    *   **(a)(2) Functional Plateau:** The member completed 5 weeks of PT with "No improvement in UMN signs." Clinical standards define functional plateau as failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement, especially for neurological symptoms in myelopathy which "are not expected to respond to PT regardless of duration." The absence of improvement in UMN signs indicates a functional plateau for the critical myelopathic symptoms.
    *   **(a)(3) Objective evidence of structural neurological compromise:** The MRI shows "C5-C6 moderate central cord compression" with confirmed "Bilateral Hoffman signs, Bilateral hyperreflexia upper extremities," and "gait mildly impaired." This constitutes objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address.
    *   **Conclusion:** Pursuant to CIC § 10169.5, HealthFirst cannot require further physical therapy for this member, as the conditions for waiver of a PT prerequisite are met. This state law overrides any plan criteria for minimum PT duration in this specific clinical scenario.

*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 — Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions:**
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** This guidance states that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression... constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent." Ms. Nair has objective cord compression and confirmed myelopathy.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** This section states plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when "surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines" and conservative treatment has failed. The clinical record indicates conservative treatment (5 weeks PT, pharmacotherapy) failed to improve UMN signs.
    *   **Conclusion:** DMHC APL 22-014 emphasizes the urgency of myelopathy and supports waiving stringent conservative treatment requirements when surgery is clinically indicated, further reinforcing that the plan's denial is inconsistent with California regulatory guidance.

*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) — Standard of Review for IMR:** This statute mandates that services meeting "generally accepted standards of medical practice" SHALL be approved, regardless of whether a plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. This provides a legal basis for clinical standards to override plan criteria.

**3. Clinical Standard Review (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

*   **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines emphasize that myelopathy is a distinct and more serious condition than radiculopathy, requiring different treatment algorithms. The "application of radiculopathy conservative treatment requirements to myelopathy patients is NOT supported by clinical evidence."
*   **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:**
    *   AO Spine Guidelines (2021) state, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
    *   NASS Guidelines (2020) recommend surgical decompression as the primary treatment for moderate to severe myelopathy.
*   **Surgical Indications:** Priya Nair's presentation (confirmed cervical myelopathy, moderate central cord compression, and objective upper motor neuron signs with lack of improvement with conservative care) meets the **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION** for surgery: "(a) Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI" and "(b) Progressive neurological deficit despite conservative treatment."
*   **Risk of Delay:** Clinical guidelines underscore the risk of delaying surgery for moderate-severe myelopathy, noting decreased likelihood of neurological recovery with prolonged compression.
*   **Conclusion on Clinical Standards:** Generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice strongly support surgical intervention for this member's condition and do not support requiring a minimum duration of physical therapy prior to surgery for moderate to severe cervical myelopathy.

**4. Hierarchy of Authority and Conflict Resolution:**

The plan's initial denial incorrectly applied its own criteria by failing to identify and apply the specific Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2A). Even if the Myelopathy Exception's 4-week PT requirement were strictly applied, it is superseded by:
*   **California State Law (CIC § 10169.5):** Which explicitly waives PT prerequisites when a functional plateau is reached, PT is unlikely to benefit, or there is objective neurological compromise, all of which are present in this case.
*   **Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine):** Which do not support mandatory PT duration for moderate to severe cervical myelopathy and recommend surgery as the primary treatment due to the risk of neurological deterioration. These clinical standards are upheld by CHSC § 1374.32(b) and DMHC APL 22-014, which state that internal plan criteria cannot be more restrictive than accepted medical practice.

Therefore, multiple higher authorities override the basis for the denial.

---

### DETERMINATION

**OVERTURN** the denial of prior authorization for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level) for member Priya Nair.

### WARRANT

The denial is overturned for the following reasons:

1.  **Incorrect Application of Plan Criteria:** The initial denial failed to correctly apply HealthFirst's own Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024). The member's diagnosis of primary cervical myelopathy triggers the Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2A), which reduces the physical therapy requirement to a 4-week minimum and waives the interventional treatment requirement when a physician documents risk. The member completed 5 weeks of physical therapy, exceeding the 4-week requirement, and the treating physician provided the necessary documentation to waive interventional treatment. Therefore, the member meets the conservative treatment requirements under the correctly applied plan criteria.
2.  **Overriding State Law:** California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(1), (a)(2), and (a)(3) prohibit conditioning surgical approval on completion of physical therapy where, as in this case, the treating physician documented PT was unlikely to benefit and halted treatment due to lack of improvement in UMN signs (functional plateau), and there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (moderate central cord compression with UMN signs) that PT cannot address. These state law provisions supersede any plan-specific minimum PT duration.
3.  **Consistency with Clinical Standards of Care:** Generally accepted clinical standards, including AANS/CNS, NASS, and AO Spine guidelines, indicate that for moderate to severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and neurological deficits, surgery is a Tier 1 strong indication, and there is no high-quality evidence to support a mandatory course of physical therapy. Delaying surgery for further conservative treatment in such cases is not supported by evidence and may lead to neurological deterioration. California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32(b) and DMHC APL 22-014 mandate that services meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice shall be approved, regardless of more restrictive internal plan criteria.

Based on the evidence presented, applying the hierarchy of authority, the requested ACDF C5-C6 is medically necessary and meets the criteria for approval.
