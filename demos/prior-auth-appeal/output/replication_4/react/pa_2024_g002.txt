**DISPOSITION: OVERTURN DENIAL**

---

**WARRANT AND RATIONALE FOR OVERTURN:**

Upon comprehensive review of the prior authorization appeal, the member’s clinical record, HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), relevant California state laws, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, the initial denial is hereby OVERTURNED. The requested Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C5-C6 is deemed medically necessary.

The denial was based on an incorrect application of HealthFirst's own clinical coverage criteria and is inconsistent with California state law and established clinical guidelines for the management of cervical myelopathy.

**Detailed Rationale:**

1.  **Incorrect Application of Plan Criteria by Initial Reviewer:**
    *   The member, Priya Nair, has a confirmed diagnosis of **Cervical Myelopathy at C5-C6**, supported by objective evidence including moderate central cord compression on MRI (2024-09-01) and definitive Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) signs on neurological exam (2024-09-05), such as bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, and impaired tandem gait. A neurologist consultation (2024-09-12) explicitly confirmed "Clinical picture consistent with cervical myelopathy. Bilateral UMN signs are pathognomonic. Diagnosis: cervical myelopathy, primary." This satisfies HealthFirst's criterion 2.1(b) for Cervical Myelopathy (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(b)).
    *   HealthFirst's own policy, CC-SPINE-2024, includes an **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY**. This specific pathway applies when myelopathy is the primary diagnosis, which it is in this case. The myelopathy pathway modifies the conservative treatment requirements by reducing the physical therapy (PT) minimum to **4 weeks** and waiving the interventional treatment requirement if the treating physician documents risk (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2A Note).
    *   The initial denial incorrectly applied the "Standard pathway" (Section 2.2), which requires a 6-week minimum of PT. The denial's stated reason—"Member does not meet conservative treatment requirements under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. Physical therapy: 5 weeks completed. Standard pathway requires 6 weeks minimum"—demonstrates a failure to apply the applicable myelopathy exception.
    *   Under the correct myelopathy pathway (Section 2.2A):
        *   **Physical Therapy:** The member completed 5 weeks of PT, which **exceeds** the 4-week minimum requirement. The treating physician halted PT at 5 weeks due to lack of improvement in UMN signs, indicating that further PT was unlikely to be beneficial for the myelopathy.
        *   **Pharmacotherapy:** The member completed a trial of Baclofen for 3 months and Methylprednisolone for 3 weeks. Baclofen for 3 months satisfies the "minimum 4 weeks each" requirement for at least one agent (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(b)). The denial did not cite pharmacotherapy as a reason for denial.
        *   **Interventional Treatment:** The treating physician, Dr. Chen, documented that interventional treatment was "not indicated — cord compression with myelopathy requires surgical evaluation, not injection." This explicitly meets the condition for waiver under the myelopathy pathway (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2A).
    *   Therefore, even strictly applying HealthFirst's *own* criteria correctly, the member meets all requirements for approval under the myelopathy exception pathway.

2.  **Overrides by California State Law:**
    *   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This state law explicitly prohibits a health insurer from conditioning surgical approval on completion of physical therapy under several conditions, all of which are met here:
        *   **(a)(1) Physician documents PT is unlikely to benefit:** The treating physician documented halting PT at 5 weeks due to the myelopathy diagnosis and lack of improvement in UMN signs, indicating PT was no longer beneficial.
        *   **(a)(2) Member reached functional plateau:** The clinical record notes "No improvement in UMN signs." Clinical standards define functional plateau as documented failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement, and note that neurological symptoms in myelopathy are not expected to respond to PT regardless of duration (Clinical Standard Section 5). The 5 weeks of PT without improvement in UMN signs indicates a functional plateau for the myelopathic condition.
        *   **(a)(3) Objective structural neurological compromise:** The MRI confirms "moderate central cord compression" with confirmed cervical myelopathy, which constitutes objective structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address.
    *   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This guidance directs plans to treat cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression as an urgent condition. It specifically states that plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when: (a) objective imaging shows progressive pathology (cord compression), (b) the treating physician documents conservative treatment failure, and (c) surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines (DMHC APL 22-014, Section II.2). All these conditions are met. This APL reinforces that plans cannot apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards for conditions like myelopathy (DMHC APL 22-014, Section II.3).

3.  **Conflict with Generally Accepted Clinical Standards of Medical Practice:**
    *   **AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine Guidelines:** These leading clinical guidelines consistently state that for moderate to severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and neurological deficits (as confirmed in Priya Nair's case), surgical decompression is a **Tier 1 strong indication** (Clinical Standard Section 3.1).
    *   Crucially, these guidelines indicate **no high-quality evidence** supporting a mandatory course or minimum duration of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for moderate to severe cervical myelopathy (Clinical Standard Section 2.1, 2.2).
    *   Delaying surgery in such patients to complete an arbitrary physical therapy trial is not supported by evidence and carries a risk of neurological deterioration (Clinical Standard Section 2.1, 2.3).
    *   The member's presentation—confirmed cervical myelopathy with cord compression and UMN signs—meets Tier 1 criteria for surgery, and clinical standards do not require a minimum course of PT as a prerequisite (Clinical Standard Section 3.2).

**Conclusion:**

The initial denial was based on an erroneous application of HealthFirst's own clinical policy, failing to recognize the specific myelopathy exception. Furthermore, even if the plan's criteria were applied correctly, the denial would be superseded by California state laws (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014), which waive or limit physical therapy requirements under the circumstances presented. Finally, requiring a minimum duration of physical therapy for confirmed moderate cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and UMN signs directly conflicts with generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, which prioritize surgical intervention for this condition without mandatory pre-operative physical therapy.

Based on the three-source hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), the denial of prior authorization for CPT code 22551 for Priya Nair is **OVERTURNED**. HealthFirst is directed to approve the requested procedure as medically necessary.
