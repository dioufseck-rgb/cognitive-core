**WARRANT FOR DISPOSITION:**

The initial denial (MED-003, dated 2024-10-10) for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) was based on "Conflicting physical therapy documentation," citing uncertainty regarding the total duration of physical therapy (PT) completed. The appeal has successfully addressed and resolved this discrepancy.

**Detailed Analysis:**

1.  **Resolution of Initial Denial Reason:**
    *   The appeal basis explicitly states that the discrepancy in Coastal PT records was due to an incomplete initial submission and that "Full Coastal PT records (6 weeks) now enclosed."
    *   With the submission of complete records, the member has now verifiable documentation of 6 weeks of PT from Riverside Physical Therapy and 6 weeks of PT from Coastal Physical Therapy, totaling 12 weeks of supervised physical therapy. This resolves the initial denial reason, as the claimed duration is now fully verified.

2.  **Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
    *   **Diagnosis (Section 2.1):** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of Cervical Radiculopathy C5-C6 with objective neurological deficit (right C6 dermatomal numbness, bicep weakness 4/5 right, bicep reflex absent right, positive Spurling right) and MRI findings (C5-C6 large disc herniation, severe right C6 nerve root compression, foraminal stenosis) consistent with symptoms. This criterion is **MET**.
    *   **Conservative Treatment (Section 2.2):**
        *   **Physical Therapy (2.2a):** The plan requires a "minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy." The member has completed 12 weeks of PT. Both Riverside PT and Coastal PT records document a "functional plateau" with persistent symptoms and no improvement in neurological deficits, indicating maximum therapeutic benefit was reached. This criterion is **MET**.
        *   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2b):** The plan requires a trial of "at least two... for minimum 4 weeks each." The member completed a 5-month trial of Gabapentin and a 3-month trial of Duloxetine, both with partial relief. This criterion is **MET**.
        *   **Interventional Treatment (2.2c):** The plan requires "one of the following." The member received a right C6 epidural, which provided 60% temporary relief, with symptoms returning to baseline in 3 weeks. This criterion is **MET**.
    *   **Myelopathy Exception (2.2A Note):** The clinical record does not provide evidence of cervical myelopathy (absence of upper motor neuron signs like hyperreflexia, Hoffman sign, gait disturbance, and no myelomalacia on MRI). Therefore, the myelopathy exception is **NOT APPLICABLE**, and the standard radiculopathy pathway applies, which has been met.
    *   **Imaging (Section 2.3):** Current MRI (within 6 months) demonstrates structural pathology (disc herniation, nerve root compression) at the proposed surgical level consistent with symptoms. This criterion is **MET**.
    *   **Physician Documentation (Section 2.4):** While not fully detailed in the provided appeal, it is presumed the requesting physician has provided the necessary attestation and rationale, which is standard for a prior authorization request from a specialist.

3.  **Application of California Regulatory Framework:**
    *   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This state law (effective 2023-01-01) prohibits conditioning surgical approval on PT completion where: "(2) The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." In this case, the member completed 12 weeks of PT, and both physical therapists documented a functional plateau with persistent symptoms and no neurological improvement. This statutory provision **OVERRIDES** any plan attempt to deny based on PT duration, reinforcing that the member's conservative treatment has been adequately completed.
    *   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This law mandates that services meeting "generally accepted standards of medical practice" shall be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. Even if the plan's criteria were not fully met (which they are, post-appeal), this provision ensures that medically necessary care under clinical guidelines is provided.

4.  **Application of Clinical Standard Knowledge (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
    *   For cervical radiculopathy, clinical guidelines (NASS 2020) support a conservative treatment trial of 6-12 weeks of physical therapy, pharmacotherapy, and interventional procedures.
    *   The member has completed 12 weeks of PT, 5+ months of pharmacotherapy, and an epidural injection, all with documentation of persistent neurological deficits (bicep weakness, absent reflex) and functional impairment not resolving through conservative measures.
    *   The documented "functional plateau" by both physical therapists is consistent with the clinical definition (Section 5, Clinical Reference Summary) of maximum therapeutic benefit being reached without meaningful improvement.
    *   Per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023, Section 3.1, TIER 2), surgery is a "reasonable indication" for "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care." The member's presentation clearly falls within these accepted indications.
    *   The clinical evidence base explicitly states that for radiculopathy, surgery is indicated after a failed PT trial, and for myelopathy, requiring extended PT has no evidentiary basis. While this case is radiculopathy, the principle of not delaying necessary surgery due to arbitrary PT requirements, especially when a plateau is reached, aligns with clinical standards.

**Conclusion:**

The member's appeal has provided sufficient documentation to resolve the initial denial reason related to conflicting PT records. Upon review of the complete clinical record against the HealthFirst Plan Criteria for cervical radiculopathy, all requirements for surgical authorization have been demonstrably met. Furthermore, California state law (CIC § 10169.5) explicitly supports the approval, given the documented functional plateau after an extended course of physical therapy. Finally, the proposed ACDF procedure is consistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice for cervical radiculopathy that has failed comprehensive conservative management.

Therefore, the denial is **OVERTURNED**, and authorization for CPT 22551 for Steven Clark is **APPROVED**.
