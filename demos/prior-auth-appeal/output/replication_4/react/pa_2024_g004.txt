**DISPOSITION: UPHOLD DENIAL**

**WARRANT:**

This appeal requests authorization for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 for Stephanie Oduya. The initial denial cited insufficient conservative treatment and a lack of objective neurological deficit or cord compression. After a thorough review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's plan criteria, California state regulations, and generally accepted clinical standards, the denial is upheld.

**1. Clinical Presentation Assessment:**
The member's presenting diagnosis is "Cervical radiculopathy C5-C6, right-sided." Objective findings from the clinical record are critical:
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-10):** Shows "C5-C6 mild to moderate disc herniation. Right C6 nerve root contact without measurable compression. **No cord contact. No cord compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia.** Mild right foraminal narrowing." (Clinical Record, imaging)
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-09-15):** Documents "Right shoulder pain and neck pain 6/10. RIGHT C6 dermatomal tingling \u2014 mild. Bicep reflex 2+ bilateral, symmetric. Grip strength 5/5 bilateral. **No weakness. No UMN signs.** Functional assessment: working full-time, no activity limitations." (Clinical Record, neurological exam)

Based on these objective findings, the member's condition is consistent with **cervical radiculopathy**, characterized by nerve root irritation and unilateral symptoms, and **does not meet the criteria for cervical myelopathy**. Myelopathy requires evidence of spinal cord compression, upper motor neuron signs, bilateral symptoms, gait disturbance, or myelomalacia (cord signal change) (Domain Knowledge; Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.1). The MRI explicitly rules out cord compression and myelomalacia, and the neurological exam shows no objective weakness or upper motor neuron signs.

**2. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
The requested procedure, CPT 22551, is covered by HealthFirst's policy. Review of Section 2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement reveals:
*   **(a) Physical Therapy:** A minimum of 6 weeks is required. The member completed 4 weeks of PT (Clinical Record, conservative treatment). This requirement is **NOT MET**.
*   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** A trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each is required. The member used Naproxen 500mg BID for 8 weeks (Clinical Record, conservative treatment). Documentation for a second pharmacotherapy agent is **NOT PRESENT**, thus this requirement is **NOT MET**.
*   **(c) Interventional Treatment:** One interventional treatment is required. The member had no documented interventional procedures (Clinical Record, conservative treatment). This requirement is **NOT MET**.

**Exception 2.2(A) Myelopathy Pathway:** This exception, which reduces PT to 4 weeks and waives interventional treatment, applies "only when myelopathy is the PRIMARY diagnosis." As established, the member does not have cervical myelopathy. Therefore, this exception **does not apply**.

The plan's denial reason, "Conservative treatment pathway not completed," is directly supported by the clinical record and HealthFirst's plan criteria.

**3. Application of California Regulatory Requirements:**
*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute allows waiver of PT requirements under specific conditions.
    *   (a)(1) "Physician documents PT contraindicated or unlikely to benefit": While Dr. Walsh halted PT, he did not provide specific documentation that PT was contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member given her specific condition at the time the PA was submitted (Clinical Record, treating surgeon statement).
    *   (a)(2) "Member completed PT and reached a functional plateau": The member completed 4 weeks of PT. The clinical standard for a "functional plateau" is <10% improvement over 3 sessions or therapist documentation of maximum benefit (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 5). The PT summary notes "Mild improvement in pain" but does not document a functional plateau as defined.
    *   (a)(3) "Objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address": This refers to severe conditions like myelomalacia with cord compression. The MRI explicitly states "No cord compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia," and the neurological exam shows "No weakness. No UMN signs." Thus, this exception **does not apply**.
    *   Therefore, no state law exception waives the PT requirement in this case.

*   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This All-Plan Letter specifically addresses time-sensitive neurological conditions such as myelopathy with cord compression and myelomalacia, allowing for waivers of conservative treatment. As the member does not have myelopathy, cord compression, or myelomalacia, this APL **does not apply** to her case.

*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard vs. Plan Criteria):** This section mandates that services meeting "generally accepted standards of medical practice" shall be approved regardless of more restrictive plan criteria. This requires evaluation against clinical standards.

**4. Application of Clinical Standard Knowledge (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
For cervical radiculopathy (the member's condition), generally accepted clinical guidelines recommend:
*   **Conservative Treatment Trial:** Surgery is typically indicated for radiculopathy with persistent symptoms **after 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment** (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.1, TIER 2(b) and Section 4 Summary Table). The clinical evidence emphasizes that physical therapy for radiculopathy is generally effective, and the risk of delaying surgery for radiculopathy is low (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 4).
*   The member has completed only 4 weeks of PT, one pharmacotherapy, and no interventional treatment. This falls short of the generally accepted duration and scope of conservative management recommended for cervical radiculopathy before surgical intervention is considered.
*   The clinical guidelines specifically state that "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1). This statement, however, applies to **myelopathy**, which is not present in this case. For radiculopathy, conservative treatment is the initial recommended pathway.

**5. Resolution of Conflicts (Hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):**
*   No state law provides an exception for this case that would override the plan's conservative treatment requirements.
*   The plan's requirements for conservative treatment (6 weeks PT, 2 pharmacotherapy agents, 1 interventional procedure) are consistent with, if not less stringent than, the generally accepted clinical standards which advise a 6-12 week *course* of conservative treatment for radiculopathy before surgery. The member has not met either the specific plan criteria or the general duration for conservative therapy recommended by clinical standards for radiculopathy.
*   Dr. Walsh's appeal argues for his clinical judgment to take precedence and implies urgency. However, the objective clinical evidence (no cord compression, no myelomalacia, no weakness, no UMN signs, full-time work with no activity limitations) does not support a finding of medical urgency or a condition that cannot be addressed by further conservative management as defined by regulatory guidance (DMHC APL 22-014) or clinical standards (AANS/CNS TIER 1 indications). The review by Dr. Rivera, a board-certified neurosurgeon, aligns with these objective findings and the necessity of completing conservative treatment.

**Conclusion:**
The denial for CPT 22551 is upheld. The member's clinical presentation is consistent with cervical radiculopathy, for which both the HealthFirst Plan Criteria and generally accepted clinical standards require a more complete trial of conservative treatment than has been documented. There are no state law provisions or clinical urgencies present in the objective record that would mandate waiving these requirements at this time. The treating surgeon's appeal, while expressing strong professional judgment, does not introduce new objective clinical information to contradict the plan's initial denial rationale or the prevailing evidence-based guidelines for this patient's condition.

The member is encouraged to complete the recommended course of conservative treatment and resubmit a prior authorization request if symptoms persist or worsen.
