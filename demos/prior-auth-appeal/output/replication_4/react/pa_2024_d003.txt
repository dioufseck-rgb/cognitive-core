**APPLICATION OF HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY:**
**(State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria)**

**A. PLAN CRITERIA (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024) — SECTION 2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
*   **Diagnosis (2.1a):** Met (Cervical Radiculopathy C6-C7).
*   **Physical Therapy (2.2a):** Not fully met (5 weeks vs. 6-week minimum).
*   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2b):** Not fully met (Only one agent, Meloxicam, met the 4-week minimum duration requirement for two agents).
*   **Interventional Treatment (2.2c):** Not met (No interventional procedure documented).
*   **Myelopathy Exception (2.2A):** Not applicable as myelopathy is not the primary diagnosis and myelopathic signs/myelomalacia are absent.

*   **Initial finding based *solely* on plan criteria:** The denial *would be upheld* if the plan's criteria were the only consideration, as the conservative treatment requirements were not fully met. However, the hierarchy of authority must be applied.

**B. REGULATORY REVIEW (CALIFORNIA STATE LAW):**

1.  **Procedural Adequacy of Denial (CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2) & (b)):**
    *   California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a)(2) mandates that a denial notice must include "the specific criteria applied, the specific clinical reason for the denial, and notice of IMR rights."
    *   CHSC § 1374.31(b) states that a denial notice failing to specify the clinical criteria or specific clinical reason for denial is "DEFECTIVE and shall be treated as if no denial was issued."
    *   **Finding:** The denial in this case was communicated verbally during a peer-to-peer call, with no written notice provided to the member detailing the specific criteria or reason, nor was an IMR notice included. This constitutes a **clear procedural defect** and is a direct violation of state law.

2.  **Physical Therapy Requirement (CIC § 10169.5):**
    *   California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a) prohibits a plan from conditioning approval of surgery on PT completion where:
        *   **(a)(2):** The member completed PT and reached a functional plateau, as documented by the treating physician. The treating physician declared a plateau after 5 weeks of PT.
        *   **(a)(3):** The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. Jessica Turner has documented C6-C7 disc herniation, right C7 nerve root compression, moderate foraminal stenosis, diminished tricep reflex, and grip 4+/5 weakness. These are objective structural neurological compromises that PT cannot reverse.
    *   **Finding:** The plan's mechanical application of a 6-week minimum PT requirement, despite the presence of objective structural neurological compromise and a physician-declared functional plateau after 5 weeks, is overridden by CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) and (a)(3).

**C. CLINICAL STANDARD REVIEW (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

1.  **Radiculopathy Treatment (AANS/CNS 2023, Section 3.1, TIER 2):**
    *   Surgery is a "Reasonable Indication" (Tier 2) for radiculopathy with "persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" (Tier 2b) and "significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care" (Tier 2c).
    *   **Finding:** Jessica Turner has persistent objective neurological deficits (numbness, tingling, diminished reflex, weakness) after 5 weeks of PT, 3 months of Meloxicam (partial relief), and 2 weeks of Prednisone (temporary improvement). This aggregate conservative trial falls within the 6-12 week timeframe and demonstrates failure to adequately resolve symptoms or functional impairment.

2.  **Interventional Procedures/Pharmacotherapy:** While the plan's criteria require an interventional procedure and two pharmacotherapy agents for 4+ weeks each, the clinical standards for radiculopathy indicate surgery when symptoms persist after *a course* of conservative treatment, without necessarily mandating every single component of a plan's specific criteria. Given the objective structural compromise and persistent neurological deficits, the overall clinical picture supports that the member has exhausted a reasonable trial of non-surgical management.

3.  **CHSC § 1374.32(b):** This state law explicitly states that IMR standards do NOT require consistency with the plan's internal criteria. If the service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, it SHALL be approved regardless of more restrictive plan criteria.

---

**CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND FINAL DETERMINATION:**

Applying the hierarchy of authority (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria):

1.  **State Law (CHSC § 1374.31) Override:** The denial is procedurally defective due to the lack of a written denial notice specifying criteria and IMR rights. This alone is sufficient grounds to overturn the denial.

2.  **State Law (CIC § 10169.5) Override:** The plan's strict 6-week PT requirement is overridden by state law, given the objective structural neurological compromise and the physician's declaration of a functional plateau after 5 weeks.

3.  **Clinical Standard Override (CHSC § 1374.32(b)):** Although the plan's conservative treatment requirements for pharmacotherapy duration and interventional procedures were not fully met, the member has undergone a significant trial of conservative care for a structural condition causing persistent objective neurological deficits. Per generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, NASS), surgery is a reasonable option for radiculopathy with persistent symptoms and functional impairment after a trial of conservative management. The plan's more restrictive criteria cannot be the sole determinative basis for denial when the service meets generally accepted standards.

**Therefore, based on the significant procedural defects in the denial and the overriding state law and clinical standards, the denial must be reversed.**

**DISPOSITION:** **OVERTURNED**

**WARRANT:**
The denial issued by HealthFirst PPO Gold is **OVERTURNED** for the following reasons:

1.  **Procedural Defect in Denial Notice:** The denial was communicated verbally only, without a written notice detailing the specific clinical criteria applied, the specific clinical reason for the denial, or information on Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights. This directly violates California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a)(2) and (b), which renders the denial procedurally defective and requires it to be treated as if no denial was issued.

2.  **State Law Override of Physical Therapy Requirement:** The plan's strict minimum 6-week physical therapy (PT) requirement is superseded by California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5. The member presents with objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (C6-C7 disc herniation, right C7 nerve root compression, moderate foraminal stenosis, diminished tricep reflex, grip 4+/5 weakness) that physical therapy cannot address (CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)). Furthermore, the treating physician has documented a functional plateau after 5 weeks of PT (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)). Therefore, the plan's mechanical application of the PT duration requirement is not permissible under state law.

3.  **Service Meets Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:** Despite not fully meeting every component of the plan's conservative treatment criteria (specifically, duration of a second pharmacotherapy agent and lack of an interventional procedure), the member has undergone a substantial course of conservative management (5 weeks PT, 3 months Meloxicam, 2 weeks Prednisone) for a structural condition causing persistent objective neurological deficits. According to the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023, Section 3.1, Tier 2), surgery is a reasonable option for radiculopathy with persistent symptoms and functional impairment after 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment. California law (CHSC § 1374.32(b)) mandates that a service meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice shall be approved, regardless of more restrictive internal plan criteria. The patient's persistent, objectively documented neurological deficits, despite conservative care, demonstrate medical necessity for surgical intervention under generally accepted standards.
