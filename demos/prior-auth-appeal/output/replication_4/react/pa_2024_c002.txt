**Decision:** **OVERTURNED**

**Warrant for Determination:**

This appeal reviews the denial of prior authorization for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 for Mr. David Santos. The initial denial (2024-10-05) stated that the Myelopathy exception (Plan Criteria Section 2.2A) was applied but not met, as the member's symptoms were primarily radicular, with only mild bilateral components, and myelopathy was not considered the primary diagnosis despite MRI findings of cord compression and T2 signal change.

Upon comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory framework, and generally accepted clinical standards, the denial is **OVERTURNED**.

**I. Clinical Presentation:**
Mr. Santos presents with a mixed clinical picture. While he exhibits predominant right C7 radicular symptoms (right tricep weakness 4/5, right finger numbness, 8/10 pain), the clinical record unequivocally documents significant objective findings consistent with cervical myelopathy:
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-01):** C6-C7 moderate cord compression with **T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the cord**. This is a critical objective finding.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-09-05):** Bilateral hyperreflexia (right > left), positive right Hoffman sign, equivocal left Hoffman sign, and mildly wide-based gait. These are classic upper motor neuron signs indicative of myelopathy.
*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy.

The presence of myelomalacia, cord compression, and objective upper motor neuron signs clearly establishes the diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, which is a condition with serious implications for neurological function.

**II. Conservative Treatment Review:**
The member completed a documented course of conservative treatment:
*   **Physical Therapy:** 6 weeks (18 sessions) following a myelopathy protocol. The physical therapy summary explicitly states "No improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms."
*   **Pharmacotherapy:** Pregabalin 150mg BID for 3 months (partial right-sided pain relief) and Naproxen 500mg BID for 2 months (inadequate).
*   **Interventional:** Right C7 epidural steroid injection, providing 50% right-sided relief for 3 weeks, with "No change in bilateral hyperreflexia."

In terms of duration and type, the member satisfied HealthFirst's standard conservative treatment requirements (Plan Criteria Section 2.2a, 2.2b, 2.2c). Crucially, the conservative measures provided no improvement in the objective myelopathic signs (hyperreflexia, Hoffman, gait, T2 signal change), indicating treatment failure for the myelopathic component.

**III. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria:**
HealthFirst's criteria require diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (Section 2.1b), which is met by the objective findings. The denial hinges on the "NOTE" in Section 2.2(A), which states the myelopathy exception applies "only when myelopathy is the PRIMARY diagnosis." The plan determined myelopathy was not primary due to predominant radicular symptoms.

**IV. Application of California Regulatory Framework (State Law):**
The plan's "primary diagnosis" restriction and the requirement for specific conservative treatment duration are overridden by California state law and regulatory guidance:

1.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (September 2022) — Priority 1: State Law**
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** This APL states that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent unless compelling clinical evidence supports otherwise." Mr. Santos's MRI clearly shows both cord compression and myelomalacia. The plan's internal criteria cannot supersede this regulatory mandate to treat the condition as urgent.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** For urgent conditions like myelopathy with myelomalacia, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when... (a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia...), AND (b) Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression, AND (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines." All these conditions are met: myelomalacia is present, conservative treatment failed to address myelopathic signs, and surgery is indicated (see below). The APL specifically identifies myelomalacia as supporting waiver of standard conservative requirements.
    *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for conditions identified in this guidance. Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs." The plan's "primary diagnosis" clause is more restrictive than clinical standards for managing myelopathy with myelomalacia.

2.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) (Effective 2023) — Priority 1: State Law**
    *   This law states that a plan "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... (3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
    *   Mr. Santos's MRI shows moderate cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia). This constitutes objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Therefore, the physical therapy requirement (whether 6 weeks standard or 4 weeks myelopathy exception) is specifically waived by state law. Furthermore, the PT notes indicate no improvement in bilateral neurological signs, supporting a functional plateau for the myelopathic component.

**V. Application of Clinical Standard (Generally Accepted Medical Practice) — Priority 2:**
Generally accepted clinical guidelines strongly support surgical intervention for Mr. Santos's condition and contradict the plan's conservative treatment requirements and "primary diagnosis" restriction:

1.  **AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023 Update):**
    *   **Section 1.2 Myelomalacia:** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) "indicates existing spinal cord injury/damage... Surgical urgency — cord signal change is a predictor of poor outcome if surgery is delayed." It is a "strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials."
    *   **Section 3.1 Accepted Indications for ACDF (CPT 22551):** Mr. Santos meets **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION** criteria: "(a) Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI" and "(c) Myelomalacia on MRI (cord signal change)."
    *   These guidelines state that the clinical standard does NOT require completion of a minimum course of physical therapy as a prerequisite to surgery for this presentation.

2.  **AO Spine International: Clinical Practice Guideline (2021):**
    *   **Section 2.1 Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
    *   **Section 2.3 Risk of Delay:** "Each month of delay in surgical treatment for moderate-severe myelopathy is associated with decreased likelihood of neurological recovery. Myelomalacia on preoperative MRI is the strongest predictor of incomplete neurological recovery postoperatively."

3.  **Critical Distinction for Treatment Decisions (Clinical Reference Summary):** Myelopathy and radiculopathy "require fundamentally different treatment algorithms. The evidence base for conservative treatment is primarily derived from radiculopathy studies. Application of radiculopathy conservative treatment requirements to myelopathy patients is NOT supported by clinical evidence." The plan's focus on radicular symptoms making myelopathy not "primary" inappropriately applies radiculopathy-based conservative treatment logic to a myelopathic condition.

**VI. Resolution of Conflicts:**
The plan's denial is based on its internal criteria (Section 2.2A Note) requiring myelopathy to be the *primary* diagnosis for the myelopathy exception to apply. This specific criterion is in direct conflict with both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards:
*   **State Law (DMHC APL 22-014 and CIC § 10169.5):** These explicitly override the plan's conservative treatment requirements, especially PT, when myelomalacia and objective neurological compromise from myelopathy are present. They mandate urgent consideration and waiver of full conservative treatment requirements in such cases, regardless of a "primary diagnosis" distinction which is not relevant for urgency due to spinal cord injury.
*   **Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine):** These guidelines identify myelomalacia with cord compression and objective neurological deficits as a strong indication for surgery, recommending against delayed surgery for conservative treatment. The "primary diagnosis" concept is not the determinant for urgency when objective myelopathy with myelomalacia is present.

Following the hierarchy of authority (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria), the state laws and clinical standards supersede the plan's restrictive "primary diagnosis" clause and conservative treatment requirements. The clinical evidence strongly supports the need for surgery and warns against delay given the myelomalacia.

**Conclusion:**
Mr. Santos has objective evidence of cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and cord compression, coupled with clear upper motor neuron signs and failure of conservative treatment to address these myelopathic symptoms. California state law and generally accepted clinical standards mandate the approval of surgical intervention in this circumstance, overriding the plan's internal criteria regarding "primary diagnosis" and conservative treatment duration. Delaying surgery for this patient carries a high risk of irreversible neurological deterioration.

Therefore, the prior authorization for ACDF C6-C7 is medically necessary and should be approved.

**Disposition:** **OVERTURNED**
