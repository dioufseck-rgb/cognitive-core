**1. Case Summary:**
Mr. David Santos, a 63-year-old member, requested prior authorization for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7. The request was denied by HealthFirst Health Plan on the basis that while myelopathy was noted, the member's symptoms were deemed "primarily right-sided (radicular) with only mild bilateral components," leading the plan to determine that myelopathy was not the "primary diagnosis." Consequently, the myelopathy exception to conservative treatment requirements was not applied, and the standard conservative treatment criteria were considered unmet.

The appeal argues that T2 signal change (myelomalacia) is present, which should trigger regulatory overrides, and that the presentation is a mixed myelopathy/radiculopathy, with objective myelopathic findings making the "primary diagnosis" distinction irrelevant.

**2. Clinical Record Review:**
*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy. The plan disputed which was primary.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-01):** C6-C7 moderate cord compression with T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord. Right C7 foraminal stenosis was noted as more prominent than left.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-09-05):**
    *   **Radicular findings:** Predominant right C7 distribution weakness (tricep 4/5), right finger numbness, 8/10 right-sided pain.
    *   **Myelopathic findings:** Bilateral hyperreflexia (right > left), positive right Hoffman, equivocal left Hoffman, gait mildly wide-based.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 6 weeks (18 sessions) following a "myelopathy protocol." Documented "no improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms."
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Pregabalin 150mg BID for 3 months (partial right-sided pain relief); Naproxen 500mg BID for 2 months (inadequate relief).
    *   **Interventional:** Right C7 epidural (2024-09-28) provided 50% right-sided relief, which returned in 3 weeks. No change in bilateral hyperreflexia.

**3. HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) Application:**
*   **Section 2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** The member objectively meets criteria for both cervical radiculopathy (2.1a) and cervical myelopathy (2.1b) based on MRI (cord compression, myelomalacia) and neurological exam (hyperreflexia, Hoffman, gait disturbance).
*   **Section 2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **Standard Pathway:** The member completed 6 weeks of PT, 2 pharmacotherapy agents for adequate duration, and one interventional procedure. However, the plan denied due to the "myelopathy exception" not being applied, based on its interpretation of "primary diagnosis."
    *   **Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A):** This exception reduces PT to 4 weeks and waives interventional treatment if risk is documented. The plan explicitly stated it applied the exception but deemed its criteria "not met" because "myelopathy as primary diagnosis" was not established. The plan states, "primary diagnosis remains radiculopathy."

**4. California Regulatory Framework Application:**
*   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This All-Plan Letter is highly relevant.
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** States that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent." Mr. Santos has moderate cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia) on MRI. This mandates the plan treat his condition as urgent.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** States that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when "(a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia...), AND (b) Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression, AND (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines." All three conditions are met for Mr. Santos: myelomalacia is present, conservative treatment failed to improve neurological signs, and surgery is indicated per clinical guidelines (see below). The APL specifically identifies myelomalacia as a finding supporting waiver of standard conservative treatment.
    *   **II.3 Plan Criteria Vs. Clinical Standard:** Mandates that plans "shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice." HealthFirst's "primary diagnosis" clause, when myelomalacia is present, creates such a restrictive criterion.
*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute prohibits conditioning surgical approval on PT completion where "(3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Mr. Santos' MRI showing cord compression with myelomalacia (T2 signal change) constitutes objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. His 6 weeks of PT showed "no improvement in bilateral neurological signs." This state law overrides any plan requirement for PT completion.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This section dictates that if a service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice," it "SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive."

**5. Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) Application:**
*   **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines emphasize that myelopathy and radiculopathy are fundamentally different conditions requiring different treatment algorithms. Myelopathy is characterized by spinal cord compression with upper motor neuron signs (hyperreflexia, Hoffman sign) and often gait disturbance, as seen in Mr. Santos.
*   **Myelomalacia Significance:** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) on MRI is a critical finding, indicating existing spinal cord injury. It is a strong predictor of permanent deficit if surgery is delayed, a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials, and a strong indicator for surgical intervention.
*   **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** AO Spine Guidelines (2021) explicitly state, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." The 6-week PT requirement in plan criteria is derived from radiculopathy evidence and is a category error when applied to myelopathy.
*   **Surgical Indications:** AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) identify "Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI" and "Myelomalacia on MRI" as TIER 1 strong surgical indications. Mr. Santos' clinical presentation (gait disturbance, hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman) combined with MRI findings (moderate cord compression, myelomalacia) clearly meets these strong indications.

**6. Conflict Resolution and Final Determination:**
The HealthFirst plan's denial is based on its interpretation that myelopathy is not the "primary diagnosis" despite objective myelopathic signs and, critically, the presence of myelomalacia. This triggers the plan's standard conservative treatment requirements (including 6 weeks of PT) instead of its own myelopathy exception.

However, applying the hierarchy of authority: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria**:

1.  **State Law Override:**
    *   DMHC APL 22-014 mandates that the presence of myelomalacia with cord compression requires the case to be treated as urgent and overrides the requirement for completion of a full course of conservative treatment. The plan's "primary diagnosis" clause is a restrictive internal criterion that directly conflicts with this state guidance and is thus invalid for this presentation.
    *   CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) explicitly overrides any PT requirement given the objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia and cord compression) which PT cannot address.
2.  **Clinical Standard Override:**
    *   Leading clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS, AO Spine) establish that myelomalacia with myelopathy is a strong indication for surgery, and delaying surgery for extended conservative treatment (especially PT) is not supported by evidence and carries a risk of neurological deterioration.
    *   CHSC § 1374.32(b) mandates approval for services meeting generally accepted medical standards, regardless of a plan's more restrictive criteria. The plan's insistence on a "primary diagnosis" of myelopathy to apply an exception, when objective myelopathy and myelomalacia are clearly present, is more restrictive than generally accepted standards.

Mr. Santos has significant objective evidence of cervical myelopathy (cord compression, myelomalacia, upper motor neuron signs, gait disturbance) which elevates his condition beyond purely radicular pathology. The presence of myelomalacia alone is a critical finding that warrants urgent consideration for surgical intervention and overrides standard conservative treatment prerequisites. The conservative therapy attempted yielded no improvement in his bilateral neurological signs, supporting that further delay or additional conservative measures are unlikely to be beneficial and could be detrimental.

**Disposition:** **OVERTURNED**

**Warrant:**
The denial of CPT 22551 for Mr. David Santos is hereby OVERTURNED. The HealthFirst Health Plan's denial, based on the determination that myelopathy was not the "primary diagnosis" and therefore the myelopathy exception criteria were not met, is inconsistent with established California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.

Specifically:
*   **California State Law (DMHC APL 22-014):** The presence of myelomalacia (T2 signal change) within the spinal cord, alongside cord compression, mandates that Mr. Santos' condition be treated as urgent. This APL explicitly states that plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when objective imaging shows myelomalacia and conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression, and surgery is indicated. The plan's restrictive "primary diagnosis" clause for its myelopathy exception is directly superseded by this state regulatory guidance.
*   **California State Law (CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)):** This statute prohibits conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy completion when the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. Mr. Santos' MRI demonstrates cord compression with myelomalacia, unequivocally meeting this condition. His 6 weeks of PT showed no improvement in his myelopathic signs, further supporting that PT is ineffective for this specific component of his condition.
*   **Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):** Current guidelines identify myelomalacia on MRI and moderate-to-severe myelopathy with objective cord compression as TIER 1 strong surgical indications. These guidelines specifically state that there is no high-quality evidence to support mandatory physical therapy prior to surgery for moderate to severe myelopathy and that delaying surgery in such cases may lead to neurological deterioration. The application of a rigid, radiculopathy-derived 6-week PT requirement to a patient with myelomalacia and myelopathic signs is a category error and is not in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice.

Therefore, under the hierarchy of authority, California state law and clinical standards override the HealthFirst plan's internal criteria and the basis for the original denial. The requested ACDF C6-C7 is deemed medically necessary.
