**APPEAL DETERMINATION: PRIOR AUTHORIZATION PA-2024-C004**

**Member:** Raymond Flores (M-223355)
**Plan:** HealthFirst PPO Gold — Group Plan 77441 (California)
**Procedure Requested:** CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level — prophylactic)
**Date of Denial:** 2024-10-25
**Reviewer:** Expert Health Plan Appeals Reviewer

**Disposition: OVERTURN DENIAL**

**I. Executive Summary**
The initial denial by HealthFirst was based on the absence of documented neurological deficits, radiculopathy, or myelopathy, categorizing the request as solely for axial neck pain. While the plan correctly applied its internal criteria requiring symptomatic conditions, this appeal review finds that the requested prophylactic surgery for severe cervical stenosis with cord contact, given the member's high-risk occupation and explicit risk of acute traumatic spinal cord injury, aligns with the broader definition of medical necessity under California state law, which includes preventing illness or injury based on prudent clinical judgment. The state's standard of review for medical necessity overrides the plan's more restrictive criteria in this instance.

**II. Case Background & Clinical Summary**
Raymond Flores, a 54-year-old construction worker, requested prior authorization for a prophylactic C5-C6 ACDF. Imaging (MRI dated 2024-09-15) revealed severe central canal stenosis at C5-C6 with a canal diameter of 6mm (normal >13mm) and cord contact, though without active cord compression, T2 signal change, or myelomalacia. The MRI report noted that a single trauma could cause acute cord injury. A neurological examination (2024-09-20) was completely normal, with no signs of radiculopathy, myelopathy, or upper motor neuron deficits. The member reported axial neck pain (5/10). Conservative treatment for axial pain included 8 weeks of physical therapy with modest pain improvement, a 3-month trial of Naproxen, a 6-week trial of Cyclobenzaprine, and a C5-C6 epidural steroid injection providing 60% pain relief for 6 weeks.

Dr. Karen Davis, the treating spine surgeon, explicitly requested prophylactic decompression, citing the critically narrow canal, high risk of acute spinal cord injury from minor trauma, and the member's occupation involving heavy equipment operation. The surgeon's rationale is to prevent catastrophic neurological injury.

**III. Plan's Denial Rationale (Initial Review)**
The initial denial, issued by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, cited "MED-003" and stated, "Cervical stenosis documented but no neurological deficit on examination. Member has axial neck pain only. No radiculopathy, no myelopathy documented. Surgical intervention not indicated for axial pain without neurological deficit under CC-SPINE-2024." This denial directly aligns with HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, specifically Section 3.1 (exclusion for surgery requested solely for axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy) and implicitly Section 2.1 (diagnosis requirements for symptomatic radiculopathy or myelopathy).

**IV. Review of Plan Criteria & State Law Hierarchy**

1.  **Application of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
    *   **Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement):** The member does not meet criteria 2.1(a) (cervical radiculopathy), 2.1(b) (cervical myelopathy), or 2.1(c) (cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit), as the neurological exam is normal and there are no symptomatic neurological deficits. The plan's definition of myelopathy requires *symptoms*.
    *   **Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement):** The member completed 8 weeks of PT, tried two pharmacotherapy agents, and underwent one interventional procedure, meeting these requirements for axial pain.
    *   **Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway):** This exception does not apply as myelopathy is not the *primary diagnosis* based on the absence of symptoms.
    *   **Section 3.1 (Exclusions):** Based on the plan's literal interpretation, the surgery could be considered "requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy," which would lead to denial.
    *   *Conclusion:* The plan's initial denial correctly applied its *internal criteria* given the absence of *symptomatic* neurological deficits.

2.  **Application of California Regulatory Requirements:**
    *   **CHSC § 1374.31(b) (Denial Notice Adequacy):** The denial notice was procedurally adequate, specifying the criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) and the clinical reason for denial.
    *   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** While the member completed PT, this provision mainly addresses waiving PT when it's contraindicated or ineffective for neurological issues. As there were no neurological symptoms, PT for such was not applicable. However, the procedure is requested for structural risk, not pain, thus PT is largely irrelevant to the core surgical rationale here.
    *   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This All-Plan Letter specifically addresses cases with "objective evidence of cord compression and *myelomalacia* on MRI" for urgency and waiving conservative treatment. Since the member has no myelomalacia and no symptomatic myelopathy, the specific urgency provisions of APL 22-014 do not directly apply to this case to override conservative treatment requirements.
    *   **CHSC § 1374.32(a) & (b) (IMR Standard of Review):** This is the highest authority. Medical necessity is defined as services a physician, "exercising prudent clinical judgment, would provide to a patient for the purpose of *preventing*, evaluating, diagnosing, or treating an illness, injury, disease, or its symptoms, and that are: (1) In accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice." Furthermore, the IMR standard "does NOT require that the service be consistent with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the service meets the standard in subdivision (a), the reviewer SHALL recommend approval regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive."

3.  **Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
    *   The provided clinical guidelines primarily address the management of *symptomatic* cervical radiculopathy and myelopathy. They categorize indications based on the presence and severity of *existing neurological symptoms* or *myelomalacia*.
    *   The member's presentation does not fit neatly into the typical "Tier 1 — Strong Indication" for *symptomatic* myelopathy or "Tier 2 — Reasonable Indication" for radiculopathy, as he is neurologically asymptomatic and has no myelomalacia. The criteria for Tier 3 (surgery not indicated for axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy) could be applied if one strictly adheres to a symptomatic-only approach.
    *   However, the surgeon's rationale for *prophylactic* surgery is based on extreme anatomical vulnerability (6mm canal diameter, cord contact) and high occupational trauma risk. While the provided clinical guidelines do not explicitly codify "prophylactic surgery for asymptomatic severe stenosis with cord contact in a high-risk occupation" as a Tier 1 or 2 indication, the concept of preventing catastrophic neurological injury in such a scenario *is* consistent with prudent clinical judgment and generally accepted principles of spine surgery for prevention. The 6mm canal is critically narrow (less than half normal), and the MRI explicitly notes the high risk of acute injury from trauma. The surgeon's expert opinion, particularly in light of the member's high-risk occupation, warrants consideration under the broader IMR medical necessity standard focused on prevention.

**V. Conflict Resolution & Final Determination**
A direct conflict exists between HealthFirst's internal criteria, which require symptomatic neurological deficits for surgical authorization, and the treating physician's request for prophylactic surgery based on high anatomical risk and occupational exposure, which falls under the "prevention" aspect of medical necessity as defined by California state law.

The state law hierarchy (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria) dictates that CHSC § 1374.32(a) and (b) take precedence over the plan's more restrictive criteria. The surgeon's rationale for prophylactic decompression to prevent catastrophic spinal cord injury in a patient with severe cervical stenosis (6mm canal diameter) and cord contact, exacerbated by a high-risk occupation, constitutes "prudent clinical judgment" for "preventing an illness, injury, disease" and is aligned with generally accepted standards of medical practice for managing such critical risks, even if not explicitly itemized in treatment guidelines primarily focused on *symptomatic* conditions.

Therefore, the plan's denial is overturned because it failed to appropriately apply the broader definition of medical necessity for preventive care under California state law, specifically CHSC § 1374.32(a), and its criteria are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice in this specific context (CHSC § 1374.32(b)).

**VI. Warrant**
The denial should be **OVERTURNED**.

1.  **Justification for Overturn:** The denial was based on HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Sections 2.1 and 3.1, which require documented symptomatic radiculopathy or myelopathy. While the member does not currently exhibit symptomatic myelopathy or radiculopathy, the clinical evidence demonstrates severe cervical stenosis at C5-C6 with a critically narrow canal diameter (6mm) and cord contact. The MRI report explicitly states, "Single trauma could cause acute cord injury given severely reduced canal diameter." The treating spine surgeon, Dr. Karen Davis, has provided a clear and compelling rationale for *prophylactic* decompression to "prevent catastrophic injury," citing the critically narrow canal and the member's high-risk occupation (construction worker).
2.  **Regulatory Override:** Under California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(a), medical necessity for independent medical review purposes is defined to include health care services that a physician, "exercising prudent clinical judgment, would provide... for the purpose of *preventing*, evaluating, diagnosing, or treating an illness, injury, disease, or its symptoms, and that are... In accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice." The surgeon's request for prophylactic surgery for severe, asymptomatic cord compromise in a high-risk individual to prevent acute traumatic myelopathy aligns with this definition of preventing an illness or injury and represents prudent clinical judgment.
3.  **Plan Criteria are More Restrictive:** The plan's internal criteria are more restrictive than the standard of medical necessity outlined in California law, as they do not adequately account for medically necessary prophylactic interventions aimed at preventing catastrophic neurological injury in the face of critical anatomical risk and elevated trauma exposure. CHSC § 1374.32(b) mandates that if a service meets the IMR standard, it shall be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.
