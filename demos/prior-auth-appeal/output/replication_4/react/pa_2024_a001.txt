**APPEAL DETERMINATION**

**Case ID:** PA-2024-A001
**Member:** Diana Torres, M-112233
**Procedure:** ACDF C5-C6, one level (CPT 22551)
**Denial Date:** 2024-11-01 (Initial Level)
**Reviewer:** Expert Health Plan Appeals Reviewer
**Date of Review:** 2024-11-08

**Summary of Request:**
Prior authorization was requested for an anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 for Ms. Diana Torres, a 61-year-old member with progressive cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia.

**Summary of Denial:**
HealthFirst Utilization Management denied the request, citing that the member had completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy, whereas the standard pathway requires a minimum of 6 weeks. Additionally, the denial stated that the pharmacotherapy requirement was not met, with only one agent trialed. The plan concluded the member did not meet conservative treatment requirements under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2.

**Appeal Basis:**
The appeal asserts that the member has severe myelomalacia on MRI, and California law prohibits a physical therapy requirement when structural neurological compromise is present. The treating physician, Dr. James Kim, declared PT contraindicated due to documented cord signal change. The appeal further invokes a regulatory override under CIC 10169.5(a)(3), arguing that plan criteria are irrelevant in this specific clinical context.

**Clinical Findings:**
Ms. Torres presents with a 4-month history of progressive bilateral hand clumsiness and gait instability. Neurological examination (2024-08-10) revealed objective upper motor neuron signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia in bilateral upper and lower extremities, positive Babinski bilaterally, and documented gait ataxia. MRI performed on 2024-08-15 showed severe central cord compression at C5-C6 with T2 hyperintensity within the spinal cord, indicating myelomalacia.

Conservative treatment consisted of:
*   **Physical Therapy:** Initiated 2024-09-01, discontinued 2024-09-14 after 2 weeks (6 sessions). The treating physician documented that PT was causing symptom exacerbation and declared it contraindicated, issuing a written order to discontinue.
*   **Pharmacotherapy:** A 2-week course of Methylprednisolone taper provided temporary mild improvement, but no sustained benefit. Only one pharmacotherapy agent was trialed.
*   **Interventional Treatments:** None.

Dr. Kim, the treating neurosurgeon, provided a letter dated 2024-10-15, stating that PT is contraindicated due to the presence of myelomalacia on initial MRI and that continuing PT risks irreversible cord injury. He declared that CIC 10169.5(a)(3) applies and surgery is urgently indicated.

**REVIEW AND DETERMINATION**

Applying the three-source hierarchy: **State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria**.

**1. Review Against Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
*   **Diagnosis (2.1):** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (2.1(b)) with severe central cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI, consistent with symptoms. This criterion is met.
*   **Conservative Treatment (2.2):**
    *   **Standard Pathway:** The member completed only 2 weeks of PT (versus 6 weeks required), trialed only one pharmacotherapy agent (versus two required for 4+ weeks each), and had no interventional procedures. Therefore, the member does not meet the *standard* conservative treatment requirements.
    *   **Myelopathy Exception (2.2(A)):** This exception reduces the PT requirement to a 4-week minimum and waives the interventional requirement if the physician documents risk. The pharmacotherapy requirement remains in full.
        *   Even with the myelopathy exception, the member only completed 2 weeks of PT (less than the 4-week minimum).
        *   The treating physician did not explicitly document risk for *interventional* treatment but did for *physical therapy*, citing myelomalacia and risk of neurological deterioration.
        *   The pharmacotherapy requirement (two agents for 4+ weeks each) was not met.
    *   **Conclusion (Plan Criteria):** Based *solely* on HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 criteria, the member does not fulfill the conservative treatment requirements, even with the myelopathy exception for the duration of physical therapy and pharmacotherapy agents. The initial denial accurately reflects the plan's internal criteria.

**2. Review Against California Regulatory Requirements (State Law):**
*   **CIC § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This statute directly addresses the plan's denial based on insufficient PT.
    *   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1):** Prohibits conditioning approval on PT completion where "The member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition." Dr. Kim explicitly documented PT as contraindicated due to myelomalacia and risk of irreversible cord injury. This criterion is met.
    *   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3):** Prohibits conditioning approval on PT completion where "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The MRI finding of "Severe central cord compression C5-C6. T2 hyperintensity within cord (myelomalacia)" constitutes objective evidence of structural neurological compromise. This criterion is met.
    *   **CIC § 10169.5(c):** States that a plan's minimum PT requirement "shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or whether the condition involves neurological urgency under subdivision (a)(3)." This reinforces that the plan's 4-week or 6-week rule cannot be mechanically applied here.
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 — Prior Authorization Requirements for Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions:**
    *   **II.1 — Myelopathy Classification:** Identifies "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI" as a condition with "potential for irreversible harm if untreated," requiring urgent treatment. The member's condition perfectly matches this description.
    *   **II.2 — Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** States that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia is specifically cited), the physician documents conservative treatment failure, and surgery is indicated by generally accepted guidelines. The presence of myelomalacia "may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements even where a full course has not been completed, when the treating physician documents urgency." This applies directly to Ms. Torres's case.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) — Standard of Review for IMR:** States that if a service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice," approval SHALL be recommended "regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." This provision ensures that higher clinical standards override restrictive plan criteria.

**3. Review Against Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
*   **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy (Section 1.1):** The clinical guidelines clearly distinguish myelopathy from radiculopathy, noting that the evidence base for conservative treatment (like PT) primarily applies to radiculopathy, not myelopathy. Applying radiculopathy-derived PT requirements to myelopathy is a "category error."
*   **Myelomalacia Significance (Section 1.2):** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) indicates existing spinal cord injury, higher risk of permanent deficit, reduced recovery, and signifies "surgical urgency." It is considered a "strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials."
*   **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy (Section 2.1):** For moderate-severe myelopathy, PT "does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement." For severe myelopathy, conservative treatment "is NOT recommended as primary treatment." The AO Spine Guidelines (2021) explicitly state, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
*   **Surgical Indications (Section 3.1 & 3.2):** Ms. Torres's presentation (moderate to severe myelopathy, objective cord compression, myelomalacia, and progressive neurological deficits) meets TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION for surgical intervention per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023). The clinical standard does not support requiring a minimum course of PT in this setting.

**CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND FINAL DETERMINATION**

The denial from HealthFirst PPO Gold was based on the member not meeting the conservative treatment requirements (Section 2.2) of its internal clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), specifically regarding the duration of physical therapy and the number of pharmacotherapy agents.

However, applying the hierarchy of authority: **State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria**, both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards override the plan's internal criteria in this case.

1.  **California State Law (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014)** directly prohibits plans from requiring physical therapy as a prerequisite for surgery when the treating physician documents it as contraindicated or when objective structural neurological compromise (like myelomalacia) is present. DMHC guidance further waives full conservative treatment requirements for urgent neurological conditions with myelomalacia.
2.  **Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine guidelines)** classify moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia as a strong indication for urgent surgery. These guidelines explicitly state that mandatory PT durations are not supported by evidence for this condition and that delaying surgery to complete conservative treatment is not recommended and may lead to irreversible harm.

Therefore, the plan's denial, while consistent with its internal criteria, is superseded by higher authorities.

**Disposition:** **OVERTURN**

**Warrant:**
The denial of prior authorization for ACDF C5-C6 for Ms. Diana Torres is **OVERTURNED** based on the following:

1.  **California State Law Override of Physical Therapy Requirement:**
    *   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) & (3):** Dr. James Kim, the treating physician, explicitly documented that physical therapy was contraindicated and caused symptom exacerbation, stating it risks irreversible cord injury due to myelomalacia. Furthermore, the MRI demonstrates "Severe central cord compression C5-C6" and "T2 hyperintensity within cord (myelomalacia)," constituting objective evidence of structural neurological compromise. California law unequivocally prohibits HealthFirst from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy under these circumstances (CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3)). The plan's mechanical application of its 6-week (or 4-week myelopathy exception) PT requirement is specifically disallowed by CIC § 10169.5(c).
2.  **California State Law Override of Comprehensive Conservative Treatment Requirements:**
    *   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (II.1 & II.2):** The member's condition of progressive cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified by the DMHC as an urgent condition with potential for irreversible harm. This APL mandates that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when objective imaging shows myelomalacia, the physician documents conservative treatment failure, and surgery is indicated by generally accepted guidelines. Ms. Torres's clinical presentation and imaging fulfill these criteria, thereby overriding the plan's requirements for a minimum number of pharmacotherapy agents and duration.
3.  **Clinical Standard Override of Plan Criteria:**
    *   **AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021:** Generally accepted clinical guidelines classify moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and objective neurological deficits (Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, gait ataxia) as a TIER 1 strong indication for surgical intervention. These guidelines explicitly state that there is "no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy" (AO Spine 2021) and that "delaying surgery in such patients... may result in neurological deterioration." The presence of myelomalacia is considered a "strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2). The plan's conservative treatment requirements for this condition are therefore inconsistent with evidence-based medical practice.
4.  **Hierarchy of Authority:** In cases where a health plan's internal criteria are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice or conflict with state law, the higher authority governs (CHSC § 1374.32(b), DMHC APL 22-014 II.3). Both California state law and prevailing clinical standards clearly support the waiver of the plan's conservative treatment requirements for this patient's urgent myelopathic condition.

For these reasons, the prior authorization for ACDF C5-C6 for Ms. Diana Torres is medically necessary and should be approved.
