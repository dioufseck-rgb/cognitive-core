**SUBJECT: Appeal Determination for Prior Authorization Denial of CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) for Diana Torres**

Dear Member Torres, Dr. Kim, and HealthFirst Utilization Management,

This letter serves as the official determination for the first-level appeal concerning the prior authorization denial for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 for Ms. Diana Torres. I have thoroughly reviewed all submitted documentation, including the initial denial, the appeal basis, the member's clinical record, HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), relevant California regulatory framework (CIC, CHSC, DMHC APL), and generally accepted clinical standards.

---

**CLINICAL PRESENTATION SUMMARY:**

Ms. Diana Torres, a 61-year-old female, presents with a 4-month history of progressive cervical myelopathy at C5-C6. Her symptoms include bilateral hand clumsiness and gait instability. Neurological examination reveals significant upper motor neuron signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia in all four extremities, positive bilateral Babinski reflex, and documented gait ataxia. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) from 2024-08-15 confirms severe central spinal cord compression at C5-C6 with objective evidence of T2 hyperintensity within the cord, indicating **myelomalacia**.

Conservative treatment consisted of 2 weeks (6 sessions) of physical therapy, which was discontinued by the treating physician due to symptom exacerbation and the documented presence of myelomalacia, making further PT contraindicated. Pharmacotherapy included a 2-week course of Methylprednisolone taper, providing only temporary mild improvement without sustained benefit. No interventional procedures were attempted.

---

**HEALTHFIRST'S INITIAL DENIAL REASONING:**

The initial denial, dated 2024-11-01, cited reason code MED-003, stating that Ms. Torres had completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy (whereas the standard pathway requires a minimum of 6 weeks) and had trialed only one pharmacotherapy agent, failing to meet the conservative treatment requirements under HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2.

---

**MEMBER'S APPEAL BASIS:**

The appeal highlights that Ms. Torres has severe myelomalacia on MRI, which, under California law (CIC 10169.5(a)(3)), prohibits a physical therapy requirement when structural neurological compromise is present. The treating physician, Dr. Kim, further declared PT contraindicated due to the documented cord signal change, citing regulatory override under CIC 10169.5(a)(3), making plan criteria irrelevant.

---

**ANALYSIS AND DETERMINATION:**

My review applies the established hierarchy of authority: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria**.

1.  **Application of HealthFirst's Clinical Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
    *   **Diagnosis (Section 2.1):** Ms. Torres meets criterion 2.1(b) for confirmed cervical myelopathy, with objective imaging and neurological signs.
    *   **Conservative Treatment (Section 2.2):**
        *   **Standard Pathway:** The member does not meet the standard 6-week PT, two pharmacotherapy agents for 4+ weeks, or one interventional procedure requirements.
        *   **Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A):** Even under this exception, the plan requires a minimum of 4 weeks of PT. Ms. Torres completed only 2 weeks. The pharmacotherapy requirement remains in full, which was not met (one agent for 2 weeks). While Dr. Kim's declaration for PT contraindication might implicitly extend to interventional treatments, the plan's criteria for pharmacotherapy remains unfulfilled by its strict terms.
    *   **Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** Based *solely* on the explicit language of HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024, the initial denial was technically consistent with the plan's internal conservative treatment requirements for both physical therapy duration and pharmacotherapy agents. However, the subsequent analysis will determine if these criteria can legally or clinically be applied in this specific case.

2.  **Application of California Regulatory Framework:**
    *   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This state law directly addresses the plan's denial based on insufficient physical therapy.
        *   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1):** Prohibits conditioning approval on PT completion where the physician documents it is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit. Dr. Kim explicitly documented that PT was contraindicated due to myelomalacia and risked irreversible cord injury. This criterion is **MET**.
        *   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3):** Prohibits conditioning approval on PT completion where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. The MRI evidence of severe central cord compression with **myelomalacia** constitutes objective structural neurological compromise. This criterion is **MET**.
        *   **CIC § 10169.5(c):** Explicitly states that a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to neurological urgency under subdivision (a)(3).
        *   **Conclusion on CIC § 10169.5:** California law **explicitly prohibits** the plan from denying this surgery based on the failure to complete a minimum duration of physical therapy. The presence of myelomalacia and the treating physician's declaration of contraindication trigger this regulatory override.
    *   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 — Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions:** This guidance letter is highly relevant to Ms. Torres's case.
        *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** States that cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated and **SHALL be treated as urgent**. This perfectly describes Ms. Torres's condition.
        *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** States that for such urgent conditions, plans **SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment** when objective imaging (myelomalacia specifically identified) shows progressive pathology, the treating physician documents conservative failure, and surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. Ms. Torres's myelomalacia, documented symptom exacerbation during PT, and Dr. Kim's declaration meet these criteria.
        *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** Mandates that plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards.
        *   **Conclusion on DMHC APL 22-014:** This APL directly mandates the waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements, including pharmacotherapy, in cases of myelopathy with myelomalacia where urgency is documented. The plan's conservative treatment requirements, as applied, are in direct conflict with this regulatory guidance.
    *   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) — Standard of Review:** Reinforces that medical necessity for services is based on generally accepted standards of medical practice, not solely on a plan's more restrictive internal criteria.

3.  **Application of Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
    *   **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** The clinical evidence clearly distinguishes cervical myelopathy from radiculopathy, emphasizing that they have fundamentally different treatment algorithms. Applying radiculopathy-derived conservative treatment protocols to myelopathy patients is a category error unsupported by evidence.
    *   **Myelomalacia Significance:** The clinical standards uniformly identify myelomalacia (T2 signal change) as a strong indicator of existing spinal cord injury, a predictor of permanent deficit if surgery is delayed, and a strong surgical indication. It is considered a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials.
    *   **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:**
        *   AANS/CNS (2023) and AO Spine (2021) guidelines state that for moderate to severe myelopathy (Ms. Torres's presentation), there is no high-quality evidence supporting mandatory physical therapy prior to surgery. Delaying surgery in such cases is not supported by evidence and may result in neurological deterioration.
        *   The Cochrane Review (2021) found that PT does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement for moderate-severe myelopathy.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy for Myelopathy:** While pharmacotherapy may be used for symptom management, no major clinical guideline mandates a specific trial of two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each as a *prerequisite* for surgical intervention in cases of moderate-to-severe myelopathy with myelomalacia. The primary and most effective treatment is surgical decompression.
    *   **Surgical Indication:** Ms. Torres's presentation of moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression, upper motor neuron signs, and myelomalacia on MRI meets the **TIER 1 strong surgical indication** criteria per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023).
    *   **Conclusion on Clinical Standards:** Generally accepted clinical standards unequivocally support urgent surgical intervention for Ms. Torres's condition and do not support mandatory, prolonged conservative treatment, especially physical therapy, when myelomalacia is present.

---

**CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND DISPOSITION:**

The plan's denial, based on Ms. Torres's failure to meet the conservative treatment requirements (physical therapy duration and pharmacotherapy agents) as outlined in CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, directly conflicts with higher authorities: California State Law and generally accepted Clinical Standards.

1.  **Physical Therapy Requirement:** California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3) explicitly prohibit the plan from requiring further physical therapy. The treating physician documented PT contraindication due to myelomalacia, and objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) is present. This state law **overrides** HealthFirst's internal criteria for PT duration.
2.  **Pharmacotherapy Requirement:** DMHC APL 22-014 mandates that for urgent conditions like myelopathy with myelomalacia, plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment. This regulatory guidance, supported by clinical standards that do not mandate specific pharmacotherapy trials as a prerequisite for surgery in this urgent setting, **overrides** HealthFirst's internal criteria for pharmacotherapy.
3.  **Overall Medical Necessity:** Given the severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and progressive neurological deficits, surgery (ACDF C5-C6) is a Tier 1 strong indication under generally accepted clinical guidelines. Delaying surgery for further conservative treatment trials is not supported by evidence and carries a risk of irreversible neurological deterioration.

Therefore, the plan's internal criteria, as applied in this denial, are more restrictive than both California state law and generally accepted standards of medical practice. Under the established hierarchy, the state law and clinical standards govern.

---

**DETERMINATION:**

The denial for prior authorization of CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) for Diana Torres (Case ID: PA-2024-A001) is **OVERTURNED**.

**WARRANT:**
The denial is overturned because HealthFirst's application of its CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 conservative treatment requirements conflicts with California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice. Specifically:
*   California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3) prohibit requiring physical therapy completion when the treating physician declares it contraindicated and when objective structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) is present, both of which are met in this case.
*   DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 mandates that plans waive full conservative treatment requirements for urgent myelopathy with myelomalacia where surgical intervention is indicated.
*   Generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021) classify Ms. Torres's condition (moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and objective neurological deficits) as a TIER 1 strong surgical indication, explicitly advising against delaying surgery for mandatory conservative treatment trials due to the risk of irreversible neurological deterioration.
*   The service requested meets the definition of medical necessity under CHSC § 1374.32(a) as it is in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice and clinically appropriate.
