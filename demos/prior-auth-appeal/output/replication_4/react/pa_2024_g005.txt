**APPEAL REVIEW FINDINGS**

Upon comprehensive review of the submitted appeal, the clinical record, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, California State Law (CIC, CHSC, DMHC APL), and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine), the following determinations are made:

**1. Primary Clinical Presentation:**
The member's presenting diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy C6-C7 with functional impairment. The MRI findings of "large disc herniation with moderate left C7 nerve root compression" and neurological exam findings of "Left C7 dermatomal numbness. Tricep reflex diminished left. Tricep weakness 4/5 left" are consistent with radiculopathy. Importantly, the MRI explicitly states "No cord contact. No myelomalacia." Based on these findings and the distinction outlined in the Clinical Reference Summary (Section 1.1), this case is classified as **Cervical Radiculopathy**, not Cervical Myelopathy. Therefore, criteria specific to myelopathy (including the plan's myelopathy exception and DMHC APL 22-014 regarding myelomalacia) are not directly applicable.

**2. Review of Denial Ground 1: Physical Therapy Shortfall (5 weeks vs. 6 required)**

*   **Factual Inaccuracy:** The original denial stated the member completed 5 weeks of physical therapy (PT). However, the clinical record definitively documents that the member completed **7 weeks of supervised physical therapy**, consisting of 21 sessions, from 2024-07-01 through 2024-08-16. (Source: Clinical record, "physical_therapy_summary"). The denial's premise of a PT shortfall is factually incorrect.
*   **Compliance with Plan Criteria:** HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a), requires a "minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy." The member's documented 7 weeks of PT exceeds this requirement. (Source: HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a)).
*   **Compliance with State Law (CIC § 10169.5):** Even if the 6-week minimum had not been met, California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2) prohibits a health insurer from denying a surgical procedure for insufficient PT if "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." The clinical record confirms that a functional plateau was documented at discharge: "'no further functional improvement expected.'" (Source: Clinical record, "physical_therapy_summary"). Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(c) states that a plan's minimum PT weeks requirement "shall not be applied mechanically."
*   **Compliance with Clinical Standards:** For radiculopathy, generally accepted clinical guidelines recommend conservative treatment for 6-12 weeks. The member's 7 weeks of PT, alongside other conservative modalities, with documented functional plateau and persistent symptoms, is consistent with this standard. (Source: Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.1, TIER 2(b)).

**Conclusion on Ground 1:** The denial based on a physical therapy shortfall is **invalid**. The member fully met and exceeded the plan's PT requirement, and the denial contained a factual error regarding the duration of therapy. Moreover, state law would supersede a mechanical application of a PT duration where a functional plateau has been reached.

**3. Review of Denial Ground 2: Poorly Controlled Type 2 Diabetes (HbA1c 8.2%)**

*   **Non-Criterion in Plan Policy:** HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 (Source Document 1) comprehensively outlines medical necessity criteria (Section 2) and exclusions (Section 3) for cervical spinal surgery. This document **does not include any criterion** related to diabetes control (e.g., specific HbA1c levels) as a condition for authorization or denial of ACDF.
*   **Defective Denial (CHSC § 1374.31(b)):** Citing a reason for denial that is not a component of the plan's own published clinical criteria constitutes a failure to "specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial" *within the framework of its own policy*. This renders the denial defective in part. (Source: California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b)).
*   **Perioperative Management:** As noted by the treating surgeon, Dr. Diallo, diabetes management is a critical aspect of perioperative planning and risk mitigation, not a prerequisite for prior authorization of the procedure itself when not specified in the plan's medical necessity criteria. Such medical optimization is the responsibility of the surgical and medical team.

**Conclusion on Ground 2:** The denial based on poorly controlled Type 2 Diabetes is **invalid**. This criterion is not specified in HealthFirst's medical necessity policy for ACDF, and therefore cannot be a valid basis for denial.

**4. Overall Assessment of Medical Necessity and Plan Criteria Compliance:**

*   **Diagnosis:** Member meets criteria 2.1(a) and 2.1(c) for cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficit.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **PT:** 7 weeks completed, meeting 2.2(a).
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Pregabalin (5 months) and Duloxetine (3 months) meet 2.2(b).
    *   **Interventional:** Left C7 epidural meets 2.2(c).
    *   All conservative treatment requirements under HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 (for radiculopathy) are met.
*   **Imaging:** Current MRI (2024-09-20) demonstrates C6-C7 large disc herniation with moderate left C7 nerve root compression, consistent with symptoms, meeting 2.3.
*   **Physician Documentation:** The request includes clinical rationale, functional limitations, and attestation of conservative treatment completion, meeting the spirit of 2.4.
*   **Clinical Standards:** The member's persistent radiculopathy and functional impairment despite an adequate trial of conservative care align with Tier 2 indications for surgery per AANS/CNS guidelines. (Source: Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.1).

---

**DISPOSITION**

Based on the factual inaccuracies in the original denial, the member's full compliance with HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria for Cervical Radiculopathy, the overriding force of California state law regarding physical therapy requirements, and the absence of diabetes control as a valid criterion in the plan's policy, the denial for prior authorization of ACDF C6-C7 for Gerald Obi (M-G005) is hereby:

**OVERTURNED.**

The requested ACDF C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is determined to be medically necessary and should be authorized.

**Warrant:**
The denial was based on two grounds, both of which are found to be invalid. The first ground, a shortfall in physical therapy duration, is contradicted by the clinical record which clearly shows 7 weeks of PT completed, exceeding the plan's 6-week requirement (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a)). Furthermore, the documentation of a functional plateau (Clinical record, "physical_therapy_summary") would, under California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2) and (c), preclude a denial based on a mechanical application of PT duration regardless of the exact number of weeks. The second ground, poorly controlled Type 2 Diabetes, is not a listed medical necessity criterion or exclusion in HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 for cervical spinal surgery. A denial cannot be based on criteria not present in the plan's own policy, making this aspect of the denial procedurally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b). The member meets all applicable plan criteria and generally accepted clinical standards for surgical intervention for cervical radiculopathy.
