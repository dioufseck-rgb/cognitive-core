**APPEAL DETERMINATION LETTER**

**Case ID:** PA-2024-G005
**Member:** Gerald Obi, ID: M-G005
**Procedure:** Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C6-C7 (CPT 22551)
**Date of Initial Denial:** 2024-11-08
**Appeal Review Date:** [Current Date]
**Reviewer:** Expert Health Plan Appeals Reviewer

**Disposition:** **OVERTURNED**

**Warrant:**

This appeal review carefully considered the member's clinical record, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), relevant California state law (CIC, CHSC, DMHC APL), and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine). Based on this comprehensive review and applying the three-source hierarchy of State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria, the initial denial is hereby OVERTURNED.

The denial was issued on two grounds:
1.  **Failure to meet conservative treatment requirements (Physical Therapy shortfall):** The denial stated the member completed 5 weeks of PT when 6 weeks were required.
2.  **Poorly controlled Type 2 Diabetes:** Citing perioperative risk and wound healing concerns due to an HbA1c of 8.2%.

Each ground for denial is addressed below:

**Ground 1: Physical Therapy Shortfall – Invalid**

1.  **Factual Inaccuracy by HealthFirst:** The denial claimed the member completed only 5 weeks of physical therapy. However, the clinical record clearly documents **7 weeks** of supervised physical therapy, with 21 sessions completed from 2024-07-01 through 2024-08-16. This exceeds HealthFirst's own minimum requirement of 6 weeks as outlined in CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a). Therefore, the plan's denial premise regarding PT duration is factually incorrect.
2.  **State Law Override – Functional Plateau:** Even if the PT duration was disputed, California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2) explicitly states that a health insurer cannot condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where "the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." The clinical record confirms that after 7 weeks of PT, a functional plateau was documented at discharge with "no further functional improvement expected."
3.  **State Law Override – Structural Neurological Compromise:** Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) provides for a waiver of PT requirements if "the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The MRI findings of a C6-C7 large disc herniation with moderate left C7 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis, coupled with objective neurological deficits (left C7 dermatomal numbness, diminished tricep reflex, 4/5 tricep weakness) constitute such structural neurological compromise. Physical therapy, while helpful for symptom management, cannot resolve the underlying structural compression.
4.  **Mechanical Application of Criteria:** CIC § 10169.5(c) prohibits plans from applying minimum PT requirements "mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or whether the condition involves neurological urgency." The plan's denial failed to consider the documented functional plateau and structural nature of the condition, making its application of the PT criterion unlawful.

**Ground 2: Poorly Controlled Type 2 Diabetes – Invalid**

1.  **Not a Plan Criterion:** HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery (CC-SPINE-2024) outlines specific medical necessity criteria for ACDF (Sections 2.1-2.4) and exclusions (Section 3). Diabetes control (HbA1c level) is **not** listed as a criterion for authorization or an exclusion for denial anywhere within this policy. Denying coverage based on a criterion not present in its own published policy is arbitrary and inconsistent.
2.  **Perioperative Risk vs. Medical Necessity:** While an HbA1c of 8.2% certainly represents a perioperative risk, as noted by the member's endocrinologist, this is a matter for the surgical team to manage preoperatively to ensure patient safety and optimize outcomes. The treating surgeon, Dr. Diallo, has acknowledged this, stating "Diabetes management will be optimized preoperatively. This is standard perioperative protocol." Generally accepted clinical guidelines for spinal surgery (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) focus on the medical necessity of the procedure for the spinal condition itself, not on the management of comorbidities as a prior authorization criterion.
3.  **State Law Reinforcement:** California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) stipulates that if a service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, it "SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." Denying a procedure based on a factor not included in the plan's criteria or generally accepted clinical guidelines effectively imposes an unstated and overly restrictive condition.

**Overall Medical Necessity:**

The member's clinical presentation, confirmed as cervical radiculopathy C6-C7 with objective neurological deficits (weakness 4/5, diminished reflex) and MRI demonstrating large disc herniation with moderate nerve root compression, meets the diagnostic requirements of CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1(a) and 2.1(c). The member has completed a comprehensive trial of conservative treatment including 7 weeks of PT with documented functional plateau, two pharmacotherapy agents for adequate durations (Pregabalin for 5 months, Duloxetine for 3 months), and an interventional procedure (Left C7 epidural with temporary relief). This fully satisfies HealthFirst's conservative treatment requirements under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2.

The clinical standard, per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), identifies radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment and significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care as a Tier 2 "reasonable indication" for surgery. The member's persistent symptoms and functional limitations (working reduced hours due to pain and hand weakness) after an extensive conservative trial align with this standard. The "Myelopathy Exception" (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A)) and DMHC APL 22-014 regarding myelomalacia are not applicable here, as the MRI specifically notes "No cord contact. No myelomalacia," confirming radiculopathy as the primary diagnosis, not myelopathy. However, the fact that this is *radiculopathy* reinforces that the standard conservative treatment pathway applies, which the member *met*.

**Conclusion:**

The initial denial was based on a factual error regarding PT duration and an impermissible application of a non-criterion (diabetes control). Furthermore, the denial regarding physical therapy violates specific California state law provisions designed to protect members from mechanical application of conservative treatment requirements when a functional plateau has been reached or structural neurological compromise exists. The requested ACDF is medically necessary, meets the plan's own criteria, and aligns with generally accepted clinical standards for cervical radiculopathy refractory to conservative management.

Therefore, the prior authorization for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C6-C7, one level) for Gerald Obi is **OVERTURNED**, and the procedure is approved.
