**APPEAL DISPOSITION: OVERTURN DENIAL**

---

**WARRANT FOR DISPOSITION:**

The initial prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 was denied by HealthFirst on the basis that the member had not completed the conservative treatment requirements outlined in HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. Specifically, the denial cited 0 weeks of physical therapy, insufficient pharmacotherapy, and no interventional procedures.

Upon review of the submitted appeal and the clinical record, applying the hierarchy of authority (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria), the denial is determined to be inconsistent with California State Law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice. Therefore, the denial must be OVERTURNED.

**Detailed Findings and Rationale:**

1.  **Clinical Presentation and Diagnosis:**
    *   The clinical record clearly documents Robert Chang's presentation with acute cervical myelopathy, characterized by rapidly progressive deficits (acute onset bilateral arm weakness 2/5, bilateral leg weakness 3/5, hyperreflexia, bilateral Babinski, urinary urgency – consistent with incomplete spinal cord syndrome).
    *   Emergency MRI on 2024-11-10 revealed severe C5-C6 central cord compression with extensive T2 hyperintensity throughout the cord (extensive myelomalacia).
    *   **Conclusion:** This is a confirmed diagnosis of acute, severe cervical myelopathy with objective neurological compromise and myelomalacia, which is a surgical emergency. This condition meets HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1(b) for cervical myelopathy as a primary diagnosis.

2.  **Application of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
    *   The plan's denial correctly identified that the member had not completed the standard 6 weeks of physical therapy, a trial of two pharmacotherapy agents for 4+ weeks each, or an interventional procedure as per CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2.
    *   Even applying the Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A), the member did not complete the reduced 4-week PT requirement or the full pharmacotherapy requirement. The interventional waiver might apply, but does not resolve the other unmet criteria under the plan's policy.
    *   **Initial Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** Based *solely* on a mechanical application of the plan's criteria, the denial might appear justified. However, the hierarchy of authority requires review against state law and clinical standards.

3.  **Application of California State Law and Regulations:**
    *   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):**
        *   This state law directly prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy under specific circumstances. Two provisions are directly applicable here:
            *   **§ 10169.5(a)(1):** "The member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition." Dr. Marchetti explicitly stated, "PT is absolutely contraindicated and would be dangerous. Initiating PT would risk converting incomplete cord syndrome to complete cord syndrome." This meets the criteria.
            *   **§ 10169.5(a)(3):** "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The MRI findings of "Severe C5-C6 central cord compression" and "Extensive T2 hyperintensity throughout cord (extensive myelomalacia)" unequivocally represent objective structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot resolve or reverse.
        *   **Conclusion on PT:** Pursuant to CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3), HealthFirst is legally prohibited from requiring completion of physical therapy as a prerequisite for this surgery. This state law *overrides* the plan's 4-6 week PT requirement.

    *   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):**
        *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** This APL states that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent unless compelling clinical evidence supports otherwise." The member's condition perfectly fits this definition and is an urgent case.
        *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** For conditions where delay of treatment risks irreversible harm (like this case), plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment... when: (a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia...); AND (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines..." The member's MRI shows "extensive myelomalacia" (cord signal change), and surgery is clinically indicated (see below). The APL specifically notes that "The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements even where a full course has not been completed, when the treating physician documents urgency." Dr. Marchetti has documented the urgency and contraindication to conservative care.
        *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** This APL reinforces that "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for conditions identified in this guidance. Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs."
        *   **Conclusion on Conservative Treatment:** DMHC APL 22-014 mandates that HealthFirst waive its standard conservative treatment requirements (including pharmacotherapy and interventional procedures) in this urgent case of myelopathy with myelomalacia. The acute administration of IV methylprednisolone is appropriate initial acute care.

    *   **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):**
        *   This provision states that services meeting "generally accepted standards of medical practice" SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. This provides the legal basis to overturn the denial if clinical standards support the surgery.

4.  **Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
    *   **Myelomalacia Significance:** Clinical guidelines uniformly identify myelomalacia (T2 signal change on MRI) as indicating existing spinal cord injury, higher risk of permanent deficit with continued compression, and reduced likelihood of recovery if surgery is delayed. It is considered a strong surgical indication and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2).
    *   **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** The AO Spine Guidelines (2021) state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1). The patient's condition falls into the moderate-to-severe category, further underscoring the clinical contraindication to PT.
    *   **Surgical Indications (TIER 1 - Strong Indication):** The AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) identify multiple TIER 1 strong indications for surgery that apply to this member: (a) Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI, (c) Myelomalacia on MRI, and (d) Acute severe myelopathy with risk of permanent deficit (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.1).
    *   **Risk of Delay:** Clinical evidence highlights that delaying surgical treatment for moderate-severe myelopathy, especially with myelomalacia, is associated with decreased neurological recovery (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.3).
    *   **Conclusion on Clinical Standard:** The requested ACDF is medically necessary and strongly indicated under generally accepted standards of medical practice for acute, severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and rapidly progressive deficits. Delaying surgery for conservative treatment is not only unsupported by evidence but is explicitly contraindicated by clinical guidelines due to the high risk of irreversible neurological harm.

**Conflict Resolution and Final Decision:**

The HealthFirst denial was based on internal plan criteria regarding conservative treatment. However, both California state law (CIC § 10169.5) and state regulation (DMHC APL 22-014) explicitly override these criteria in cases like Robert Chang's, where physical therapy is contraindicated due to neurological compromise and urgent myelopathy with myelomalacia warrants waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements. Furthermore, generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) strongly support immediate surgical intervention for this presentation and advise against delaying surgery for conservative trials.

Applying the hierarchy of authority, State Law and Clinical Standard take precedence over the HealthFirst plan criteria. The denial for lack of conservative treatment is in direct conflict with these higher authorities.

**Therefore, the prior authorization denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) for Robert Chang is OVERTURNED.** The requested service is determined to be medically necessary.
