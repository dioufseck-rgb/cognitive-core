**I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

The denial for your requested ACDF at C5-C6 is **OVERTURNED**.

The initial denial was based on a misinterpretation of HealthFirst's own clinical criteria, stating that "Member's symptoms have been present for only 6 weeks" and that "Plan criteria require at least 6 weeks of symptoms before surgical consideration." HealthFirst's policy, CC-SPINE-2024, specifies a minimum of 6 weeks of *physical therapy*, which you demonstrably completed. Furthermore, California state law (CIC § 10169.5) explicitly prohibits conditioning surgical approval on further physical therapy once a functional plateau has been reached, which is well-documented in your case.

---

**II. CASE OVERVIEW**

*   **Member Presentation:** Mr. Kevin Watkins, a 41-year-old professional violinist, presents with acute cervical radiculopathy at C5-C6, resulting in right C6 dermatomal numbness, diminished bicep reflex, 4/5 grip strength, and inability to perform violin fingering tasks, significantly impacting his livelihood.
*   **Imaging:** MRI dated 2024-10-01 shows a C5-C6 large disc herniation with moderate right C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis. Importantly, the MRI findings explicitly state "No cord contact. No myelomalacia."
*   **Conservative Treatment:** Over the past 6 weeks, Mr. Watkins completed:
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 6 weeks of intensive PT (18 sessions), with functional plateau documented at 6 weeks and no improvement in fine motor deficits or radicular symptoms. Occupational therapy confirms inability to perform professional duties.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** 6 weeks of Methylprednisolone (temporary partial relief) and 5 weeks of Gabapentin (partial pain relief, no motor improvement).
    *   **Interventional:** One Right C6 epidural steroid injection (2024-10-28) providing only 25% temporary relief, no motor improvement, and returned to baseline within 2 weeks.
*   **Original Denial Reason:** "MED-003: Member meets conservative treatment requirements. However, surgical request is premature. Member's symptoms have been present for only 6 weeks. Plan criteria require at least 6 weeks of symptoms before surgical consideration. Recent onset radiculopathy has a high rate of spontaneous resolution."

---

**III. REVIEWER'S ANALYSIS**

**1. Primary Clinical Presentation:**
Based on the clinical record, including MRI findings ("No cord contact. No myelomalacia.") and neurological exam findings (unilateral C6 dermatomal symptoms, no upper motor neuron signs), Mr. Watkins' primary diagnosis is **Cervical Radiculopathy**, not Cervical Myelopathy. This distinction is crucial as treatment algorithms and conservative treatment requirements differ significantly for these conditions.

**2. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

*   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** Met. Confirmed diagnosis of Cervical Radiculopathy (2.1(a)) and Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit (2.1(c)) supported by MRI and neurological exam.
*   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **(a) Physical Therapy:** Met. "minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy." Mr. Watkins completed 6 weeks of intensive PT.
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** Met. "trial of at least two... for minimum 4 weeks each." Mr. Watkins completed 6 weeks of Methylprednisolone and 5 weeks of Gabapentin.
    *   **(c) Interventional Treatment:** Met. "One... interventional treatment." Mr. Watkins received a Right C6 epidural steroid injection.
*   **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY:** Not applicable, as Mr. Watkins' diagnosis is radiculopathy, not myelopathy.
*   **2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:** Met. Current MRI (2024-10-01) demonstrates structural pathology consistent with symptoms.
*   **3.0 EXCLUSIONS:** The original denial cited conservative treatment not being met; however, based on the above, all specified requirements of Section 2.2 *were met*.

**Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The member **meets all applicable criteria** outlined in HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 for cervical spinal surgery. The original denial's reasoning that "Plan criteria require at least 6 weeks of symptoms before surgical consideration" is inaccurate. HealthFirst's policy requires 6 weeks of *physical therapy*, which was fulfilled. The policy does not specify a minimum duration of *symptoms*. The plan's denial is based on a misapplication of its own criteria.

**3. Application of California Regulatory Requirements:**

*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This state law explicitly states that a health insurer cannot condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where: "(2) The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
    *   Mr. Watkins completed 6 weeks of intensive PT, and a functional plateau was explicitly documented, with no improvement in his fine motor deficits or radicular symptoms. The occupational therapist confirmed his inability to perform professional duties. This directly falls under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), overriding any implicit or explicit requirement for further physical therapy.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard is NOT Plan Criteria):** This regulation mandates that the Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard requires approval if the service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice," regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. This provision emphasizes that clinical judgment and established guidelines take precedence over overly rigid plan policies.
*   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** While this APL primarily addresses myelopathy, Section II.3 states: "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for conditions identified in this guidance. Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs." This general principle supports prioritizing evidence-based clinical standards over restrictive plan criteria.

**4. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

*   **Conservative Treatment for Radiculopathy:** While clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, Tier 2) often suggest "6-12 weeks conservative tx" for radiculopathy, they also indicate surgery is a "reasonable option" for "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care."
*   **Functional Plateau:** The clinical standard defines a functional plateau as "therapist documentation that patient has reached maximum therapeutic benefit." Mr. Watkins' clinical record explicitly documents a functional plateau after 6 weeks of intensive PT, with no improvement in fine motor deficits or radicular symptoms, and the inability to perform his professional duties.
*   **Occupational Impact:** For a professional violinist, right hand weakness is "career-ending." The severe and sustained functional impairment, despite a robust 6-week trial of conservative treatment that reached a documented plateau, aligns with a reasonable indication for surgical intervention under generally accepted standards of medical practice. Delaying surgery further in this context is unlikely to yield better results and risks prolonged and potentially irreversible career-ending impairment.

**5. Hierarchy Resolution:**

*   **Plan Error:** The plan's denial was based on an inaccurate interpretation of its own policy regarding the duration of symptoms versus the duration of physical therapy. Mr. Watkins *met* the actual 6-week physical therapy requirement.
*   **State Law Override:** Even if the plan criteria were somehow interpreted to require further conservative treatment, California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2) explicitly mandates approval when a functional plateau has been reached after a course of physical therapy, as clearly documented in Mr. Watkins' case.
*   **Clinical Standard:** The extensive and intensive conservative treatment, coupled with a documented functional plateau and severe functional impairment (career-ending), indicates that surgical intervention is consistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice for cervical radiculopathy, especially given the protections afforded by state law regarding functional plateau.

---

**IV. CONCLUSION**

The HealthFirst prior authorization denial for Mr. Kevin Watkins' ACDF C5-C6 is **OVERTURNED**.

The denial was procedurally flawed due to a misapplication of HealthFirst's own clinical criteria and is in direct conflict with California state law regarding physical therapy requirements and functional plateaus. Furthermore, the clinical evidence supports surgical intervention given the documented failure of intensive conservative treatment, the achievement of a functional plateau, and the severe, career-ending functional impairment experienced by the member.

HealthFirst Health Plan is directed to approve the prior authorization for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level) for Kevin Watkins at Stanford Medical Center.
