**I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

This appeal reviews the denial for anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 for Patricia Osei. The original denial was based on the member not meeting conservative treatment requirements as per HealthFirst's Section 2.2 criteria, specifically regarding the duration of physical therapy and completion of an interventional procedure.

Upon comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria, relevant California state regulations, and generally accepted clinical standards, it is determined that the denial should be **OVERTURNED**. The member's primary diagnosis of progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia warrants the application of specific exceptions within HealthFirst's criteria, and more critically, triggers state laws and clinical standards that override standard conservative treatment requirements.

---

**II. CLINICAL REVIEW FINDINGS**

**Presenting Diagnosis:** Progressive cervical myelopathy C4-C5 with myelomalacia.
**Clinical Presentation:**
*   **Imaging:** MRI (2024-08-20) performed with pacemaker-compatible protocol shows C4-C5 severe central cord compression with T2 signal change (myelomalacia) confirming progressive cord injury.
*   **Neurological Exam:** (2024-08-25) findings include bilateral lower extremity spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor bilateral hands, and positive Lhermitte sign \u2014 all consistent with upper motor neuron signs of cervical myelopathy.
*   **Comorbidities:** Permanent pacemaker (implanted 2019), Type 2 diabetes, Hypertension.

**Conservative Treatment History:**
*   **Physical Therapy:** 5 weeks completed (15 sessions). Stopped due to pacemaker precautions limiting certain modalities and physician determination that further PT was not addressing neurological progression.
*   **Pharmacotherapy:** Baclofen 10mg TID for 3 months (partial spasticity relief); Methylprednisolone taper (10 days x2, temporary improvement only).
*   **Interventional Treatment:** Not performed. Documented as **CONTRAINDICATED** due to the member's permanent pacemaker. Fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injection was advised against by anesthesiology (Dr. Rivera, 2024-09-10), with documentation in the chart.

**III. APPEAL BASIS**

The appeal asserts three key points:

**IV. APPLICABLE CRITERIA AND ANALYSIS**

This review applies the hierarchy: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.**

**A. HealthFirst Plan Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

1.  **Diagnosis (Section 2.1):** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (2.1(b)) with objective neurological deficits and MRI evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia. **MET.**
2.  **Conservative Treatment (Section 2.2):**
    *   **Original Denial Reason:** The denial cited failure to meet Section 2.2 requirements due to 5/6 weeks PT and no CESI.
    *   **Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2(A)):** The member's primary diagnosis is cervical myelopathy, which triggers Exception 2.2(A):
        *   **Physical Therapy:** Requirement is reduced to 4 weeks minimum. The member completed 5 weeks. **MET.**
        *   **Interventional Treatment:** Waived if treating physician documents risk of neurological deterioration. The treating physician documented that CESI is contraindicated due to the member's pacemaker, which presents a clear medical risk. **MET.**
        *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Requirement remains in full. The member trialed Baclofen (3 months) and Methylprednisolone (10 days x2), meeting the requirement for two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. **MET.**
    *   **Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The initial denial failed to correctly apply HealthFirst's own "Myelopathy Pathway" exception (Section 2.2(A)). When this exception is correctly applied, the member **DOES MEET** the plan's criteria for conservative treatment.

**B. California State Regulatory Framework:**

1.  **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute prohibits a health plan from conditioning surgical approval on PT completion where:
    *   **(a)(1) Physician documents PT contraindicated or unlikely to benefit:** The physician documented that further PT was not addressing neurological progression.
    *   **(a)(3) Condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address:** The member's MRI demonstrates severe central cord compression with myelomalacia (T2 signal change) and progressive cord injury. Myelomalacia is objective evidence of existing cord damage that PT cannot reverse.
    *   **(c) Plan's minimum PT requirement shall not be applied mechanically:** The original denial mechanically applied the 6-week rule without considering the member's neurological compromise.
    *   **Conclusion:** CIC § 10169.5 **OVERRIDES** any remaining PT requirement for this member due to the objective evidence of myelomalacia and progressive cord injury.

2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This guidance explicitly addresses myelopathy with myelomalacia.
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** States that cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm, to be treated as urgent.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** Mandates that plans **SHALL NOT** require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when:
        *   **(a) Objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (myelomalacia):** Confirmed by MRI.
        *   **(b) Physician documents conservative treatment failed:** PT was stopped as it wasn't addressing neurological progression, and pharmacotherapy provided only partial/temporary relief.
        *   **(c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines:** As detailed below, it is.
    *   **Specific Exemption:** APL 22-014 specifically identifies myelomalacia as supporting the waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements.
    *   **Conclusion:** DMHC APL 22-014 **MANDATES** the waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements, including physical therapy and interventional procedures, for this member's condition.

**C. Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

1.  **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines emphasize that myelopathy requires fundamentally different treatment algorithms than radiculopathy. The evidence base for conservative treatment, including PT, is primarily for radiculopathy, not moderate-to-severe myelopathy.
2.  **Myelomalacia Significance:** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) on MRI is a critical finding indicating existing spinal cord injury, a higher risk of permanent deficit, and reduced recovery even post-surgery. It is a strong indication for surgical intervention and a **contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials** (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2).
3.  **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** For moderate-severe myelopathy, PT does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement, and there is **no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of PT prior to surgery** (AO Spine 2021, NASS 2020, Clinical Reference Summary Section 2.1, 2.2). Delaying surgery in such patients for PT is not supported by evidence and may result in neurological deterioration.
4.  **Surgical Indication:** Patricia Osei's presentation meets **TIER 1 \u2014 STRONG INDICATION for surgery** per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), specifically for:
    *   Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI.
    *   Progressive neurological deficit despite conservative treatment.
    *   Myelomalacia on MRI (cord signal change).
    *   **Conclusion:** Clinical standards overwhelmingly support surgical intervention for this member and contradict any requirement for further conservative treatment or delay.

**V. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND FINAL DETERMINATION**

The original denial stated the member did not meet Section 2.2 requirements, citing insufficient PT and lack of interventional treatment.

1.  **Plan Criteria:** The denial incorrectly applied HealthFirst's own criteria. Patricia Osei's case clearly falls under the **Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A)**. Under this exception, the 5 weeks of PT completed exceed the reduced 4-week requirement, and the interventional procedure is waived due to documented medical contraindication (pacemaker). Therefore, the member **DOES MEET** the plan's criteria when correctly applied.

2.  **State Law and Clinical Standard Override:** Even if there were a conflict with the plan's internal criteria, both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards govern in this situation:
    *   **State Law (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014):** These regulations **mandate** the waiver of conservative treatment requirements, including physical therapy and interventional procedures, for patients with objective structural neurological compromise like myelomalacia and progressive myelopathy. The mechanical application of PT duration by the initial reviewer is explicitly prohibited by state law.
    *   **Clinical Standard:** Current, evidence-based guidelines from AANS/CNS, NASS, and AO Spine explicitly state that for moderate-to-severe myelopathy with myelomalacia, surgery is strongly indicated, and delaying surgery for conservative treatment (such as mandatory PT) is not supported and can lead to irreversible neurological deterioration.

Applying the three-source hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), the requirements of California state law and the generally accepted clinical standards override any conflicting or more restrictive plan criteria. The plan's initial denial failed to acknowledge or apply its own myelopathy exception, and its application of conservative treatment requirements directly conflicts with state law and established clinical best practices for progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia.

**VI. DECISION**

The prior authorization denial for procedure CPT 22551 (ACDF C4-C5, one level) for Patricia Osei (M-334455) is **OVERTURNED**.

---

**VII. WARRANT**

The denial is overturned for the following reasons:

1.  **Plan Criteria Met (Myelopathy Exception):** The member's diagnosis of progressive cervical myelopathy triggers HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A) \u2014 Myelopathy Pathway. This exception reduces the physical therapy requirement to a 4-week minimum (member completed 5 weeks) and waives the interventional treatment requirement when medically contraindicated (documented pacemaker contraindication for CESI). Therefore, the member meets the plan's internal medical necessity criteria when correctly applied.
2.  **State Law Override (CIC § 10169.5):** California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(3) prohibits conditioning surgical approval on PT completion where there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. Patricia Osei's MRI findings of severe central cord compression with myelomalacia represent such compromise, mandating a waiver of further PT requirements.
3.  **State Law Override (DMHC APL 22-014):** DMHC All-Plan Letter 22-014, Section II.2, explicitly states that plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia) and the physician documents conservative treatment failure, and surgery is indicated. Myelomalacia is specifically identified as a finding supporting the waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements.
4.  **Clinical Standard Override:** Generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) classify moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia as a TIER 1 strong surgical indication. These guidelines provide no evidence to support mandatory physical therapy prior to surgery in such cases and caution against delaying surgery due to the high risk of irreversible neurological deterioration associated with myelomalacia and progressive cord compression.

Based on the hierarchy of authority, state law and generally accepted clinical standards take precedence over plan-specific criteria when in conflict. In this case, both state law and clinical standards strongly support immediate surgical intervention and the waiver of further conservative treatment requirements for Patricia Osei's condition.
