**I. BACKGROUND**
This is an appeal of a prior authorization denial for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 for Helen Brooks, a 49-year-old member with a diagnosis of early cervical myelopathy. The initial denial (MED-003) stated that the myelopathy exception did not apply, citing minimal and potentially artifactual T2 signal change on MRI. The denial further noted that while physical therapy and pharmacotherapy requirements were met, the interventional procedure requirement of the standard pathway was not completed.

The appeal basis contends that a second radiologist opinion confirms early myelomalacia, that the initial radiologist's finding of T2 signal change was not artifact, and that the treating physician independently documented myelopathy on clinical grounds.

**II. CLINICAL RECORD SUMMARY**
*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Early cervical myelopathy, with disputed myelomalacia status.
*   **Imaging:**
    *   MRI (2024-10-01): C4-C5 disc herniation with cord contact. Original radiologist noted "Possible early T2 signal change — equivocal, may represent artifact. Clinical correlation required."
    *   Second MRI Read (2024-10-15): C4-C5 cord compression with *early myelomalacia*. Radiologist B confirmed T2 hyperintensity is reproducible and represents early cord signal change, not artifact.
    *   Plan Radiologist Review (2024-11-05): T2 signal change is below threshold for clinical significance, not consistent with myelomalacia, and represents artifact or normal variant. (This directly contradicts the second radiologist's opinion).
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-10-05):** Bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia in upper extremities, subtle balance impairment on tandem gait. (These are objective upper motor neuron signs, indicative of myelopathy).
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 8 weeks, 24 sessions. Documented "No improvement in UMN signs." (Exceeds plan's 6-week standard).
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Gabapentin (3 months, partial benefit), Cyclobenzaprine (6 weeks, minimal benefit). (Meets plan's requirement for two agents for >4 weeks each).
    *   **Interventional Procedure:** Not performed. Treating physician documented the reason: "did not refer for epidural due to myelopathy concern — epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated in cord compression." (This is the specific point of denial).

**III. DETERMINATION**
The denial should be **OVERTURNED**. The requested ACDF is medically necessary based on a comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's own criteria, generally accepted clinical standards, and California state law. The plan's denial is based on a misapplication of its own policy and is contrary to state regulations and clinical evidence.

**IV. WARRANT**

**A. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
The HealthFirst denial stated that the myelopathy exception did not apply and that an interventional procedure was required per the standard pathway. This is incorrect.

1.  **Diagnosis (Section 2.1(b)):** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy. The neurological exam clearly demonstrates objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs (bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, gait impairment) and imaging shows cord compression. This satisfies the plan's diagnostic criteria for myelopathy.
2.  **Conservative Treatment (Section 2.2):**
    *   **Physical Therapy (2.2(a)):** The member completed 8 weeks of PT, exceeding the 6-week standard.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2(b)):** Two agents (Gabapentin, Cyclobenzaprine) were trialed for sufficient duration, meeting this requirement.
    *   **Interventional Treatment (2.2(c)):** While an interventional procedure was not completed, the plan has a specific **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY**. This exception waives the interventional treatment requirement if the treating physician documents that the treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration. The treating physician explicitly documented that an epidural was not performed "due to myelopathy concern – epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated in cord compression." This documentation directly triggers the waiver for the interventional procedure requirement under the plan's own policy.
3.  **Misapplication of Myelopathy Exception:** The plan's reviewer incorrectly stated that the myelopathy exception does not apply, relying solely on their radiologist's interpretation of "minimal" T2 signal change. However, the myelopathy exception (2.2(A)) only requires "confirmed cervical myelopathy (criterion 2.1(b))" and physician documentation of risk. The member meets these conditions through clinical UMN signs, cord compression, and the treating physician's contraindication note. The degree of T2 signal change is not the sole determinant for applying the myelopathy exception when other definitive signs of myelopathy are present.

**B. Application of Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
Generally accepted clinical standards strongly support surgical intervention in this case and contradict the plan's requirement for an interventional procedure.

1.  **Myelopathy Classification:** The member's presentation (cord compression, bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, gait disturbance) unequivocally points to cervical myelopathy, not radiculopathy. Clinical guidelines emphasize that myelopathy and radiculopathy are distinct diseases with different treatment algorithms, and conservative treatment requirements for radiculopathy should not be applied to myelopathy.
2.  **Surgical Indication:** Per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), a diagnosis of moderate-to-severe myelopathy with objective cord compression and neurological deficits (UMN signs) constitutes a **TIER 1 strong surgical indication**. The presence of myelomalacia (as confirmed by the second radiology opinion), further strengthens this indication, being a predictor of permanent deficit if surgery is delayed.
3.  **Conservative Treatment Efficacy/Duration for Myelopathy:** Clinical guidelines from NASS and AO Spine explicitly state that there is **no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy or other conservative treatment prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy**. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a conservative treatment trial is not supported by evidence and may result in neurological deterioration. The treating physician's decision to avoid an epidural due to myelopathy concern aligns with these guidelines, which consider interventional procedures potentially harmful or ineffective in cases of direct cord compression.

**C. Application of California Regulatory Framework:**
California state law and regulations provide an independent basis for overturning this denial.

1.  **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This All-Plan Letter specifically addresses prior authorization for time-sensitive neurological conditions like myelopathy.
    *   **Section II.1:** States that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated and SHALL be treated as urgent. Here, we have clear cord compression and strong clinical evidence for myelopathy, with myelomalacia confirmed by one radiologist.
    *   **Section II.2:** States that plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment where objective imaging shows progressive pathology (cord signal change/myelomalacia) AND the treating physician documents conservative treatment failure AND surgery is indicated. The treating physician's documentation of contraindication for epidurals due to myelopathy concerns clearly satisfies this exception. The APL specifically identifies myelomalacia as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements.
    *   **Section II.3:** Mandates that plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. As established above, the plan's requirement for an interventional procedure in this myelopathy case is more restrictive than clinical standards.
2.  **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** While this section specifically addresses physical therapy, its underlying principle applies to other conservative treatments when structural neurological compromise is present. Subsection (a)(3) states that a plan cannot condition approval on PT where "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." This member has objective cord compression and clinical myelopathy (UMN signs), which represents structural neurological compromise. The physician's documentation of contraindication for epidural injections extends this principle to other conservative measures that may exacerbate or fail to address the underlying structural cord compromise.
3.  **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This section mandates that if a service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, it SHALL be approved regardless of the plan's internal criteria if they are more restrictive. As demonstrated, this surgical procedure meets generally accepted clinical standards.

**V. CONCLUSION**
The denial of prior authorization for ACDF C4-C5 for Helen Brooks is inconsistent with HealthFirst's own criteria, conflicts with generally accepted clinical standards for managing cervical myelopathy, and violates California state law and regulations. The treating physician's clinical assessment and treatment plan are supported by the evidence and align with best practices for a time-sensitive neurological condition.

Therefore, the request for CPT code 22551 for ACDF C4-C5 is medically necessary and the denial is **OVERTURNED**. Authorization for the requested procedure is hereby approved.
