**Level of Review:** First-Level Appeal

**Summary of Request and Denial:**
The member, Helen Brooks, a 49-year-old female, requested prior authorization for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 for a diagnosis of early cervical myelopathy. The initial authorization request was denied by HealthFirst PPO Gold, citing that the myelopathy exception did not apply due to the plan's imaging reviewer determining the MRI T2 signal change to be minimal/artifact, thus not meeting the threshold for clinical significance. Consequently, the plan denied the request for failure to complete an interventional procedure, as required by the standard conservative treatment pathway. The appeal argues that the T2 signal change is not artifact and represents early myelomalacia, confirmed by a second radiologist, and that the treating physician has documented myelopathy on clinical grounds.

**Reviewer Analysis:**

**1. Primary Clinical Presentation:**
The clinical record clearly indicates that Helen Brooks presents with signs and symptoms consistent with cervical myelopathy. Her neurological exam on 2024-10-05 documents bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia in upper extremities, and subtle balance impairment on tandem gait. These are hallmark upper motor neuron signs, unequivocally indicative of myelopathy. Imaging shows C4-C5 disc herniation with cord contact. The treating physician's primary diagnosis is "Early cervical myelopathy."
*   **Source:** Clinical record (Neurological exam, Presenting diagnosis); Clinical Standard Knowledge (DOMAIN KNOWLEDGE – CLASSIFICATION, Clinical Reference Summary SECTION 1.1)

**2. Application of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**

*   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** The member meets criterion 2.1(b) for cervical myelopathy, as demonstrated by objective cord compression on MRI and characteristic myelopathic symptoms (Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, gait disturbance).
*   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **(a) Physical Therapy:** 8 weeks completed (24 sessions) with documented "No improvement in UMN signs." This exceeds the plan's standard 6-week requirement and the myelopathy exception's 4-week requirement. **MET.**
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** Gabapentin (3 months, partial benefit) and Cyclobenzaprine (6 weeks, minimal benefit) were utilized. This meets the plan's requirement of two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. **MET.**
    *   **(c) Interventional Treatment:** No interventional procedure was completed. This is the basis for the plan's denial under the standard pathway.
*   **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY:** This exception reduces PT to 4 weeks and waives the interventional treatment requirement if the "treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses risk of neurological deterioration."
    *   The treating physician explicitly documented that "epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated in cord compression" due to myelopathy concern. This documentation directly satisfies the waiver condition for interventional treatment under the plan's own myelopathy exception.
    *   The plan's denial states "Myelopathy exception does not apply," primarily due to their radiologist's interpretation of the T2 signal change as minimal/artifact. However, the plan's note states the exception applies "only when myelopathy is the PRIMARY diagnosis." In this case, early cervical myelopathy is the presenting and primary diagnosis, supporting the applicability of the exception, regardless of the myelomalacia dispute.

**3. Application of California Regulatory Requirements (State Law):**

*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Section II.1 & II.2):** This state guidance is highly relevant. It classifies "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI" as a condition with potential for irreversible harm, requiring urgent treatment. It mandates that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" where objective imaging shows progressive pathology (like myelomalacia), the physician documents conservative treatment failure, and surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. The APL specifically states that "The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements even where a full course has not been completed, when the treating physician documents urgency."
    *   **Application:** While the plan's radiologist disputed myelomalacia, Radiologist B (treating hospital) confirmed "early myelomalacia" which was reproducible. Combined with clear clinical myelopathy and cord compression, this falls under APL 22-014. The treating physician documented risk for interventional treatment (conservative failure/contraindication) and surgery is indicated (see Clinical Standard below). Thus, the state mandates waiving the interventional procedure requirement.
*   **CIC § 10169.5 (a)(3) (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This code prohibits conditioning surgery on PT completion where "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." While the member completed PT, this statute reinforces that for myelopathy with neurological compromise, rigid adherence to conservative treatment for a specific duration (or type, like interventional procedures) is not mandated if not clinically beneficial or contraindicated. Helen's PT showed "no improvement in UMN signs," indicating a functional plateau for the neurological deficits that PT cannot address.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard is NOT Plan Criteria):** This section dictates that if a service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, it SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. This provides an overarching principle for resolution when plan criteria conflict with clinical standards, as seen here.

**4. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

*   **Diagnosis & Myelomalacia:** Clinical standards define myelopathy by cord compression and upper motor neuron signs (Hoffman, hyperreflexia, gait disturbance), all present in Helen Brooks. Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) indicates existing cord injury and is a "strong predictor of permanent deficit if surgery delayed" and a "strong surgical indication." The consistent findings of clinical myelopathy and confirmatory imaging from the treating team's radiologist outweigh the plan's internal reviewer's dissenting opinion. (Clinical Reference Summary SECTION 1.1, 1.2)
*   **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy (SECTION 2.1, 2.2):** Clinical guidelines strongly emphasize that "Application of radiculopathy conservative treatment requirements to myelopathy patients is NOT supported by clinical evidence." Specifically, the AO Spine Guidelines (2021) state, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." This principle extends to interventional procedures, particularly when contraindicated.
*   **Surgical Indications (SECTION 3.1, 3.2):** Helen Brooks' presentation (confirmed cervical myelopathy, objective cord compression, myelomalacia confirmed by treating radiologist, and objective neurological deficits) meets TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION for surgery per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023). The clinical standard explicitly states that for this presentation, a minimum course of conservative treatment is NOT required.

**5. Resolution of Conflicts and Final Disposition:**

The plan's denial hinges on its internal radiologist's interpretation dismissing the clinical significance of the T2 signal change and thus invalidating its own myelopathy exception, leading to a requirement for an interventional procedure. This conflicts directly with:

1.  **Plan's Own Criteria:** The treating physician documented a contraindication for interventional procedures, satisfying the waiver condition of HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(A), regardless of the myelomalacia dispute.
2.  **California State Law (Hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):**
    *   DMHC APL 22-014 mandates that myelopathy with myelomalacia and cord compression should be treated as urgent, and explicitly supports waiving standard conservative treatment requirements under such conditions, especially when a physician documents urgency/contraindication. The plan's refusal to waive the interventional procedure requirement in light of the treating physician's documented contraindication, and confirmatory imaging (Radiologist B), violates this state directive.
    *   CHSC 1374.32(b) states that if a service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, it shall be approved regardless of the plan's more restrictive internal criteria.
3.  **Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:** Major spine societies (AANS/CNS, AO Spine) indicate that for documented cervical myelopathy with cord compression and myelomalacia, surgery is a strong indication, and delaying surgery for unproven conservative treatments (especially if contraindicated) is not supported and potentially harmful.

Based on the hierarchy of authority, California state law and generally accepted clinical standards override the plan's more restrictive criteria and its misapplication of its own myelopathy exception. The plan's reliance on an outlier imaging review to deny an essential exception, despite compelling clinical evidence and other expert radiological opinions, is inconsistent with prudent clinical judgment and regulatory mandates.

**Disposition:** **OVERTURNED**

**Warrant:**
The denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C4-C5) for Helen Brooks is OVERTURNED. The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and definitive clinical signs (bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, gait impairment). While there was an initial dispute regarding early T2 signal change, a second radiologist's opinion confirmed early myelomalacia, which is a strong indicator for surgical intervention and urgency. The treating physician explicitly documented that interventional treatment (epidural steroid injections) is relatively contraindicated in the presence of cord compression and myelopathy, thereby fulfilling the waiver condition for interventional treatment under HealthFirst's own Myelopathy Pathway exception (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2A).

Furthermore, California state law, specifically DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014, mandates that plans treat myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia as urgent conditions and "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when objective imaging (myelomalacia) is present and the treating physician documents conservative treatment failure/contraindication. The plan's denial to apply its myelopathy exception, based on an internal radiologist's interpretation that conflicts with other expert opinions and the clinical picture, is directly contrary to this state mandate.

Finally, generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021) classify moderate-severe myelopathy with cord compression and myelomalacia as a TIER 1 strong surgical indication, explicitly stating that delaying surgery for conservative treatment in this context is not supported by evidence and may lead to neurological deterioration. Adherence to a mandatory interventional procedure requirement in this case is inconsistent with these standards.

Therefore, applying the three-source hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), the denial by HealthFirst is not supported.

**Action:** Approve CPT 22551 (ACDF C4-C5) as medically necessary.
