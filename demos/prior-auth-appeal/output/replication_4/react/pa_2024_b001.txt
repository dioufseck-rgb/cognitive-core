Our review applies the following hierarchy of authority to determine medical necessity:

Where a conflict exists between these sources, the higher authority governs.

**Detailed Analysis:**

**1. Identification of Clinical Condition:**
The clinical record, including MRI findings and neurological exam, consistently describes a presentation of **Cervical Radiculopathy at C5-C6**.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-01):** "C5-C6: large disc herniation with moderate-severe nerve root compression, moderate foraminal stenosis. Clear correlation with C6 radicular symptoms." Importantly, there is **no evidence of spinal cord compression or myelomalacia (cord signal change)** at this level.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-09-05):** Confirms "Right C6 dermatomal numbness and tingling, bicep reflex diminished right, grip strength 4/5 right." There are **no C7 findings, no bilateral findings, and no upper motor neuron signs** (such as Hoffman's sign or hyperreflexia), which are indicative of cervical myelopathy.
*   **Distinction:** As per clinical standard knowledge (SOURCE DOCUMENT 3, Section 1.1), cervical radiculopathy involves individual nerve root compression and unilateral symptoms, distinct from myelopathy which involves spinal cord compression and upper motor neuron signs. This distinction is critical as treatment algorithms and conservative treatment requirements differ significantly. Therefore, the myelopathy exceptions in plan criteria and state regulations do not apply to this case.

**2. Review of C5-C6 Level (Radiculopathy):**

*   **HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
    *   **2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** Met. Diagnosis of "Cervical radiculopathy – nerve root compression with radicular symptoms" (2.1(a)) and "Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit" (2.1(c)) is clearly supported for C5-C6.
    *   **2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:** Met.
        *   **(a) Physical Therapy:** 8 weeks of supervised PT completed, exceeding the 6-week minimum. The documentation states "C6 radicular symptoms persist," indicating a lack of meaningful improvement and thus a functional plateau (SOURCE DOCUMENT 5, Section 5).
        *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** Two agents (Gabapentin for 4 months, Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks) completed, meeting the 4-week minimum for each.
        *   **(c) Interventional Treatment:** One right C6 epidural completed, meeting the requirement.
    *   **2.3 Imaging Requirement:** Met. MRI demonstrates clear structural pathology (large disc herniation, nerve root compression) at C5-C6 consistent with reported C6 radicular symptoms.
    *   **Conclusion for C5-C6:** The requested ACDF at C5-C6 fully meets all applicable HealthFirst medical necessity criteria.

*   **California State Law:**
    *   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute specifies that a plan cannot condition approval on PT completion where the member has completed PT and reached a functional plateau (10169.5(a)(2)). Given 8 weeks of PT with persistent C6 radicular symptoms, Ms. Park has met the intent of this requirement by reaching a functional plateau.
    *   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions):** This APL primarily addresses myelopathy with myelomalacia. Since Ms. Park's condition is radiculopathy without myelomalacia, the specific conservative treatment waivers within this APL do not apply. However, the plan's requirements are met.
    *   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This standard emphasizes that services meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice SHALL be approved regardless of plan's more restrictive criteria. As detailed below, the clinical standards support ACDF for C5-C6.
    *   **Conclusion for C5-C6:** State law supports the approval of ACDF for C5-C6 given the completion and failure of conservative treatment.

*   **Clinical Standard (SOURCE DOCUMENT 3):**
    *   **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), NASS Guidelines (2020), AO Spine Guidelines (2021):** For cervical radiculopathy, surgical decompression (ACDF) is a "Reasonable Indication" (TIER 2) for "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative treatment." Ms. Park has failed 8 weeks of PT, 4 months of pharmacotherapy, and an epidural, with persistent C6 radicular symptoms.
    *   **Conclusion for C5-C6:** Clinical standards strongly support the medical necessity of ACDF at C5-C6.

**3. Review of C6-C7 Level (Adjacent Segment Degenerative Disease):**

*   **HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
    *   **2.3 Imaging Requirement and 3.3 Exclusions:** The MRI findings for C6-C7 show "mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing, no cord contact, no nerve root compression identifiable." Crucially, the neurological exam found "No C7 findings documented." HealthFirst's criteria require imaging to demonstrate structural pathology *consistent with the member's symptoms and proposed surgical level*. For C6-C7, there is no documented pathology requiring surgical intervention and no correlating symptoms. Therefore, this level fails to meet criterion 2.3, and exclusion 3.3 applies.
    *   **Conclusion for C6-C7:** The requested ACDF at C6-C7 does not meet HealthFirst's medical necessity criteria.

*   **California State Law:**
    *   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** The IMR standard requires the service to be "in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice." While the surgeon's rationale regarding the prevention of adjacent segment disease (ASD) is noted, prophylactic fusion of an asymptomatic level with minimal degenerative changes and no neurological deficits is not considered a generally accepted standard of medical practice for medical necessity for the *initial* surgery.
    *   **Conclusion for C6-C7:** State law does not override the denial for C6-C7, as this level does not meet the "generally accepted standards of medical practice" for surgical intervention.

*   **Clinical Standard (SOURCE DOCUMENT 3):**
    *   **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023):** "TIER 3 – SURGERY NOT INDICATED" for "Imaging findings without correlating symptoms." This directly applies to the C6-C7 level where MRI shows mild changes but no nerve root compression, and the patient has no C7 symptoms. Clinical guidelines focus on treating existing pathology causing symptoms or neurological deficits, not prophylactic fusion of asymptomatic levels. While ASD is a recognized concern, current guidelines do not support elective, preventative fusion of an asymptomatic and non-pathological adjacent segment as a primary indication for surgery.
    *   **Conclusion for C6-C7:** Clinical standards do not support the medical necessity of ACDF at C6-C7.

---

**Appeal Determination:**

Based on the thorough review of the clinical information, HealthFirst's clinical criteria, relevant California state laws, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, the denial is **PARTIALLY OVERTURNED and PARTIALLY UPHELD**.

*   **OVERTURNED for C5-C6:** The medical necessity for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (single level) is established. Ms. Park's diagnosis of C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits and failed comprehensive conservative treatment aligns with all plan criteria, state law exceptions regarding physical therapy, and accepted clinical guidelines for surgical intervention.

*   **UPHELD for C6-C7:** The medical necessity for fusion at the C6-C7 level is not established. Imaging at C6-C7 shows only mild degenerative changes without significant compression or nerve root involvement, and there are no documented C7 radicular symptoms or neurological deficits. While the treating surgeon's concern for adjacent segment disease is noted, prophylactic fusion of an asymptomatic level is not considered a generally accepted standard of medical practice for initial surgical intervention under current clinical guidelines or HealthFirst's criteria.

**Therefore, authorization is granted for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551).**
