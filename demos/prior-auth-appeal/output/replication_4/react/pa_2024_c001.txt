**Appeal Determination:**

The original denial is **OVERTURNED**. The requested ACDF C5-C6 procedure is approved as medically necessary based on a review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's criteria, California state law, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.

**Warrant for Determination:**

Our review identifies a conflict between HealthFirst's internal criteria and both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards regarding the diagnosis of cervical myelopathy and the mandatory duration of conservative physical therapy. In accordance with the established hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), the higher authority governs.

**1. Diagnosis of Cervical Myelopathy:**
*   **Plan's Denial (Reason 1, 2, 3):** HealthFirst denied the myelopathy exception, stating "imaging shows mild cord contact only — no cord compression, no cord signal change" and that "upper motor neuron signs on exam are not conclusive for myelopathy in the absence of imaging confirmation."
*   **Clinical Standard:** The AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine (2023 Update) explicitly states that "Clinical myelopathy diagnosis requires UMN signs on examination. Imaging confirmation supports but is *not required* for diagnosis. Imaging-negative myelopathy is a recognized entity where clinical signs precede MRI changes. Multiple positive UMN signs in the absence of other explanation = clinical myelopathy."
*   **Analysis:** The member's neurological exam findings (positive Hoffman bilateral, hyperreflexia bilateral upper extremities, sustained clonus, positive finger escape sign, wide-based gait) unequivocally establish a clinical diagnosis of cervical myelopathy. The plan's requirement for definitive imaging evidence of cord compression or T2 signal change to confirm myelopathy and its assertion that UMN signs are not conclusive without imaging, directly contradicts generally accepted clinical standards.
*   **Conflict Resolution:** Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) and DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, health plans cannot apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. Therefore, the clinical diagnosis of myelopathy, established by objective UMN signs, is sufficient for this appeal, regardless of the absence of "measurable cord compression" or "T2 signal change" on imaging. The "mild cord contact" further supports the structural basis of the clinical findings.

**2. Physical Therapy (PT) Requirement:**
*   **Plan's Denial (Reason 4):** HealthFirst denied because the member completed 5 weeks of PT, falling short of the standard 6-week requirement, as the myelopathy exception was deemed not applicable. The plan's myelopathy exception (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2A) reduces PT to 4 weeks minimum.
*   **State Law (CIC § 10169.5):** California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a) states that a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where:
    *   (1) The treating physician has documented in writing that PT is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition. (The physician "halted PT citing myelopathy concern," indicating a lack of benefit.)
    *   (2) The member has completed a course of PT and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms. (The clinical record notes "Neurological signs unchanged" after 5 weeks, supporting a functional plateau. Clinical standards define functional plateau as failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement, particularly for neurological signs in myelopathy.)
    *   (3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. (Clinically established myelopathy with mild cord contact constitutes structural neurological compromise that PT is not designed to address effectively for the underlying cord pathology.)
*   **Clinical Standard (AO Spine, NASS):** AO Spine Guidelines (2021) state, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." NASS Guidelines (2020) concur that conservative treatment has limited efficacy for established myelopathy.
*   **Analysis:** Given the confirmed clinical diagnosis of myelopathy, the plan's PT requirement, whether 6 weeks or the 4-week myelopathy exception, is superseded. The treating physician's decision to halt PT due to lack of neurological improvement and concern for myelopathy satisfies multiple conditions for waiver under California state law. Furthermore, generally accepted clinical guidelines specifically caution against delaying surgery for myelopathy to complete a PT course, as it lacks evidentiary support and may be detrimental.
*   **Conflict Resolution:** California Insurance Code § 10169.5, being state law, overrides the plan's internal PT requirement. The clinical standards further reinforce that mandatory PT for myelopathy is not evidence-based.

**3. Medical Necessity per Clinical Standard:**
*   The AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) classify moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression as a TIER 1 strong surgical indication. While measurable cord compression is not present on imaging, the robust clinical presentation of myelopathy with mild cord contact, and failure of conservative measures to improve neurological signs, aligns with the spirit of Tier 1/Tier 2 indications for surgical intervention in symptomatic cervical myelopathy where conservative management has failed.

**Conclusion:**

Based on the foregoing analysis, the member's clinical presentation meets the generally accepted standards for cervical myelopathy requiring surgical intervention. The plan's denial, relying on criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted medical practice and state law, cannot be upheld.

Therefore, the requested procedure, CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level), is deemed medically necessary.

**Disposition:** **OVERTURNED**
