### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This appeal involves a prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) for suspected cervical myelopathy. The initial denial was based on the assertion that myelopathy was not definitively confirmed by imaging (lacking measurable cord compression or signal change), rendering the myelopathy exception inapplicable. Consequently, the standard 6-week physical therapy (PT) requirement was applied, which the member did not fully meet (5 weeks completed).

Upon review of the clinical record against the hierarchy of authority (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria), it is determined that the plan's denial relies on an interpretation of myelopathy diagnosis and conservative treatment requirements that is inconsistent with generally accepted clinical standards and California state regulations. The member's clinical presentation strongly supports a diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, and the physical therapy requirement for this condition is not supported by evidence or regulatory mandates.

Therefore, the initial denial is **OVERTURNED**.

---

### DETERMINATION AND WARRANT

**Disposition:** **OVERTURNED**

**Warrant:** The initial denial for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) is overturned because the member's clinical presentation establishes a diagnosis of cervical myelopathy according to generally accepted clinical standards, and the plan's conservative treatment requirements for this condition are inconsistent with both clinical evidence and California state law.

**Specific findings supporting this determination:**

1.  **Diagnosis of Cervical Myelopathy (Conflict between Plan Criteria and Clinical Standard):**
    *   **Plan's Denial:** The denial states, "Upper motor neuron signs on exam are not conclusive for myelopathy in the absence of imaging confirmation," and implies that "measurable cord compression" or "cord signal change" is required for a confirmed myelopathy diagnosis (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(b)).
    *   **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine):** The clinical record's embedded note, confirmed by SOURCE DOCUMENT 3 (Section 1.1), explicitly states: "Clinical myelopathy diagnosis requires UMN signs on examination. Imaging confirmation supports but is *not required* for diagnosis. Imaging-negative myelopathy is a recognized entity where clinical signs precede MRI changes. Multiple positive UMN signs in the absence of other explanation = clinical myelopathy."
    *   **Member's Clinical Record:** The neurological exam (2024-09-05) reveals multiple definitive upper motor neuron signs: Positive Hoffman bilateral, Hyperreflexia bilateral upper extremities, Sustained clonus at bilateral ankles (3 beats), and a mildly wide-based gait. These findings, in the absence of other explanation, are sufficient to establish a clinical diagnosis of cervical myelopathy per generally accepted standards of medical practice. The MRI finding of "mild cord contact," even without measurable compression or T2 signal change, combined with these strong clinical signs, supports the diagnosis of myelopathy.
    *   **Resolution:** The clinical standard (AANS/CNS) governs over more restrictive plan criteria (CHSC § 1374.32(b)). The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy based on clinical findings, making the plan's myelopathy exception (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Exception 2.2(A)) applicable.

2.  **Conservative Treatment Requirement – Physical Therapy (Conflict between Plan Criteria, State Law, and Clinical Standard):**
    *   **Plan's Denial:** The denial states, "Standard 6-week PT requirement applies; member completed 5 weeks," incorrectly applying the standard pathway because it did not recognize the myelopathy diagnosis.
    *   **Plan's Myelopathy Exception:** Even if the myelopathy exception (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Exception 2.2(A)) were correctly applied, the PT requirement is "reduced to 4 weeks minimum." The member completed 5 weeks of PT. Therefore, even by the plan's *own* myelopathy exception criteria, the PT requirement was met.
    *   **California Insurance Code (CIC § 10169.5):** This state law explicitly prohibits a health plan from conditioning approval of surgery on completion of physical therapy where:
        *   **(a)(1) Physician documents PT contraindicated or unlikely to benefit:** The treating physician halted PT "citing myelopathy concern," which implies PT was unlikely to benefit the condition.
        *   **(a)(2) Member completed PT and reached a functional plateau:** The clinical record states "Neurological signs unchanged" after 5 weeks (15 sessions) of PT under a myelopathy protocol. For myelopathy, neurological signs (UMN signs, gait disturbance) are not expected to improve with PT, and their persistence indicates a functional plateau for the primary myelopathic condition (SOURCE DOCUMENT 3, Section 5).
        *   **(c) Plan's internal requirement... shall not be applied mechanically:** The plan's mechanical application of a 6-week PT requirement (or even a 4-week minimum if the myelopathy exception were correctly applied) is directly contradicted by this state regulation.
    *   **Clinical Standard (AO Spine 2021, NASS 2020, Cochrane Review 2021):** SOURCE DOCUMENT 3 (Section 2.1, 2.2) explicitly states that "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy." It further notes that "Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration," and that applying radiculopathy-derived PT requirements to myelopathy patients is a "category error."
    *   **Resolution:** Both California state law (CIC § 10169.5) and generally accepted clinical standards (AO Spine, NASS) override the plan's PT duration requirements for a patient with confirmed cervical myelopathy. The member completed 5 weeks of PT, exceeding the 4-week minimum in the plan's own myelopathy exception, and the cessation of PT due to unchanged neurological signs meets the criteria for waiver under state law and clinical guidelines.

3.  **Plan's Application of Criteria (CHSC § 1374.32(b)):** The plan's denial failed to appropriately apply its own myelopathy exception due to an overly restrictive interpretation of myelopathy diagnosis, and applied conservative treatment criteria not supported by the clinical evidence for this condition. California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32(b) mandates that if a service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, it shall be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.

**In summary, the plan's denial is based on a fundamental mischaracterization of the member's condition and an inappropriate application of conservative treatment requirements that are contradicted by prevailing clinical evidence and California state law. The requested ACDF is medically necessary under generally accepted standards of medical practice.**
