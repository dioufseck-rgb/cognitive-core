**Appeal Determination:**
The denial is **PARTIALLY OVERTURNED and PARTIALLY UPHELD.**

**Rationale for Decision:**

1.  **Primary Clinical Presentation:** The member presents with objective signs of both cervical myelopathy (spinal cord compression) and radiculopathy (nerve root compression). Crucially, the presence of severe cord compression and myelomalacia at C4-C5, coupled with upper motor neuron signs and gait disturbance, establishes **cervical myelopathy as the primary and most urgent clinical driver for surgical consideration.** Myelopathy and radiculopathy have distinct treatment algorithms, and conservative treatment requirements for myelopathy are less stringent due to the risk of irreversible harm.

2.  **Conservative Treatment Requirements:**
    *   **HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2):** Standard criteria require 6 weeks PT, 2 pharmacotherapy agents (4+ weeks each), and 1 interventional procedure. The myelopathy exception reduces PT to 4 weeks and waives interventional if risk is documented.
    *   **Review:** The member completed 9 weeks of physical therapy, 3-4 months of two different pharmacotherapy agents, and one interventional procedure. This exceeds the minimum requirements for both the standard pathway and the myelopathy exception in the plan's criteria.
    *   **California Law (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) & (3)):** State law prohibits conditioning surgery on PT completion where a member has reached a functional plateau, or where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. The clinical record explicitly documents a functional plateau and the presence of myelomalacia at C4-C5 represents severe structural neurological compromise.
    *   **Clinical Standard (AO Spine 2021, AANS/CNS 2023):** Guidelines state there is no high-quality evidence supporting mandatory PT prior to surgery for moderate-severe myelopathy, and delaying surgery may result in neurological deterioration.
    *   **Conclusion:** The conservative treatment requirements have been met and, in this case, would have been superseded by state law and clinical standards had they been a barrier to approval. The initial denial was not based on unmet conservative treatment, so this aspect does not conflict with the denial.

3.  **Pathology and Surgical Indication by Level:**

    *   **C4-C5 Level (Myelopathy with Radiculopathy):**
        *   **Clinical Evidence:** MRI shows severe cord compression with myelomalacia. Neurological exam confirms myelopathy (bilateral Hoffman, hyperreflexia, gait disturbance) and C5 radiculopathy (bilateral deltoid weakness).
        *   **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, Section 3.1):** Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI are TIER 1 strong surgical indications. Myelomalacia specifically indicates existing cord injury and surgical urgency, contraindicating extended conservative trials (AO Spine 2021, NASS 2020).
        *   **California Law (DMHC APL 22-014, Section II.1 & II.2):** Mandates treating myelopathy with myelomalacia as urgent and supports waiver of standard conservative requirements.
        *   **Disposition:** The plan's implicit approval of surgery at this level (by denying the other levels) is consistent with the overwhelming evidence supporting surgical intervention for moderate-severe myelopathy with myelomalacia.

    *   **C5-C6 Level (Radiculopathy):**
        *   **Plan's Denial:** "C5-C6 do not demonstrate pathology of sufficient severity to warrant surgical intervention."
        *   **Clinical Evidence:** MRI shows moderate disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression, and foraminal stenosis. Neurological exam shows objective left C6 weakness (bicep 4-/5) and diminished left bicep reflex. Symptoms persist after a completed course of conservative treatment.
        *   **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, Section 3.1, TIER 2):** Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks of adequate conservative treatment is a "reasonable indication" for surgical intervention. The member completed 9 weeks of PT, 3-4 months of pharmacotherapy, and an epidural, with persistent symptoms and objective neurological deficit.
        *   **California Law (CHSC § 1374.32(b)):** The Independent Medical Review standard (which governs appeals where conflicts exist) dictates that services meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice SHALL be approved, regardless of more restrictive internal plan criteria.
        *   **Disposition:** **OVERTURNED.** The plan's assessment that C5-C6 lacks sufficient severity is directly contradicted by the objective imaging and neurological examination findings, and the failure of conservative treatment. Surgical intervention at this level is medically necessary under generally accepted clinical standards.

    *   **C3-C4 Level:**
        *   **Plan's Denial:** "C3-C4 do not demonstrate pathology of sufficient severity to warrant surgical intervention."
        *   **Clinical Evidence:** MRI shows "mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing." Neurological exam at the C4 level was "unclear," with neck pain but "no specific radicular pattern." While the appeal states all levels are symptomatic, objective neurological correlation and pathology severity for surgical intervention are not clearly documented for C3-C4.
        *   **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020):** Surgical indications typically require clear correlation between imaging findings and objective neurological deficits or severe, intractable radicular/myelopathic symptoms refractory to conservative care. "Mild disc bulge" and "minimal foraminal narrowing" without clear neurological deficit or radicular pattern generally do not meet surgical criteria for fusion.
        *   **Disposition:** **UPHELD.** The documentation does not provide sufficient objective evidence of a surgically actionable lesion with correlating neurological deficit at the C3-C4 level to meet generally accepted standards for surgical intervention.

---

**Conclusion:**

Based on the comprehensive review of the clinical information, HealthFirst Health Plan's criteria, California state laws, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, the request for a three-level ACDF (CPT 22551) is approved for two levels: C4-C5 and C5-C6. The request for the C3-C4 level is denied due to insufficient objective evidence of pathology correlating with a neurological deficit to warrant surgical intervention at this time.

Therefore, the prior authorization denial is **PARTIALLY OVERTURNED**. Surgical authorization is granted for ACDF at **C4-C5 and C5-C6**.
