**APPEAL DETERMINATION LETTER**

**Case ID:** PA-2024-E002
**Member:** Catherine Mills, M-778811
**Procedure:** ACDF C3-C4, C4-C5, AND C5-C6 (three levels)
**Requested By:** Dr. James Torres, MD (Neurosurgery)

**Reviewer:** Expert Health Plan Appeals Reviewer

**Disposition: PARTIALLY OVERTURNED**

**Decision:** The denial for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at **C4-C5 and C5-C6 levels is OVERTURNED**. Surgical intervention for these two levels is deemed medically necessary and approved. The denial for ACDF at the **C3-C4 level is UPHELD**.

**Warrant:**

This determination is made following a comprehensive review of the member's clinical record, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), relevant California state laws and regulations (California Insurance Code, California Health & Safety Code, DMHC All-Plan Letters), and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine guidelines). The hierarchy of authority applied is State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.

**I. Clinical Presentation & Initial Denial Review:**

The member, Catherine Mills, presents with multi-level cervical spondylosis with significant myelopathy at C4-C5 and radiculopathy at C5-C6. The initial denial from HealthFirst (Dr. Alan Foster, 2024-11-25) approved surgical intervention at C4-C5 only, denying C3-C4 and C5-C6 due to these levels not demonstrating "pathology of sufficient severity to warrant surgical intervention at this time."

The clinical record unequivocally demonstrates:
*   **C4-C5:** Large disc herniation, severe cord compression, and T2 signal change (myelomalacia) on MRI. Neurological exam reveals myelopathy signs (bilateral hyperreflexia, bilateral Hoffman, wide-based gait, bilateral C5 weakness). This is moderate-severe cervical myelopathy.
*   **C5-C6:** Moderate disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression, foraminal stenosis on MRI. Neurological exam shows left bicep weakness (C6) and diminished bicep reflex. This is cervical radiculopathy.
*   **C3-C4:** Mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing on MRI. Neurological exam notes "C4 level: unclear — neck pain with C3-C4 palpation but no specific radicular pattern."

Conservative treatment included 9 weeks of physical therapy, 3-4 months of two pharmacotherapy agents (Baclofen, Pregabalin), and a cervical epidural. The physical therapy summary indicates "Myelopathic symptoms stable but not improved. C5-C6 radicular symptoms persist. C3-C4 contribution unclear."

**II. Application of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

1.  **Diagnosis (2.1):** The member meets criteria for Cervical Myelopathy (2.1(b)) at C4-C5 and Cervical Radiculopathy (2.1(a)) at C5-C6. The diagnosis of myelopathy at C4-C5 is primary given its severity and urgency.
2.  **Conservative Treatment (2.2):**
    *   **Myelopathy Exception (2.2A) for C4-C5:** As myelopathy is the primary diagnosis, the physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks minimum (member completed 9 weeks). Two pharmacotherapy agents for 4+ weeks each were completed. One interventional treatment was completed (epidural). All requirements for the myelopathy exception are met.
    *   **Standard Pathway for C5-C6:** The member completed 9 weeks of PT (exceeds 6 weeks), 2 pharmacotherapy agents (exceeds 4 weeks each), and one interventional procedure. These requirements are met.
3.  **Imaging (2.3):**
    *   **C4-C5 & C5-C6:** Imaging demonstrates structural pathology consistent with symptoms.
    *   **C3-C4:** Imaging shows mild pathology, but the symptoms are "unclear" and "no specific radicular pattern." This level is questionable regarding its consistency with symptoms requiring surgery per this criterion.
4.  **Physician Documentation (2.4):** The appeal basis states the treating surgeon documented reasoning and conservative treatment completion, which is assumed to meet these requirements.

**III. Application of California Regulatory Requirements (State Law):**

1.  **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):**
    *   For **C4-C5 (Myelopathy):** The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (severe cord compression, myelomalacia) that physical therapy cannot address. Per CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), a plan cannot condition approval on completion of PT in this scenario.
    *   For **C5-C6 (Radiculopathy):** The member completed 9 weeks of PT, and "radicular symptoms persist," indicating a functional plateau. Per CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), a plan cannot condition approval on further PT once a functional plateau is reached.
2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):**
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** The presence of myelomalacia with objective cord compression at C4-C5 requires this case to be treated as urgent, with potential for irreversible harm if untreated.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** For conditions with myelomalacia, plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment where surgery is indicated. This reinforces that the plan's standard PT duration cannot be rigidly applied to Catherine's myelopathy.
    *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** This APL mandates that plans shall not apply internal criteria more restrictive than generally accepted standards. This is crucial for evaluating the plan's "insufficient severity" argument for C5-C6.
3.  **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (Standard of Review for IMR):** This statute explicitly states that the Independent Medical Review standard (which focuses on "generally accepted standards of medical practice") governs, and a service meeting this standard SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.

**IV. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

1.  **C4-C5 (Myelopathy):**
    *   The member's presentation of moderate-severe myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI, along with upper motor neuron signs and gait disturbance, meets **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION** for surgical intervention (AANS/CNS 2023, Clinical Standard 3.1).
    *   Myelomalacia is a strong surgical indication and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials, predicting worse outcomes with delay (Clinical Standard 1.2, 2.3).
    *   Physical therapy for moderate-severe myelopathy does not produce meaningful functional improvement, and delaying surgery for PT is not supported by evidence (AO Spine 2021, Clinical Standard 2.1).
    *   **Conclusion:** Surgical intervention at C4-C5 is strongly indicated by clinical standards.

2.  **C5-C6 (Radiculopathy):**
    *   The member's persistent radicular symptoms with objective findings (C6 nerve root compression, C6 weakness) after a 9-week course of PT (exceeding 6-12 weeks), two pharmacotherapy agents, and an epidural, meets **TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION** for surgical intervention (AANS/CNS 2023, Clinical Standard 3.1). The documented persistence of symptoms after conservative treatment indicates failure and a functional plateau (Clinical Standard 5).
    *   **Conclusion:** Surgical intervention at C5-C6 is a reasonable and medically appropriate option per clinical standards. The plan's "insufficient severity" argument for this level is more restrictive than generally accepted clinical guidelines for radiculopathy failing conservative management.

3.  **C3-C4:**
    *   The MRI shows mild findings (mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing), but the neurological exam does not correlate with specific radicular symptoms ("unclear — neck pain with C3-C4 palpation but no specific radicular pattern"). This falls under **TIER 3 — SURGERY NOT INDICATED** for "imaging findings without correlating symptoms" (AANS/CNS 2023, Clinical Standard 3.1).
    *   **Conclusion:** Surgical intervention at C3-C4 is not supported by clinical standards.

**V. Conflict Resolution & Final Determination:**

*   **C4-C5:** The plan's implicit approval of C4-C5 is consistent with state law and clinical standards, which overwhelmingly support urgent surgical intervention for myelopathy with myelomalacia.
*   **C5-C6:** The plan's denial for "insufficient severity" at C5-C6 is overridden by state law (DMHC APL 22-014 and CHSC 1374.32(b)) and clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023). State law prohibits applying internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards. The clinical standard clearly indicates surgery for radiculopathy that has failed an adequate course of conservative treatment, which Catherine's case demonstrates.
*   **C3-C4:** The plan's denial for "insufficient severity" at C3-C4 aligns with the lack of clear correlating symptoms in the clinical record, which translates to "imaging findings without correlating symptoms" (TIER 3, not indicated) under generally accepted clinical standards. There is no state law or clinical standard mandating approval for this level based on the current evidence.

Therefore, HealthFirst's denial is **PARTIALLY OVERTURNED**. Surgical intervention for ACDF at C4-C5 and C5-C6 is approved. The denial for ACDF at C3-C4 is upheld.
