### FORMAL DISPOSITION: OVERTURN

After a comprehensive clinical and regulatory review of the medical record, the initial adverse determination dated October 1, 2024, is **OVERTURNED**. The requested Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C3-C4 (CPT 22551) is **APPROVED** as medically necessary.

---

### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The member is a 67-year-old female with a history of a successful C4-C5 ACDF in 2019 who presented with a new, progressive left C4 radiculopathy (left shoulder/lateral arm numbness, deltoid weakness graded at 3+/5, and a diminished bicep reflex) secondary to adjacent segment disease. The initial denial was based on the assertion that an MRI from August 15, 2024, showed only "mild degenerative changes" and failed to demonstrate sufficient pathology at C3-C4. 

However, the clinical record demonstrates that the August MRI was severely limited by metallic hardware artifact from the prior 2019 fusion, rendering it diagnostic-grade inadequate for the C3-C4 level. A subsequent CT myelogram—the clinical gold standard for evaluating spinal pathology in the presence of hardware artifact—confirmed moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression and significant foraminal stenosis correlating directly with the member’s objective neurological deficits. 

Because the member has completed and failed an exhaustive 10-week course of conservative management (including physical therapy, dual-agent pharmacotherapy, and a targeted epidural steroid injection), and because objective clinical and imaging findings establish clear surgical indications, the requested procedure is medically necessary under California law, generally accepted clinical standards, and the plan's own clinical coverage criteria.

### DETAILED EVALUATION PLAN ANALYSIS

#### Step 1: Procedural Adequacy and Regulatory Compliance Check
*   **Analysis**: The initial denial notice issued on October 1, 2024, cited reason code MED-003 and stated that imaging did not show sufficient pathology at C3-C4. The plan’s notice did contain a clinical rationale (relying on the MRI) and provided notice of Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights under California law. 
*   **Finding**: The denial met the baseline procedural requirements of California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b) and CHSC § 1367.01. Therefore, we proceed directly to the substantive clinical review of the medical necessity.

#### Step 2: Clinical Diagnostic Classification (Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy)
*   **Analysis**: Clinical neurological examination notes dated August 20, 2024, document that the member exhibits strictly unilateral sensory and motor deficits. Specifically, findings include left C4 dermatomal numbness, 3+/5 weakness of the left deltoid, and a diminished left bicep reflex. There is no documentation of bilateral upper extremity symptoms, gait/balance instability, or upper motor neuron signs (such as hyperreflexia, clonus, or a positive Hoffman's sign).
*   **Finding**: The member's presentation is classified as **Cervical Radiculopathy** (specifically, a symptomatic left C4 radiculopathy due to adjacent segment disease). Consequently, the clinical evaluation must follow the Radiculopathy pathway under the Plan's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) and generally accepted clinical standards.

#### Step 3: Objective Imaging Analysis and Diagnostic Verification
*   **Analysis**: The initial reviewer, Dr. Foster, denied authorization on the basis that the MRI dated August 15, 2024, showed only "mild degenerative changes." However, the radiologist's report for that MRI explicitly noted: *"SIGNIFICANT ARTIFACT from C4-C5 hardware limiting evaluation. Cannot exclude moderate compression due to artifact."* Under standard clinical practice guidelines, an MRI obscured by metallic hardware artifact is insufficient to rule out surgical pathology.
*   **CT Myelogram Verification**: To resolve this diagnostic limitation, a CT myelogram was performed on September 10, 2024. The CT myelogram is the diagnostic gold standard for adjacent segment disease where prior spinal instrumentation is present. The myelogram confirmed:
    *   Moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression.
    *   Significant left-sided foraminal stenosis at the C3-C4 level.
    *   Direct correlation between the radiographic findings and the member's clinical deficits (left deltoid weakness and diminished reflex).
*   **Finding**: The imaging requirement under Plan Criteria Section 2.3 is fully satisfied. The CT myelogram objectively demonstrates severe structural pathology consistent with the symptomatic level, overriding the initial reviewer’s flawed reliance on the artifact-obscured MRI.

#### Step 4: Conservative Treatment and Waiver Applicability Review
*   **Analysis**: The clinical record was assessed against the conservative treatment standards of CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 and California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5:
    1.  **Physical Therapy**: The member completed 10 weeks (30 sessions) of supervised physical therapy with documented failure to improve. This exceeds the plan’s standard requirement of 6 weeks (Section 2.2(a)).
    2.  **Pharmacotherapy**: The member completed trials of two neuropathic agents: Pregabalin 300mg BID for 6 months (partial relief) and Duloxetine 60mg for 3 months (minimal benefit). This fully satisfies the 2-agent, 4-week requirement (Section 2.2(b)).
    3.  **Interventional Treatment**: The member underwent a left C4 epidural steroid injection on September 25, 2024, which provided only transient benefit (60% relief returning to baseline by week 3). This satisfies the interventional requirement (Section 2.2(c)).
*   **Finding**: The member has fully satisfied and failed the plan's standard conservative treatment criteria. Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), the member has documented a clinical and functional plateau. Conservative options have been exhausted.

#### Step 5: Generally Accepted Clinical Standards vs. Plan Criteria Reconciliation
*   **Analysis**: Under **California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32(b)**, the standard for medical necessity during appeal and independent review is based on generally accepted standards of medical practice, which override any internal health plan policies that are more restrictive or rigidly applied.
*   **Clinical Guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023 / NASS 2020)**: Generally accepted clinical standards strongly support surgical intervention (ACDF) for members with cervical radiculopathy who exhibit:
    1.  Objective neurological deficits (3+/5 motor weakness and reflex changes).
    2.  Radiographic confirmation of corresponding nerve root compression (demonstrated here by CT myelogram).
    3.  Failure of more than 6–12 weeks of comprehensive conservative management.
*   **Finding**: The member meets Tier 1/Tier 2 surgical indications established by the American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS) and the Congress of Neurological Surgeons (CNS). Denying surgical decompression in the presence of active, progressive motor weakness (deltoid 3+/5) and confirmed severe nerve root compression violates generally accepted standards of care and places the member at high risk for irreversible motor deficit.

### HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY RESOLUTION

$$\text{California State Law (CIC / CHSC)} > \text{Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS)} > \text{Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)}$$

1.  **California Insurance Code § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014**: Protects members from rigid applications of conservative treatment policies when objective structural neurological compromise is present. Although the member fully met the conservative criteria, state law supports prioritizing definitive surgical care when objective motor deficits exist.
2.  **Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023 / NASS 2020)**: Validate the use of a CT myelogram to diagnose pathology in the presence of MRI artifact. They dictate that a C3-C4 decompression is the standard of care for a patient with persistent, symptomatic radiculopathy and deltoid weakness who has failed a 10-week multidisciplinary conservative regimen.
3.  **Plan Criteria CC-SPINE-2024**: While the plan’s policy is designed to prevent unnecessary surgery, it explicitly allows for CT myelogram documentation (Section 2.1) and was misapplied by the initial reviewer who ignored the myelogram findings in favor of an artifact-limited MRI.

The requested **Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C3-C4 (CPT 22551)** is medically necessary and is hereby **APPROVED**.
