### FORMAL DISPOSITION

Pursuant to California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 and the terms of the HealthFirst Gold PPO plan, the initial decision to deny prior authorization for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C3-C4) is **OVERTURNED**. 

The requested surgical procedure is **approved** as medically necessary. The plan shall authorize the procedure with an effective date matching the original request, valid for the standard 90-day authorization window.

---

### I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This case involves a 67-year-old female member with a history of a successful C4-C5 ACDF in 2019, who now presents with progressive, severe left-sided C4 radiculopathy secondary to adjacent segment disease at C3-C4. 

The health plan’s initial reviewer, Dr. Alan Foster, MD, denied the request on 2024-10-01 citing a lack of objective imaging pathology on the cervical spine MRI dated 2024-08-15. The denial letter stated: *"imaging does not show sufficient pathology at C3-C4 to support surgical intervention. MRI shows mild degenerative changes. Procedure not medically necessary."*

Upon exhaustive clinical and administrative review of the entire case file, this determination finds that the initial denial was clinically and procedurally invalid. The plan’s reviewer relied exclusively on an MRI report that was compromised by severe hardware artifact from the member's 2019 fusion, while completely ignoring a subsequent diagnostic **CT Myelogram** (dated 2024-09-10). The CT myelogram successfully bypassed the hardware artifact and confirmed **moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression and significant foraminal stenosis** at C3-C4, which correlates precisely with the member's severe clinical deficits (including 3+/5 deltoid weakness). 

Furthermore, the member has completed a exhaustive 10-week course of physical therapy, two neuropathic medications, and a targeted cervical epidural steroid injection without long-term benefit, satisfying and exceeding all plan conservative care criteria.

Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5, California Health and Safety Code § 1374.32(b), and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, NASS), surgical decompression is highly indicated. The denial is therefore overturned.

### III. EVALUATION OF PLAN CRITERIA (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)

The health plan's initial denial asserted that the member met conservative treatment requirements but failed the imaging criteria. A direct comparison of the clinical record against **HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024** demonstrates that the member fully satisfied all plan criteria:

1.  **Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement):** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of **Cervical Radiculopathy** under Section 2.1(a), characterized by nerve root compression with corresponding radicular symptoms (left shoulder/arm numbness, 3+/5 deltoid weakness, and diminished bicep reflex).
2.  **Section 2.1 (Supporting Documentation):** The criteria require an *"MRI or CT myelogram within 6 months of request demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms."* The CT myelogram from 2024-09-10 (completed less than 1 month prior to the denial) unequivocally demonstrated moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression, satisfying this requirement.
3.  **Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment):** The member completed:
    *   *PT (2.2(a)):* 10 weeks completed (exceeding the 6-week minimum requirement).
    *   *Pharmacotherapy (2.2(b)):* Trial of two agents (Pregabalin and Duloxetine) for >4 weeks each (exceeding the requirement).
    *   *Interventional (2.2(c)):* One cervical epidural injection completed on 2024-09-25.
4.  **Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement):** The CT myelogram, interpreted by a specialist, demonstrated structural pathology (severe foraminal stenosis and left C4 nerve root compression) that is a direct, perfect anatomical match for the member's clinical symptoms (left C4 radiculopathy and deltoid weakness).

**Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The plan's initial reviewer committed a factual and clinical error by ignoring the CT myelogram. By utilizing a compromised MRI report to deny the procedure under Section 3.3, the plan misapplied its own criteria. The member met all requirements of CC-SPINE-2024.

### IV. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY & STATUTORY COMPLIANCE ANALYSIS

Under the **Hierarchy of Authority**, California state laws and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) regulations strictly supersede the plan’s internal policies. The plan’s initial denial violates multiple state statutes:

#### 1. Procedural Defect under CHSC § 1374.31(b)
The plan's denial notice dated 2024-10-01 failed to cite the specific clinical coverage criteria applied (e.g., CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.3) and failed to address or explain why the CT myelogram findings were excluded from the medical necessity determination. Under **California Health and Safety Code § 1374.31(b)**, a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria or provide a comprehensive clinical reason is **legally defective**.

#### 2. Statutory Protection on Physical Therapy under CIC § 10169.5
Under **California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2)**, a health plan is legally prohibited from conditioning the approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy if the member has completed therapy and the physician documents that the member reached a **functional plateau** without adequate resolution. 
*   The clinical record contains therapist documentation that 10 weeks (30 sessions) of PT yielded "no improvement in left C4 radicular symptoms." 
*   The member reached a documented functional plateau. Applying any further conservative treatment or PT demands is a violation of CIC § 10169.5.

#### 3. Administrative Review Bias and Artifact Failure
The health plan's medical director ignored the diagnostic hierarchy of spine care. It is a fundamental medical standard that when metal hardware artifact obscures an MRI, a CT myelogram is the legally and clinically accepted secondary test. By failing to evaluate the CT myelogram, the plan made an arbitrary determination based on incomplete data, violating the fair review requirements mandated by CHSC § 1367.01.

### V. CLINICAL STANDARD RECONCILIATION (CHSC § 1374.32(b))

Under **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**, the standard for Independent Medical Review (IMR) requires that medical necessity be evaluated based on **generally accepted standards of medical practice**, which override more restrictive internal plan criteria.

1.  **AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine (2023 Guidelines):**
    *   The guidelines establish that patients presenting with cervical radiculopathy, adjacent segment disease, and an objective, profound motor deficit (deltoid weakness of **3+/5**) who have failed more than 6 weeks of structured physical therapy, neuropathic medications, and selective interventional injections are candidates for **Tier 1 (Strongly Recommended) surgical decompression**.
    *   Delaying surgery in the presence of a progressive motor deficit (deltoid weakness) risks permanent nerve root damage, axoplasmic flow arrest, and irreversible muscle atrophy. 
2.  **North American Spine Society (NASS) Guidelines (2020):**
    *   Surgical decompression via ACDF (CPT 22551) is highly indicated and represents the gold standard of care for single-level adjacent segment radiculopathy with correlating advanced imaging pathology (CT myelogram) that has failed to respond to a comprehensive 10-week conservative regimen.
3.  **Diagnostic Modality Standards:**
    *   Both NASS and AO Spine clinical guidelines state that in patients with prior spinal fusion hardware, standard MRI imaging is frequently nondiagnostic due to susceptibility artifacts. In these cases, a CT myelogram is the gold-standard diagnostic tool. Denying a procedure because an MRI was obscured by hardware while ignoring a positive CT myelogram is completely contrary to generally accepted standards of medical practice.

### VI. DECISION & ORDER

Based on the clinical evidence, plan criteria, California statutory law, and national clinical guidelines:

1.  **The member has confirmed adjacent segment disease** presenting as severe, symptomatic, left-sided C4 cervical radiculopathy.
2.  **Objective pathology is confirmed** via a cervical CT myelogram dated 2024-09-10, demonstrating moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis.
3.  **All conservative treatment requirements have been satisfied and exhausted**, including 10 weeks of physical therapy (reaching a documented functional plateau), a dual-agent neuropathic trial, and a cervical epidural steroid injection.
4.  **The initial denial notice is procedurally defective** under CHSC § 1374.31(b) and clinically unsound.
5.  **Surgical decompression and fusion (ACDF C3-C4, CPT 22551) is medically necessary** under AANS/CNS and NASS guidelines.

Therefore, the health plan's prior authorization denial is **OVERTURNED**. 

The health plan is hereby **ordered to approve and authorize** CPT code 22551 for the requested ACDF C3-C4 procedure, to be performed by Dr. Paul Nguyen at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center. This authorization is effective immediately.
