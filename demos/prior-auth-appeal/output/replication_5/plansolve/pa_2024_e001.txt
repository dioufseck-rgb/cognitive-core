### **EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**
This case involves a first-level administrative and clinical appeal for a 60-year-old male member seeking authorization for a two-level ACDF (C5-C6 and C6-C7). The Health Plan initially approved the C5-C6 level but denied the adjacent C6-C7 level, asserting that the imaging for C6-C7 showed "mild changes only" and that plan criteria do not support a two-level fusion when only one level meets criteria. 

An exhaustive evaluation of the administrative record, clinical records, and governing legal frameworks was performed. This review applied the mandatory **Source Hierarchy for Conflict Resolution**: **California State Law & Regulations > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Health Plan Internal Criteria**.

The denial is overturned based on a critical, fatal **procedural notice defect** under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b). Under California law, this defect invalidates the health plan's denial, requiring an immediate **Overturn** of the decision. Additionally, a complete clinical analysis is provided below to establish a comprehensive baseline for this determination.

### **DETAILED ANALYSIS & EVALUATION**

#### **STEP 1: PROCEDURAL ADEQUACY AND DEFECT CHECK (CHSC § 1374.31(b))**
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), any health plan medical necessity denial must meet strict statutory notice requirements. Specifically, a denial notice must explicitly specify the clinical criteria applied (e.g., *HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024*) and detail the specific clinical/imaging reasons supporting the denial. 

A review of the health plan's initial denial notice dated November 1, 2024, reveals the following:
1. **Failure to Cite Specific Criteria:** The denial notice failed to cite the health plan's specific internal clinical coverage guideline utilized for the review (e.g., *HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024*).
2. **Defective Clinical Explanation:** The denial text consisted of a brief, conclusory assertion (*"C6-C7 shows mild changes only. Two-level fusion not approved when one level meets criteria and one does not."*). It failed to provide the necessary objective radiological or clinical details mapping the imaging findings to the member’s presentation.

**Statutory Finding:** Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is legally **defective** and "shall be treated as if no denial was issued." 

**Procedural Determination:** Because the plan's initial denial is procedurally defective under California law, the denial is legally invalid. Applying the Source Hierarchy of Authority, California statutory requirements supersede any internal health plan policies. Therefore, this procedural defect triggers an automatic **OVERTURN** of the plan's denial of CPT 22552.

---

#### **STEP 2: DIAGNOSTIC CLASSIFICATION AND SEVERITY EVALUATION**
To establish a complete clinical baseline, the patient’s primary clinical diagnosis was reviewed to determine if standard or modified conservative treatment pathways apply.

* **Cervical Myelopathy Criteria:** Diagnostic standards (AANS/CNS 2023; AO Spine 2021) require the presence of spinal cord compression on MRI, objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs (e.g., positive Hoffman sign, hyperreflexia, clonus, gait instability), and bilateral symptoms.
* **Review of Clinical Record:** 
  * The neurological exam dated September 20, 2024, shows right bicep weakness (3+/5), an absent right bicep reflex, and right C6 dermatomal numbness and tingling.
  * No UMN signs were documented. Hoffman sign was absent, reflex findings were diminished/absent (consistent with lower motor neuron/nerve root compression), and no gait disturbances were noted.
  * MRI findings from September 15, 2024, confirmed moderate-severe right C6 nerve root compression at C5-C6 but showed no spinal cord contact or central cord compression at either level.

**Clinical Classification:** The member does not have Cervical Myelopathy. The clinical presentation is classified as **Cervical Radiculopathy** (specifically, severe right-sided C6 radiculopathy due to structural nerve root compression at C5-C6). Therefore, standard conservative treatment guidelines apply, and the Myelopathy Exception under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(A) is not triggered.

---

#### **STEP 3: IMAGING AND MULTI-LEVEL PATHOLOGY CORRELATION**
A clinical evaluation of the requested surgical levels was performed to correlate the MRI findings with the physical examinations:

1. **C5-C6 Level (Medically Necessary):**
   * **MRI (2024-09-15):** Large disc herniation, moderate-to-severe right C6 nerve root compression, and foraminal stenosis.
   * **Neurological Exam (2024-09-20):** 3+/5 right bicep weakness, absent right bicep reflex, and right C6 dermatomal paresthesias.
   * **Conservative Trial:** The patient completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of physical therapy where C6 symptoms persisted. The patient trialed Pregabalin (4 months) and Naproxen (3 months) with partial relief. A right C6 epidural steroid injection on October 15, 2024, provided 70% relief, but symptoms fully returned after 4 weeks.
   * **Correlation:** The clinical and radiographic findings perfectly match. This level satisfies all criteria under *HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024* Section 2.1(a) and 2.2.

2. **C6-C7 Level (Clinically Indeterminate):**
   * **MRI (2024-09-15):** Small disc bulge, mild foraminal narrowing, no identifiable nerve root compression, and no cord contact.
   * **Neurological Exam (2024-09-20):** Right tricep reflex slightly diminished. No definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms. The physician noted mild C7 symptoms were "possibly present but not clearly documented."
   * **Conservative Trial:** Physical therapy notes at week 8 explicitly state that any potential C7 symptoms were "minimal and possibly resolving."
   * **Correlation:** The imaging findings of mild, age-related changes at C6-C7 do not correlate with a definitive, objective neurological deficit. While the surgeon argued that adjacent-level decompression and fusion should be addressed simultaneously to prevent accelerated degeneration, generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023; NASS 2020) do not support prophylactic multi-level fusions in the absence of matching structural and clinical pathology.

**Clinical Finding:** From a purely clinical standpoint, the C6-C7 level does not independently meet standard medical necessity criteria. However, because the health plan issued a legally defective denial notice, this clinical deficit is superseded by the statutory remedy under California law.

#### **STEP 4: STATUTORY AND CLINICAL STANDARD OVERRIDE ANALYSIS**
According to the Source Hierarchy of Authority:
$$\text{California State Law \& Regulations} > \text{Clinical Standards} > \text{Health Plan Internal Criteria}$$

1. **Plan Criteria Application:** Under internal plan criteria (*CC-SPINE-2024* Section 3.3), the health plan denied C6-C7 due to lack of correlating imaging findings.
2. **Clinical Standards Application:** Generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023) agree that prophylactic adjacent-segment fusion is not indicated for mild, asymptomatic degenerative changes.
3. **California State Law Application (CHSC § 1374.31(b)):** California law mandates that a plan's denial notice must be procedurally adequate, specifying the criteria and clinical reasoning applied. A failure to do so renders the denial **void**.

Because California State Law holds the highest position in the hierarchy, its statutory requirements override both the health plan's internal medical necessity criteria and the clinical standards. The health plan's procedural failure to issue a valid denial notice legally invalidates its decision to deny the C6-C7 level.
