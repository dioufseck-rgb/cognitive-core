### FINAL DETERMINATION
The initial denial of coverage for CPT code 22551 (Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion, C5-C6, one level) is **OVERTURNED**. 

HealthFirst Health Plan is directed to **immediately authorize** the requested surgical procedure. Under California law and generally accepted standards of medical practice, the plan’s conservative treatment requirements are clinically and legally waived due to the presence of objective structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) and documented clinical urgency.

---

### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Diana Torres is a 61-year-old female with a progressive, 4-month history of cervical myelopathy characterized by bilateral hand clumsiness, gait instability, and severe central spinal cord compression with T2 hyperintensity (myelomalacia) at the C5-C6 level. HealthFirst Utilization Management denied the request on November 1, 2024, citing a failure to complete a mandatory 6-week course of physical therapy (the member completed only 2 weeks) and a failure to trial at least two pharmacotherapy agents (the member trialed one) under internal clinical policy CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2.

This appeal review resolves the conflict between HealthFirst's internal criteria, generally accepted clinical standards, and California regulatory requirements. Applying the governing **Hierarchy of Authority**:
$$\text{California State Law \& Regulations} > \text{Generally Accepted Clinical Standards} > \text{Plan Internal Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)}$$

The plan's denial is overturned. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 prohibit health plans from requiring conservative therapy trials in patients with objective structural neurological compromise where delay risks irreversible harm. Furthermore, peer-reviewed clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021) classify this presentation as a Tier 1 Strong Surgical Indication, rendering the plan's 6-week physical therapy requirement clinically contraindicated.

### EVALUATION AND ANALYSIS BY STEP

#### Step 1: Procedural Defect and Notice Adequacy Review (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
The initial denial notice issued on November 1, 2024, was reviewed for procedural compliance under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b). 
*   **Findings:** The denial notice specified the exact internal clinical criteria applied (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2), detailed the specific clinical reasons for the denial (completion of 2 weeks of physical therapy instead of 6 weeks; trial of only one pharmacotherapy agent instead of two), and included required information regarding Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights.
*   **Conclusion:** The denial notice is procedurally adequate. There are no fatal administrative defects under CHSC § 1374.31(b). The evaluation proceeds to substantive clinical and regulatory review.

#### Step 2: Clinical Classification and Diagnostic Verification (Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy)
A critical diagnostic distinction must be established, as cervical radiculopathy (nerve root compression) and cervical myelopathy (spinal cord compression) follow entirely separate therapeutic pathways.
*   **Objective Imaging Findings:** The cervical spine MRI dated August 15, 2024, demonstrates severe central cord compression at C5-C6 with T2-weighted hyperintensity within the cord. This T2 hyperintensity is diagnostic of **myelomalacia**—an objective indicator of existing, structural spinal cord injury.
*   **Clinical Neurological Examination:** The physical examination dated August 10, 2024, reveals progressive upper motor neuron (UMN) signs: bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia in both the upper and lower extremities, bilateral positive Babinski signs, and documented gait ataxia.
*   **Conclusion:** The clinical presentation is verified as **progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia** (C5-C6). This is a severe, central spinal cord disease, not a simple radiculopathy or axial neck pain condition.

#### Step 3: State Regulatory Override & Exception Analysis (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)
California statutes and regulatory mandates strictly regulate the application of step-therapy and conservative treatment prerequisites in patients with structural neurological compromise.

1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) Override:** 
    Under CIC § 10169.5, a health plan *shall not* condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the condition involves "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Severe spinal cord compression with myelomalacia is a fixed, physical lesion within the spinal canal. Physical therapy cannot expand the spinal canal or decompress the cord; thus, the PT requirement is legally waived under § 10169.5(a)(3).
2.  **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) Contraindication Override:**
    A plan cannot mandate physical therapy if the treating physician documents that PT is contraindicated. Dr. James Kim documented on October 15, 2024, that physical therapy is contraindicated due to myelomalacia and that continuing PT risks irreversible, permanent cord injury. Additionally, clinical records demonstrate that during a brief 2-week trial (Valley PT, September 1, 2024, to September 14, 2024, 6 sessions), the patient experienced severe symptom exacerbation, leading to a documented medical order to discontinue therapy. This satisfies the exemption under § 10169.5(a)(1).
3.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 Override:**
    APL 22-014 mandates that plans *shall not* require a full course of conservative treatment where objective imaging shows progressive pathology (such as cord signal change/myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents clinical urgency. The presence of myelomalacia on Ms. Torres's MRI, paired with her progressive gait instability and Dr. Kim's attestation of urgency, triggers a mandatory waiver of all standard conservative trials, including the plan's pharmacotherapy and physical therapy timelines.
*   **Conclusion:** State regulatory overrides apply in full. The plan's conservative therapy prerequisites are legally superseded and waived.

#### Step 4: Generally Accepted Clinical Standards Review (AANS/CNS 2023 & AO Spine 2021)
Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), medical necessity must be determined using generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersede more restrictive internal plan policies.

*   **Clinical Guidelines (AANS/CNS Joint Section 2023 Guidelines):**
    For moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with corresponding myelomalacia (T2 cord signal change), surgical decompression is classified as a **Tier 1 Strong Surgical Indication**. 
*   **AO Spine Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021) & Cochrane Review (2021):**
    There is no clinical evidence supporting a mandatory course of conservative management or physical therapy prior to surgery for moderate-to-severe myelopathy. As noted by AO Spine (2021), delaying surgical decompression to complete a physical therapy trial has no therapeutic basis, does not produce functional recovery, and actively exposes the patient to a high risk of permanent, irreversible neurological deficit. 
*   **Scientific Validity of the Plan's Denial:**
    The plan’s 6-week physical therapy and multi-drug pharmacotherapy requirements are derived from evidence-based guidelines for *cervical radiculopathy* (such as the NASS 2020 guidelines). Applying these parameters to *cervical myelopathy* constitutes a fundamental clinical category error. 
*   **Conclusion:** The requested ACDF (CPT 22551) at C5-C6 is highly medically necessary, and delaying surgery for conservative treatment is clinically contraindicated.

#### Step 5: Plan Criteria and Internal Exception Review (CC-SPINE-2024)
*   **Plan Application:** Under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, the plan requires 6 weeks of physical therapy and 4 weeks of two pharmacotherapy agents. Under Exception 2.2(A) ("Myelopathy Pathway"), the physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks, and the interventional therapy requirement is waived, but the 2-agent pharmacotherapy trial remains mandatory.
*   **Deficiency in Plan Review:** The member completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy (discontinued due to worsening symptoms) and trialed only one pharmacotherapy agent (methylprednisolone taper). Thus, the member did not technically meet even the modified requirements of the plan's internal Exception 2.2(A).
*   **Conflict Resolution:** While the member did not satisfy the plan's internal Exception 2.2(A), the plan's utilization reviewer failed to apply the higher-level authorities in the governing hierarchy. Under California law (CHSC § 1374.32(b)), the plan's internal criteria cannot be the sole or determinative basis for a medical necessity denial when they conflict with state regulatory overrides (CIC § 10169.5, APL 22-014) and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023). Because these higher authorities mandate the waiver of conservative trials for this specific clinical presentation, the plan's internal criteria are rendered inapplicable.

### DISPOSITION SUMMARY

The evaluation plan's disposition decision tree yields the following pathway:
1.  **Procedural Defect?** No (Proceed to clinical and regulatory evaluation).
2.  **Does California State Law (APL 22-014 / CIC 10169.5) apply due to myelomalacia/cord compression?** Yes. Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) & (3) and DMHC APL 22-014, the physical therapy and conservative management requirements are legally waived.
3.  **Does the case meet Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS Tier 1 Met)?** Yes. Moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia is a Tier 1 surgical indication where delay is clinically contraindicated.
4.  **Final Decision:** **OVERTURN** the denial.

HealthFirst is ordered to authorize coverage for CPT code 22551 for member Diana Torres immediately.
