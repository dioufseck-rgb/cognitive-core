## **EVALUATION & DETERMINATION REPORT**

### **Step 1: Procedural Adequacy and Defect Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))**
A review of the initial denial notice dated November 18, 2024, written by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, was conducted to assess compliance with California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b).
*   **Findings:** The denial letter specified the internal medical criteria applied (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2) and detailed the specific clinical reason for the denial (lack of conservative treatment, specifically citing 0 weeks of physical therapy, no documented pharmacotherapy, and no interventional procedures). 
*   **Determination:** The initial denial was **Procedurally Adequate**. The plan met its statutory notice obligations under CHSC § 1374.31(b), allowing this appeal review to proceed directly to the clinical and statutory merits of the case.

---

### **Step 2: Diagnosis Verification & Disease Classification (Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy)**
The clinical documentation and imaging reports from UCSF Medical Center were analyzed to determine whether the patient presents with cervical radiculopathy (nerve root compression) or cervical myelopathy (spinal cord compression).
*   **Clinical Presentation:** On November 10, 2024, the member presented to the Emergency Department with an acute, 72-hour onset of bilateral arm weakness (2/5) and bilateral leg weakness (3/5), accompanied by gait/walking difficulties.
*   **Neurological Examination:** Objective physical findings include hyperreflexia in all extremities, bilateral Babinski signs, and urinary urgency. This constellation of upper motor neuron (UMN) signs and bilateral deficits establishes a diagnosis of an **incomplete spinal cord syndrome**.
*   **Objective Imaging:** An urgent MRI performed on November 10, 2024, revealed severe central spinal cord compression at the C5-C6 level with extensive T2-weighted hyperintensity throughout the cord, indicating extensive **myelomalacia** (acute structural spinal cord injury).
*   **Classification:** The member's primary presentation is definitively classified as **Acute Cervical Myelopathy**. This is a severe, progressive spinal cord disease, distinct from radiculopathy.

---

### **Step 3: Statutory Overrides and Regulatory Compliance Check (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)**
California law provides strict statutory overrides regarding the mechanical application of conservative treatment criteria by health plans.

*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 Compliance:**
    *   Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1)**, a plan cannot condition surgical approval on physical therapy (PT) if the treating physician documents in writing that PT is contraindicated or would cause harm. Dr. Marchetti documented that PT is absolutely contraindicated because manipulating the cervical spine in the presence of severe cord compression and myelomalacia risks converting an incomplete cord syndrome into a complete cord syndrome (permanent quadriplegia).
    *   Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)**, a plan is explicitly prohibited from requiring PT when there is objective evidence of *structural neurological compromise* that physical therapy cannot physically address. Severe C5-C6 central cord compression and myelomalacia represent profound structural compromise.
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 Compliance:**
    *   APL 22-014 mandates that plans *shall not* require a course of conservative treatment where objective imaging shows progressive pathology or myelomalacia, and the treating physician documents clinical urgency. The presence of myelomalacia on the MRI, combined with acute bilateral weakness and urinary urgency, meets this state standard.
*   **Determination:** Statutory protections are **fully triggered**. The health plan is legally prohibited under California law from requiring a trial of physical therapy or conservative treatment as a condition of surgical approval in this case.

---

### **Step 4: Clinical Standard Assessment (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021, NASS 2020)**
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), the standard of review for medical necessity is based on generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersede restrictive internal health plan policies.
*   **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023):** These guidelines designate moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective spinal cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI as a **Tier 1 (Strong Surgical Indication)**. Immediate surgical decompression is the recommended standard of care. Prolonged conservative trials are clinically contraindicated due to the high risk of irreversible neurological deficit.
*   **AO Spine International Guidelines (2021):** These guidelines clarify that there is no high-quality evidence supporting mandatory physical therapy prior to surgery for moderate-to-severe myelopathy. Requiring a 6-week PT trial in patients with active myelomalacia has no clinical basis and may result in catastrophic neurological deterioration.
*   **Cochrane Review (2021):** This literature establishes that conservative management has no efficacy in preventing progression or promoting recovery in moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy.
*   **Determination:** The requested ACDF (CPT 22551) at C5-C6 is **Medically Necessary** under generally accepted clinical standards.

---

### **Step 5: Plan Criteria Alignment & Exception Review (CC-SPINE-2024)**
The health plan's internal medical policy (CC-SPINE-2024) was reviewed.
*   **Standard Pathway (Section 2.2):** Requires 6 weeks of physical therapy, a trial of two pharmacotherapy agents for 4 weeks each, and one interventional procedure.
*   **Exception 2.2(A) — Myelopathy Pathway:** For primary myelopathy, the PT requirement is reduced to 4 weeks and the interventional procedure is waived if the physician documents risks.
*   **Analysis:** Although Exception 2.2(A) applies because myelopathy is the primary diagnosis, the patient has completed 0 weeks of physical therapy and has only received 24 hours of IV methylprednisolone (acute spinal cord injury protocol) in the emergency room. Strictly under the plan’s CC-SPINE-2024 criteria, the member does not meet the plan's 4-week PT or 4-week pharmacotherapy thresholds.
*   **Conflict Resolution:** The **Governing Hierarchy of Authority** dictates that California State Law (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014) and generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023 and AO Spine 2021) supersede internal health plan criteria. Because CHSC § 1374.32(b) mandates that clinical standards govern and statutory overrides apply, the plan's internal requirements for conservative trials are legally waived.

---

## **CONCLUSION & FORMAL ORDER**

This is an acute, emergency neurosurgical situation. Robert Chang presents with an acute, incomplete cervical spinal cord injury (myelopathy) with severe central cord compression and active myelomalacia on MRI. 

1.  **Denial Reversal:** The initial medical necessity denial by Dr. Patricia Huang on November 18, 2024, was a category error, incorrectly applying radiculopathy-derived conservative trial requirements (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2) to an acute, severe cervical myelopathy presentation.
2.  **Statutory Waiver:** Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)** and **DMHC APL 22-014**, the health plan cannot legally condition surgical authorization on physical therapy or conservative trials for this diagnosis.
3.  **Clinical Necessity:** Under **AANS/CNS 2023** and **AO Spine 2021 Guidelines**, immediate surgical decompression via ACDF (CPT 22551) is the standard of care to prevent permanent, irreversible paralysis.

**Order:** The health plan's initial denial is **OVERTURNED**. Immediate authorization is **GRANTED** for **CPT Code 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level)** to be performed by Dr. Elena Marchetti, MD at UCSF Medical Center.
