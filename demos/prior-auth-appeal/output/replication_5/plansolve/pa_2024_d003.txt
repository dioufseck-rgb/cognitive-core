### FINAL DISPOSITION
**UPHOLD:** The Health Plan’s decision to deny coverage for CPT Code 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) is **UPHELD**. 

While the initial denial notice was procedurally defective under California law, a comprehensive clinical evaluation demonstrates that the member has not completed the required conservative treatment trials for cervical radiculopathy. No statutory overrides apply to the clinical facts of this case, and the requested surgical intervention is not currently medically necessary under generally accepted clinical standards.

### I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
This appeal arises from the denial of prior authorization for a single-level anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7. The initial denial on October 15, 2024, was conducted via a peer-to-peer telephone call and was not followed by a legally compliant written denial notice. The appellant appeals on the basis of procedural non-compliance, citing California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31. 

A review of the case highlights a severe procedural defect by the plan: the failure to provide written clinical criteria or include the mandatory Independent Medical Review (IMR) notice. However, under the governing clinical and regulatory evaluation framework, a procedural defect alone does not mandate the authorization of an elective surgical procedure if clinical necessity is absent and the medical record is complete. 

The member’s clinical presentation is classic **Cervical Radiculopathy** without myelopathic signs or spinal cord compromise. Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 and generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020), radiculopathy requires a robust 6- to 12-week trial of conservative treatment. Because the clinical record reveals that the member has only completed five weeks of physical therapy, failed to complete a second four-week pharmacotherapy trial, and underwent no interventional procedures, the clinical criteria for surgery are not met. Therefore, the denial is upheld.

### III. PROCEDURAL ADEQUACY & REGULATORY COMPLIANCE CHECK (STEP 1)
The member’s appeal asserts that the plan’s denial was communicated verbally-only during a peer-to-peer call and that no written clinical criteria or IMR rights were provided.

#### Statutory Analysis under CHSC § 1374.31:
*   **CHSC § 1374.31(a)** mandates that when a health plan issues a denial based on medical necessity, it *shall* provide a written notice of the denial containing the specific clinical criteria applied, the clinical reasons for the denial, and a formal notification of the member's right to an Independent Medical Review (IMR).
*   **CHSC § 1374.31(b)** explicitly states: *"A denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is defective and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."*
*   **Finding:** The plan's review process was highly non-compliant. The written denial basis was documented as *"Verbal only — peer-to-peer call,"* the clinical criteria were never sent to the member or provider in writing, and the IMR notice was omitted (*"imr_notice_included: false"*). 

**Procedural Determination:** The plan’s initial denial was **procedurally defective**. Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), this triggers an automatic escalation of the appeal. However, because the clinical record is complete, this reviewer must proceed through the clinical and statutory evaluation steps to render a final determination on the merits of medical necessity.

### V. STATUTORY OVERRIDE EVALUATION (STEP 3)
We must evaluate if California statutory provisions override and waive the plan’s conservative treatment requirements.

#### 1. Evaluation of CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Waiver):
Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)**, a plan cannot condition surgical approval on physical therapy completion if any of the following are met:
*   *(a)(1) PT is contraindicated:* No clinical documentation supports contraindication.
*   *(a)(2) Functional plateau reached:* The treating physician declared a plateau in the appeal letter after 5 weeks of therapy. Under **CIC § 10169.5(b)**, the treating physician's written declaration of a functional plateau is authoritative. This legally waives the remaining 1 week of physical therapy. **[PT REQUIREMENT WAIVED UNDER STATE LAW]**
*   *(a)(3) Structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address:* This sub-clause applies to spinal cord compression or progressive myelomalacia. Standard radiculopathy with foraminal stenosis does not satisfy this sub-clause.

#### 2. Evaluation of DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014:
APL 22-014 prevents plans from requiring conservative therapy trials for urgent, progressive neurological pathology (specifically citing myelomalacia and spinal cord injury).
*   **Finding:** Because the member’s MRI shows isolated nerve root compression without spinal cord signal changes or myelomalacia, **APL 22-014 does not apply**, and the plan’s requirements for non-PT conservative treatments (pharmacotherapy and interventional treatments) remain legally active.

#### 3. Summary of Statutory Overrides:
While **CIC § 10169.5** legally waives the outstanding week of physical therapy due to the documented functional plateau, **no statutory authority waives the requirements for pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)) or interventional treatment (Section 2.2(c))** for cervical radiculopathy.

### VI. CLINICAL STANDARDS & MEDICAL NECESSITY ALIGNMENT (STEP 4)
Under **CHSC § 1374.32(b)** (the Independent Medical Review standard), a service must be approved if it meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, irrespective of restrictive plan criteria.

#### 1. Peer-Reviewed Consensus Guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023 & NASS 2020):
*   For **Cervical Radiculopathy**, surgical intervention (ACDF, CPT 22551) is classified as a **Tier 2 (Reasonable) Indication**, appropriate *only* after an adequate trial of conservative management, typically defined as 6 to 12 weeks of active care including physical therapy, multi-agent pharmacotherapy, and/or interventional injections.
*   Immediate surgery without a full conservative trial is reserved for **Tier 1 (Strong/Urgent) Indications**, such as progressive motor deficits, bowel/bladder dysfunction, or cervical myelopathy with cord compression/myelomalacia. 
*   **AO Spine (2021)** clinical guidelines note that physical therapy and interventional injections (such as cervical epidural steroid injections) have high-quality evidence of efficacy in resolving radicular symptoms, with 70–80% of radiculopathy cases resolving without surgery.
*   **Clinical Application:** The member’s clinical presentation exhibits mild, stable motor weakness (grip strength 4+/5) and sensory changes. There is no progressive, profound neurological deficit or myelopathy. Generally accepted clinical standards mandate the completion of conservative management—specifically an adequate course of a second oral medication and a trial of an interventional cervical injection—prior to exposing the member to the inherent risks of cervical fusion surgery (ACDF).

### VIII. RATIONALE FOR DETERMINATION
The decision tree directs an **UPHOLD** of the plan's denial based on the following integrated findings:

1.  **Procedural Defect Identified (Step 1):** The plan violated **CHSC § 1374.31** by failing to issue a written denial citing specific criteria and omitting IMR rights. This is a severe administrative failure.
2.  **No Comprehensive Statutory Override (Step 3):** Under California law, procedural defects do not automatically transmute medically unnecessary care into covered benefits. While **CIC § 10169.5** successfully overrides and waives the 6-week physical therapy duration (accepting the 5-week course as complete due to the documented functional plateau), no state law waives the remaining conservative prerequisites for radiculopathy.
3.  **Failure to Meet Clinical and Plan Criteria (Step 2 & 4):** The clinical records confirm the diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy. The member has failed to complete a second 4-week trial of pharmacotherapy (the Prednisone trial was only 2 weeks) and has had no interventional treatments (such as a cervical epidural steroid injection).
4.  **Clinical Practice Standards (Step 4):** Under **AANS/CNS (2023)** and **NASS (2020)** clinical standards, elective single-level ACDF (CPT 22551) is not clinically indicated or medically necessary for stable radiculopathy until conservative options, including interventional pain management and completed pharmacotherapy, have been fully exhausted.

### IX. DIRECTIVES TO THE PLAN
Although the denial of coverage for the surgery is sustained on clinical grounds, the health plan is issued the following **mandatory directives**:

1.  **Procedural Corrective Action:** The plan is ordered to immediately cease the practice of issuing "verbal-only" prior authorization denials for surgical procedures.
2.  **Compliance Mandate:** Pursuant to **CHSC § 1374.31**, the plan must issue a written denial notice for all future medical necessity adverse determinations, which must explicitly cite the specific internal coverage criteria applied, detail the precise clinical rationale, and enclose the mandatory DMHC/IMR notice of appeal rights.
3.  **Evidence of Compliance:** The plan must submit evidence of staff training on **CHSC § 1374.31** and **CIC § 10169.5** to the Department within 45 days of this order.
