## II. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY OF FINDINGS
An independent, hierarchy-based clinical appeal review has been conducted regarding the medical necessity of a three-level anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C3-C4, C4-C5, and C5-C6. 

Based on California statutory mandates (CIC § 10169.5, CHSC § 1374.32(b), and DMHC APL 22-014), generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021), and HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), a **Partial Approval** is mandated:
1.  **C4-C5** is approved due to severe spinal cord compression and active myelomalacia (T2 signal change) representing structural neurological compromise.
2.  **C5-C6** is approved because the clinical record demonstrates a moderate disc herniation with left C6 nerve root compression and correlating motor deficits (left bicep weakness 4-/5, diminished bicep reflex) that has failed a comprehensive, 9-week course of conservative treatment (exceeding plan criteria).
3.  **C3-C4** is denied because the imaging shows only mild degenerative changes without spinal cord or nerve root compression, and clinical evaluations confirm no localizing neurological deficits or clear symptomatic contribution, representing axial neck pain only.

## IV. STEP-BY-STEP EVALUATION & ANALYSIS

### STEP 1: Procedural Adequacy Review (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
The initial denial notice issued by Dr. Alan Foster on November 25, 2024, was analyzed. 
*   **Finding:** The denial notice stated: *"Three-level ACDF not approved. Review of imaging and clinical record supports surgical intervention at one level (C4-C5) only. C3-C4 and C5-C6 do not demonstrate pathology of sufficient severity to warrant surgical intervention at this time."*
*   **Procedural Deficiency:** This notice failed to cite the specific plan clinical policy document (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024) and failed to provide a detailed, individualized clinical explanation or specific clinical criteria applied to distinguish the pathology of C4-C5 from C5-C6.
*   **Regulatory Impact:** Under **CHSC § 1374.31(b)**, a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason is **procedurally defective**. While this deficiency legally compromises the validity of the initial denial, this appeal proceeds to substantive clinical evaluation to issue a definitive, clinically sound decision.

### STEP 4: State Law & Regulatory Override Analysis (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)
California statutory provisions override plan clinical criteria where restrictive practices delay necessary neurological care:

*   **Application to C4-C5 (Myelopathy):**
    Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)** and **DMHC APL 22-014**, plans cannot condition surgical approval on conservative therapy when objective imaging demonstrates structural neurological compromise (specifically myelomalacia/cord signal change) that physical therapy cannot address. Delaying surgery in the presence of active myelomalacia risks irreversible spinal cord injury. Statutory overrides mandate immediate approval of the C4-C5 level.
*   **Application to C5-C6 (Radiculopathy):**
    Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)**, the plan is prohibited from requiring further conservative trials as the member completed a comprehensive PT course (9 weeks, 27 sessions) and the physical therapist/physician documented a functional plateau ("radicular symptoms persist").
*   **Application to C3-C4:**
    No statutory overrides apply to C3-C4. There is no cord compression, myelomalacia, or structural compromise at this level. The standard plan exclusions for axial neck pain are legally and clinically valid for C3-C4.

---

### STEP 5: Clinical Standard & Evidence-Based Guidelines Review (CHSC § 1374.32(b))
As mandated by **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**, the standard of review for medical necessity is based on generally accepted clinical standards, which override restrictive plan criteria:

*   **C4-C5 (Myelopathy):**
    *   *AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) / AO Spine Guidelines (2021):* Decompression is classified as a **Tier 1 (Strong) Indication** for moderate-to-severe myelopathy with spinal cord compression and myelomalacia. Standard clinical guidelines state there is no evidentiary basis for requiring physical therapy for moderate-to-severe myelopathy, and delay is strongly associated with permanent neurological deficits.
*   **C5-C6 (Radiculopathy):**
    *   *NASS Guidelines (2020) / AANS/CNS Guidelines (2023):* Decompression and fusion are indicated (**Tier 2**) for cervical radiculopathy with objective motor weakness (4-/5 left bicep weakness) that has failed to improve after 6–12 weeks of conservative management (9 weeks completed). Surgical decompression is medically necessary to prevent progressive motor deficits.
*   **C3-C4 (Axial Neck Pain):**
    *   *AANS/CNS Guidelines (2023) / NASS Guidelines (2020):* Surgery is classified as **Tier 3 (Not Indicated)** for axial neck pain in the absence of radicular symptoms, myelopathy, or correlating structural compression. Prophylactic multi-level fusion at an asymptomatic level showing only mild degenerative changes is not supported by clinical evidence and carries unnecessary surgical risk.

## V. LEVEL-BY-LEVEL DETERMINATION DETAILS

### 1. Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C4-C5 — APPROVED
*   **Clinical Basis:** The patient exhibits severe spinal cord compression with active myelomalacia (T2 signal change) on MRI, presenting clinically with gait disturbance, bilateral deltoid weakness (4/5), bilateral hyperreflexia, and a positive Hoffman sign.
*   **Authority Citation:** **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Tier 1**; **DMHC APL 22-014** (myelomalacia constitutes a time-sensitive neurological condition overriding standard conservative requirements); **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)**.

### 2. Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C5-C6 — APPROVED
*   **Clinical Basis:** The patient has a moderate left-sided C5-C6 disc herniation causing moderate left C6 nerve root compression. This correlates directly with objective clinical findings of C6 radiculopathy, including left bicep weakness (4-/5) and a diminished left bicep reflex. The member has failed a robust 9-week course of PT, multiple medications, and a cervical epidural steroid injection, meeting all criteria.
*   **Authority Citation:** **HealthFirst Plan Policy CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2**; **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)** (functional plateau reached); **NASS Evidence-Based Guidelines (2020)**.

### 3. Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C3-C4 — DENIED
*   **Clinical Basis:** MRI shows only a mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing. The physical exam shows neck pain on palpation without any radiating dermatomal symptoms, motor deficits, or myelopathic signs localizing to this level. The physical therapy notes state that any clinical contribution from the C3-C4 level is "unclear." Surgical fusion at this level would constitute treatment for non-specific axial neck pain, which is clinically inappropriate.
*   **Authority Citation:** **HealthFirst Plan Policy CC-SPINE-2024 Section 3.1 & 3.3**; **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Tier 3 (Not Indicated)**.
