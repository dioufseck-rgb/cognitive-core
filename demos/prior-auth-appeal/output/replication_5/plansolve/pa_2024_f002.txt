### FINAL DISPOSITION

### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The member, Steven Clark, a 46-year-old male with California-based coverage, has been recommended for a single-level C5-C6 ACDF (CPT 22551) by his treating neurosurgeon, Dr. Sarah Nguyen, MD, due to symptomatic, progressive C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficit. 

The initial prior authorization request was denied on October 10, 2024, citing reason code **MED-003** (conflicting physical therapy documentation). The plan’s initial review noted an inability to verify the required duration of conservative physical therapy (PT) because the records initially submitted for Coastal Physical Therapy documented only 4 weeks, creating a conflict with the member's claimed 10 weeks of total therapy.

Upon review of the comprehensive appeal file, the documentation conflict is successfully and completely resolved. The member's appeal enclosed the full, complete records from Coastal Physical Therapy, which confirm a full 6-week course (18 sessions) from June 1, 2024, to July 12, 2024. Combined with the previously verified 6-week course (18 sessions) at Riverside Physical Therapy (April 1, 2024, to May 10, 2024), the member has completed a total of **12 weeks (36 sessions) of supervised physical therapy**. 

This conservative trial meets and significantly exceeds the plan’s 6-week criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a)). Furthermore, the therapy records document a "functional plateau" at both providers, triggering a statutory waiver of additional conservative therapy under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2). Because all clinical, imaging, pharmacotherapy, and interventional criteria under both plan guidelines and California state law are satisfied, the initial denial is overturned, and the procedure is approved.

### STEP-BY-STEP EVALUATION & FINDINGS

#### STEP 1: Procedural Adequacy Review (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
The initial denial notice issued on October 10, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang, cited reason code MED-003, outlined the specific documentation discrepancy regarding the physical therapy duration, and referenced the plan's verification requirements. Procedural adequacy under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b) is confirmed; the denial provided the specific clinical reason for the initial determination. However, the plan's clinical reasoning must now be re-evaluated under Step 2 through Step 4 using the newly submitted complete appeal records.

#### STEP 2: Clinical Classification & Diagnostic Verification
A detailed review of the clinical record and diagnostic imaging was performed to differentiate between Cervical Radiculopathy and Cervical Myelopathy:
*   **Imaging (MRI dated August 15, 2024):** Demonstrates a large disc herniation at C5-C6 resulting in severe right C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis. Crucially, there is no spinal cord compression, cord signal change, or myelomalacia documented on this MRI.
*   **Neurological Examination (dated August 20, 2024):** Documents unilateral, right-sided findings: right C6 dermatomal numbness, bicep weakness (4/5 motor strength on the right), an absent right bicep reflex, and a positive right Spurling sign. No upper motor neuron signs (such as hyperreflexia, Hoffman sign, clonus, bilateral weakness, or gait disturbances) are present.
*   **Diagnostic Classification:** The patient’s clinical presentation and imaging are classic for **Cervical Radiculopathy C5-C6 with objective, functional neurological deficit** (right bicep weakness and reflex loss). 
*   **Clinical Pathway:** Because there is no evidence of spinal cord compression or myelopathy, the patient is classified under the **Standard Conservative Pathway** (requiring evaluation of conservative trial completion).

#### STEP 3: Statutory and Clinical Standard Override Evaluation (Myelopathy Pathway)
Because the patient is classified under the standard radiculopathy pathway (no active myelomalacia or progressive spinal cord injury), the high-urgency myelopathy exception under DMHC APL 22-014 and Tier 1 clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023) does not apply. The physical therapy requirements cannot be waived based on acute spinal cord danger. Therefore, conservative treatment compliance must be assessed in Step 4.

#### STEP 4: Plan Criteria Compliance & PT Documentation Conflict Resolution

##### A. Resolution of the Physical Therapy Documentation Conflict
The initial denial occurred because the member's submitted Coastal PT records only documented 4 weeks (weeks 1–4 of the course), creating a conflict with the member's reported 10 weeks of total PT. 

The member's appeal has successfully resolved this discrepancy:
1.  **Riverside Physical Therapy:** Complete records received and verified. 
    *   *Duration:* 6 weeks (April 1, 2024, to May 10, 2024).
    *   *Volume:* 18 sessions.
    *   *Outcome:* Therapist documents a clear functional plateau; symptoms persist.
2.  **Coastal Physical Therapy:** Complete records have now been submitted with the appeal (the final 2 weeks of records, previously missing, were faxed separately and are now integrated). 
    *   *Duration:* 6 weeks (June 1, 2024, to July 12, 2024).
    *   *Volume:* 18 sessions.
    *   *Outcome:* No improvement in neurological deficits. Therapist documents a clear functional plateau ("maximum therapeutic benefit reached").

*   **PT Summary Findings:** In total, the member completed **12 weeks of supervised PT and 36 sessions** across two providers. Both providers documented that the member reached a formal **functional plateau** where further therapy would not yield clinical benefit. This completely satisfies and exceeds the 6-week requirement outlined in **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a)**.

##### B. Statutory Alignment (Tier 1 Authority — CIC § 10169.5)
Under **California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2)**, a health plan is legally prohibited from conditioning the approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of further physical therapy if:
> *"The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."*

Because both Riverside PT and Coastal PT documented a "functional plateau" (defined clinically as a failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement and reaching maximum therapeutic benefit), forcing any further PT trials or denying surgery based on PT duration is a direct violation of California statutory law.

##### C. Evaluation of Remaining Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)
To ensure comprehensive compliance for approval, all other criteria in Section 2 of CC-SPINE-2024 were cross-referenced against the clinical record:
1.  **Section 2.1 (Diagnosis):** Satisfied. Confirmed C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficit (4/5 bicep weakness, absent bicep reflex) and corresponding MRI (August 15, 2024) within 6 months of the request.
2.  **Section 2.2(b) (Pharmacotherapy):** Satisfied. The member completed two neuropathic agent trials for >4 weeks each:
    *   *Gabapentin 600mg TID:* Taken for 5 months (partial relief).
    *   *Duloxetine 60mg daily:* Taken for 3 months (partial neuropathic benefit).
3.  **Section 2.2(c) (Interventional Treatment):** Satisfied. The member underwent a Right C6 epidural steroid injection on August 28, 2024, which yielded 60% temporary relief before symptoms returned to baseline within 3 weeks.
4.  **Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement):** Satisfied. MRI of August 15, 2024, is within the 6-month window and shows severe C6 nerve root compression matching the right-sided C5-C6 ACDF request.
5.  **Section 2.4 (Physician Documentation):** Satisfied. Dr. Sarah Nguyen provided complete documentation detailing the failure of conservative treatment, clinical rationale, and functional limitations.

### CLINICAL EVIDENCE ALIGNMENT (TIER 2)
The approval of surgical decompression for this member is fully supported by generally accepted standards of medical practice (NASS 2020, AANS/CNS 2023). Under **California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32(b)**, the standard of medical necessity requires alignment with clinical guidelines over restrictive or administrative plan interpretations. 

The clinical evidence demonstrates:
*   **Indication for Surgery:** According to the AANS/CNS 2023 Joint Guidelines, surgical decompression via ACDF is a **Tier 2 Reasonable Indication / Standard of Care** for patients with cervical radiculopathy who exhibit objective neurological deficits (motor weakness and reflex loss) and have failed a structured 6-to-12-week trial of conservative management.
*   **Conservative Failure:** The member has undergone a highly robust 12-week trial of physical therapy, multiple neuropathic medications, and an epidural steroid injection without long-term relief. Prolonging surgical intervention in the face of an active motor deficit (4/5 bicep weakness) and reflex loss carries a risk of permanent, irreversible neurological damage.
