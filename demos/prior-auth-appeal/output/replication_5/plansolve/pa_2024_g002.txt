### IV. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY & FINDINGS OF FACT

#### A. Clinical Presentation & Physical Examination
The medical record documents a 56-year-old female with a progressive, symptomatic cervical myelopathy. A comprehensive neurological examination performed by a consulting neurologist on September 5, 2024, revealed the following objective findings:
*   **Bilateral Hoffman signs** (pathognomonic for upper motor neuron compromise).
*   **Bilateral hyperreflexia** in the upper extremities.
*   **Mildly impaired tandem gait** (demonstrating early spinal cord-related ataxia).
*   Documented **bilateral hand clumsiness** and fine motor dysfunction.
*   *Note:* No lower extremity upper motor neuron signs were present at the time of examination.

A subsequent specialist neurological consultation on September 12, 2024, explicitly concluded: *"Clinical picture consistent with cervical myelopathy. Bilateral upper motor neuron (UMN) signs are pathognomonic. Diagnosis: cervical myelopathy, primary."* The condition was coded under ICD-10 G35.89 (cervical myelopathy).

#### B. Diagnostic Imaging
An MRI of the cervical spine dated September 1, 2024, demonstrates **moderate central spinal cord compression at the C5–C6 level** secondary to degenerative changes, alongside bilateral foraminal stenosis. Consistent with early-to-moderate myelopathy, the imaging did not show T2 signal changes or overt myelomalacia at this stage, establishing that the structural compression is severe enough to cause clinical symptoms but has not yet progressed to permanent, irreversible focal myelomalacia.

#### C. Conservative Management History
The member has attempted and failed a structured course of conservative therapy targeted to her cervical condition:
1.  **Physical Therapy:** Completed **5 weeks (15 sessions)** of physical therapy under a specialized myelopathy protocol. The physical therapy was halted at week 5 by the treating neurologist because the member demonstrated **zero improvement in her upper motor neuron signs** (hand clumsiness and gait impairment), and the physician determined that continuing PT would not resolve the structural compression.
2.  **Pharmacotherapy:**
    *   *Baclofen (10mg TID):* Completed a **3-month trial** with minimal relief of spasticity.
    *   *Methylprednisolone dose pack:* Completed a **3-week course** which provided only temporary, transient improvement.
3.  **Interventional Procedures:** Not performed. Dr. James Chen, MD, formally documented a clinical waiver: *"Interventional procedure not indicated — cord compression with myelopathy requires surgical evaluation, not injection."*

### V. CLINICAL & LEGAL ANALYSIS

#### 1. Factual and Clinical Error in Plan Criteria Application
The plan’s utilization review denied the procedure under **CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 (Standard Pathway)**, which requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. This represents a clear factual and clinical application error.

Section 2.2 of the plan's guidelines contains **Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway)**. Under this exception:
*   The conservative treatment requirement is modified specifically for patients with a confirmed primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (defined in Section 2.1(b) as spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms such as gait disturbance, hyperreflexia, and positive Hoffman signs).
*   The physical therapy requirement is reduced from 6 weeks to **a minimum of 4 weeks**.
*   The interventional treatment requirement (Section 2.2(c)) is **waived** if the treating physician documents that interventional procedures pose a risk of neurological deterioration or are otherwise contraindicated.

**Application to this case:** 
*   The member has a confirmed primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy supported by pathognomonic physical signs (Hoffman's, hyperreflexia, clumsiness) and objective imaging showing moderate central spinal cord compression.
*   The member completed **5 weeks** of physical therapy, which **exceeds** the plan's 4-week myelopathy requirement.
*   The treating neurosurgeon documented a clinical waiver for the interventional injections, stating that injections are not indicated for central cord compression and myelopathy. This satisfies the waiver criteria under Section 2.2(A).
*   The member trialed two conservative pharmacological agents (muscle relaxants and oral corticosteroids). 

By applying the 6-week standard radiculopathy pathway instead of the 4-week Myelopathy Exception, the plan misapplied its own clinical coverage criteria. Under the correct plan pathway, the member met all authorized requirements.

#### 2. Statutory Overrides Under California Law
Even if the plan's internal criteria mandated 6 weeks of physical therapy without exception, the plan's physical therapy requirement is legally overridden by California statutory law:

*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2) [Functional Plateau]:** A health plan cannot condition surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy if the member has completed a course of therapy and reached a functional plateau. The member completed 5 weeks (15 sessions) of PT, and the treating specialist documented that the member reached a functional plateau with no improvement in neurological or upper motor neuron signs, leading the physician to halt the therapy.
*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) [Structural Neurological Compromise]:** A health plan is prohibited from requiring physical therapy where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. The member's MRI demonstrates objective, moderate central spinal cord compression. Physical therapy cannot anatomically relieve central spinal cord compression; therefore, requiring further PT violates § 10169.5(a)(3).
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 [Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions]:** This regulatory guidance states that health plans shall not require a full course of conservative treatment where delaying surgery risks irreversible neurological harm. Myelopathy with objective cord compression is a progressive, time-sensitive condition. Delaying decompression to complete an arbitrary 6-week administrative trial of physical therapy in a patient with progressive upper motor neuron signs risks permanent cord damage.

#### 3. Standard of Medical Necessity and Generally Accepted Clinical Standards
Under **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b)**, the standard of review for medical necessity is not determined solely by a health plan's internal policies, but by generally accepted standards of medical practice.

*   **AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023):** Moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective spinal cord compression on imaging constitutes a **Tier 1 strong surgical indication**. Immediate or early surgical decompression is the recommended primary treatment of choice.
*   **AO Spine Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):** There is **no high-quality scientific evidence** supporting a mandatory trial of physical therapy prior to surgical decompression in patients with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy. The guidelines state: *"Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."*
*   **Clinical Category Error:** Applying radiculopathy-derived conservative treatment timelines (6 weeks of PT) to a patient with myelopathy is a clinical category error. Radiculopathy (nerve root compression) responds well to conservative measures, whereas established cervical myelopathy (spinal cord compression) is a mechanical, structural pathology that does not resolve with physical therapy and is progressive in nature.
