**Disposition: OVERTURN DENIAL**

**Warrant:**

This prior authorization appeal for an ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) for member Frances Kim has been reviewed against HealthFirst PPO Gold's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), relevant California state laws and regulations, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice. As an expert health plan appeals reviewer, I apply the three-source hierarchy: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria, meaning that higher authority governs in cases of conflict.

The initial denial (reason code MED-003) stated: "Myelopathy exception does not apply. Imaging shows mild cord contact only — no cord compression, no cord signal change. Upper motor neuron signs on exam are not conclusive for myelopathy in the absence of imaging confirmation. Standard 6-week PT requirement applies; member completed 5 weeks."

Upon thorough review, the plan's denial is found to be inconsistent with both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.

**1. Myelopathy Diagnosis and Imaging Requirements:**
The plan denied the application of its myelopathy exception (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A)) by asserting that myelopathy was not "confirmed." This determination was based on the MRI showing "mild cord contact only — no cord compression, no cord signal change" and the assertion that "upper motor neuron signs on exam are not conclusive for myelopathy in the absence of imaging confirmation."
*   **Clinical Presentation:** The member's neurological examination (2024-09-05) definitively presents with multiple positive upper motor neuron (UMN) signs. These include bilateral Hoffman sign, bilateral upper extremity hyperreflexia, sustained clonus at bilateral ankles (3 beats), positive finger escape sign, and a mildly wide-based gait. These findings are classic for clinical cervical myelopathy.
*   **Clinical Standard:** Generally accepted clinical standards for cervical myelopathy, as cited in the provided "CLINICAL REFERENCE SUMMARY" (AANS/CNS Guidelines 2023, Section 1.1), clarify that a diagnosis of clinical myelopathy requires UMN signs on examination. Imaging confirmation is supportive but *not required* for the diagnosis, and "imaging-negative myelopathy is a recognized entity where clinical signs precede MRI changes" (Clinical Record, "clinical_standard_note"). The presence of multiple positive UMN signs, in the absence of other explanations, establishes clinical myelopathy.
*   **State Law:** California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) mandates that medical necessity be determined based on "generally accepted standards of medical practice," which overrides a plan's internal coverage criteria if they are more restrictive.
*   **Conflict Resolution:** The plan's position that UMN signs are inconclusive without imaging confirmation, and its requirement for measurable cord compression or signal change to "confirm" myelopathy, directly contradicts generally accepted clinical standards. The member's robust clinical findings establish a diagnosis of cervical myelopathy. Therefore, the plan's interpretation of its own diagnostic criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(b)) is superseded by higher authority.

**2. Physical Therapy Requirement:**
The plan denied the request because the member completed 5 weeks of PT, stating that the "Standard 6-week PT requirement applies." This indicates the plan did not apply its own myelopathy exception which reduces the PT duration.
*   **Plan Criteria (Internal Inconsistency):** HealthFirst's own myelopathy exception (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A)) for confirmed myelopathy reduces the physical therapy requirement to a "minimum 4 weeks." The member completed 5 weeks of physical therapy under a myelopathy protocol. Had the plan correctly acknowledged the clinical diagnosis of myelopathy, the member would have met the plan's *reduced* PT requirement. The plan's failure to apply its own exception, even based on its own criteria, is an internal inconsistency.
*   **State Law (CIC § 10169.5):** California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a) explicitly prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where specific criteria are met. The member's case meets these exemptions:
    *   (a)(1): The treating physician documented "Physician halted PT citing myelopathy concern," indicating PT was unlikely to benefit given the nature of cervical myelopathy.
    *   (a)(2): The member completed 5 weeks of PT, and "Neurological signs unchanged" after 15 sessions indicates a functional plateau has been reached for the neurological symptoms, which PT cannot address (Clinical Standard Knowledge, Section 5).
    *   (a)(3): Clinical cervical myelopathy, even with "mild cord contact" as noted on imaging and objectively confirmed by multiple UMN signs, constitutes "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
*   **Clinical Standard (AO Spine 2021, NASS 2020):** The "CLINICAL REFERENCE SUMMARY" clearly states, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration" (AO Spine Guidelines 2021, Section 2.1). Furthermore, applying radiculopathy-derived PT requirements (like a 6-week minimum) to myelopathy patients is considered a "category error" not supported by evidence (Clinical Standard Knowledge, Section 2.2).
*   **Conflict Resolution:** The plan's insistence on a 6-week PT requirement is overridden by California state law (CIC § 10169.5), as the conditions for waiver of PT have been met. Additionally, this requirement is contrary to generally accepted clinical standards for the management of cervical myelopathy, which do not support mandatory PT prior to surgery for moderate-to-severe myelopathy.

**Conclusion:**
Frances Kim presents with a clear clinical diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, supported by objective neurological findings consistent with generally accepted medical standards. The plan's denial, which relies on a restrictive interpretation of imaging requirements for myelopathy diagnosis and a mandatory physical therapy duration, is directly contradicted by California state law and established clinical guidelines. The member has met the criteria for surgical intervention under generally accepted standards of medical practice, and the requirement for further physical therapy is inappropriate in this clinical context.

Therefore, based on the application of the three-source hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), the denial for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level) for member Frances Kim is **OVERTURNED**. HealthFirst PPO Gold is directed to approve the prior authorization request for this procedure.
