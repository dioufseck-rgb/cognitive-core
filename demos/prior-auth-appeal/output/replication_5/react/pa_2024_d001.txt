### I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) for member Sandra White is **OVERTURNED**.

The primary basis for this overturn is the procedural inadequacy of the initial denial notice, which violates California state law. The denial notice failed to specify the exact clinical criteria applied and did not include notice of the member's Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights, as explicitly required by California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b).

While the plan's substantive assessment regarding incomplete conservative treatment, when reviewed against its criteria for radiculopathy, may hold some merit, the fundamental procedural defects in the denial notice preclude its legal validity.

### IV. REVIEW AGAINST CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK (STATE LAW)

1.  **Procedural Adequacy of Denial Notice (CHSC § 1374.31(b)):**
    *   **Finding:** The denial notice failed to specify the exact clinical criteria applied and did not include notice of the member's right to Independent Medical Review (IMR).
    *   **Law:** CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2) mandates that a denial notice include "the specific criteria applied, the specific clinical reason for the denial, and notice of IMR rights." CHSC § 1374.31(b) explicitly states that "A denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is DEFECTIVE and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."
    *   **Conclusion:** The denial notice is procedurally defective. This is a violation of state law, which holds the highest authority in our review hierarchy.

2.  **Physical Therapy as Prerequisite (CIC § 10169.5):**
    *   **Law:** This section prohibits conditioning surgical approval on PT completion where: (1) PT is contraindicated/unlikely to benefit, (2) member completed PT and reached functional plateau, OR (3) condition involves objective structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address.
    *   **Finding:**
        *   (a)(1): No physician documentation indicates PT is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit for radiculopathy.
        *   (a)(2): While the member *claims* a functional plateau at 4 weeks, the PT discharge note does not use that language, nor does the record provide objective measures (e.g., <10% improvement over 3 sessions) or therapist documentation of maximum benefit. Furthermore, the member did not complete the 6-week minimum required by the plan (or the 6-12 weeks often cited in clinical guidelines for radiculopathy).
        *   (a)(3): The MRI explicitly states "No cord contact. No myelomalacia." This finding is crucial for this provision, which refers to conditions like myelomalacia with cord compression. Therefore, this exception **does not apply** to this radiculopathy case.
    *   **Conclusion:** CIC § 10169.5 does not provide a basis to override the plan's conservative treatment requirements in this specific radiculopathy case.

3.  **Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions (DMHC APL 22-014):**
    *   **Law:** This APL provides exceptions to conservative treatment requirements for "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI" or other progressive pathology risking irreversible harm.
    *   **Finding:** As established, the member has radiculopathy, not myelopathy. There is no cord compression or myelomalacia.
    *   **Conclusion:** DMHC APL 22-014 **does not apply** to this case.

### V. REVIEW AGAINST CLINICAL STANDARD (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)

1.  **Distinction (Section 1.1):** Clinical guidelines strongly differentiate myelopathy from radiculopathy, emphasizing different treatment algorithms. This case is radiculopathy.
2.  **Myelomalacia (Section 1.2):** Myelomalacia is a strong surgical indication. **Not present in this case.**
3.  **Conservative Treatment for Radiculopathy (Section 2.2, 3.1):**
    *   For radiculopathy, surgical intervention is generally a "Reasonable Indication" (TIER 2(b)) "after 6-12 weeks conservative tx."
    *   The member completed 4 weeks of PT, 1 pharmacotherapy agent, and no interventional procedures. This level of conservative treatment is generally considered incomplete for radiculopathy under "generally accepted standards of medical practice" when compared to the 6-12 week recommendation.
    *   The clinical standard for radiculopathy does not provide an override for the plan's specific conservative treatment requirements based on the information provided in this case.

**Conclusion on Clinical Standard:** The clinical standard for radiculopathy suggests a more robust conservative trial (6-12 weeks) than what the member completed. It does not provide an exception for this specific radiculopathy presentation.

### VI. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND FINAL DETERMINATION

The hierarchy of authority is **State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria**.

1.  **Procedural Defect (State Law):** The denial notice issued by HealthFirst is fundamentally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b) due to its failure to specify the exact clinical criteria applied and to include notice of IMR rights. This is a direct violation of state law.
2.  **Substantive Clinical Review:**
    *   For this radiculopathy case, neither California state law (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) nor the clinical standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) provide a basis to *waive* the plan's conservative treatment requirements given the clinical presentation (no myelopathy, no myelomalacia, no documented functional plateau by therapist after "completed" PT).
    *   The member's conservative treatment (4 weeks PT, 1 pharmacotherapy, 0 interventional) falls short of both the plan's specific requirements (6 weeks PT, 2 pharmacotherapy, 1 interventional) and the general recommendation in clinical guidelines for radiculopathy (6-12 weeks conservative treatment).
    *   Therefore, *if* the denial notice had been procedurally sound, the plan's denial based on incomplete conservative treatment for radiculopathy would likely have been upheld on clinical grounds.

**Disposition:** **OVERTURN**

**Warrant:** The denial is OVERTURNED because the denial notice issued by HealthFirst is procedurally defective under California state law. Specifically, the notice fails to specify the exact clinical criteria applied for the denial and does not include the member's right to Independent Medical Review, in direct violation of CHSC § 1374.31(b) and (a)(2) (Clinical Record: procedural_defects; California Regulatory Framework: CHSC § 1374.31). This state law is the highest authority in the review hierarchy. A procedurally defective denial cannot stand.

This overturn does not preclude HealthFirst from re-reviewing the prior authorization request and issuing a new, procedurally compliant denial if, upon re-evaluation, the service still does not meet medically necessary criteria as defined by state law, clinical standards, and the plan's criteria. However, any such new denial must adhere strictly to all state and federal notice requirements.
