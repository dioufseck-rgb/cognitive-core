**DISPOSITION: OVERTURNED**

**WARRANT:**

The denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) for Ms. Sandra White is **OVERTURNED** due to significant procedural defects in the initial denial notice, which constitute a violation of California state law. Per the established hierarchy of authority (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), state law governs in cases of conflict.

**1. Procedural Defects in Denial Notice (Violation of California State Law):**
The initial denial notice, dated 2024-10-05, contained critical deficiencies that violate the California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b). This statute explicitly requires that when a health plan denies a service based on medical necessity, the denial notice SHALL:
    *   Specify the exact clinical criteria applied.
    *   State the specific clinical reason for the denial.
    *   Include notice of the member's right to Independent Medical Review (IMR).

In this case, the denial notice:
    *   Provided only a "Generic reference to plan criteria" without specifying any section number or specific criterion, thus failing to inform the member of the precise basis for the denial.
    *   Did not include the required notice of IMR rights, as confirmed by the clinical record ("imr_notice_missing": true).

CHSC § 1374.31(b) unequivocally states that "A denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is DEFECTIVE and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines." These procedural flaws prevent the member from meaningfully understanding the denial or exercising their state-mandated appeal rights. Such a procedurally defective denial cannot be upheld.

**2. Clinical Presentation and Plan Criteria Review:**

**a. Diagnosis Classification:**
The clinical record indicates a primary diagnosis of "Cervical radiculopathy C6-C7," supported by MRI findings of "C6-C7 disc herniation, moderate right C7 nerve root compression. No cord contact. No myelomalacia" and neurological exam findings of "Right C7 dermatomal numbness, tricep weakness 4/5 right, tricep reflex diminished." This presentation is consistent with cervical radiculopathy (nerve root compression, unilateral dermatomal symptoms, no upper motor neuron signs), not cervical myelopathy (spinal cord compression, myelomalacia, bilateral symptoms, upper motor neuron signs) (Domain Knowledge: CLASSIFICATION; Clinical Standard: SECTION 1.1).

**b. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
For cervical radiculopathy, HealthFirst's plan criteria (Section 2.2) require completion of conservative treatment, specifically:
    *   **Physical Therapy (2.2(a)):** Minimum 6 weeks. The member completed 4 weeks of PT.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2(b)):** Trial of at least two agents for minimum 4 weeks each. The member completed a trial of only one agent (Ibuprofen 800mg TID for 6 weeks).
    *   **Interventional Treatment (2.2(c)):** At least one interventional procedure. The member had no documented interventional treatment.

Based on a substantive review, the member did not meet HealthFirst's conservative treatment requirements for radiculopathy as outlined in Section 2.2 of CC-SPINE-2024.

**c. Myelopathy Exception:**
The plan's "EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY" modifies conservative treatment requirements for confirmed cervical myelopathy. However, as established in section 2a, the member's primary diagnosis is radiculopathy, not myelopathy. Crucially, the MRI specifically notes "No cord contact. No myelomalacia," which are key indicators for myelopathy. Therefore, this exception does not apply to this case.

**3. Application of California Regulatory Requirements (beyond procedural defect):**

**a. CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Prerequisite):**
This statute prevents plans from requiring PT completion where specific conditions are met, such as documented functional plateau (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)) or objective structural neurological compromise (CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)). In this case:
    *   The member claims a functional plateau after 4 weeks of PT, but the "PT discharge does not use that language." CIC § 10169.5(b) requires such a plateau to be "determined by the treating physical therapist or physician." The current documentation does not meet this requirement.
    *   There is no "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address" (e.g., myelomalacia is absent), which would trigger CIC § 10169.5(a)(3).
    *   Therefore, the available documentation does not definitively qualify for a waiver of the PT requirement under CIC § 10169.5.

**b. DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):**
This All-Plan Letter specifically addresses myelopathy, especially cases with myelomalacia, and allows for waiver of conservative treatment requirements in urgent conditions. Since the member has radiculopathy and no myelomalacia, this APL does not apply to this case.

**4. Clinical Standard Review for Radiculopathy:**
Generally accepted clinical guidelines (e.g., AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020) for cervical radiculopathy typically recommend a course of conservative treatment (often 6-12 weeks of PT, pharmacotherapy, and potentially injections) before surgical intervention is considered. The member's conservative treatment (4 weeks PT, one medication, no injections) is generally considered incomplete for radiculopathy under these guidelines (Clinical Standard: SECTION 3.1, TIER 2(b) & TIER 3(c)).

**Conclusion on Conflicts:**
While a substantive review suggests the member's conservative treatment for radiculopathy was incomplete based on both plan criteria and generally accepted clinical standards, these substantive issues are secondary to the primary, overriding legal violation. The plan's failure to issue a procedurally compliant denial notice, as mandated by CHSC § 1374.31(b), renders the denial invalid regardless of the underlying medical necessity.

---

**DETERMINATION:**

For the reasons stated above, particularly the clear violation of California Health & Safety Code § 1374.31(b) regarding the adequacy of denial notices, the initial denial (PA-2024-D001) for Ms. Sandra White is **OVERTURNED**.

HealthFirst is directed to issue a new, procedurally compliant determination for Ms. White's requested service, addressing the specific clinical criteria applied and including all required state-mandated notices, if a denial is still warranted after reconsideration.
