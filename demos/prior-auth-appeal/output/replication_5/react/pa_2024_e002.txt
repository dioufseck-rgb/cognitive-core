**EXPERT APPEALS REVIEWER FINDINGS AND DETERMINATION**

**1. Summary of Clinical Information:**
Catherine Mills is a 53-year-old female presenting with multi-level cervical spondylosis with myeloradiculopathy.

*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-10-01):**
    *   **C3-C4:** Mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing.
    *   **C4-C5:** Large disc herniation, severe cord compression, **T2 signal change (myelomalacia)**.
    *   **C5-C6:** Moderate disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression, foraminal stenosis.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-10-05):**
    *   **Myelopathy Findings:** Bilateral hyperreflexia, bilateral Hoffman sign, wide-based gait.
    *   **Radiculopathy Findings:** Bilateral deltoid weakness 4/5 (C5 level), left bicep weakness 4-/5, bicep reflex diminished left (C6 level).
    *   **C3-C4 Level:** Unclear neurological contribution; neck pain with palpation but no specific radicular pattern.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 9 weeks (27 sessions) completed. Myelopathic symptoms stable but not improved. C5-C6 radicular symptoms persist. Functional plateau documented.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Baclofen 10mg TID for 3 months (minimal spasticity relief), Pregabalin 300mg BID for 4 months (partial pain relief).
    *   **Interventional:** Cervical epidural steroid injection (2024-10-20), resulting in modest improvement in radicular symptoms but **no change in myelopathy signs**.

**2. Analysis of Denial Against HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

*   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **C4-C5:** Confirmed cervical myelopathy with severe cord compression and myelomalacia, correlating with clear myelopathic signs (Hoffman, hyperreflexia, gait disturbance, bilateral weakness). *Meets criterion 2.1(b).*
    *   **C5-C6:** Confirmed cervical radiculopathy with moderate disc herniation, C6 nerve root compression, and left C6 weakness. *Meets criterion 2.1(a).*
    *   **C3-C4:** Mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing with unclear radicular pattern. *Does not clearly meet criterion 2.1(a) or (b) based on the record.*
*   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **Standard Pathway:** Requires 6 weeks PT, 2 pharma agents (4+ weeks each), 1 interventional.
        *   The member completed 9 weeks of PT, 3-4 months of two pharmacotherapy agents, and one interventional procedure. *The duration/type requirements of the standard pathway are met.*
    *   **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY:**
        *   The clinical record confirms myelopathy as the primary diagnosis due to myelomalacia, severe cord compression, and prominent myelopathic signs.
        *   This exception reduces PT to a 4-week minimum and waives interventional if risk is documented. The member completed 9 weeks of PT (exceeds 4-week minimum), and the performed epidural failed to impact myelopathic symptoms. Pharmacotherapy was completed.
        *   **Crucially, myelopathic symptoms were stable but not improved after 9 weeks of PT, and the epidural had "no change in myelopathy signs." A functional plateau for myelopathy has been reached.**
        *   *Conclusion: The conservative treatment requirements under the myelopathy exception are met, and the treatment has failed to address the primary myelopathic condition.*
*   **2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **C4-C5:** Demonstrates severe structural pathology (large disc herniation, severe cord compression, myelomalacia) consistent with myelopathy. *Meets criterion 2.3.*
    *   **C5-C6:** Demonstrates structural pathology (moderate disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression, foraminal stenosis) consistent with radiculopathy. *Meets criterion 2.3.*
    *   **C3-C4:** Demonstrates mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing. The plan reviewer's assessment of "insufficient severity" for this level, coupled with unclear neurological findings, appears consistent with plan criteria as insufficient to warrant surgery.
*   **2.4 PHYSICIAN DOCUMENTATION:** The appeal states the treating surgeon documented reasoning for three-level fusion. Assuming this is compliant, this criterion is met.

**3. Analysis Against California Regulatory Framework (State Law):**

*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):**
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** The presence of cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and *myelomalacia* on MRI (at C4-C5) "constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent." The initial denial failed to acknowledge the urgency and primary nature of myelopathy.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** For conditions with myelomalacia, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when (a) objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia at C4-C5), (b) conservative treatment has been attempted and failed (9 weeks PT, pharma, epidural, with no myelopathy improvement), and (c) surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted guidelines (as described below). This provision **overrides** any plan requirement for a more extensive or "successful" conservative trial for myelopathy.
    *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice." The plan's implied application of conservative treatment requirements as a barrier for myelopathy is more restrictive.
*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):**
    *   **(a)(3):** This code states a plan shall not condition approval on PT completion where "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." *Myelomalacia with severe cord compression at C4-C5 directly falls under this provision.* PT cannot address existing cord injury or severe mechanical compression.
    *   **(a)(2):** This code also applies if "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau." The clinical record indicates that after 9 weeks of PT, myelopathic symptoms were "stable but not improved," and radicular symptoms persisted, with functional plateau documented.
    *   *Conclusion: State law explicitly waives or modifies the PT requirement in this case.*
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (Standard of Review for IMR):** This explicitly states that the "IMR standard does NOT require that the service be consistent with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the service meets the standard in subdivision (a), the reviewer SHALL recommend approval regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." This ensures that generally accepted standards of medical practice take precedence.

**4. Analysis Against Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

*   **Distinction between Myelopathy and Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines emphasize that myelopathy and radiculopathy have different treatment algorithms. Myelopathy, especially with myelomalacia, is more urgent.
*   **Myelomalacia Significance (Section 1.2):** T2 signal change (myelomalacia) indicates existing spinal cord injury, a higher risk of permanent deficit, reduced recovery likelihood, and surgical urgency. It is a "strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials."
*   **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy (Section 2):**
    *   "No high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy." (AO Spine 2021)
    *   "Delaying surgery in such patients... may result in neurological deterioration."
    *   The 6-week PT requirement is derived from radiculopathy evidence, not myelopathy.
    *   For Ms. Mills, the 9 weeks of PT and other conservative treatments failed to improve her myelopathic symptoms.
*   **Surgical Indications (Section 3.1):**
    *   **C4-C5 (Myelopathy):** The member meets **TIER 1 (STRONG INDICATION)** for surgery due to: (a) Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI, (c) Myelomalacia on MRI, and (d) Acute severe myelopathy with risk of permanent deficit (given myelomalacia). This is a compelling indication for surgical intervention.
    *   **C5-C6 (Radiculopathy):** The member meets **TIER 2 (REASONABLE INDICATION)** for surgery due to (b) Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment. Her symptoms persist after 9 weeks of PT and an epidural.
    *   **C3-C4:** The imaging findings (mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing) and neurological exam (unclear) do not meet Tier 1 or Tier 2 criteria for surgical intervention under generally accepted clinical guidelines.

**5. Hierarchy of Authority & Conflict Resolution:**

Applying the three-source hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria):

*   **Conservative Treatment:** HealthFirst's conservative treatment requirements, if interpreted as requiring *further* conservative care or *successful* conservative care for myelopathy, are explicitly overridden by California state law (DMHC APL 22-014, CIC 10169.5(a)(3), CIC 10169.5(a)(2)). Furthermore, they are inconsistent with clinical standards which contraindicate extended conservative trials for myelopathy with myelomalacia. The member has completed an adequate trial, which has failed for the primary myelopathic condition and persistent radicular symptoms.
*   **Severity of Pathology:**
    *   **C4-C5:** The plan's implicit acceptance of C4-C5 pathology as warranting surgery is in full agreement with State Law (DMHC APL 22-014 on myelomalacia) and Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS TIER 1).
    *   **C5-C6:** The plan's denial of this level based on "insufficient severity" is contrary to clinical standards for radiculopathy that has failed an adequate conservative trial (AANS/CNS TIER 2). The objective findings and persistent symptoms warrant surgical consideration.
    *   **C3-C4:** The plan's denial of this level based on "insufficient severity" is consistent with the lack of clear clinical or imaging evidence to meet generally accepted surgical indications.

**6. Disposition:**

**OVERTURNED (Partial)**

The prior authorization denial for the three-level ACDF is **OVERTURNED** for levels **C4-C5 and C5-C6**. The denial for level C3-C4 is **UPHELD**.

**7. Warrant:**

1.  **C4-C5 (Myelopathy):** The member presents with cervical myelopathy characterized by severe cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia) at C4-C5, correlating with objective myelopathic signs (bilateral hyperreflexia, Hoffman sign, wide-based gait). This constitutes a **Tier 1 strong surgical indication** per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update, Section 3.1). California state law (DMHC APL 22-014, Section II.1 & II.2; CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)) explicitly identifies myelomalacia as a condition that should be treated as urgent and for which standard conservative treatment requirements shall be waived, as physical therapy cannot address such structural neurological compromise. The 9 weeks of PT and other conservative treatments failed to improve myelopathic symptoms, indicating a functional plateau. Therefore, surgical intervention for C4-C5 is medically necessary and meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, overriding any more restrictive plan criteria.

2.  **C5-C6 (Radiculopathy):** The member has moderate disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression, and persistent left C6 weakness despite completing 9 weeks of physical therapy, 3-4 months of pharmacotherapy, and an epidural injection which only provided "modest improvement in radicular symptoms." This meets **Tier 2 reasonable surgical indication** criteria per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update, Section 3.1), which recommends surgery for radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment. The conservative treatment requirements outlined in HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2) were met in terms of duration and type, and the treatment has failed to resolve symptoms at this level. The plan's denial based on "insufficient severity" for this level is contrary to generally accepted clinical standards when conservative care has failed.

3.  **C3-C4:** The clinical record for C3-C4 indicates only "mild disc bulge" and "minimal foraminal narrowing" on MRI, and the neurological exam explicitly states findings for this level are "unclear" with no specific radicular pattern. These findings do not meet the objective criteria for surgical intervention as either a Tier 1 (strong) or Tier 2 (reasonable) indication under AANS/CNS Guidelines (2023 Update, Section 3.1) or HealthFirst's own imaging requirement for structural pathology consistent with symptoms (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.3). The treating surgeon's documentation for a three-level fusion, while noted, is not sufficiently supported by the objective clinical evidence for this specific level as presented in the record. Therefore, the denial for C3-C4 is upheld.

**Conclusion:**
The denial by HealthFirst Health Plan for procedure CPT 22551 (ACDF C3-C4, C4-C5, C5-C6) is **partially overturned**. Surgical authorization is granted for ACDF at levels C4-C5 and C5-C6. Authorization for level C3-C4 is denied.

This determination is based on the hierarchy of authority: State law (DMHC APL 22-014, CIC § 10169.5) and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) which supersede HealthFirst's internal criteria where they are more restrictive.
