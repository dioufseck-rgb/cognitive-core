**DISPOSITION: OVERTURN**

**WARRANT:**

The denial for CPT 22551 for a three-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C3-C4, C4-C5, and C5-C6 is **OVERTURNED**. The requested procedure is medically necessary and meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, overriding the plan's more restrictive criteria where conflicts arise, as mandated by California state law.

**Detailed Rationale:**

1.  **Primary Diagnosis of Urgent Cervical Myelopathy:**
    *   The member, Catherine Mills, presents with a confirmed diagnosis of moderate to severe cervical myelopathy, which is the primary and most urgent clinical concern.
    *   **Clinical Evidence:** MRI (2024-10-01) reveals severe cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia) at C4-C5. Neurological exam (2024-10-05) documents objective upper motor neuron signs including bilateral hyperreflexia, bilateral Hoffman sign, wide-based gait, and bilateral deltoid weakness. The presenting diagnosis includes "Multi-level cervical spondylosis with myeloradiculopathy," and conservative treatment notes confirm myelopathic symptoms stable but not improved.
    *   **Applicable Standards:** This clinical presentation unequivocally meets HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(b) (Diagnosis Requirement for Cervical Myelopathy). Furthermore, AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) and NASS Guidelines (2020) classify this as a TIER 1 strong surgical indication (Section 3.1 of Clinical Evidence Base). Myelomalacia is a critical finding, indicating existing cord injury and surgical urgency (Section 1.2, Clinical Evidence Base).

2.  **Conservative Treatment Requirements Overridden by State Law and Clinical Standards:**
    *   The initial denial implicitly relies on HealthFirst's conservative treatment requirements, despite approving C4-C5. The denial of C3-C4 and C5-C6 based on "insufficient severity" may also be related to an expectation of further conservative treatment efficacy which is not applicable here.
    *   **California State Law Mandates Exception:**
        *   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Section II.1 & II.2):** Explicitly states that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia SHALL be treated as urgent, and plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment where objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia) and conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression. This directly applies to Ms. Mills' case.
        *   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3):** Prohibits conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy (PT) completion where there is "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Myelomalacia with severe cord compression falls squarely under this provision.
        *   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2):** Also waives the PT requirement if the member has reached a functional plateau without adequate symptom resolution. Ms. Mills completed 9 weeks of PT, with myelopathic symptoms noted as "stable but not improved," indicating a functional plateau. Neurological symptoms in myelopathy are not expected to respond to PT regardless of duration (Section 5, Clinical Evidence Base).
    *   **Clinical Standards:** AO Spine Guidelines (2021) and AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) explicitly state there is no high-quality evidence supporting mandatory PT prior to surgery for moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients for PT is not supported and may result in neurological deterioration (Section 2.1 & 2.3, Clinical Evidence Base). Myelomalacia is a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials (Section 1.2, Clinical Evidence Base).
    *   **Conclusion:** The member has exceeded the conservative treatment duration that would be clinically appropriate or legally required for her condition. HealthFirst's standard conservative treatment requirements, or even its myelopathy exception, are superseded by state law and generally accepted clinical standards in this case.

3.  **Medical Necessity of All Three Levels (C3-C4, C4-C5, C5-C6):**
    *   **C4-C5 (Approved by initial reviewer):** Meets TIER 1 strong surgical indication due to severe cord compression, myelomalacia, and objective myelopathic signs (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020).
    *   **C5-C6 (Denied by initial reviewer):** Presents with moderate disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression, and objective neurological deficits including left bicep weakness (4-/5) and diminished bicep reflex. These persistent radicular symptoms with objective deficits, despite 9 weeks of PT and an epidural, meet HealthFirst's 2.1(a) and 2.1(c) diagnostic criteria and generally accepted clinical standards for surgical intervention (TIER 2 indication per AANS/CNS 2023). The initial denial's claim of "insufficient severity" is contradicted by the documented neurological findings and failed conservative treatment.
    *   **C3-C4 (Denied by initial reviewer):** Shows mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing. While less severe than the other levels, in the context of multi-level cervical spondylosis with significant myelopathy, surgical practice often includes contiguous levels with mild pathology that may contribute to symptoms or spinal instability, or to prevent adjacent segment disease. The treating surgeon's documented rationale for a three-level fusion, as stated in the appeal basis, supports this comprehensive approach. CHSC § 1374.32(b) mandates that if a service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice," it SHALL be approved, even if the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. Performing ACDF at three levels is not considered experimental (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 3.4).

**Conclusion:**

The member's severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia at C4-C5, combined with symptomatic radiculopathy at C5-C6 and multi-level spondylosis, necessitates surgical intervention. The conservative treatment requirements are overridden by California law and clinical standards due to the urgency and nature of myelopathy with myelomalacia. The denial of C3-C4 and C5-C6 on the basis of "insufficient severity" is not supported by the comprehensive clinical picture, objective findings, and accepted surgical principles for multi-level degenerative cervical disease. Therefore, the denial is **OVERTURNED**, and authorization for CPT 22551 (three-level ACDF C3-C4, C4-C5, C5-C6) is **APPROVED**.
