**I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

This appeal concerns the denial of prior authorization for an ACDF at C5-C6 for Mr. Michael Green. The initial denial cited that the "Member does not meet criteria for requested procedure under plan policy." Our review, applying the three-source hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), finds multiple grounds to overturn the denial. Specifically, the initial denial was procedurally defective, the plan misapplied its own coverage criteria, and the member has met the requirements for conservative treatment as defined by state law and generally accepted clinical standards for cervical radiculopathy.

**III. APPEAL BASIS**

The appeal asserts the following:
1.  No criteria document was attached despite being referenced in the denial.
2.  An Independent Medical Review (IMR) notice was not included.
3.  The member cannot evaluate the denial without the specific criteria that were applied.

**IV. CLINICAL RECORD & PRESENTATION**

*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Cervical radiculopathy C5-C6 with functional impairment.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-20):** C5-C6 large disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression. **Crucially, "No cord contact" noted.** There is no evidence of myelomalacia.

There are no documented signs of cervical myelopathy (e.g., spinal cord compression, upper motor neuron signs like Hoffman's sign or hyperreflexia, bilateral symptoms, gait disturbance, or myelomalacia). This distinction is critical for applying appropriate criteria and guidelines.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 7 weeks (21 sessions), with documented plateau at week 6. Radicular symptoms persist.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Gabapentin 600mg TID for 4 months (partial relief); Naproxen 500mg BID for 3 months (inadequate).
    *   **Interventional:** Left C6 epidural on 2024-10-28, providing 40% relief which returned to baseline in 3 weeks.

**V. APPLICATION OF PLAN CRITERIA (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)**

The plan's denial stated that the member did not meet criteria. We will assess this against the provided HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 document.

*   **Section 2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:**
    *   The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (2.1a) and cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit (2.1c), supported by MRI.
    *   **Meets Plan Criteria.**

*   **Section 2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **(a) Physical therapy:** Requires a minimum of 6 weeks. Mr. Green completed 7 weeks, with documentation of a functional plateau at week 6. This meets the plan's duration requirement.
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** Requires a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. Mr. Green completed 4 months of Gabapentin and 3 months of Naproxen, meeting both the agent and duration requirements.
    *   **(c) Interventional treatment:** Requires one procedure. Mr. Green had a Left C6 epidural.
    *   **Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A):** This exception is not applicable as the member's diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, not cervical myelopathy. The plan explicitly states this exception applies "only when myelopathy is the PRIMARY diagnosis."
    *   **Conclusion:** Based on the clinical record, Mr. Green **does meet** HealthFirst's conservative treatment requirements for radiculopathy as outlined in Section 2.2. Therefore, the plan's assertion that the member "does not meet criteria" for conservative treatment is incorrect based on its own policy.

*   **Section 2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:**
    *   Current imaging (MRI 2024-09-20) demonstrates pathology consistent with symptoms and the proposed surgical level.
    *   **Meets Plan Criteria.**

**VI. APPLICATION OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK**

1.  **CHSC § 1374.31(b) — Denial Notice Defects:** The denial notice was procedurally defective because:
    *   It referenced an "attached criteria document" which was not provided (as confirmed by the member and provider).
    *   It failed to include notice of IMR rights.
    *   A denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is deemed defective. This alone is sufficient grounds for an overturn or remand.

2.  **CIC § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:**
    *   This state law prohibits plans from conditioning surgical approval on PT completion where: "(2) The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms; OR (3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
    *   Mr. Green completed 7 weeks of PT, and the notes document a functional plateau at week 6. This satisfies section (a)(2) of the state law. Even if the plan had argued for more PT, this law would override that argument.

3.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 — Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions:**
    *   This APL specifically addresses cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia, providing exceptions for conservative treatment requirements in urgent cases.
    *   As established, Mr. Green's diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, not myelopathy, and there is no evidence of myelomalacia or cord contact. Therefore, this APL's specific provisions for myelopathy do not directly apply to this case.

4.  **CHSC § 1374.32(b) — IMR Standard:** This section states that a service meeting generally accepted standards SHALL be approved regardless of the plan's more restrictive internal criteria. We will apply this principle in conjunction with clinical standards.

---

**VII. APPLICATION OF CLINICAL STANDARD (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)**

1.  **Diagnosis:** The clinical evidence base clearly distinguishes cervical radiculopathy from myelopathy, noting different treatment algorithms. Mr. Green's presentation is consistent with **radiculopathy** (nerve root compression, unilateral symptoms, no UMN signs, no cord contact).
2.  **Conservative Treatment for Radiculopathy:**
    *   The clinical guidelines (NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) recommend conservative treatment for radiculopathy, typically for 6-12 weeks, before considering surgery.
    *   Mr. Green has undergone an extensive trial: 7 weeks of PT with documented plateau, 3-4 months of two different pharmacotherapy agents, and an epidural injection that provided only temporary, partial relief. This course of conservative treatment is well within, and indeed exceeds, the duration and scope typically recommended prior to surgical consideration for persistent radiculopathy.
3.  **Surgical Indications (AANS/CNS 2023):**
    *   Mr. Green's case falls under **TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION:** "(b) Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx."
    *   His symptoms (left C6 numbness, 4/5 weakness, diminished bicep reflex) are persistent despite an adequate and documented trial of conservative care, including a functional plateau in PT.

**Conclusion:** The requested ACDF is medically necessary and consistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice for cervical radiculopathy that has failed an adequate course of conservative treatment.

---

**VIII. ANALYSIS AND CONFLICT RESOLUTION**

Applying the hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria):

1.  **State Law:** The initial denial was **procedurally defective** as it failed to provide the referenced criteria document and omitted the IMR notice, directly violating CHSC § 1374.31(b). Furthermore, Mr. Green met the state law's criteria for physical therapy completion, having reached a functional plateau (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)).
2.  **Plan Criteria:** The plan incorrectly determined that the member did not meet its own criteria. Mr. Green *did* complete the required duration and components of conservative treatment (7 weeks PT with plateau, 2 pharmacotherapy agents >4 weeks, 1 interventional procedure) as specified in HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2.
3.  **Clinical Standard:** Mr. Green's presentation of persistent cervical radiculopathy after an adequate course of conservative treatment meets the generally accepted clinical indications for ACDF, aligning with AANS/CNS Tier 2 recommendations.

**Resolution:** State law mandates that procedurally defective denials be treated as if no denial was issued. Beyond this procedural flaw, the plan's substantive denial was based on an incorrect application of its own criteria. The member meets both the plan's actual criteria and the generally accepted standards of medical practice for this condition.

---

**IX. DISPOSITION**

The prior authorization denial for an ACDF at C5-C6 for Mr. Michael Green (PA-2024-D002) is **OVERTURNED**.

**Warrant:**
The denial is overturned for the following reasons:
1.  **Procedural Defects:** The initial denial notice was procedurally defective as it failed to include the referenced clinical criteria document and the required IMR notice, in violation of California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b). This defect alone renders the denial invalid.
2.  **Misapplication of Plan Criteria:** HealthFirst's denial stated the member did not meet plan criteria. However, a thorough review of the clinical record against HealthFirst's own Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2) demonstrates that Mr. Green *did* meet all specified conservative treatment requirements for cervical radiculopathy (7 weeks of physical therapy with a documented plateau, two pharmacotherapy agents for adequate durations, and one interventional procedure). The plan's myelopathy exception (Section 2.2A) was correctly deemed inapplicable as Mr. Green has radiculopathy, not myelopathy.
3.  **Adherence to State Law Regarding PT:** Mr. Green completed 7 weeks of physical therapy and reached a documented functional plateau at week 6. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2) stipulates that a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on further physical therapy when a member has completed a course and reached a functional plateau.
4.  **Consistency with Clinical Standards:** The requested ACDF is consistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice. Guidelines from AANS/CNS, NASS, and AO Spine support surgical intervention for radiculopathy with persistent symptoms and functional impairment after an adequate trial of conservative treatment, which Mr. Green has demonstrably completed.
