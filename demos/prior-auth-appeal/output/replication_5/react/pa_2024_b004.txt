**REVIEWER'S DETERMINATION: OVERTURNED**

---

**REASONING FOR DETERMINATION:**

This appeal was reviewed applying the three-source hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria. Based on a thorough review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's internal criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial denial is **OVERTURNED**.

**1. Clinical Presentation Clarification:**
The clinical record, including the MRI dated 2024-10-01, shows a large disc herniation at C5-C6 with moderate right C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis. Crucially, the MRI explicitly states "No cord contact. No myelomalacia." The neurological exam documented right C6 dermatomal numbness, diminished right bicep reflex, and 4/5 grip strength on the right, with inability to perform fine motor tasks essential for his profession. These findings are consistent with **cervical radiculopathy**, not cervical myelopathy. Therefore, any plan criteria or regulatory guidance pertaining specifically to myelopathy (e.g., HealthFirst's Myelopathy Exception or DMHC APL 22-014 regarding myelomalacia) are not directly applicable to this case for overturning the denial. The initial reviewer's implicit classification of this as a radiculopathy case (referencing "Recent onset radiculopathy") is accurate.

**2. Misapplication or Contradiction within Plan Criteria by Initial Denial:**
The initial denial states, "Member meets conservative treatment requirements. However, surgical request is premature. Member's symptoms have been present for only 6 weeks. Plan criteria require at least 6 weeks of symptoms before surgical consideration."
*   **Contradiction:** The denial itself is contradictory. If the member "meets conservative treatment requirements," then the request should not be considered premature based on the *completion* of those requirements.
*   **Misstatement of Criteria:** HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a), requires a "minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy" for conservative treatment. It does **not** specify a minimum duration of "symptoms" before surgical consideration as a separate criterion. The member completed 6 weeks of intensive physical therapy (18 sessions), two appropriate pharmacotherapy agents for 5-6 weeks, and one interventional procedure, thereby meeting **all** documented conservative treatment requirements outlined in HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. The denial's stated reason for prematurity is based on a criterion that is not explicitly found in HealthFirst's policy.

**3. State Law Override: California Insurance Code § 10169.5 (Functional Plateau):**
Even if HealthFirst's policy intended to imply a longer conservative treatment period or symptom duration, California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2) directly prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
*   The clinical record explicitly states: "6 weeks intensive PT. Functional plateau documented at 6 weeks. No improvement in fine motor deficits or radicular symptoms. OT confirms inability to perform professional duties." This documentation directly meets the conditions of CIC § 10169.5(a)(2).
*   Per CIC § 10169.5(c), a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau. The documentation of functional plateau after 6 weeks of intensive physical therapy, despite the completion of the policy's required duration, legally waives any further requirement for extended conservative therapy in this case.

**4. Alignment with Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:**
*   The AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023, Section 3.1) classify surgery as a "TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION" for "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" or "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care."
*   The member has completed an intensive 6-week conservative regimen, meeting the minimum duration often cited in guidelines. Critically, he has reached a documented functional plateau, with persistent and career-ending motor deficits (inability to perform violin fingering tasks).
*   The clinical standard defines "functional plateau" as less than 10% improvement over 3 sessions OR therapist documentation of maximum benefit, and explicitly notes that neurological symptoms (like weakness) are not expected to respond to PT. The documentation confirms this criterion is met.
*   Given the severity of the functional impairment to the member's livelihood and the documented failure of an intensive conservative trial meeting plan duration requirements, further delay for conservative care is not supported by clinical evidence or state law.

**Conclusion:**
The initial denial of ACDF C5-C6 for Kevin Watkins is based on a misinterpretation of HealthFirst's own criteria and is directly contradicted by California state law concerning functional plateaus in conservative treatment. Furthermore, the clinical presentation and documented failure of conservative management, coupled with severe functional impairment, align with generally accepted clinical standards for surgical intervention.

**ACTION REQUIRED BY HEALTHFIRST:**
HealthFirst is directed to **approve** the prior authorization request for procedure CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) for member Kevin Watkins (M-889900) at Stanford Medical Center. This approval is based on medical necessity supported by the clinical record, compliance with California state law, and generally accepted standards of medical practice.
