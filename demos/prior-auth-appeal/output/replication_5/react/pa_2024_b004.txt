**I. BACKGROUND**

This determination addresses the appeal of a prior authorization denial for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 for Mr. Kevin Watkins, a 41-year-old professional violinist. The initial denial (MED-003) stated the "surgical request is premature. Member's symptoms have been present for only 6 weeks. Plan criteria require at least 6 weeks of symptoms before surgical consideration. Recent onset radiculopathy has a high rate of spontaneous resolution."

The member's appeal asserts that all conservative treatment was completed and failed within the 6-week period, a functional plateau was reached, and the physician recommends surgery due to career-ending functional impairment.

**II. CLINICAL SUMMARY**

Mr. Kevin Watkins presents with acute cervical radiculopathy at C5-C6, causing significant functional impairment. MRI on 2024-10-01 showed a large disc herniation with moderate right C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis. Importantly, there was **no cord contact and no myelomalacia** documented, confirming a primary diagnosis of radiculopathy rather than myelopathy. Neurological exam on the same date revealed right C6 dermatomal numbness, diminished right bicep reflex, 4/5 right grip strength, and inability to perform violin fingering tasks, which is critical for his profession.

Over the past 6 weeks, Mr. Watkins has undergone a comprehensive course of conservative treatment:
*   **Physical Therapy:** 6 weeks (18 intensive sessions), with documentation of a functional plateau and no improvement in fine motor deficits or radicular symptoms. Occupational therapy confirms inability to perform professional duties.
*   **Pharmacotherapy:** 6 weeks of Methylprednisolone dose packs (with temporary partial relief) and 5 weeks of Gabapentin (partial pain relief, no motor improvement).
*   **Interventional:** One right C6 epidural steroid injection (2024-10-28) providing only 25% temporary relief, no motor improvement, and symptoms returning to baseline within 2 weeks.

**III. REVIEW AND ANALYSIS**

This review applies the three-source hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.

**1. Assessment of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**

*   **Diagnosis (2.1):** Mr. Watkins has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficit and supporting MRI, meeting criteria 2.1(a) and 2.1(c).
*   **Conservative Treatment (2.2):**
    *   **(a) Physical Therapy:** The plan requires "minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy." The member completed exactly 6 weeks (18 sessions) of intensive PT, meeting this minimum. Furthermore, a functional plateau was documented.
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** The plan requires "at least two... for minimum 4 weeks each." The member received Methylprednisolone for 6 weeks and Gabapentin for 5 weeks, meeting this criterion.
    *   **(c) Interventional:** The plan requires "one... interventional treatment." The member received a right C6 epidural injection, meeting this criterion.
*   **Myelopathy Exception (2.2(A) Note):** This exception does not apply as Mr. Watkins' primary diagnosis is radiculopathy, not myelopathy, with no evidence of spinal cord contact or myelomalacia.
*   **Imaging (2.3) and Physician Documentation (2.4):** These requirements are met by the provided clinical record.

**Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The initial denial reason, "Member's symptoms have been present for only 6 weeks. Plan criteria require at least 6 weeks of symptoms before surgical consideration," is factually inaccurate based on the provided HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 policy. The plan's criteria specify a *minimum of 6 weeks of physical therapy*, not a minimum duration of symptoms. The member *met* the plan's minimum duration for *all components* of conservative treatment and demonstrated failure. Therefore, the denial is inconsistent with the plan's *own written criteria*.

**2. Assessment of California Regulatory Requirements:**

*   **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b) - Denial Notice Adequacy:** The denial notice stated "Plan criteria require at least 6 weeks of symptoms before surgical consideration." As established above, this specific criterion is not present in HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024. Therefore, the denial notice is procedurally defective for failing to "specify the clinical criteria applied" or misstating them.
*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 - Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This state law explicitly limits a plan's ability to condition approval on physical therapy. Section (a)(2) states a plan "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
    *   The clinical record clearly documents that Mr. Watkins completed 6 weeks of intensive PT and reached a functional plateau, with no improvement in fine motor deficits or radicular symptoms, and occupational therapy confirming inability to perform professional duties. This directly satisfies CIC § 10169.5(a)(2).
    *   Furthermore, Section (c) states that a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy "shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau." The plan's denial, asserting prematurity despite the member having completed the minimum PT and reaching a functional plateau, constitutes a mechanical application prohibited by state law.
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 - Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** While this APL primarily addresses myelopathy, Section II.3 states, "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice... Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs." This reinforces the hierarchy of authority.

**3. Assessment of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

*   **Radiculopathy Management:** The clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020) consider surgery a "reasonable indication" (Tier 2) for radiculopathy with "persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and "significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care."
*   **Functional Plateau:** The clinical standard defines a functional plateau as <10% improvement over 3 sessions OR therapist documentation of maximum benefit. The clinical record explicitly states "Functional plateau documented at 6 weeks. No improvement in fine motor deficits or radicular symptoms. OT confirms inability to perform professional duties," meeting this definition.
*   Mr. Watkins has completed 6 weeks of comprehensive conservative treatment with documented failure and persistent, career-ending functional impairment. This falls squarely within the "reasonable indication" for surgery under generally accepted clinical standards. The notion that 6 weeks of failed comprehensive treatment is "premature" for radiculopathy, especially with objective and professionally debilitating neurological deficits, is not supported by current clinical guidelines.

**IV. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND DISPOSITION**

The denial for Mr. Kevin Watkins' ACDF request must be **OVERTURNED**.

1.  **Plan Criteria Violation:** The denial misrepresents HealthFirst's own Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024. The member *met* all conservative treatment requirements, including the minimum 6 weeks of physical therapy. The denial's stated reason ("6 weeks of symptoms is premature") is not a criterion found in the policy.
2.  **State Law Preemption:** Even if the plan's criteria were interpreted to support the denial, California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2) and (c) explicitly supersede such criteria. The member completed 6 weeks of physical therapy and a functional plateau was documented, making any further delay for "prematurity" due to therapy duration a violation of state law. The structural neurological compromise (disc herniation with nerve root compression and objective motor deficits) also falls under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), indicating physical therapy cannot address it.
3.  **Clinical Standard Support:** Generally accepted clinical standards for radiculopathy recognize that surgery is a reasonable option after 6-12 weeks of failed conservative treatment, particularly in the presence of significant functional impairment that impacts livelihood. Mr. Watkins meets these criteria.
4.  **Procedural Defect:** The denial notice itself is procedurally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b) for failing to accurately specify the clinical criteria applied.

Given the member's severe, career-ending functional impairment despite a thorough and failed course of conservative treatment that meets (and exceeds) the plan's own requirements, and considering the clear guidance from California state law and generally accepted clinical standards, continued denial is inappropriate.

**V. DETERMINATION**

The denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level) for member Kevin Watkins (PA-2024-B004) is **OVERTURNED**.

Authorization for the requested procedure is **APPROVED**.
