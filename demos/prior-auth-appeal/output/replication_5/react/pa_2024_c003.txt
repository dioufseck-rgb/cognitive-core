### I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The prior authorization denial for an anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 for member Helen Brooks is **OVERTURNED**.

The denial was based on the plan's assertion that the myelopathy exception did not apply due to the plan's radiologist disputing the clinical significance of T2 signal change (myelomalacia), leading to the unmet requirement for an interventional procedure under the standard pathway.

However, a comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's own plan criteria, California state laws (CIC 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014), and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) demonstrates that:
1.  The member presents with confirmed cervical myelopathy, meeting the plan's diagnostic criteria.
2.  The plan's own myelopathy exception (Section 2.2(A)) should have been applied, which waives the interventional procedure requirement given the treating physician's documented risk.
3.  California state law (DMHC APL 22-014) explicitly mandates waiver of full conservative treatment requirements for myelopathy with objective cord signal change (myelomalacia), and states that clinical standards override restrictive plan criteria.
4.  California state law (CIC 10169.5) overrides physical therapy requirements when structural neurological compromise exists or a functional plateau has been reached, both of which apply here.
5.  Generally accepted clinical standards overwhelmingly support surgical intervention for this presentation of myelopathy and advise against extended conservative trials, which are deemed ineffective and potentially harmful.

Applying the three-source hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), the denial for lack of an interventional procedure is invalid. The requested ACDF C4-C5 is medically necessary for Helen Brooks.

### II. CLINICAL SUMMARY

Helen Brooks is a 49-year-old female presenting with symptoms of cervical myelopathy. Her neurological examination reveals bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral upper extremity hyperreflexia, and subtle balance impairment on tandem gait, which are classic upper motor neuron (UMN) signs consistent with myelopathy.

An MRI from 2024-10-01 shows C4-C5 disc herniation with cord contact. While the initial radiologist noted "possible early T2 signal change — equivocal, may represent artifact," a second read by Radiologist B (treating hospital) on 2024-10-15 explicitly confirmed "C4-C5 cord compression with early myelomalacia. T2 hyperintensity is reproducible on multiple sequences and represents early cord signal change, not artifact. Consistent with early myelopathy." The plan's radiologist subsequently disputed the clinical significance of this finding.

Conservative treatment included 8 weeks of physical therapy (24 sessions) with documented "No improvement in UMN signs." Pharmacotherapy with Gabapentin (3 months, partial benefit) and Cyclobenzaprine (6 weeks, minimal benefit) was also attempted. An interventional procedure was not performed because the "Treating physician did not refer for epidural due to myelopathy concern — epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated in cord compression."

The treating physician, Dr. James Park, MD, has requested C4-C5 ACDF for early cervical myelopathy.

### IV. REVIEW & ANALYSIS

This review applies the hierarchy of authority: **State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.**

**A. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

1.  **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** **MET.** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (2.1(b)) supported by clinical UMN signs and objective cord compression on MRI. The dispute over myelomalacia's *significance* does not negate the primary diagnosis of myelopathy.
2.  **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **(a) Physical Therapy:** **MET.** Member completed 8 weeks of PT, exceeding the 6-week minimum. The denial acknowledges this ("PT completed (8 weeks)").
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** **MET.** Member completed trials of Gabapentin (3 months) and Cyclobenzaprine (6 weeks), meeting the "at least two... for minimum 4 weeks each" requirement.
    *   **(c) Interventional Treatment:** **NOT MET under standard pathway, BUT WAIVED under Myelopathy Exception.** This is the core of the denial. The plan denied because no interventional procedure was completed. However, HealthFirst's own **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY** states: "Interventional treatment requirement (2.2(c)) is WAIVED if treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses risk of neurological deterioration." The treating physician *did* document this risk ("epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated in cord compression"). The plan's denial incorrectly asserted the myelopathy exception did not apply based on its radiologist's disputed interpretation of myelomalacia, rather than the confirmed clinical diagnosis of myelopathy and documented risk.
3.  **2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:** **MET.** Current MRI (2024-10-01) demonstrates C4-C5 disc herniation with cord contact, consistent with symptoms.
4.  **2.4 PHYSICIAN DOCUMENTATION:** Appears **MET**, as not challenged in the denial.

**Plan Criteria Conclusion:** The plan misapplied its own criteria by failing to activate the myelopathy exception (2.2(A)), which would have waived the interventional treatment requirement. The basis for not applying the exception (disputed myelomalacia significance) is further challenged by state law and clinical standards.

**B. Application of California Regulatory Requirements:**

1.  **CHSC § 1374.31(b) (Denial Notice Adequacy):** The denial notice specified a reason code and text, seemingly meeting procedural requirements, though the substance of the denial is flawed.
2.  **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This All-Plan Letter is highly relevant and overrides the plan's denial.
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** States that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated." The member has objective cord compression and myelopathy, and "early myelomalacia" confirmed by the treating hospital's radiologist (Radiologist B).
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** Mandates that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when:
        *   (a) "Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia...)": **MET.** Radiologist B confirmed "early myelomalacia" and "T2 hyperintensity is reproducible on multiple sequences."
        *   (b) "Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression": **MET.** 8 weeks of PT with "No improvement in UMN signs" and partial/minimal benefit from pharmacotherapy demonstrates failure to arrest progression of myelopathy.
        *   (c) "Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines": As detailed below, surgery is strongly indicated per clinical guidelines.
    *   The APL specifically states, "The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements..."
    *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** States, "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice... Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs."
    **DMHC APL Conclusion:** This state law explicitly overrides the plan's requirement for an interventional procedure and establishes the urgency of myelopathy with myelomalacia, indicating that the denial is inconsistent with California regulations.
3.  **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This law further supports overturning the denial. It states that a plan cannot condition approval on physical therapy where:
    *   **(a)(2) Member reached a functional plateau:** **MET.** Helen Brooks completed 8 weeks of PT with "No improvement in UMN signs." Clinical standards define functional plateau as failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement, especially in neurological symptoms, after 6-8 weeks (Source 3, Section 5).
    *   **(a)(3) Condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address:** **MET.** The MRI shows C4-C5 disc herniation with cord contact, and the clinical exam reveals bilateral Hoffman signs and hyperreflexia, indicating objective spinal cord compromise. PT cannot address the structural compression or reverse existing cord injury.
    **CIC 10169.5 Conclusion:** Even if the PT requirement were still in question, it is overridden by state law.

**C. Application of Clinical Standard Knowledge (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

1.  **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines clearly distinguish myelopathy from radiculopathy, emphasizing that they require different treatment algorithms and that conservative treatment evidence is primarily for radiculopathy (Source 3, Section 1.1). Helen Brooks has myelopathy.
2.  **Myelomalacia Significance:** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) indicates existing spinal cord injury, a higher risk of permanent deficit with delay, and surgical urgency. It is a "strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials" (Source 3, Section 1.2). The plan's radiologist's dismissal of myelomalacia's clinical significance directly contradicts this accepted clinical standard.
3.  **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** "For moderate-severe myelopathy... physical therapy does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement" (Cochrane Review 2021, Source 3, Section 2.1). "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients... may result in neurological deterioration" (AO Spine 2021, Source 3, Section 2.1).
    *   **Duration:** "No randomized controlled trials support a defined minimum duration of conservative treatment before surgery" for myelopathy (Source 3, Section 2.2).
    *   **Risk of Delay:** "Each month of delay in surgical treatment for moderate-severe myelopathy is associated with decreased likelihood of neurological recovery" (Source 3, Section 2.3).
4.  **Surgical Indications (ACDF, CPT 22551):** Helen Brooks meets TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION criteria (Source 3, Section 3.1):
    *   "(a) Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI"
    *   "(c) Myelomalacia on MRI (cord signal change)" (confirmed by treating hospital radiologist)
    *   The guidelines explicitly state that for patients meeting TIER 1 criteria, "The clinical standard does NOT require completion of a minimum course of physical therapy as a prerequisite to surgery for this presentation. Requiring PT completion in this setting is inconsistent with accepted standards of medical practice" (Source 3, Section 3.2).
**Clinical Standard Conclusion:** Accepted clinical guidelines strongly support surgical intervention for Helen Brooks' myelopathy with myelomalacia and negate the need for further conservative treatment (including interventional procedures), especially given the documented risks and lack of efficacy for myelopathy.

---

### V. CONFLICT RESOLUTION & FINAL DISPOSITION

The HealthFirst plan's denial is based on a misinterpretation of the clinical evidence regarding myelopathy and a failure to apply its own myelopathy exception, which is then further contradicted by higher authorities: California state law and generally accepted clinical standards.

*   The plan's radiologist's opinion regarding the clinical significance of myelomalacia is superseded by the treating hospital's radiologist, the treating physician's clinical assessment, and crucially, by **California State Law (DMHC APL 22-014)** and **Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine)**, all of which identify myelomalacia as a significant indicator for surgical urgency and a reason to waive standard conservative therapy.
*   The plan's interventional procedure requirement is specifically waived by its **own Section 2.2(A) Myelopathy Pathway Exception**, given the treating physician's documented risk for such procedures in the context of cord compression. This waiver is reinforced by **DMHC APL 22-014**, which waives full conservative treatment for myelopathy with myelomalacia, and **Clinical Standards**, which find such interventions inappropriate for advanced myelopathy.
*   The physical therapy requirement, though met by the member, is deemed an inappropriate prerequisite for this condition by **CIC 10169.5** (due to structural compromise and functional plateau) and **Clinical Standards** (lack of efficacy for myelopathy).

Therefore, based on the three-source hierarchy, the plan's criteria, as applied, are in direct conflict with both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice. State law and clinical standards govern, making the denial untenable.

**DISPOSITION: OVERTURNED**

The requested ACDF at C4-C5 (CPT 22551) is medically necessary for Helen Brooks. Authorization for the procedure is granted.
