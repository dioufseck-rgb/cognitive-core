**I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

This appeal reviews the denial of prior authorization for an ACDF at C4-C5 for Helen Brooks. The initial denial cited a lack of interventional procedures and stated that the myelopathy exception did not apply due to the plan's radiologist deeming T2 signal change as minimal/artifactual.

Based on a comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria, applicable California state laws, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, the denial is hereby **OVERTURNED**.

The member presents with clear clinical signs of cervical myelopathy and objective cord compression. The plan failed to correctly apply its own myelopathy exception. Furthermore, the denial is in direct conflict with California state law and current evidence-based clinical guidelines, which do not support delaying surgery for moderate-to-severe myelopathy to complete arbitrary conservative treatment requirements.

---

**II. REASON FOR DENIAL (Initial Review)**

The initial denial, dated 2024-11-20, cited reason code MED-003, stating:
"Myelopathy exception does not apply. MRI T2 signal change is minimal and may represent artifact. Plan's imaging reviewer notes this finding is below threshold for clinical significance. Standard pathway: PT completed (8 weeks), pharmacotherapy met, but no interventional procedure completed."

---

**III. APPEAL BASIS**

The appeal submitted on behalf of Helen Brooks cites the following:
*   Radiologist specifically noted T2 signal change in report — not artifact.
*   Second radiologist opinion confirms early myelomalacia.
*   Treating physician has documented myelopathy on clinical grounds independently of imaging.

**IV. CLINICAL REVIEW AND APPLICATION OF CRITERIA**

**A. Clinical Presentation:**
Helen Brooks, a 49-year-old female, presents with clinical signs consistent with cervical myelopathy. Her neurological exam (2024-10-05) reveals bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia in the upper extremities, and subtle balance impairment on tandem gait. These are hallmark upper motor neuron (UMN) signs. MRI imaging (2024-10-01) demonstrates C4-C5 disc herniation with cord contact.

The presence of spinal cord compression on MRI combined with definitive upper motor neuron signs (Hoffman's sign, hyperreflexia, gait disturbance) unequivocally establishes a diagnosis of cervical myelopathy. The dispute regarding the significance of T2 signal change (myelomalacia) does not negate the primary diagnosis of myelopathy based on clinical and other imaging findings.

**B. Application of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

1.  **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:**
    *   Criterion 2.1(b) for "Cervical myelopathy" requires "spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms (gait disturbance, bilateral upper extremity weakness, hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign...)". The member's clinical record clearly demonstrates cord compression on MRI and all listed myelopathic symptoms (bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, balance impairment/gait disturbance). **This criterion is MET.**
    *   Supporting MRI within 6 months demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms is also present. **This is MET.**

2.  **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **Standard Pathway:** The plan's standard pathway requires 6 weeks of PT, 2 pharmacotherapy agents for 4+ weeks each, and 1 interventional procedure.
        *   **Physical Therapy:** Member completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of PT, exceeding the 6-week minimum. Documented "No improvement in UMN signs." **This part is MET.**
        *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Member completed Gabapentin (3 months, partial benefit) and Cyclobenzaprine (6 weeks, minimal benefit), meeting the requirement for two agents for 4+ weeks. **This is MET.**
        *   **Interventional Treatment:** Not completed. This was the basis for the denial under the standard pathway.
    *   **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY:**
        *   This exception applies "For members with confirmed cervical myelopathy (criterion 2.1(b))". As established above, Helen Brooks has confirmed cervical myelopathy.
        *   Under this exception, the PT requirement is reduced to 4 weeks minimum (member completed 8 weeks). **This is MET.**
        *   The interventional treatment requirement (2.2(c)) is WAIVED if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration. The treating physician explicitly documented that "epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated in cord compression." **This waiver condition is MET.**
        *   The pharmacotherapy requirement remains in full and has been met.
        *   **Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The plan's internal denial failed to correctly apply its own "MYELOPATHY PATHWAY" exception. Despite clear clinical evidence of myelopathy and the treating physician's documentation justifying the waiver of interventional procedures, the plan incorrectly asserted that the myelopathy exception did not apply by focusing solely on a disputed imaging finding of myelomalacia, rather than the undisputed clinical signs and cord compression.

**C. Application of California Regulatory Requirements:**

1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) – Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This state law prohibits conditioning surgical approval on PT where "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Helen Brooks has objective cord compression and upper motor neuron signs (bilateral Hoffman, hyperreflexia, gait impairment) which constitute structural neurological compromise. Her 8 weeks of PT showed "no improvement in UMN signs," demonstrating that PT could not address this specific neurological deficit. Therefore, any plan requirement for further PT would be overridden by state law.
2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 – Prior Authorization Requirements for Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions:**
    *   **Section II.1 (Myelopathy Classification):** This APL states that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated." While the plan's radiologist disputed myelomalacia, the treating hospital's radiologist confirmed it, and the presence of severe clinical myelopathy with cord compression warrants urgency.
    *   **Section II.2 (Conservative Treatment Exceptions):** This APL mandates that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization" when objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia), the treating physician documents conservative failure, and surgery is indicated. The APL specifically identifies "The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements." The plan's denial, which hinges on disputing myelomalacia to avoid this waiver, directly contradicts this regulatory guidance.
    *   **Section II.3 (Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard):** This section dictates that "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice." The plan's insistence on a conservative treatment pathway designed for radiculopathy, despite clear myelopathy and contraindications, is more restrictive than generally accepted standards.
3.  **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) – Standard of Review:** This code specifies that for IMR, service meeting "generally accepted standards of medical practice" SHALL be approved "regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." This reinforces the need to prioritize clinical standards over restrictive plan criteria.

**D. Application of Generally Accepted Clinical Standards of Medical Practice:**

1.  **Diagnosis and Urgency:** The AANS/CNS, NASS, and AO Spine guidelines clearly distinguish myelopathy from radiculopathy. Myelopathy, especially with objective cord compression and UMN signs, is considered a serious condition with potential for irreversible harm if surgery is delayed. Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) is a strong indicator of existing cord injury and predicts worse outcomes if surgery is delayed (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2, 2.3). The plan's attempt to dismiss myelomalacia as "artifact" is contrary to the treating hospital's specialist opinion and the clinical significance ascribed by guidelines.
2.  **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** The AO Spine Guidelines (2021) explicitly state, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1). The plan's standard PT requirement is derived from radiculopathy evidence and is a "category error" when applied to myelopathy. Helen Brooks has already completed 8 weeks of PT with no improvement in UMN signs, further demonstrating its ineffectiveness for her myelopathic symptoms.
    *   **Interventional Procedures:** Clinical guidelines do not recommend epidural steroid injections for cervical myelopathy with cord compression, and they are considered relatively contraindicated due to potential for neurological deterioration (Clinical Record, Interventional note). The treating physician's decision not to pursue interventional procedures is clinically appropriate and aligned with cautious practice in myelopathy.
3.  **Surgical Indication:** The AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) classify "Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI" and "Myelomalacia on MRI" as TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION for surgical intervention. Helen Brooks meets these criteria based on her clinical presentation and the second radiologist's findings.

---

**V. CONFLICT RESOLUTION (Hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria)**

The plan's denial is fundamentally flawed at multiple levels:

1.  **Plan Criteria Misapplication:** The plan failed to recognize and apply its own myelopathy exception (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A)), despite the member clearly meeting the myelopathy diagnosis criteria (Section 2.1(b)) and the explicit waiver conditions for interventional procedures being met by the treating physician's documentation of risk. The plan's internal radiologist's disputation of myelomalacia, contrary to another specialist's opinion and the overriding clinical picture, led to an incorrect denial.
2.  **Conflict with California State Law:** The denial directly contravenes CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) by requiring PT for a condition involving structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. It also violates DMHC APL 22-014, which mandates waiver of conservative treatment for myelopathy cases with myelomalacia and prohibits plans from applying criteria more restrictive than generally accepted standards.
3.  **Conflict with Clinical Standards:** The plan's conservative treatment requirements (especially for interventional procedures) are inappropriate and unsupported by clinical evidence for a patient with established cervical myelopathy and objective UMN signs. Leading spine society guidelines (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) strongly recommend surgery for this presentation and advise against delaying it for extensive conservative trials, particularly when myelomalacia is present.

Under the established hierarchy, California State Law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice supersede HealthFirst's more restrictive interpretation of its own criteria.

---

**VI. DISPOSITION**

The prior authorization denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C4-C5, one level) for Helen Brooks is **OVERTURNED**.

**Warrant:**
The denial by HealthFirst is inconsistent with its own clinical coverage criteria when correctly applied, and is directly contradicted by California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice. The member's presentation of cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and UMN signs (bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, gait impairment), coupled with the treating hospital's radiologist confirming myelomalacia, warrants surgical intervention. The plan's insistence on further conservative treatment (specifically interventional procedures) is medically inappropriate, unsupported by evidence for myelopathy, and potentially harmful due to the risk of delaying necessary surgery for a progressive neurological condition. The treating physician appropriately waived interventional treatment due to medical contraindication in the setting of cord compression, which is permissible under the plan's own myelopathy exception and consistent with clinical caution.
