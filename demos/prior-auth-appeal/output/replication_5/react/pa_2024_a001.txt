**DECISION RATIONALE:**

Upon thorough review of the prior authorization request, the initial denial, the member's appeal basis, and the submitted clinical record, applying the three-source hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), the decision to deny prior authorization for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) is **OVERTURNED**.

**1. Member's Clinical Presentation:**
The member, Diana Torres, presents with a clear diagnosis of progressive cervical myelopathy at C5-C6, confirmed by:
*   **Symptoms:** 4-month history of bilateral hand clumsiness and gait instability, with documentation of upper motor neuron signs (bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, positive Babinski bilateral, gait ataxia).
*   **Imaging:** MRI dated 2024-08-15 revealing severe central cord compression C5-C6 and, critically, T2 hyperintensity within the spinal cord (myelomalacia).
*   Myelomalacia signifies existing spinal cord injury and is a strong indicator of the severity and urgency of the condition. (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2).

**2. Review against HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
The initial denial cited CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, specifically the "Standard pathway requires minimum 6 weeks" of PT and the pharmacotherapy requirement.
*   **Diagnosis (2.1):** The member clearly meets criterion 2.1(b) for cervical myelopathy.
*   **Conservative Treatment (2.2):**
    *   The initial reviewer failed to apply **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY**, which reduces the physical therapy requirement to a 4-week minimum and waives the interventional requirement if the physician documents risk. This was an error in applying the plan's *own* criteria.
    *   Even with the myelopathy exception, the member completed only 2 weeks of PT (vs. 4 weeks required by the exception) and trialed only one pharmacotherapy agent (Methylprednisolone taper for 2 weeks, vs. two agents for 4+ weeks each required by the exception).
    *   Therefore, *solely based on the plan's conservative treatment duration requirements*, the criteria would appear not to be met, even with the myelopathy exception. However, the hierarchy of authority requires review against state law and clinical standards.

**3. Review against California Regulatory Requirements (State Law):**
California law directly addresses and overrides the plan's conservative treatment requirements in this specific clinical scenario.
*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This statute, effective January 1, 2023, prohibits conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where:
    *   **(a)(1) Treating physician documented PT is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit:** Dr. Kim's letter (2024-10-15) explicitly states PT is contraindicated due to myelomalacia and the risk of irreversible cord injury, noting symptoms worsened during PT. **This criterion is met.**
    *   **(a)(3) Objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address:** The MRI finding of severe central cord compression with myelomalacia constitutes objective evidence of structural neurological compromise. The physician's declaration further supports that PT cannot address this and poses a risk. **This criterion is met.**
    *   **(c) Plan's internal requirement for minimum weeks of PT shall not be applied mechanically:** The plan's denial applied its minimum PT duration requirement mechanically, without reference to the explicit exceptions provided by state law in subsections (a)(1) and (a)(3). This is a direct violation of CIC 10169.5(c).
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 — Prior Authorization Requirements for Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions (Issued September 2022):** This guidance is directly applicable:
    *   **II.1 — Myelopathy Classification:** The DMHC states that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI "SHALL be treated as urgent" due to potential for irreversible harm. The member's condition meets this classification.
    *   **II.2 — Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** This section states that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when:
        *   (a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (myelomalacia present on MRI). **Met.**
        *   (b) Treating physician documents conservative treatment has failed (PT caused symptom exacerbation, discontinued, and declared contraindicated). **Met.**
        *   (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines (as detailed below). **Met.**
    *   The APL explicitly states, "The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements even where a full course has not been completed, when the treating physician documents urgency." This provision directly overrides the plan's PT and pharmacotherapy duration requirements in this case.
    *   **II.3 — Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** The APL mandates that plans "shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice."

**4. Review against Clinical Standards:**
Generally accepted clinical guidelines support urgent surgical intervention for this condition and do not support mandatory extended conservative treatment.
*   **AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine (2023 Update):** Classifies moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia as a **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION** for surgery. These guidelines explicitly state that myelomalacia is a strong surgical indication and a contraindication to extended conservative trials.
*   **AO Spine International (2021):** States, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
*   **NASS Guidelines (2020):** Recommends surgical decompression as the primary treatment for moderate to severe myelopathy.
*   The clinical record indicates progressive neurological deficit (bilateral hand clumsiness, gait instability, UMN signs) and myelomalacia, which are all strong predictors of permanent deficit if surgery is delayed.

**5. Conflict Resolution (Hierarchy: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria):**
In this case, a direct conflict exists between the HealthFirst plan's conservative treatment criteria (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2) and both California State law and generally accepted clinical standards.
*   **State law (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014) is the highest authority.** It unequivocally overrides the plan's requirements for minimum physical therapy duration and the full course of pharmacotherapy in the presence of myelopathy with myelomalacia and physician-documented contraindication/urgency.
*   **Clinical standards** from major spine societies (AANS/CNS, AO Spine, NASS) further reinforce that mandatory conservative treatment, particularly physical therapy, is not supported by evidence for moderate-severe myelopathy with myelomalacia and can be detrimental due to delay.
*   Therefore, the plan's criteria, being more restrictive than state law and clinical standards for this specific urgent condition, cannot be applied.

**CONCLUSION:**

The member, Diana Torres, has a confirmed diagnosis of progressive cervical myelopathy with severe cord compression and myelomalacia, presenting with significant neurological deficits. This condition requires urgent surgical intervention as per generally accepted clinical standards.

The HealthFirst Plan's denial, based on the member not meeting conservative treatment requirements, is invalid because:
1.  It failed to adequately apply the plan's own myelopathy exception.
2.  More importantly, California state law (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014) explicitly prohibits the imposition of such conservative treatment requirements when a physician documents physical therapy as contraindicated, when objective structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) is present, and when urgent surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted guidelines.
3.  Leading clinical guidelines do not support delaying surgery for this presentation to complete a mandatory course of conservative therapy.

Based on the hierarchy of authority, California State Law and generally accepted Clinical Standards override the plan's internal criteria in this case. The procedure is medically necessary.

**ORDER:**

HealthFirst Health Plan is directed to **OVERTURN** the denial for prior authorization of CPT code 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level) for member Diana Torres (M-1122233) and issue an approval.
