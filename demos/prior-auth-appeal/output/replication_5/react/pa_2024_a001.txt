**I. INTRODUCTION**

This letter communicates the determination of the internal appeal for prior authorization of an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) procedure for Diana Torres. The original denial was based on the member not meeting conservative treatment requirements as outlined in HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2.

---

**II. APPEAL BASIS**

The treating physician, Dr. James Kim, and the member's appeal assert that the denial should be overturned based on the following:
1.  Presence of severe myelomalacia on MRI, which California law prohibits the application of standard physical therapy requirements.
2.  Treating physician's declaration that physical therapy is contraindicated due to documented cord signal change.
3.  Regulatory override under California Insurance Code (CIC) 10169.5(a)(3) which makes plan criteria for physical therapy irrelevant in this specific clinical scenario.

---

**III. CLINICAL RECORD SUMMARY**

**Presenting Diagnosis:** Progressive cervical myelopathy C5-C6 with myelomalacia.
**Initial Presentation (2024-08-10):** 4-month history of bilateral hand clumsiness and gait instability. Upper motor neuron signs present.
**Imaging (MRI 2024-08-15):** Severe central cord compression C5-C6 with T2 hyperintensity within the cord (myelomalacia).
**Neurological Exam (2024-08-10):** Bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia bilateral upper and lower extremities, positive Babinski bilateral, gait ataxia.
**Conservative Treatment History:**
*   **Physical Therapy:** Initiated 2024-09-01, discontinued 2024-09-14 after 2 weeks (6 sessions). Treating physician discontinued PT due to symptom exacerbation, documenting PT as contraindicated. Myelomalacia was present at the time PT was initiated.
*   **Pharmacotherapy:** Methylprednisolone taper for 2 weeks, resulting in temporary mild improvement but no sustained benefit. Only one agent trialed.
**Physician Declaration (Dr. Kim, 2024-10-15):** Confirms PT is contraindicated, myelomalacia is present on initial MRI, states continuing PT risks irreversible cord injury, and asserts CIC 10169.5(a)(3) exception applies, concluding surgery is urgently indicated.

**IV. REVIEW AND ANALYSIS**

This appeal determination is made in accordance with the established hierarchy of authority: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.**

**A. Plan Criteria Review (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)**

1.  **Diagnosis (2.1(b)):** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms, including gait disturbance, bilateral upper extremity weakness, hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign). MRI confirms structural pathology consistent with symptoms (severe central cord compression C5-C6 with myelomalacia). **This criterion is met.**

2.  **Conservative Treatment (2.2):** The denial states the member did not meet the standard pathway requirements (6 weeks PT, 2 pharmacotherapy agents).
    *   **Myelopathy Exception (2.2(A)):** For confirmed cervical myelopathy, the plan's policy reduces the PT requirement to 4 weeks minimum and waives interventional treatment if physician documents risk. Pharmacotherapy remains at two agents for 4 weeks each.
    *   **Application:** The initial denial applied the *standard* 6-week PT requirement, explicitly stating "Standard pathway requires minimum 6 weeks," indicating a failure to apply the plan's own myelopathy exception (2.2(A)). However, even if the myelopathy exception were applied, the member only completed 2 weeks of PT (not 4) and trialed only one pharmacotherapy agent (not two).
    *   **Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** Based solely on the plan's internal criteria, even with the myelopathy exception, the member technically does not meet the specified *duration* of conservative treatment required (PT and pharmacotherapy). This leads to the need to review higher in the hierarchy.

**B. California Regulatory Review (State Law)**

1.  **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where:
    *   **(a)(1):** "The member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition." Dr. Kim explicitly documented that PT was contraindicated due to symptom exacerbation and risk of irreversible cord injury, stating it was unlikely to benefit. **This criterion is met.**
    *   **(a)(3):** "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The MRI findings of severe central cord compression C5-C6 with T2 hyperintensity (myelomalacia) constitute clear objective evidence of structural neurological compromise. **This criterion is met.**
    *   **Conclusion:** Under CIC § 10169.5, the plan is explicitly prohibited from requiring further physical therapy as a prerequisite for this surgical procedure. This state law directly overrides the plan's Section 2.2(a) and Exception 2.2(A) regarding physical therapy duration.

2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This guidance is highly relevant:
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent..." The member's MRI clearly shows myelomalacia with severe cord compression. **This case is classified as urgent under state guidance.**
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** For urgent conditions where delay risks irreversible harm, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when:"
        *   **(a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia):** Myelomalacia is present on the MRI.
        *   **(b) Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression:** PT was attempted for 2 weeks, discontinued due to symptom exacerbation, and declared contraindicated. Pharmacotherapy provided only temporary, mild benefit.
        *   **(c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines:** As detailed below, surgical intervention is strongly indicated.
        *   "The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements even where a full course has not been completed, when the treating physician documents urgency." Dr. Kim documented urgency.
    *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice... Where a conflict exists..., the clinical standard governs."
    *   **Conclusion:** DMHC APL 22-014 provides strong regulatory support for waiving the plan's conservative treatment requirements (both PT and pharmacotherapy duration) given the urgent nature of myelopathy with myelomalacia and the physician's documentation.

**C. Clinical Standard Review (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)**

1.  **Myelomalacia Significance:** Clinical guidelines universally identify myelomalacia (T2 signal change) as indicating existing spinal cord injury, a higher risk of permanent deficit with continued compression, and a strong surgical urgency. It is considered a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials. (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2).

2.  **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:**
    *   For moderate-severe myelopathy (which the member presents with, given UMN signs and gait instability), physical therapy does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement. (Cochrane Review 2021, cited in Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1).
    *   AO Spine Guidelines (2021) state, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1).
    *   The 6-week (or even 4-week) PT requirement in payer policies is derived from radiculopathy evidence, not myelopathy. Applying it to myelopathy is a "category error not supported by evidence." (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.2).

3.  **Surgical Indication:** The member's presentation (moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression, myelomalacia on MRI, and progressive neurological deficits) meets AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION for surgery. (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.1, 3.2). The clinical standard does not require a minimum course of PT for this presentation.

4.  **Risk of Delay:** Delay in surgical treatment for moderate-severe myelopathy, particularly with myelomalacia, is associated with decreased likelihood of neurological recovery and worse outcomes. (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.3).

**D. Conflict Resolution (Hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria)**

*   The plan's denial was based on its internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2), which requires a minimum of 6 weeks of PT (or 4 weeks under the myelopathy exception) and two pharmacotherapy agents.
*   California State Law (CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3)) directly prohibits the plan from requiring further physical therapy due to the treating physician's documentation of contraindication and the objective evidence of myelomalacia with structural neurological compromise.
*   California State Law (DMHC APL 22-014) mandates that myelopathy with myelomalacia be treated as urgent, and explicitly supports the waiver of "full course of conservative treatment" when objective imaging shows myelomalacia and the treating physician documents failed conservative therapy and surgical indication.
*   Generally accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine, NASS) confirm that for moderate-to-severe myelopathy with myelomalacia, surgical intervention is a strong indication, and delaying surgery for mandatory conservative treatment trials, particularly physical therapy, is not supported by evidence and carries a risk of irreversible harm.
*   **Conclusion:** Both California state law and the generally accepted clinical standards unequivocally override the plan's more restrictive internal criteria regarding conservative treatment requirements for this member's specific diagnosis of cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia. The standard 6-week PT requirement, and even the modified 4-week requirement under the plan's myelopathy exception, are inconsistent with higher authorities. Similarly, the requirement for a second pharmacotherapy agent is superseded by the overall urgency and the guidance to waive full conservative treatment for myelomalacia.

---

**V. DETERMINATION**

Based on a comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria, applicable California state laws, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, the denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level) for Diana Torres must be **OVERTURNED**.

---

**VI. WARRANT FOR DECISION**

The member presents with severe cervical myelopathy with confirmed myelomalacia on MRI, bilateral upper motor neuron signs, and gait instability, meeting the highest-tier (Tier 1) strong indication for surgical intervention under AANS/CNS (2023) and NASS (2020) guidelines. Clinical evidence from AO Spine (2021) and the Cochrane Review (2021) confirms that a mandatory course of physical therapy for moderate-to-severe myelopathy, especially with myelomalacia, is not supported by high-quality evidence and may result in neurological deterioration.

Furthermore, California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3) explicitly prohibits HealthFirst from denying coverage based on incomplete physical therapy when the treating physician has documented it as contraindicated or when objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) is present. Both conditions are met in this case. Additionally, DMHC All-Plan Letter 22-014 classifies myelopathy with myelomalacia as an urgent condition where delay risks irreversible harm, specifically identifying myelomalacia as a finding that supports the waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements.

The plan's internal criteria, CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, are more restrictive than California state law and generally accepted clinical standards for this urgent and severe presentation. The initial denial's failure to recognize the myelopathy exception, and more importantly, the superior legal and clinical overrides, constitutes an incorrect application of review principles. Therefore, the requested ACDF procedure is medically necessary and should be authorized.
