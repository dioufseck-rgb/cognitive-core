**I. BACKGROUND**

A prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) for member Stephanie Oduya was submitted by Dr. Patrick Walsh. The request was denied by HealthFirst on October 15, 2024, citing that imaging showed mild-moderate disc herniation with no cord compression and no neurological deficit, and that the conservative treatment pathway had not been completed (Reason Code MED-003). A peer-to-peer review was conducted with Dr. Maria Rivera, a board-certified neurosurgeon, who upheld the initial denial.

Dr. Walsh appealed the denial, asserting that his clinical judgment as the treating surgeon should take precedence, disputing Dr. Rivera's interpretation, highlighting his extensive surgical experience, and warning of potential permanent disability if surgery is delayed.

This appeal determination has been conducted by an expert health plan appeals reviewer applying the three-source hierarchy: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.

**III. REVIEW AGAINST APPLICABLE CRITERIA AND STANDARDS**

The appeal was reviewed against HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards.

**A. HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)**

1.  **Section 2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** Met. Member has a confirmed diagnosis of Cervical Radiculopathy (2.1a) with supporting MRI.
2.  **Section 2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:** Not met.
    *   **(a) Physical Therapy:** Plan requires a minimum of 6 weeks. Member completed only 4 weeks.
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** Plan requires a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. Member tried one agent (Naproxen) for 8 weeks.
    *   **(c) Interventional Treatments:** Plan requires one interventional treatment (e.g., epidural steroid injection). Member had none.
3.  **Exception 2.2(A) — Myelopathy Pathway:** Not applicable. This exception applies only when myelopathy is the primary diagnosis. Ms. Oduya's presentation is cervical radiculopathy, not myelopathy, as confirmed by the clinical record. The denial correctly identified "no cord compression and no neurological deficit on examination."

**Conclusion (Plan Criteria):** The request fails to meet the plan's conservative treatment requirements for cervical radiculopathy. The plan's denial is consistent with its own medical necessity criteria.

**B. California Regulatory Requirements**

1.  **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute specifies conditions under which PT completion cannot be required.
    *   **(a)(1) PT contraindicated or unlikely to benefit:** Dr. Walsh elected to halt PT, but there is no documentation that PT was contraindicated or definitively unlikely to benefit Ms. Oduya. The PT summary noted "mild improvement in pain."
    *   **(a)(2) Member reached a functional plateau:** While PT was halted, there is no documentation from the physical therapist or objective measures (e.g., <10% improvement over 3 sessions in NDI/VAS) to confirm a functional plateau was reached at 4 weeks, especially considering "mild improvement" in pain.
    *   **(a)(3) Objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address:** This section applies to conditions like myelopathy with cord compression or myelomalacia. The clinical record explicitly states "No cord compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia." Ms. Oduya has mild radicular tingling and no weakness, not a severe neurological compromise that PT cannot address.
    **Conclusion (CIC § 10169.5):** None of the statutory exceptions for waiving physical therapy requirements are met based on the objective clinical documentation.
2.  **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This All-Plan Letter specifically addresses myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia, defining such cases as potentially urgent and waiving certain conservative treatment requirements.
    **Conclusion (DMHC APL 22-014):** This guidance is not applicable to Ms. Oduya's condition, as she does not have cervical myelopathy, cord compression, or myelomalacia.
3.  **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This states that IMR is not bound by plan criteria if the service meets generally accepted standards. We will evaluate the generally accepted clinical standards below to see if they contradict the plan's criteria or state law.

**C. Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)**

1.  **Cervical Radiculopathy vs. Myelopathy:** The clinical evidence base *critically distinguishes* between radiculopathy and myelopathy, noting they require different treatment algorithms (Source Document 3, Section 1.1). Ms. Oduya presents with radiculopathy.
2.  **Conservative Treatment for Radiculopathy:**
    *   For cervical radiculopathy, conservative treatment is "typically effective (70-80% resolution)" (Source Document 3, Section 1.1).
    *   The 6-week PT requirement for radiculopathy is generally supported by evidence, unlike for myelopathy where it is not (Source Document 3, Section 2.2).
    *   Clinical guidelines do not support early surgical intervention for mild radiculopathy without a comprehensive trial of conservative management.
    *   A functional plateau definition (less than 10% improvement over 3 sessions, or therapist documentation of maximal benefit) is applied clinically, which is not clearly met by Ms. Oduya's 4-week PT with "mild improvement" and no therapist's statement of maximal benefit (Source Document 3, Section 5).
3.  **Myelomalacia:** Myelomalacia is a strong surgical indication (Source Document 3, Section 1.2), but it is *not present* in Ms. Oduya's MRI.
4.  **Surgical Indications (ACDF, CPT 22551):**
    *   **TIER 1 (Strong Indication):** Ms. Oduya does not meet any Tier 1 criteria (no moderate-severe myelopathy, no progressive neurological deficit *despite* conservative treatment, no myelomalacia, no acute severe myelopathy) (Source Document 3, Section 3.1).
    *   **TIER 2 (Reasonable Indication):** The most relevant criterion, "(b) Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx," has not been met. Ms. Oduya has had 4 weeks of PT and 8 weeks of a single pharmacotherapy agent, with no interventional treatment. Her symptoms are mild (tingling, mild pain) and she has no activity limitations.
    **Conclusion (Clinical Standard):** Generally accepted clinical standards for cervical radiculopathy recommend a comprehensive trial of conservative treatment (typically 6-12 weeks of PT, multiple pharmacotherapy agents, and consideration of interventional procedures) *before* surgical intervention is considered medically necessary. Ms. Oduya has not completed such a trial, and her current condition (mild radiculopathy without objective cord compression, myelomalacia, or severe neurological deficit) does not meet generally accepted indications for immediate surgical intervention.

**IV. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND TREATING SURGEON'S STATEMENT**

Applying the hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), there is **no conflict** in this case.
*   **Plan criteria** require a more extensive conservative treatment pathway, which Ms. Oduya has not completed.
*   **State law (CIC § 10169.5)** does not provide an exception to the physical therapy requirement for Ms. Oduya's specific clinical presentation. The DMHC APL 22-014 is also not applicable.
*   **Generally accepted clinical standards** for cervical radiculopathy support the requirement for a comprehensive conservative treatment trial prior to surgery, and do not indicate immediate surgical necessity for this patient's condition.

**Regarding Dr. Walsh's appeal basis:**
*   **Dispute of Dr. Rivera's interpretation:** Dr. Rivera's interpretation ("no cord compression and no neurological deficit on examination") is directly supported by the objective MRI and neurological exam findings.
*   **Treating surgeon's experience and judgment:** While Dr. Walsh's experience is noted, clinical judgment must be supported by objective findings and adherence to generally accepted medical standards. In this case, the objective findings do not align with an urgent need for surgery or an inability to complete further conservative care for radiculopathy.
*   **Risk of permanent disability:** This assertion is not supported by the objective clinical evidence for Ms. Oduya's mild cervical radiculopathy without cord compression, myelomalacia, or significant neurological deficit. Such risks are typically associated with myelopathy or progressive neurological deficits, which are absent here.

---

**V. DISPOSITION**

**The denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) for Stephanie Oduya is UPHELD.**

**Warrant:** The member's clinical presentation is consistent with cervical radiculopathy without objective evidence of spinal cord compression, myelomalacia, or significant neurological deficit. The request for surgical intervention does not meet HealthFirst's medical necessity criteria (Section 2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement) because the member has not completed the required duration of physical therapy (4/6 weeks), has not adequately trialed two distinct pharmacotherapy agents (1/2 agents), and has not undergone an interventional procedure. These plan criteria are consistent with California state law (CIC § 10169.5, as none of its exceptions apply to this case) and generally accepted clinical guidelines for the management of cervical radiculopathy (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine). The evidence base for radiculopathy supports conservative management as the initial approach, and the patient's objective findings do not indicate an urgent need for surgical intervention that would override conservative treatment requirements or warrant application of the myelopathy exception or urgent care guidance (DMHC APL 22-014). The treating surgeon's arguments are noted but are not supported by the objective clinical record or medical necessity criteria.

---

**VI. RECOMMENDATIONS**

HealthFirst recommends that Ms. Oduya complete the conservative treatment pathway as outlined in CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2, which includes:
*   Completion of at least 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy, with functional outcomes tracking.
*   Trial of a second distinct pharmacotherapy agent for at least 4 weeks.
*   Consideration of a cervical epidural steroid injection or other interventional treatment.

Following completion of the recommended conservative treatment, a new prior authorization request with updated clinical documentation, including functional assessments and response to all conservative therapies, may be submitted for review.
