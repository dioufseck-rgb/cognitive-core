**I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

This appeal concerns a prior authorization request for an ACDF at C5-C6 for Stephanie Oduya, denied by HealthFirst based on the lack of objective findings of cord compression or neurological deficit and incomplete conservative treatment. The treating physician, Dr. Walsh, asserts the patient requires immediate surgery.

Upon comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's plan criteria, relevant California state laws, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial denial is **UPHELD**. The clinical evidence consistently supports a diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy, not myelopathy. For cervical radiculopathy, the patient has not completed the required conservative treatment pathway as outlined in the plan criteria, nor does her current clinical presentation meet generally accepted clinical standards for surgical intervention without further conservative measures. Applicable California regulations do not mandate approval in this specific clinical scenario.

**IV. APPLICATION OF HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY**

**1. PLAN CRITERIA (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**

*   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:**
    *   The diagnosis of Cervical Radiculopathy (2.1(a)) and Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit (2.1(c)) are met based on the objective findings of C5-C6 disc herniation, C6 nerve root contact, and right C6 dermatomal tingling.
    *   The diagnosis of Cervical Myelopathy (2.1(b)) is NOT met, as there is no documented cord compression, myelopathic symptoms, or UMN signs.
*   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
    *   The patient fails to meet all three components of the standard conservative treatment requirement:
        *   (a) Physical therapy: 4 weeks completed, 6 weeks required.
        *   (b) Pharmacotherapy: 1 agent used, 2 agents required.
        *   (c) Interventional treatment: None completed, 1 required.
*   **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY:** This exception applies only to confirmed cervical myelopathy (criterion 2.1(b)). As the patient does not have confirmed myelopathy, this exception does not apply.
*   **Conclusion based on Plan Criteria:** The requested surgery does not meet HealthFirst's medical necessity criteria due to incomplete conservative treatment (Section 2.2) and the inapplicability of the myelopathy exception. **Denial is UPHELD under plan criteria.**

**2. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK (State Law):**

*   **CHSC § 1374.31(b) (Procedural Adequacy of Denial):** The initial denial notice specified "imaging shows mild-moderate disc herniation with no cord compression and no neurological deficit on examination. Conservative treatment pathway not completed. Request does not meet medical necessity criteria." This notice clearly states the specific clinical reason and criteria, therefore it is procedurally adequate.
*   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This APL provides exceptions to conservative treatment for "myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia."
    *   **Reviewer Finding:** As established, the patient does not have myelopathy with cord compression or myelomalacia. The primary diagnosis is radiculopathy. Therefore, the provisions of DMHC APL 22-014 regarding waiver of conservative treatment for urgent myelopathy cases do not apply to this case.
*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute prohibits conditioning surgery on PT completion where specific conditions are met:
    *   (a)(1) Physician documents PT contraindicated or unlikely to benefit: Dr. Walsh halted PT but did not document it as contraindicated or unlikely to benefit for *radiculopathy* given the nature and severity (mild symptoms, working full-time). His statement focuses on immediate surgical need, not the futility of further PT.
    *   (a)(2) Member reached a functional plateau: The record states "Mild improvement in pain" with PT. There is no therapist documentation of maximum benefit or failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement (functional plateau as defined by the clinical standard: <10% improvement over 3 sessions OR therapist documentation of maximum benefit). The patient's reported functional status ("working full-time, no activity limitations") does not indicate a plateau requiring immediate surgery.
    *   (a)(3) Objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address: This refers to conditions like myelomalacia with cord compression. The MRI explicitly states "No cord contact. No cord compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia." Therefore, this condition is not met.
    *   **Reviewer Finding:** None of the conditions in CIC § 10169.5 that would override the plan's PT requirement are met in this case.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard - Generally Accepted Standards):** This section dictates that a service meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice SHALL be approved regardless of more restrictive plan criteria. This requires evaluating the request against clinical standards.

**3. CLINICAL STANDARD (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

*   **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy (Section 1.1):** The guidelines emphasize the critical distinction, stating "Myelopathy and radiculopathy require fundamentally different treatment algorithms." Conservative treatment evidence is primarily for radiculopathy.
*   **Myelomalacia (Section 1.2):** Myelomalacia is a strong indicator for surgical urgency. However, the patient's MRI explicitly states "No myelomalacia."
*   **Conservative Treatment for Radiculopathy (Section 2.1, 2.3, Summary Table):**
    *   For cervical radiculopathy, conservative treatment is "typically effective (70-80% resolution)."
    *   The 6-week PT requirement in payer policies "derives from radiculopathy evidence."
    *   For radiculopathy, the "risk of PT delay" is generally "Low."
    *   There is no evidence supporting mandatory PT for *myelopathy*, but for *radiculopathy*, it's standard first-line therapy.
*   **Surgical Indications for ACDF (Section 3.1):**
    *   TIER 1 (Strong Indication): Applies to moderate-severe myelopathy, progressive neurological deficit, or myelomalacia. None of these are present.
    *   TIER 2 (Reasonable Indication): Includes "(b) Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and "(c) Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care."
        *   The patient has not completed 6-12 weeks of a comprehensive conservative trial (missing 2 weeks of PT, a second pharmacotherapy agent, and an interventional procedure).
        *   Her "mild" tingling and "working full-time, no activity limitations" do not indicate "significant functional impairment."
    *   TIER 3 (Surgery Not Indicated): Includes "(c) Mild radiculopathy not yet trialed on conservative treatment." The patient's symptoms are mild, and the conservative treatment pathway is incomplete.
*   **Functional Plateau (Section 5):** The patient showed "mild improvement in pain" with 4 weeks of PT and does not have significant functional limitations. The criteria for functional plateau (e.g., <10% improvement over 3 sessions or therapist documentation of maximal benefit) are not met.
*   **Reviewer Finding:** For cervical radiculopathy without features of myelopathy, myelomalacia, or significant/progressive neurological deficit, generally accepted clinical standards support a comprehensive trial of conservative management (typically 6-12 weeks) before surgical intervention. The patient has not completed such a trial, and her current clinical status does not indicate an urgent need for surgery that would bypass conservative measures under clinical guidelines.

**V. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND FINAL DISPOSITION**

The three sources of authority (Plan Criteria, California State Law, and Clinical Standards) are applied in a hierarchical manner. In this case, there is no direct conflict where a higher authority overrides a lower one to compel approval.

1.  **Plan Criteria:** The request fails to meet the conservative treatment requirements (Section 2.2) and the myelopathy exception is not applicable.
2.  **State Law:**
    *   CHSC § 1374.31(b): The denial notice was procedurally adequate.
    *   DMHC APL 22-014 and CIC § 10169.5: These state laws provide specific waivers for conservative treatment for *myelopathy* or when other clear conditions are met. However, the patient has *radiculopathy*, and the specific conditions for waiver are not met in this record.
    *   CHSC § 1374.32(b): This provision mandates approval if the service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice." As detailed below, the clinical standard does not support immediate surgery for this patient's presentation.
3.  **Clinical Standards:** For mild cervical radiculopathy without myelopathy, myelomalacia, or significant/progressive neurological deficit, generally accepted clinical guidelines recommend a more complete course of conservative treatment (typically 6-12 weeks) before considering surgery. The patient's 4 weeks of PT, single pharmacotherapy agent, and lack of interventional treatment falls short of this standard. Her current functional status also does not suggest urgency.

Dr. Walsh's arguments regarding his experience and clinical judgment, while noted, are not supported by the objective clinical findings in the submitted record, which explicitly rule out cord compression, myelomalacia, and significant neurological deficits that would indicate surgical urgency for a patient with radiculopathy. The risk of permanent disability for radiculopathy, without myelopathy, is generally low, and conservative management is typically very effective.

Since the proposed surgery does not meet the plan's criteria and is not supported by generally accepted clinical standards for this specific clinical presentation (cervical radiculopathy with mild symptoms and incomplete conservative treatment), and no state law mandates override, the initial denial is appropriate.

**Therefore, the prior authorization denial for CPT 22551 for Stephanie Oduya is hereby UPHELD.**

**VI. WARRANT**

The denial is upheld because Stephanie Oduya's clinical presentation is consistent with cervical radiculopathy, not cervical myelopathy. Her imaging (MRI) explicitly shows no spinal cord compression, no T2 signal change, and no myelomalacia, while her neurological exam shows no weakness and no upper motor neuron signs. For cervical radiculopathy, both HealthFirst's plan criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2) and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) recommend a more complete course of conservative treatment (typically 6-12 weeks of PT, two pharmacotherapy agents, and consideration of interventional treatment) prior to surgical intervention, especially for mild symptoms and without signs of neurological urgency. The patient has completed only 4 weeks of PT, used only one pharmacotherapy agent, and had no interventional treatment. Applicable California state laws (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) do not override the conservative treatment requirements in this specific clinical scenario, as the conditions for waiver (e.g., myelopathy, documented contraindication to PT, functional plateau) are not met by the evidence in the record.
