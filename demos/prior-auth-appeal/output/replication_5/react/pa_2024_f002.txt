**APPEAL DETERMINATION: PA-2024-F002**

**Member:** Steven Clark, M-101133
**Procedure:** ACDF C5-C6, one level (CPT 22551)
**Date of Denial:** 2024-10-10
**Level of Review:** First-level Appeal

**BACKGROUND:**
Mr. Steven Clark, a 46-year-old member of HealthFirst PPO Gold, was denied prior authorization for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6. The initial denial (MED-003) cited conflicting physical therapy documentation, stating uncertainty regarding the total duration of PT completed and that the plan required verification of PT duration before approval. The member's appeal asserts that both sets of PT records (Riverside PT and Coastal PT) are authentic, the discrepancy was due to incomplete initial submission for Coastal PT, and complete records demonstrating 6 weeks from each provider (12 weeks total) are now enclosed.

**CLINICAL PRESENTATION & DIAGNOSIS:**
The clinical record indicates a presenting diagnosis of Cervical Radiculopathy C5-C6 with functional impairment.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-08-15):** Revealed a C5-C6 large disc herniation with severe right C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis. No evidence of myelomalacia or spinal cord compression was reported, consistent with radiculopathy rather than myelopathy.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-08-20):** Demonstrated right C6 dermatomal numbness, bicep weakness 4/5 right, absent right bicep reflex, and a positive Spurling sign on the right. These findings are consistent with C6 radiculopathy. No upper motor neuron signs suggestive of myelopathy were documented.

**CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT HISTORY:**
*   **Physical Therapy:**
    *   Riverside Physical Therapy: 6 weeks (April 1 - May 10, 2024), 18 sessions. Records received complete. Documented "Plateau. Symptoms persist."
    *   Coastal Physical Therapy: 6 weeks (June 1 - July 12, 2024), 18 sessions. Complete records now submitted, initial submission was partial. Documented "No improvement in neurological deficit. Therapist documents functional plateau — maximum therapeutic benefit reached."
    *   **Total PT:** 12 weeks, 36 sessions. All records are now complete and verified.
*   **Pharmacotherapy:**
    *   Gabapentin 600mg TID for 5 months, with partial relief.
    *   Duloxetine 60mg daily for 3 months, with partial neuropathic benefit.
*   **Interventional Treatment:**
    *   Right C6 epidural steroid injection (August 28, 2024), providing 60% temporary relief, with symptoms returning to baseline within 3 weeks.

**REVIEW OF PLAN CRITERIA (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**

1.  **2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** Member has a confirmed diagnosis of Cervical Radiculopathy with objective nerve root compression on MRI and correlating radicular symptoms/neurological deficits. **MET.**
2.  **2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:**
    *   **(a) Physical therapy:** Minimum 6 weeks of supervised PT with documented functional outcomes. The member completed 12 weeks of PT with two separate providers, both documenting a functional plateau and persistent symptoms/neurological deficits. **MET.**
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** Trial of at least two agents for minimum 4 weeks each. The member completed trials of Gabapentin (5 months) and Duloxetine (3 months). **MET.**
    *   **(c) Interventional Treatment:** One interventional treatment. The member received a right C6 epidural steroid injection. **MET.**
    *   *Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A):* This exception is not applicable as the primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, not myelopathy.
3.  **2.3 Imaging Requirement:** Current MRI (within 6 months) demonstrates structural pathology (C5-C6 disc herniation, severe right C6 nerve root compression, foraminal stenosis) consistent with symptoms and proposed surgical level. **MET.**
4.  **2.4 Physician Documentation:** While specific attestations are not provided in the submitted summary, the request for surgery implies the treating physician's rationale, and functional limitations (bicep weakness) are documented.

**Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The member has met all of HealthFirst's Medical Necessity Criteria for ACDF for cervical radiculopathy. The initial denial basis (conflicting PT documentation) has been fully resolved by the appeal.

**REVIEW OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK:**

1.  **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):**
    *   This statute dictates that a plan shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where:
        *   (a)(2) "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." The member completed 12 weeks of PT, and both PT providers explicitly documented functional plateau and persistent neurological deficits. This condition is **MET**.
        *   (a)(3) "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The MRI demonstrates severe C6 nerve root compression, and the neurological exam confirms objective bicep weakness (4/5) and absent reflex. This constitutes objective structural neurological compromise. This condition is **MET**.
    *   Therefore, even if the plan's PT requirement was at issue (which it is not, as 12 weeks were completed), state law would prevent denial based on this factor.
2.  **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):**
    *   While primarily focused on myelopathy, Section II.3 states: "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice... Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs." This reinforces the need to consider generally accepted standards of care.
3.  **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (Standard of Review for IMR):**
    *   This section, applicable to IMR, specifies that medical necessity is determined by "generally accepted standards of medical practice," not solely by the plan's internal criteria. This principle implies that care consistent with clinical standards should be approved.

**Conclusion on State Law:** California state law strongly supports the approval of this request, particularly given the documented functional plateau from conservative treatment and objective neurological compromise.

**REVIEW OF CLINICAL STANDARD (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

1.  **Diagnostic Classification (Section 1.1):** The member's presentation clearly aligns with Cervical Radiculopathy: compression of an individual nerve root (C6), unilateral symptoms (right C6 dermatomal numbness, bicep weakness, absent reflex), and absence of upper motor neuron signs.
2.  **Conservative Treatment Duration (Section 2.1, 2.2, Summary Table):** For radiculopathy, clinical guidelines support a trial of 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment. The member completed an extensive 12-week course of PT, alongside pharmacotherapy and interventional procedures, all with documented lack of sustained improvement and a functional plateau.
3.  **Functional Plateau Definition (Section 5):** The clinical standard defines functional plateau as "therapist documentation that patient has reached maximum therapeutic benefit" or "no improvement in neurological symptoms (weakness, reflex changes) after 6-8 weeks of PT." Both PT providers documented a functional plateau, and the member continues to exhibit objective bicep weakness (4/5) and an absent reflex after 12 weeks of PT.
4.  **Surgical Indications (Section 3.1):** Per AANS/CNS guidelines, surgery is a reasonable option (Tier 2) for "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care." The member meets both of these criteria.

**Conclusion on Clinical Standard:** The requested ACDF is consistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice for cervical radiculopathy, given the severe nerve root compression, objective neurological deficit, and failure of an extensive course of conservative treatment with documented functional plateau.

**CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND FINAL DETERMINATION:**
The initial denial was based on a documentation issue that has been fully resolved by the appeal. Upon review of the complete clinical record, it is evident that the member has fulfilled and exceeded HealthFirst's own criteria for conservative treatment for cervical radiculopathy, including 12 weeks of physical therapy, two pharmacotherapy agents, and one interventional procedure, all with documented inadequate response and functional plateau.

Furthermore, California state law (CIC § 10169.5) explicitly prohibits plans from denying surgical approval based on physical therapy requirements when a functional plateau has been reached and objective structural neurological compromise exists, both of which are present in this case. The requested ACDF is also fully supported by generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice for cervical radiculopathy with persistent symptoms and objective neurological deficits after conservative treatment failure.

There are no conflicts among the three sources of authority; all support the approval of the requested procedure.

**DISPOSITION:** OVERTURN

**WARRANT:**
The denial for prior authorization of ACDF C5-C6 is **OVERTURNED**. The appeal successfully addressed the original denial reason by providing complete and verified physical therapy documentation, demonstrating that the member completed 12 weeks of PT with documented functional plateaus from both providers. This duration significantly exceeds the HealthFirst plan's minimum 6-week PT requirement for radiculopathy. Additionally, the member has completed trials of two pharmacotherapy agents (Gabapentin for 5 months, Duloxetine for 3 months) and one interventional procedure (right C6 epidural with temporary relief), all with persistent objective neurological deficits (right C6 dermatomal numbness, bicep weakness 4/5, absent bicep reflex) and functional impairment.

The clinical presentation, characterized by severe right C6 nerve root compression on MRI and correlating neurological deficits, fully meets the criteria for surgical intervention for cervical radiculopathy under HealthFirst's own Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Sections 2.1(a), 2.2(a,b,c), 2.3). Moreover, California Insurance Code § 10169.5 (a)(2) and (a)(3) explicitly state that a plan cannot condition surgical approval on further PT when a functional plateau has been reached or when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address, both conditions being met in this case. Finally, the requested ACDF is consistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice for cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits unresponsive to an adequate trial of conservative management, as outlined in AANS/CNS, NASS, and AO Spine guidelines.
