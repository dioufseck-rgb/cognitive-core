**HEALTHFIRST HEALTH PLAN — PRIOR AUTHORIZATION APPEAL DETERMINATION**

**Case ID:** PA-2024-C002
**Member:** David Santos (M-101122), Age 63
**Plan:** HealthFirst PPO Gold — Group Plan 77441, California
**Procedure:** ACDF C6-C7, one level (CPT 22551)
**Date of Initial Denial:** 2024-10-05
**Reviewer of Initial Denial:** Dr. Patricia Huang, MD
**Level of Review:** First-Level Internal Appeal

---

**Clinical Summary:**
Mr. David Santos, a 63-year-old member, requested prior authorization for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 due to mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy. Imaging from 2024-09-01 shows moderate cord compression at C6-C7 and, critically, T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord, along with right C7 foraminal stenosis. His neurological exam on 2024-09-05 revealed predominant right C7 radicular symptoms (weakness, numbness, pain) but also definitive myelopathic signs: bilateral hyperreflexia (right > left), positive right Hoffman sign, equivocal left Hoffman, and a mildly wide-based gait.

Conservative treatment included 6 weeks of physical therapy (18 sessions) under a myelopathy protocol, with documented "no improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms." Pharmacotherapy included Pregabalin for 3 months (partial right-sided pain relief) and Naproxen for 2 months (inadequate). An interventional right C7 epidural provided temporary right-sided relief but no change in bilateral hyperreflexia.

**Initial Denial Reason:**
The initial denial (MED-003) stated: "Myelopathy exception applied but criteria not met. MRI shows cord compression and T2 signal change. However, member's symptoms are primarily right-sided (radicular) with only mild bilateral components. Primary diagnosis remains radiculopathy. Myelopathy exception requires myelopathy as primary diagnosis."

**Appeal Basis Submitted by Member/Provider:**
1.  T2 signal change (myelomalacia) is present — regulatory override applies.
2.  Mixed myelopathy/radiculopathy presentation — both diagnoses are present.
3.  Plan cannot deny myelopathy exception when myelomalacia is objectively documented.

---

**APPEAL DETERMINATION: OVERTURN**

**Warrant for Decision:**

The initial denial is **OVERTURNED**. The denial's rationale, which hinges on the interpretation of "primary diagnosis" in the context of the plan's myelopathy exception, is in conflict with both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.

**1. Objective Evidence Confirms Clinically Significant Myelopathy:**
The member's clinical record presents compelling and objective evidence of cervical myelopathy.
*   **Imaging:** The MRI demonstrates C6-C7 moderate spinal cord compression and, crucially, **T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord** (Clinical record, MRI findings). Myelomalacia is a direct indicator of existing spinal cord injury/damage (CLINICAL STANDARD 1.2, DOMAIN KNOWLEDGE).
*   **Neurological Exam:** Objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs are present, including **bilateral hyperreflexia (right > left), positive right Hoffman sign, equivocal left Hoffman sign, and a mildly wide-based gait** (Clinical record, Neurological exam; DOMAIN KNOWLEDGE). These are hallmark signs of myelopathy (CLINICAL STANDARD 1.1).
*   While the member experiences prominent right-sided radicular pain, the presence of these objective myelopathic findings, particularly myelomalacia, elevates myelopathy to a condition of significant clinical concern. The plan's assertion that "Primary diagnosis remains radiculopathy" based on symptomatic predominance over objective neurological compromise is inconsistent with a comprehensive clinical assessment.

**2. California State Law Overrides Plan Criteria Regarding Conservative Treatment:**

*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This state law specifies conditions under which a health insurer cannot mandate physical therapy prior to surgery. Subsection (a)(3) is directly applicable: a plan cannot require physical therapy where "The condition involves **objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address**" (SOURCE DOCUMENT 2, Part III). The presence of **T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord** with associated cord compression unequivocally constitutes such objective evidence (DOMAIN KNOWLEDGE). Therefore, any plan requirement for further physical therapy, regardless of duration or the "primary diagnosis" clause, is legally superseded. The member completed 6 weeks of PT, and the documentation notes "No improvement in bilateral neurological signs," indicating a functional plateau for myelopathic symptoms (Clinical record, PT summary; CLINICAL STANDARD 5).

*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This regulatory guidance explicitly addresses cases like Mr. Santos'.
    *   Section II.1 states that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent" (SOURCE DOCUMENT 2, Part II). Mr. Santos' condition precisely fits this description.
    *   Section II.2 further mandates that for such urgent conditions, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when "Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia)" and "Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression" (SOURCE DOCUMENT 2, Part II). Both criteria are met: myelomalacia is present on MRI, and conservative treatments (PT, epidural) failed to improve the bilateral neurological signs (Clinical record).
    *   The APL specifically states that myelomalacia "may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements" (SOURCE DOCUMENT 2, Part II). The plan's internal "primary diagnosis" clause, which prevents the application of the myelopathy exception despite the presence of myelomalacia, directly conflicts with this state regulatory directive.

**3. Clinical Standards Support Surgical Intervention and Contraindicate Extended Conservative Trials:**

*   **Strong Surgical Indication:** Major spine societies' guidelines (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) universally identify myelopathy with objective cord compression and **myelomalacia on MRI** as a **TIER 1 strong indication for surgical intervention** (CLINICAL STANDARD 3.1, 3.2). Myelomalacia is recognized as a "strong predictor of permanent deficit if surgery delayed" and a "contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials" (CLINICAL STANDARD 1.2).
*   **Limited Efficacy of Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy:** The AO Spine Guidelines (2021) state, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration" (CLINICAL STANDARD 2.1). The plan's conservative treatment requirements, particularly those for physical therapy, are primarily derived from radiculopathy evidence and are not clinically appropriate for established myelopathy (CLINICAL STANDARD 2.2). The member's 6 weeks of PT specifically failed to improve myelopathic signs.
*   **Risk of Delay:** Clinical evidence consistently shows that delaying surgical treatment for moderate-to-severe myelopathy, especially with myelomalacia, is associated with a decreased likelihood of neurological recovery (CLINICAL STANDARD 2.3).

**4. Conflict Resolution (Hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):**
HealthFirst's internal clinical criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A) NOTE), which restricts its myelopathy exception based on myelopathy being the "PRIMARY diagnosis," conflicts with both California state law and established clinical standards of care.
*   State law (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) mandates a waiver of conservative treatment requirements when objective neurological compromise (like myelomalacia) is present.
*   Clinical standards strongly recommend surgery and advise against delaying it for extended conservative trials in cases of myelopathy with myelomalacia.
*   Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), services meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice SHALL be approved regardless of the plan's more restrictive internal criteria (SOURCE DOCUMENT 2, Part I).

**Conclusion:**
Based on the hierarchy of authority, California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice supersede HealthFirst's internal criteria. The member's cervical myelopathy with documented T2 signal change (myelomalacia) constitutes a time-sensitive neurological condition for which surgical intervention is strongly indicated, and further delays for conservative treatment are not medically appropriate.

The requested ACDF C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is medically necessary.

**DISPOSITION:** DENIAL OVERTURNED. Authorization for ACDF C6-C7 is Approved.
