**DISPOSITION: OVERTURN**

**WARRANT:**

The denial for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) for Mr. David Santos is **OVERTURNED**. The initial denial incorrectly applied the plan's internal conservative treatment criteria and myelopathy exception, which are superseded by California state law and generally accepted clinical standards for the member's specific presentation of cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia.

**1. Clinical Presentation Analysis:**
Mr. Santos, a 63-year-old male, presents with a complex picture of cervical pathology. The clinical record clearly documents objective signs of both cervical radiculopathy and cervical myelopathy:
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-01):** Reveals moderate cord compression at C6-C7 and, critically, T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord. Right C7 foraminal stenosis is also noted. Myelomalacia is objective evidence of existing spinal cord injury.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-09-05):** Shows predominant right C7 radicular symptoms (weakness 4/5, numbness). However, it also conclusively demonstrates myelopathic signs, including bilateral hyperreflexia (right > left), positive right Hoffman sign, equivocal left Hoffman, and a mildly wide-based gait. These are definitive upper motor neuron signs and gait disturbance consistent with cervical myelopathy.
*   **Conclusion:** The member has a mixed presentation, but the presence of objective cord compression, myelomalacia, and multiple upper motor neuron signs firmly establishes a diagnosis of moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy, not merely "mild bilateral components" or a secondary finding.

**2. Application of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
*   **Diagnosis Requirement (2.1):** The member meets criteria 2.1(a) for cervical radiculopathy and 2.1(b) for cervical myelopathy. Imaging confirms structural pathology.
*   **Conservative Treatment Requirement (2.2):** The member completed:
    *   Physical Therapy: 6 weeks (18 sessions), noting "No improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms." This meets the standard 6-week requirement (2.2(a)) and exceeds the 4-week myelopathy exception requirement.
    *   Pharmacotherapy: Pregabalin (3 months) and Naproxen (2 months), meeting 2.2(b).
    *   Interventional: One right C7 epidural injection, meeting 2.2(c).
*   **Myelopathy Exception (2.2A):** The plan's initial denial focused on the note for this exception, stating it "applies only when myelopathy is the PRIMARY diagnosis" and concluding that radiculopathy remained the primary diagnosis. This interpretation, while adhering to the *letter* of the plan's internal note, is clinically inappropriate and in conflict with higher authority as detailed below.

**3. Application of California Regulatory Requirements:**
California state law and regulatory guidance directly address and override the plan's denial logic:

*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (September 2022) — Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions:**
    *   **Section II.1:** "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent unless compelling clinical evidence supports otherwise." Mr. Santos's MRI findings meet this definition. The plan's attempt to classify myelopathy as non-primary, thereby applying standard conservative treatment requirements, is in direct conflict with this mandate.
    *   **Section II.2:** For conditions where delay risks irreversible harm, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment... when (a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia...), AND (b) Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed... AND (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines..." Mr. Santos's case meets all these criteria: myelomalacia is present, PT failed to improve neurological signs, and surgery is indicated (see clinical standard below). The APL specifically identifies myelomalacia as a finding that supports waiver of standard conservative treatment.
    *   **Section II.3:** "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for conditions identified in this guidance. Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs." The plan's rigid "primary diagnosis" requirement for the myelopathy exception, and its insistence on a full conservative trial despite myelomalacia, is more restrictive than the standards mandated by this APL.

*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (January 1, 2023) — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:**
    *   **Subsection (a)(3):** This code states that a health insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... [t]he condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) with cord compression is precisely such objective evidence, indicating existing cord damage that PT cannot reverse or adequately address, especially for the myelopathic symptoms.
    *   **Subsection (a)(2):** PT completion cannot be required where the "member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." The physical therapy summary explicitly states "No improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms," indicating a functional plateau, particularly for the myelopathic signs.

**4. Application of Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
*   **Myelomalacia Significance:** The clinical reference summary (Section 1.2) identifies myelomalacia as indicating existing cord injury, higher risk of permanent deficit, reduced likelihood of recovery, and surgical urgency. It is considered a strong indication for surgical intervention and a *contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials*.
*   **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy (Section 2.1, 2.2):** Major guidelines state that physical therapy does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement for moderate-severe myelopathy, and there is "no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention" for these patients (AO Spine 2021). The 6-week (or even 4-week) PT requirement derived from radiculopathy evidence is a "category error" when applied to myelopathy.
*   **Risk of Delay (Section 2.3):** Delaying surgery for moderate-severe myelopathy, especially with myelomalacia, is associated with decreased neurological recovery and worse outcomes.
*   **Surgical Indications (Section 3.1, 3.2):** Mr. Santos's presentation (confirmed myelopathy, myelomalacia, objective neurological deficits including UMN signs and gait disturbance) meets **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION** for surgery according to AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023). The clinical standard does NOT require completion of a minimum course of physical therapy as a prerequisite for surgery in this context.

**5. Hierarchy of Authority & Conflict Resolution:**
The three-source hierarchy (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria) dictates the resolution:
*   The plan's denial is based on its internal criterion requiring myelopathy to be the "primary diagnosis" for its exception, and its implicit requirement for a specific duration of conservative treatment for myelopathy.
*   **State Law (DMHC APL 22-014, CIC § 10169.5)** directly overrides these plan criteria by:
    *   Mandating urgent treatment for myelopathy with myelomalacia.
    *   Waiving conservative treatment requirements (including specific PT duration) when myelomalacia is present and neurological compromise exists.
*   **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine)** further supports the ineffectiveness and potential harm of delaying surgery for mandatory conservative treatment in myelopathy with myelomalacia, classifying the plan's approach as a "category error" and inconsistent with evidence-based practice.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b)** confirms that for Independent Medical Review (and by extension, internal appeals governed by similar principles), a service meeting generally accepted standards SHALL be approved regardless of the plan's more restrictive internal criteria.

**Conclusion:**
The member's clinical presentation, specifically the presence of myelomalacia and objective myelopathic signs with documented failure of conservative treatment to improve neurological symptoms, falls squarely within the scope of California regulatory protections and strong clinical recommendations for timely surgical intervention. The plan's internal criteria, as applied, are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice and are superseded by state law.

**ACTION:**
The prior authorization denial for ACDF C6-C7 (CPT 22551) for Mr. David Santos is **OVERTURNED**. Authorization for the requested procedure is to be issued immediately.
