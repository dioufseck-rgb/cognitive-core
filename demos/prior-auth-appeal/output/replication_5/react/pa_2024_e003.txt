## APPEAL DETERMINATION: Prior Authorization for Revision ACDF (CPT 22551)

**Case ID:** PA-2024-E003
**Member:** George Harris (M-889922), 48
**Plan:** HealthFirst PPO Gold — Group Plan 77441
**Procedure:** Revision ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551)
**Date of Denial:** 2024-10-30
**Reviewer:** Expert Health Plan Appeals Reviewer

---

### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The initial denial for revision ACDF C5-C6 for Mr. George Harris is **OVERTURNED**.

The denial was based on a misapplication of the plan's stated revision criteria, failing to acknowledge objective imaging evidence confirming pseudarthrosis, which is explicitly listed as a covered condition for revision surgery. Furthermore, Mr. Harris has completed an adequate course of conservative treatment without improvement in his radicular symptoms, consistent with both HealthFirst's own criteria and California state law provisions regarding the completion of physical therapy. The requested procedure is medically necessary and meets generally accepted standards of medical practice.

---

### APPEAL REVIEW DETAILS

**1. Initial Denial Summary:**
The initial denial (reason code MED-003) stated: "Revision surgery not covered under standard ACDF criteria. Member had prior C5-C6 ACDF in 2021. Revision criteria require documentation of hardware failure, pseudarthrosis, or new pathology at previously treated level. Documentation submitted does not confirm hardware failure." (Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, 2024-10-30)

**2. Appeal Basis:**
The appeal asserts that a CT scan confirms pseudarthrosis (failure of fusion) at C5-C6, symptoms have returned and worsened since the 2021 fusion, and revision criteria are met per the treating surgeon.

**3. Clinical Record Review:**
*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Pseudarthrosis C5-C6 following prior ACDF 2021 — failed fusion, leading to recurrent C6 radiculopathy.
*   **Prior History:** C5-C6 ACDF in 2021 with initial good outcome, symptoms recurred at 18 months.
*   **Imaging:**
    *   MRI (2024-09-01): Hardware artifact limited assessment; possible non-union/pseudarthrosis but not definitive. No mention of spinal cord compression or myelomalacia.
    *   **CT (2024-09-15): "CT without artifact limitation: C5-C6 — incomplete bridging bone formation. Lucency around hardware suggesting pseudarthrosis. Findings consistent with failed fusion/pseudarthrosis. Hardware appears intact without fracture."**
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-09-20):** Objective evidence of C6 radiculopathy: return of right C6 symptoms, bicep weakness 4/5 right, bicep reflex diminished right, C6 dermatomal numbness. Symptoms are worse than preoperative baseline from 2021.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 8 weeks, 24 sessions. Documented "No improvement — treating physician notes PT cannot address structural hardware/fusion failure."
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Naproxen 500mg BID for 4 months (partial pain relief); Gabapentin 300mg TID for 3 months (partial neuropathic relief).
    *   **Interventional:** Epidural injection (2024-10-10) with "Minimal improvement — treating physician notes this is expected when structural failure is present."
*   **Clinical Presentation:** The member's symptoms are characteristic of cervical radiculopathy (unilateral, dermatomal symptoms, weakness, diminished reflex). There is no documentation of myelopathy (bilateral symptoms, gait disturbance, hyperreflexia, Hoffman sign, myelomalacia).

### ANALYSIS AND DETERMINATION

The determination is based on the hierarchy: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.

**A. Evaluation of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**

1.  **Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1):** Mr. Harris has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (2.1(a)) with objective neurological deficit (right C6 weakness, numbness, diminished reflex) and imaging showing structural pathology (pseudarthrosis C5-C6) consistent with symptoms. This criterion is **MET**.
2.  **Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2):**
    *   **Physical Therapy (2.2(a)):** Member completed 8 weeks of PT, exceeding the 6-week minimum. Documented outcome is "no improvement," and the treating physician stated PT cannot address the structural failure. This criterion is **MET**.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2(b)):** Member completed trials of Naproxen (4 months) and Gabapentin (3 months), exceeding the minimum 4 weeks for two agents. This criterion is **MET**.
    *   **Interventional Treatment (2.2(c)):** Member underwent one cervical epidural steroid injection. This criterion is **MET**.
    *   **Myelopathy Exception (2.2(A)):** This exception does not apply as the primary diagnosis is radiculopathy, not myelopathy.
    All conservative treatment requirements under HealthFirst's standard criteria are **MET**.
3.  **Imaging Requirement (Section 2.3):** The CT scan (2024-09-15), performed within 6 months, demonstrates "incomplete bridging bone formation. Lucency around hardware suggesting pseudarthrosis," which is consistent with the member's radicular symptoms. This criterion is **MET**.
4.  **Physician Documentation (Section 2.4):** The clinical record contains documentation from the treating physician regarding conservative treatment, rationale for surgery, and functional limitations. This criterion is **MET**.

**Initial Denial Flaw:** The denial notice stated that "Revision criteria require documentation of hardware failure, pseudarthrosis, or new pathology at previously treated level" but then only denied because documentation "does not confirm hardware failure." This demonstrates a **misapplication of the plan's own stated criteria**. The CT scan **clearly and unequivocally confirms pseudarthrosis**, which is one of the three conditions HealthFirst's *own denial notice* stated were acceptable for revision surgery. Therefore, the denial is internally inconsistent and flawed.

**B. Application of California Regulatory Framework:**

1.  **CHSC § 1374.31(b) — Defective Denial Notice:** The denial notice, by failing to properly apply its own stated criteria (specifically ignoring the documented pseudarthrosis while stating pseudarthrosis is a valid criterion), may be considered procedurally defective in its reasoning.
2.  **CIC § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** Even if the plan had attempted to argue insufficient conservative treatment (which it did not, as its denial was based on criteria for revision surgery), state law would prohibit such a denial.
    *   Subsection (a)(1): The treating physician documented that PT "cannot address structural hardware/fusion failure," indicating it is unlikely to benefit.
    *   Subsection (a)(2): The member completed 8 weeks of PT with "No improvement," indicating a functional plateau was reached.
    *   Subsection (a)(3): Pseudarthrosis is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address.
    These provisions override any mechanical application of a minimum PT duration.
3.  **DMHC APL 22-014 — Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** While this APL primarily focuses on myelopathy, Section II.3 states, "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice..." This reinforces the principle that medically necessary care should not be denied due to overly restrictive or misapplied plan criteria.
4.  **CHSC § 1374.32(b) — IMR Standard:** This provision emphasizes that services meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice SHALL be approved regardless of the plan's more restrictive internal criteria.

**C. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

1.  **Diagnosis:** The member presents with persistent, worsening C6 radiculopathy due to a structural mechanical problem (pseudarthrosis).
2.  **Conservative Treatment Efficacy:** For structural issues like failed fusion/pseudarthrosis, conservative measures like PT and injections are expected to have limited or no long-term efficacy, as indicated by the treating physician and observed clinical outcomes. The clinical standard for radiculopathy (AANS/CNS TIER 2) suggests surgery for "persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and "significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care." Mr. Harris meets these criteria, especially given the underlying structural failure (pseudarthrosis).
3.  **Surgical Indication:** Revision surgery for symptomatic pseudarthrosis causing persistent radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits, after failed conservative treatment, is a **generally accepted indication for surgical intervention** per major spine societies. The AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) classify radiculopathy with persistent symptoms and significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care as a TIER 2 reasonable indication for surgery.
4.  **Functional Plateau:** The definition of functional plateau by clinical standard (Section 5, Source Document 3) is met, as the patient had "No improvement" in 8 weeks of PT and the physician documented maximum benefit reached given the structural issue.

**D. Conflict Resolution (Hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):**

*   The plan's initial denial reasoning was flawed, failing to correctly apply its own stated revision criteria by ignoring the clear evidence of pseudarthrosis.
*   Even if the plan's criteria for conservative treatment were somehow deemed unmet (which they were not), California state law (CIC 10169.5) explicitly overrides such a denial when a functional plateau has been reached or the treating physician documents that PT is unlikely to benefit due to structural issues, as is the case here.
*   The requested revision ACDF for symptomatic pseudarthrosis leading to radiculopathy, after failed conservative treatment, aligns with generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine).

---

### DISPOSITION

**OVERTURN**

---

### WARRANT

The initial denial by HealthFirst is **OVERTURNED** because it was based on a flawed application of the plan's stated "revision criteria" and is contrary to both the plan's own coverage requirements when properly interpreted, California state law, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.

1.  **Misapplication of Plan Criteria:** HealthFirst's denial explicitly stated that "Revision criteria require documentation of hardware failure, **pseudarthrosis**, or new pathology at previously treated level." However, the denial then only focused on the absence of "hardware failure," completely ignoring the clear and definitive CT imaging evidence from 2024-09-15 that confirms "**pseudarthrosis** (failure of fusion) at C5-C6." This constitutes a direct misapplication of the plan's own stated criteria by the initial reviewer. (Denial Reason; Clinical record: imaging - CT_1)
2.  **Adherence to Plan's General ACDF Criteria:** Even reviewing against HealthFirst's standard ACDF criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), Mr. Harris meets all requirements:
    *   **Diagnosis:** Objective evidence of C6 radiculopathy consistent with pseudarthrosis. (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(a), 2.3)
    *   **Conservative Treatment:** Mr. Harris completed 8 weeks of physical therapy (exceeding the 6-week minimum), trials of two pharmacotherapy agents for more than 4 weeks each, and one interventional epidural injection. Despite these efforts, his neurological symptoms worsened. (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a,b,c); Clinical record: conservative_treatment)
3.  **California State Law Override:** California Insurance Code § 10169.5 explicitly prohibits a health plan from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where:
    *   The treating physician documents PT is unlikely to benefit given the nature and severity of the condition (i.e., structural fusion failure/pseudarthrosis). (CIC § 10169.5(a)(1); Clinical record: physical_therapy_summary)
    *   The member has completed PT and reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms, which occurred after 8 weeks of PT with "No improvement." (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2); Clinical record: physical_therapy_summary; Clinical Standard: Section 5 functional plateau definition)
4.  **Consistency with Clinical Standards:** Revision surgery for symptomatic pseudarthrosis causing persistent radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits, after failed conservative treatment, is a recognized and accepted indication for surgical intervention under generally accepted standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023, TIER 2 indication). The presence of a failed fusion (pseudarthrosis) as the structural cause for persistent radiculopathy justifies surgical correction. (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023, Section 3.1)

Based on the compelling clinical evidence, the clear misapplication of the plan's own criteria in the initial denial, and the overriding provisions of California state law and generally accepted clinical standards, the requested revision ACDF is medically necessary.
