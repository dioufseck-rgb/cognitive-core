**APPEAL DETERMINATION LETTER**

**Case ID:** PA-2024-G002
**Member:** Priya Nair (M-G002), Age 56
**Procedure:** ACDF C5-C6, one level (CPT 22551)
**Date of Denial:** 2024-10-25
**Reviewer:** Appeals Reviewer, HealthFirst Health Plan
**Date of Appeal Determination:** 2024-11-01

**DENIAL REASON (Initial Decision):**
The initial denial, issued by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, cited "Member does not meet conservative treatment requirements under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. Physical therapy: 5 weeks completed. Standard pathway requires 6 weeks minimum. Member has not completed the required minimum conservative treatment."

**APPEAL BASIS:**
The member's appeal asserts that:
1.  The plan incorrectly applied the standard pathway (Section 2.2) instead of the myelopathy pathway (Section 2.2A).
2.  The member has a confirmed primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, supported by bilateral upper motor neuron (UMN) signs and cord compression on MRI.
3.  The myelopathy pathway (Section 2.2A) requires a minimum of 4 weeks of physical therapy (PT), which the member exceeded by completing 5 weeks.
4.  Under the correct myelopathy pathway, all applicable criteria are met.

**CLINICAL RECORD SUMMARY:**
Ms. Priya Nair is a 56-year-old female presenting with a confirmed primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy at C5-C6. This diagnosis is supported by a neurological exam (bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia in upper extremities, mildly impaired tandem gait, bilateral hand clumsiness – all consistent with UMN signs) and MRI findings (C5-C6 moderate central cord compression). A neurologist consultation confirmed the diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, noting "Bilateral UMN signs are pathognomonic."

Conservative treatment included:
*   **Physical Therapy:** 5 weeks (15 sessions) completed under a myelopathy protocol. The treating physician halted PT due to the myelopathy diagnosis and documented "No improvement in UMN signs."
*   **Pharmacotherapy:** Baclofen 10mg TID for 3 months (minimal spasticity relief) and Methylprednisolone dose pack for 3 weeks (temporary improvement only).
*   **Interventional Treatment:** Waived, as the treating physician (Dr. Chen) documented that "Interventional procedure not indicated — cord compression with myelopathy requires surgical evaluation, not injection," which aligns with the plan's myelopathy pathway exception for interventional treatment.

**APPEAL REVIEW AND DISPOSITION:**

Applying the three-source hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), the denial for CPT 22551 for Ms. Priya Nair is determined to be **OVERTURNED**.

**WARRANT AND CITATIONS:**

1.  **Incorrect Application of Plan Criteria (Initial Denial Error):**
    *   The initial denial cited HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, which requires a minimum of 6 weeks of physical therapy (PT). However, the clinical record unequivocally confirms a primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy C5-C6 with objective neurological signs and imaging.
    *   HealthFirst's own criteria, Section 2.2, **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY**, explicitly states that for members with confirmed cervical myelopathy (criterion 2.1(b)) as the primary diagnosis, the physical therapy requirement is reduced to a minimum of 4 weeks.
    *   The member completed 5 weeks of PT, which exceeds the 4-week minimum required under the applicable myelopathy pathway.
    *   **Conclusion:** The initial denial was based on an incorrect application of HealthFirst's own policy, failing to recognize and apply the myelopathy exception for PT duration.

2.  **Plan Criteria Compliance (Corrected Pathway Analysis):**
    *   **Diagnosis (2.1(b)):** Met. Confirmed cervical myelopathy by neurological exam and MRI showing cord compression.
    *   **Physical Therapy (2.2(A)):** Met. Member completed 5 weeks, exceeding the 4-week minimum for myelopathy. Furthermore, treating physician documented "No improvement in UMN signs," indicating a functional plateau.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2(b)):** Under the myelopathy exception, the pharmacotherapy requirement "remains in full" (two agents for minimum 4 weeks each). While Baclofen (3 months) meets this, Methylprednisolone (3 weeks) does not. Therefore, this specific component of the plan's pharmacotherapy criterion is not fully met *by plan criteria alone*.
    *   **Interventional Treatment (2.2(c) waiver):** Met. Dr. Chen documented that interventional treatment was not indicated, meeting the waiver condition for neurological urgency in the myelopathy pathway.
    *   **Imaging (2.3):** Met. Current MRI (2024-09-01) demonstrates C5-C6 moderate central cord compression, consistent with symptoms and proposed surgical level.
    *   **Conclusion:** While the PT and interventional criteria under the myelopathy pathway are met, the pharmacotherapy criterion for two agents >4 weeks is not fully met by plan criteria. This is where higher authorities (State Law and Clinical Standard) become critical.

3.  **California State Law Override:**
    *   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This code prohibits conditioning surgical approval on PT completion where:
        *   **(a)(2):** The member has completed a course of PT and the physician documents a functional plateau. The record states 5 weeks of PT with "No improvement in UMN signs," indicating a functional plateau for myelopathy-related symptoms.
        *   **(a)(3):** The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. The MRI shows "C5-C6 moderate central cord compression," which constitutes structural neurological compromise that PT cannot resolve.
        *   **(c):** A plan's internal PT duration requirement "shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or whether the condition involves neurological urgency." The plan's mechanical application of a PT duration requirement (even the 4-week one) is overridden by these provisions.
    *   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):**
        *   **II.1:** Classifies cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression as an urgent condition with potential for irreversible harm. Ms. Nair's diagnosis meets this.
        *   **II.2:** States plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when a physician documents conservative failure and surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. Dr. Chen's letter and the lack of improvement in UMN signs support conservative failure.
        *   **II.3:** Crucially, states "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for conditions identified in this guidance. Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs."
    *   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This provision mandates that if a service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice," it SHALL be approved "regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." This provision overrides any plan criteria that are more restrictive than clinical standards, including the pharmacotherapy duration if the clinical standard deems the procedure medically necessary.
    *   **Conclusion:** California state law significantly limits a plan's ability to mechanically enforce conservative treatment requirements, particularly for conditions involving structural neurological compromise and urgent presentations like myelopathy.

4.  **Clinical Standard Override:**
    *   **Diagnosis and Urgency:** Ms. Nair's confirmed cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and UMN signs meets the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) "TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION" for surgical intervention (Clinical Reference, Section 3.1).
    *   **Efficacy of PT for Myelopathy:** The clinical evidence (AO Spine Guidelines 2021, Cochrane Review 2021) strongly indicates that physical therapy does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement for moderate-severe myelopathy and there is "no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention" for this condition. Furthermore, delaying surgery for PT is "not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration" (Clinical Reference, Section 2.1). Applying radiculopathy-derived PT requirements to myelopathy is a "category error" (Clinical Reference, Section 1.1).
    *   **Conservative Treatment Duration:** For myelopathy, "No randomized controlled trials support a defined minimum duration of conservative treatment before surgery" (Clinical Reference, Section 2.2).
    *   **Risk of Delay:** "Each month of delay in surgical treatment for moderate-severe myelopathy is associated with decreased likelihood of neurological recovery" (Clinical Reference, Section 2.3).
    *   **Conclusion:** Generally accepted clinical standards strongly recommend surgery for Ms. Nair's condition and explicitly caution against delaying surgery to complete arbitrary conservative treatment durations like PT. The overall conservative efforts (5 weeks PT, two pharmacotherapy agents attempted) combined with the clear myelopathy diagnosis and UMN signs are sufficient to indicate surgery per clinical standards, overriding any remaining plan-specific requirements.

**FINAL DETERMINATION:**
The initial denial was procedurally flawed by applying the incorrect plan pathway. Even after correcting this, a minor aspect of the plan's pharmacotherapy criterion was not strictly met. However, the patient's condition (cervical myelopathy with cord compression and UMN signs) represents a Tier 1 strong surgical indication under generally accepted clinical guidelines. Both California state law (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) and the clinical standard (AANS/CNS, AO Spine) override the plan's conservative treatment duration requirements in this context due to the nature of myelopathy, the demonstrated lack of PT benefit for myelopathic symptoms, the presence of structural neurological compromise, and the risk of delaying surgery.

Therefore, the requested ACDF C5-C6 is medically necessary under generally accepted standards of medical practice and the denial must be overturned.

**DISPOSITION:** **OVERTURNED**

**Action:** Authorization for CPT code 22551 for ACDF C5-C6 for Ms. Priya Nair is approved.
