This letter serves as the HealthFirst Health Plan's determination regarding the appeal of the prior authorization denial for your requested Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at levels C5-C6.

**Decision: OVERTURN the Denial**

The denial for CPT code 22551 is **OVERTURNED**. Authorization for the requested ACDF C5-C6 is hereby granted.

---

### **Review Summary and Rationale**

The initial denial, dated 2024-10-25, stated that the member did not meet conservative treatment requirements under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, specifically citing a requirement for 6 weeks of physical therapy (PT), with 5 weeks completed.

The appeal argues that the plan applied the incorrect pathway, asserting that the Myelopathy Pathway (Section 2.2A) of CC-SPINE-2024 applies, which reduces the PT requirement to 4 weeks, and that the member completed 5 weeks, exceeding this applicable requirement.

Our review confirms that the initial denial incorrectly applied HealthFirst's own criteria and that, even considering any minor discrepancies with internal plan criteria, state law and generally accepted clinical standards supersede these criteria, mandating approval of the requested procedure.

---

### **Detailed Analysis**

**1. Clinical Presentation:**
The member, Priya Nair (age 56), presents with a confirmed diagnosis of **Cervical Myelopathy C5-C6**. This diagnosis is well-supported by:
*   **Imaging:** MRI dated 2024-09-01 demonstrates moderate central cord compression at C5-C6.
*   **Neurological Exam:** Findings from 2024-09-05 include bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia in upper extremities, mildly impaired tandem gait, and bilateral hand clumsiness. These are definitive upper motor neuron (UMN) signs pathognomonic for myelopathy.
*   **Neurologist Confirmation:** Consultation on 2024-09-12 confirms "Clinical picture consistent with cervical myelopathy. Bilateral UMN signs are pathognomonic. Diagnosis: cervical myelopathy, primary." (ICD-10: G35.89).

**2. Application of HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

*   **Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1):** The member clearly meets criterion 2.1(b) for Cervical Myelopathy. **MET.**
*   **Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2):** This was the sole basis for the initial denial.
    *   **Initial Review Error:** The initial denial incorrectly applied the "Standard pathway" (Section 2.2), which requires 6 weeks of PT.
    *   **Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2A) Applies:** The clinical record confirms cervical myelopathy as the *primary* diagnosis. Therefore, the "EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY" of CC-SPINE-2024 should have been applied.
        *   **Physical Therapy (PT):** Under Section 2.2A, the PT requirement is reduced to **4 weeks minimum**. The member completed **5 weeks** of PT, which exceeds this reduced requirement. The treating physician also halted PT at 5 weeks due to lack of improvement in UMN signs, indicating functional plateau for the neurological condition. **MET.**
        *   **Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)):** The plan's myelopathy exception states this requirement "remains in full" (trial of at least two agents for minimum 4 weeks each). The member underwent a trial of Baclofen for 3 months (meets criteria) and Methylprednisolone for 3 weeks (does not strictly meet the 4-week minimum *each* requirement). While this is a technical deviation from the plan's strict internal pharmacotherapy duration requirement, as explained below, this is superseded by state law and clinical standards for myelopathy.
        *   **Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)):** This requirement is **WAIVED** under Section 2.2A if the treating physician documents that it poses a risk of neurological deterioration. Dr. Chen documented this, stating: "Interventional procedure not indicated — cord compression with myelopathy requires surgical evaluation, not injection." **WAIVED.**
*   **Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The initial denial was fundamentally flawed by failing to apply the correct section of its *own* policy (Myelopathy Exception 2.2A). Had the correct section been applied, the PT requirement would have been met. While the pharmacotherapy requirement was not strictly met per the plan's duration, higher authorities govern this.

**3. Application of California Regulatory Requirements:**

*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This state law explicitly prohibits conditioning surgical approval on PT completion where:
    *   (a)(1) The physician documents PT is unlikely to benefit: Dr. Chen halted PT at 5 weeks given the myelopathy diagnosis and lack of improvement in UMN signs.
    *   (a)(2) The member reached a functional plateau: "No improvement in UMN signs" after 5 weeks for a neurological condition constitutes a functional plateau, as PT is not expected to resolve cord compression or UMN signs.
    *   (a)(3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address: The MRI shows C5-C6 moderate central cord compression, which PT cannot resolve.
    *   **Conclusion:** Under CIC § 10169.5, the plan *cannot* require further PT in this case, regardless of its internal policy.

*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 — Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions:** This guidance states that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm, and plans **SHALL** treat such cases as urgent (II.1). For such conditions, plans **SHALL NOT** require completion of a full course of conservative treatment (including strict PT or pharmacotherapy durations) when:
    *   (a) Objective imaging demonstrates pathology: Moderate central cord compression is present.
    *   (b) Treating physician documents conservative treatment failure: Dr. Chen's notes on halting PT due to lack of improvement in UMN signs and the minimal response to pharmacotherapy confirm this.
    *   (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines: This is confirmed below.
    *   **Conclusion:** DMHC APL 22-014 overrides the plan's conservative treatment requirements (including pharmacotherapy duration) for this urgent myelopathy case.

*   **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) — Standard of Review for IMR:** This law dictates that the Independent Medical Review standard is NOT the plan's internal criteria. If a service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, it SHALL be approved, even if the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.

**4. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

*   **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines clearly distinguish myelopathy from radiculopathy, emphasizing different treatment algorithms. Applying radiculopathy-derived conservative treatment durations (like a 6-week PT minimum) to myelopathy is a "category error not supported by evidence" (Source 3, Section 1.1, 2.2).
*   **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** For moderate-severe myelopathy, physical therapy "does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement" (Cochrane Review, Source 3, Section 2.1). Critically, AO Spine Guidelines (2021) state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." (Source 3, Section 2.1).
*   **Surgical Indication:** The member's presentation—confirmed cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and UMN signs (bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, gait impairment, hand clumsiness)—meets the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION** for surgery (Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression; Progressive neurological deficit despite conservative treatment). (Source 3, Section 3.1).
*   **Conclusion:** Generally accepted clinical standards strongly support surgical intervention for this presentation of myelopathy and explicitly contraindicate delaying surgery for arbitrary conservative treatment durations like PT.

**5. Conflict Resolution (Hierarchy: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria):**
In this case, all three levels of authority point to overturning the denial:
*   **Plan's Internal Error:** The initial denial failed to correctly apply HealthFirst's own Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2A) which would have approved the PT duration.
*   **State Law Override:** CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014 explicitly override any plan requirements for physical therapy and a "full course" of conservative treatment (including strict pharmacotherapy durations) for urgent myelopathy cases with objective neurological compromise where surgery is indicated.
*   **Clinical Standard Override:** AANS/CNS, NASS, and AO Spine guidelines clearly indicate that surgery is strongly recommended for this member's presentation, and delaying surgery for conservative treatment, particularly extended PT, is not supported by evidence and may lead to neurological deterioration. These guidelines take precedence over any more restrictive plan criteria (CHSC § 1374.32(b)).

Therefore, despite the minor technical non-conformance with the plan's pharmacotherapy duration, the overarching state laws and clinical standards mandate approval of the requested surgery.

---

### **Determination**

Based on the foregoing analysis, the initial denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) is **OVERTURNED**. The member has a confirmed primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and UMN signs, strongly indicating surgical intervention per generally accepted clinical standards. Furthermore, California state laws provide specific protections that override the plan's conservative treatment requirements in this context.

**Authorization is hereby granted for procedure CPT 22551.**
