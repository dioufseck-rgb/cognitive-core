### I. SUMMARY OF REQUEST AND DENIAL

Mr. Raymond Flores, a 54-year-old construction worker, requested prior authorization for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6. The request was explicitly for "prophylactic decompression" due to severe cervical stenosis with cord contact.

The initial prior authorization request was denied by HealthFirst on 2024-10-25 (Dr. Patricia Huang, MD) with Reason Code MED-003. The denial stated: "Cervical stenosis documented but no neurological deficit on examination. Member has axial neck pain only. No radiculopathy, no myelopathy documented. Surgical intervention not indicated for axial pain without neurological deficit under CC-SPINE-2024."

### II. APPEAL BASIS

The appeal was submitted based on the following:
1.  Member has severe cervical stenosis with cord contact, indicating a risk of myelopathy from minor trauma.
2.  Prophylactic surgery is indicated for severe stenosis even without current neurological deficits.
3.  Member's occupation as a construction worker presents a high trauma risk.

### III. CLINICAL RECORD REVIEW

*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Severe cervical stenosis C5-C6, prophylactic decompression requested.
*   **Occupation:** Construction worker, heavy equipment operation.
*   **Imaging (MRI, 2024-09-15):** Revealed C5-C6 severe central canal stenosis. Findings note "Cord contact without compression." No T2 signal change (no myelomalacia) was observed. The canal diameter was measured at 6mm (normal >13mm). The report concludes that a single trauma could cause acute cord injury given the severely reduced canal diameter.
*   **Neurological Examination (2024-09-20):** Documented as "Completely normal neurological examination. No radiculopathy. No myelopathy signs. No UMN signs." The member reported axial neck pain only, rated 5/10.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 8 weeks (24 sessions) completed for axial pain. Modest improvement in pain (7/10 to 5/10) was noted. No neurological symptoms were present to treat with PT. This exceeds the plan's 6-week minimum.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Naproxen 500mg BID for 3 months (moderate pain relief) and Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks (sleep improvement). This meets the plan's requirement of two agents for 4+ weeks each.
    *   **Interventional:** A C5-C6 epidural performed on 2024-10-10 provided 60% pain relief lasting 6 weeks. This meets the plan's requirement for one interventional procedure.
*   **Surgeon Note (Dr. Karen Davis):** Emphasized the "critically narrow canal diameter" and the high likelihood of acute spinal cord injury from "any significant cervical trauma." The surgeon highlighted the elevated trauma risk due to the member's occupation (operating heavy equipment) and concluded that "Prophylactic decompression is indicated to prevent catastrophic injury."

### IV. APPLICATION OF HEALTHFIRST PLAN CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)

1.  **Section 2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** The plan requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (a), cervical myelopathy (b), or cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit (c).
    *   Mr. Flores's clinical record explicitly states a "Completely normal neurological examination," with "No radiculopathy. No myelopathy signs. No UMN signs." The MRI notes "cord contact without compression" and "no T2 signal change." Therefore, the member does not meet criteria 2.1(a), 2.1(b), or 2.1(c) as there are no current neurological deficits or symptoms of radiculopathy or myelopathy. The request is for *prophylactic* intervention, not for an *existing symptomatic* diagnosis as defined.

2.  **Section 2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:** The member has completed 8 weeks of physical therapy, trials of two pharmacotherapy agents, and one interventional procedure. This adequately meets the plan's requirements for conservative treatment duration and types (6 weeks PT, 2 pharmacotherapy agents for 4+ weeks, 1 interventional procedure).
    *   **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY:** This exception applies "For members with confirmed cervical myelopathy (criterion 2.1(b))." Since Mr. Flores does not have confirmed cervical myelopathy, this exception is not applicable.

3.  **Section 3 EXCLUSIONS:**
    *   **3.1:** "Surgery is requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy." Mr. Flores currently presents with axial neck pain and *no documented* radiculopathy or myelopathy. While the surgeon's rationale emphasizes prevention of *future* myelopathy, the current clinical picture aligns with this exclusion.

**Conclusion for Plan Criteria:** The denial is consistent with HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria, specifically regarding the lack of a qualifying diagnosis under Section 2.1 and the exclusion in Section 3.1.

### V. APPLICATION OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK

1.  **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute specifies conditions under which a plan cannot require PT completion.
    *   (a)(1) PT is unlikely to benefit: While PT for axial pain showed modest benefit, it would not address the structural stenosis or the risk of acute cord injury.
    *   (a)(2) Functional plateau reached: The member completed 8 weeks of PT for axial pain with only modest improvement. No neurological symptoms were present for PT to address.
    *   (a)(3) Objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address: The severe stenosis (6mm canal) and cord contact clearly represent structural neurological compromise that PT cannot resolve.
    *   **Relevance:** While this statute acknowledges that PT may not be beneficial or applicable in certain structural conditions, Mr. Flores *did* complete the required PT. The plan's primary denial is not based on unfulfilled conservative treatment, but on the lack of a qualifying diagnosis with neurological deficits. Therefore, this statute does not mandate overturning the denial on the stated grounds.

2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This guidance addresses prior authorization for time-sensitive neurological conditions.
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** The APL states, "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and *myelomalacia* on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm." Critically, Mr. Flores's MRI shows *no T2 signal change (no myelomalacia)*, and his neurological exam is normal. Therefore, this specific classification for urgent conditions, as defined by the DMHC, does not apply to this case.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** This section also refers to "objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (*cord signal change, myelomalacia*)." As myelomalacia is absent, and there is no evidence of *progressive* pathology, this section does not apply to waive standard conservative treatment (though conservative treatment was completed anyway).
    *   **Relevance:** This key regulatory guidance for myelopathy urgency is not met due to the absence of myelomalacia and current myelopathic symptoms.

3.  **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (Standard of Review for IMR):** This statute mandates that the Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard uses "generally accepted standards of medical practice" and that a service meeting this standard "SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." This requires a thorough review against established clinical guidelines.

**Conclusion for Regulatory Framework:** While California law prioritizes generally accepted standards over plan criteria, and provides for exceptions to conservative treatment, the specific conditions outlined in DMHC APL 22-014 for urgency (e.g., myelomalacia, existing myelopathy) are not present in this case. The ultimate determination will rely on whether prophylactic surgery for asymptomatic severe stenosis with cord contact (without myelomalacia) is supported by "generally accepted standards of medical practice."

### VI. APPLICATION OF CLINICAL STANDARD (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)

1.  **SECTION 1 — DIAGNOSTIC CLASSIFICATION:**
    *   The guidelines clearly distinguish Cervical Myelopathy and Radiculopathy, stating they require "fundamentally different treatment algorithms." Mr. Flores presents with neither.
    *   **1.2 MYELOMALACIA — SIGNIFICANCE:** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) is identified as indicating "existing spinal cord injury," "higher risk of permanent deficit," and "surgical urgency." Mr. Flores's MRI explicitly states "No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia." The absence of this critical finding significantly impacts the applicability of urgency arguments based on clinical guidelines.

2.  **SECTION 2 — EVIDENCE ON CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT FOR MYELOPATHY:** These sections primarily argue against mandatory PT for *moderate-severe myelopathy* and cases *with myelomalacia*. Since Mr. Flores does not have existing myelopathy or myelomalacia, these arguments do not directly apply to his case for waiving conservative treatment (which he completed anyway for his axial pain).

3.  **SECTION 3 — SURGICAL INDICATIONS: CLINICAL STANDARD (ACDF, CPT 22551):**
    *   **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION:** These criteria require *existing* moderate-to-severe myelopathy with objective cord compression, *progressive* neurological deficit, *myelomalacia*, or *acute severe myelopathy*. Mr. Flores meets none of these criteria. He has no current myelopathy symptoms, no neurological deficits, and no myelomalacia.
    *   **TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION:** These criteria include mild myelopathy with stable symptoms, radiculopathy with persistent symptoms, or significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care. Mr. Flores does not have myelopathy or radiculopathy. His axial pain, while partially relieved by conservative care, is not presented as a "significant functional impairment" warranting surgery in the context of the requested "prophylactic decompression."
    *   **TIER 3 — SURGERY NOT INDICATED:** This tier explicitly states that surgery is "NOT INDICATED" for "(a) Axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy." This description precisely matches Mr. Flores's current clinical presentation.

**Conclusion for Clinical Standard:** The provided clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) do not explicitly support prophylactic decompression for asymptomatic severe cervical stenosis with cord contact, *especially in the absence of myelomalacia or current neurological deficits*. The guidelines consistently link surgical indication to the presence of *symptoms* (myelopathy, radiculopathy) or *objective signs of existing cord injury* (myelomalacia, progressive neurological deficit). Furthermore, TIER 3 explicitly excludes surgery for axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy, which describes the member's current status. While the surgeon's rationale regarding the high risk of future injury due to occupation is a valid concern, the *provided clinical guidelines* do not formalize this specific scenario as a surgical indication.

### VII. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND DISPOSITION

The hierarchy of authority is State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.

1.  **State Law:** The relevant California state laws (CIC 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) do not override the plan's denial in this specific case. DMHC APL 22-014, which addresses urgency for myelopathy, specifically references myelomalacia and existing myelopathy symptoms, neither of which are present. CIC 10169.5 regarding PT is not the primary basis of denial here. CHSC 1374.32(b) requires assessment against "generally accepted standards of medical practice."

2.  **Clinical Standard:** The AANS/CNS, NASS, and AO Spine guidelines, which represent generally accepted standards, *do not provide an explicit indication for prophylactic surgery* in an asymptomatic patient with severe cervical stenosis and cord contact, particularly in the *absence of myelomalacia*. Instead, these guidelines explicitly classify surgery as "NOT INDICATED" for axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy, which is Mr. Flores's current neurological status.

3.  **Plan Criteria:** The plan's denial is consistent with its own criteria, specifically the lack of a qualifying neurological diagnosis and the exclusion for axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy.

**Conflict Analysis:** There is no conflict between the plan's criteria and the provided clinical standards for this specific case. Both sources align in not providing a specific indication for prophylactic surgery for asymptomatic severe cervical stenosis with cord contact in the absence of myelomalacia or other neurological deficits.

While the surgeon's clinical judgment regarding the potential for catastrophic injury due to the combination of severe stenosis and a high-risk occupation is noted, the provided "generally accepted standards of medical practice" for spinal surgery do not formalize this specific scenario as a surgical indication. The guidelines focus on treatment of existing neurological conditions or clear signs of ongoing cord damage (e.g., myelomalacia).

Therefore, based on the provided evidence, the service does not meet the "generally accepted standards of medical practice" for surgical intervention in this specific context, nor does it meet the plan's medical necessity criteria.

**DISPOSITION: UPHELD**

### VIII. WARRANT

The prior authorization denial for an ACDF at C5-C6 for Mr. Raymond Flores is **UPHELD**.

The denial is consistent with HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), specifically Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement) and Section 3.1 (Exclusions). The member currently presents with axial neck pain only, and a neurological examination documented no radiculopathy, no myelopathy signs, and no upper motor neuron signs. Imaging showed severe cervical stenosis with cord contact but *without compression* and critically, *without T2 signal change (myelomalacia)*.

California regulatory frameworks, including DMHC APL 22-014, specifically identify *myelomalacia* as a key indicator for urgent neurological conditions that may warrant exceptions to conservative treatment. The absence of myelomalacia in Mr. Flores's MRI prevents the application of this particular urgency guidance. While CIC 10169.5 acknowledges structural compromise, it does not mandate surgery for asymptomatic conditions.

Furthermore, a review of the generally accepted standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS Guidelines 2023, NASS Guidelines 2020, AO Spine Guidelines 2021) indicates that prophylactic surgery for *asymptomatic* severe cervical stenosis with cord contact, in the absence of myelomalacia or other neurological deficits, is not explicitly supported as a Tier 1 or Tier 2 indication. To the contrary, these guidelines explicitly state that surgery is "NOT INDICATED" (Tier 3) for "Axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy," which accurately describes Mr. Flores's current clinical presentation.

While the treating surgeon's rationale regarding the high risk of acute spinal cord injury given the member's occupation and severe canal narrowing is a serious consideration for personalized patient management, the *provided clinical guidelines*, which define "generally accepted standards of medical practice" for this review, do not establish prophylactic decompression under these specific asymptomatic circumstances as a standard indication.

Therefore, since the requested procedure does not meet the medical necessity criteria of the plan or the "generally accepted standards of medical practice" as defined by the provided clinical guidelines for a surgical indication, the denial is upheld.
