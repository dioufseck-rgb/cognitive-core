**Case ID:** PA-2024-C004
**Member:** Raymond Flores (M-223355), Age 54
**Procedure:** ACDF C5-C6, one level (CPT 22551)
**Date of Review:** 2024-11-15
**Reviewer:** [Your Name/Title - Health Plan Appeals Reviewer]

**1. CASE SUMMARY**
Mr. Raymond Flores, a 54-year-old construction worker, requested prior authorization for a prophylactic anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) dated 2024-09-15 demonstrates severe central canal stenosis at C5-C6 with cord contact, but explicitly notes "without compression," "no T2 signal change," and "no myelomalacia." The canal diameter is documented as 6mm (normal >13mm). A neurological examination on 2024-09-20 was completely normal, with no signs of radiculopathy or myelopathy, and no upper motor neuron (UMN) signs. Mr. Flores's only complaint is axial neck pain (5/10), for which he completed 8 weeks of physical therapy, 3 months of Naproxen, 6 weeks of Cyclobenzaprine, and a C5-C6 epidural steroid injection with modest pain relief (60% lasting 6 weeks).

**2. DENIAL REASON (Initial Level)**
The initial denial, issued by Dr. Patricia Huang on 2024-10-25, cited reason code MED-003, stating: "Cervical stenosis documented but no neurological deficit on examination. Member has axial neck pain only. No radiculopathy, no myelopathy documented. Surgical intervention not indicated for axial pain without neurological deficit under CC-SPINE-2024."

**3. APPEAL BASIS**
The appeal argues that Mr. Flores has severe cervical stenosis with cord contact, posing a risk of myelopathy from minor trauma, especially given his occupation as a construction worker with high trauma risk. It asserts that prophylactic surgery is indicated for severe stenosis even without current deficits.

**4. REVIEW FRAMEWORK**
This appeal determination is made in accordance with the following hierarchy of authority:
1.  **State Law** (California Insurance Code, Health & Safety Code, DMHC All-Plan Letters)
2.  **Clinical Standard** (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine Guidelines)
3.  **Plan Criteria** (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)

Where conflicts exist between sources, the higher authority governs.

**5. ANALYSIS**

**5.1 Clinical Presentation Summary:**
Mr. Flores presents with severe asymptomatic cervical spinal canal stenosis (6mm) at C5-C6 with cord contact, but importantly, *without cord compression, T2 signal change, or myelomalacia*. He has *no objective neurological deficits* (no radiculopathy, no myelopathy, no UMN signs). His primary symptom is axial neck pain, which has shown modest improvement with conservative treatment. The surgical request is for *prophylactic* decompression to prevent future spinal cord injury, particularly given his occupation.

**5.2 Review Against Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
*   **Section 2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** This section requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (2.1a), cervical myelopathy (2.1b), or cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit (2.1c). Mr. Flores's clinical record explicitly states "Completely normal neurological examination. No radiculopathy. No myelopathy signs. No UMN signs." The MRI shows "cord contact without compression. No T2 signal change." Therefore, Mr. Flores does not meet the diagnostic requirements for existing radiculopathy or myelopathy as defined by the plan, nor does he have an objective neurological deficit.
*   **Section 2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:** Mr. Flores has completed 8 weeks of PT, tried two pharmacotherapy agents for appropriate durations, and received an epidural steroid injection with documented pain relief. For *axial neck pain*, these requirements have been met. However, the plan's denial is not based on inadequate conservative treatment for axial pain, but on the lack of a qualifying surgical diagnosis.
*   **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY:** This exception does not apply as Mr. Flores does not have confirmed cervical myelopathy (no symptoms, no UMN signs, no myelomalacia).
*   **Section 3.1 EXCLUSIONS:** This section states that authorization will be denied if "Surgery is requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy." Mr. Flores's current presentation aligns with this exclusion, as he has axial neck pain only, and no documented radiculopathy or myelopathy.

**Conclusion for Plan Criteria:** The denial aligns with HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 criteria, specifically Section 3.1, due to the absence of documented radiculopathy or myelopathy, and the presence of axial neck pain as the sole symptom.

**5.3 Review Against California Regulatory Requirements:**
*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute specifies conditions under which PT completion cannot be required. While Mr. Flores has objective structural compromise (severe stenosis), he does not have *current structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address* in the context of *existing neurological symptoms*. His PT was for axial pain, which improved modestly. This statute does not compel approval of surgery for an asymptomatic condition.
*   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This APL addresses *existing* myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia, and exceptions for conservative treatment in such cases. Mr. Flores explicitly does *not* have myelomalacia or symptomatic myelopathy. Thus, this APL does not apply to this case of prophylactic surgery for asymptomatic severe stenosis.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This critical statute mandates approval if a service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice," irrespective of plan criteria. Therefore, the next step is to evaluate the clinical standards for prophylactic surgery in this scenario.

**Conclusion for State Law:** While state law prioritizes generally accepted standards, it does not *prescribe* a specific indication for prophylactic surgery in this asymptomatic context. Its primary impact is to elevate clinical standards above plan criteria.

**5.4 Review Against Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
*   **Section 1.1 Cervical Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** The guidelines clearly distinguish between these conditions, emphasizing that conservative treatment evidence for radiculopathy does not apply to myelopathy. Crucially, Mr. Flores currently has neither symptomatic radiculopathy nor myelopathy.
*   **Section 1.2 Myelomalacia Significance:** Myelomalacia indicates existing cord injury and surgical urgency. Mr. Flores's MRI explicitly states "No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia," indicating no current cord injury.
*   **Section 2.1/2.3 Conservative Treatment/Risk of Delay for Myelopathy:** These sections discuss the limited efficacy of conservative treatment and the risks of delay for *established myelopathy*. These are not directly applicable to Mr. Flores, who is asymptomatic neurologically.
*   **Section 3.1 Accepted Indications for ACDF (CPT 22551) per AANS/CNS (2023):**
    *   **TIER 1 (Strong Indication):** Mr. Flores does not meet any Tier 1 criteria (no moderate-severe myelopathy, no progressive neurological deficit, no myelomalacia, no acute myelopathy).
    *   **TIER 2 (Reasonable Indication):** Mr. Flores does not meet any Tier 2 criteria (no mild myelopathy, no radiculopathy with persistent symptoms, his axial pain is not a "significant functional impairment" requiring surgery under these guidelines).
    *   **TIER 3 (Surgery Not Indicated):** The guidelines explicitly state surgery is *not indicated* for:
        *   (a) "Axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy." This describes Mr. Flores's current symptoms.
        *   (b) "Imaging findings without correlating symptoms." This describes his severe stenosis with cord contact but no neurological symptoms.

The clinical guidelines provided *do not support prophylactic surgical intervention for asymptomatic severe cervical stenosis with cord contact in the absence of neurological deficits or myelomalacia*, even in the context of occupational risk. While the surgeon's rationale regarding the risk of future injury is noted, the consensus clinical standards for surgical indication focus on *existing symptomatic pathology* or objective signs of *current cord injury* (like myelomalacia). The specific scenario of prophylactic surgery for asymptomatic severe stenosis with cord contact due to occupational trauma risk is not listed as an indication under Tier 1 or Tier 2.

**5.5 Conflict Resolution:**
The plan's denial is consistent with its own criteria (Section 3.1 exclusion).
California state law (CHSC 1374.32(b)) mandates approval if the service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice."
However, the provided clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) do not categorize prophylactic surgery for *asymptomatic* severe cervical stenosis with cord contact as a "strong" or "reasonable" indication for surgery. Instead, they classify situations like Mr. Flores's (axial neck pain without neurological deficit, imaging findings without correlating symptoms) as cases where surgery is "not indicated" (Tier 3).

Therefore, there is no conflict between the plan's criteria and the clinical standards in this specific case. Both sources, when applied directly, lead to the conclusion that surgical intervention is not indicated for Mr. Flores's current presentation. The state law does not override this, as the service does not meet the "generally accepted standards of medical practice" as outlined in the provided guidelines for *asymptomatic* patients.

**6. DISPOSITION**
**UPHELD**

**7. WARRANT/RATIONALE**
The denial for ACDF C5-C6 for Mr. Raymond Flores is Upheld because the requested procedure does not meet either the HealthFirst PPO Gold plan criteria or generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice for surgical intervention.

1.  **Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):** The denial correctly identified that Mr. Flores does not present with documented cervical radiculopathy or myelopathy, nor an objective neurological deficit. His current symptoms are limited to axial neck pain. Section 3.1 of the plan's criteria explicitly excludes authorization for "Surgery requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy." Mr. Flores's presentation falls squarely within this exclusion.
2.  **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine Guidelines):** The AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines classify surgical indications into Tiers. Mr. Flores does not meet any Tier 1 ("Strong Indication") or Tier 2 ("Reasonable Indication") criteria, as he has no moderate-to-severe myelopathy, progressive neurological deficits, myelomalacia, or symptomatic radiculopathy. His presentation—"Axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy" and "Imaging findings without correlating symptoms"—is explicitly listed under TIER 3: "SURGERY NOT INDICATED." While severe stenosis with cord contact is present, there is no cord compression, T2 signal change, or myelomalacia, which would indicate existing cord injury or symptomatic myelopathy. The provided clinical guidelines do not establish prophylactic surgery for *asymptomatic* severe cervical stenosis, even with occupational risk factors, as a generally accepted standard of medical practice.
3.  **California Regulatory Requirements:** While state law (CHSC 1374.32(b)) requires approval if a service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice" regardless of plan criteria, in this instance, the provided clinical standards do not support the requested prophylactic surgery for an asymptomatic patient. The other state laws (CIC 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) are not applicable to this case, as they primarily address situations involving *existing* symptomatic neurological compromise or myelopathy with objective cord injury (myelomalacia), neither of which is present.

Therefore, the initial denial was consistent with both HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria and the overriding generally accepted standards of medical practice as documented in current clinical guidelines.
