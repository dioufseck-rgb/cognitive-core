**I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

This appeal concerns the denial of prior authorization for an anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 for member Patricia Osei. The initial denial cited non-completion of conservative treatment, specifically 5/6 required weeks of physical therapy and no cervical epidural steroid injection (CESI), per HealthFirst's Section 2.2 criteria.

Upon review of the clinical record, appeal basis, HealthFirst's own criteria, relevant California state law, and generally accepted clinical standards, it is determined that the denial was in error. The member's condition of progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia warrants a waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements under HealthFirst's own policy exceptions, California state law, and prevailing clinical guidelines. The denial is therefore **OVERTURNED**, and authorization for CPT 22551 is granted.

---

**II. CASE BACKGROUND & CLINICAL FINDINGS**

**A. Member Presentation:**
Patricia Osei is a 55-year-old member diagnosed with progressive cervical myelopathy at C4-C5 with myelomalacia. Her neurological exam reveals significant upper motor neuron signs consistent with myelopathy, including bilateral lower extremity spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor skills bilaterally, and a positive Lhermitte sign. Imaging (MRI 2024-08-20) confirms severe central cord compression at C4-C5, with T2 signal change (myelomalacia) and documented progressive cord injury.

**B. Conservative Treatment History:**
*   **Physical Therapy (PT):** 5 weeks (15 sessions) completed. PT was stopped due to pacemaker precautions limiting certain modalities and the physician's determination that further PT was not addressing neurological progression.
*   **Pharmacotherapy:** Baclofen 10mg TID for 3 months (partial spasticity relief) and Methylprednisolone taper for 10 days x2 (temporary improvement only). This meets the plan's requirement of two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
*   **Interventional Treatment:** No cervical epidural steroid injection (CESI) was performed. The treating physician documented that CESI is contraindicated due to the member's permanent pacemaker, confirmed by anesthesiology consultation (Dr. Rivera, 2024-09-10).

**C. Denial Reason:**
"Member has not completed required conservative treatment. Physical therapy: 5 weeks completed, 6 required. No cervical epidural steroid injection on record. Member does not meet Section 2.2 requirements."

**D. Appeal Basis:**
1.  Pacemaker medically contraindicates cervical epidural steroid injection.
2.  Physician documented interventional contraindication in chart.
3.  DMHC APL 22-014 myelopathy provisions apply due to documented myelomalacia.

**III. APPLICATION OF HIERARCHICAL REVIEW**

The review process applies a three-source hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.

**A. HealthFirst Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**

1.  **Diagnosis (Section 2.1):** The member's diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (2.1(b)) with objective cord compression, myelomalacia, and neurological deficits is confirmed. **MET.**
2.  **Conservative Treatment (Section 2.2):**
    *   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2(b)):** Member completed trials of Baclofen (3 months) and Methylprednisolone (10 days x2). **MET.**
    *   **Physical Therapy (2.2(a)):** The plan requires 6 weeks of PT; the member completed 5 weeks.
    *   **Interventional Treatment (2.2(c)):** The plan requires one interventional procedure; none was performed.
3.  **Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2(A)):** This critical exception applies to members with confirmed primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (which Patricia Osei has).
    *   **PT requirement is reduced to 4 weeks minimum:** The member completed 5 weeks of PT. This **MEETS** the reduced requirement.
    *   **Interventional treatment requirement is WAIVED if treating physician documents risk:** The physician documented a permanent pacemaker as a contraindication for a fluoroscopy-guided CESI, confirmed by an anesthesiology consultation. This clearly constitutes a medical risk. Therefore, the interventional treatment requirement is **WAIVED.**

**Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The initial denial was based on a misapplication of HealthFirst's own criteria. The reviewer failed to apply **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) \u2014 MYELOPATHY PATHWAY**, which would deem the conservative treatment requirements **MET** for Patricia Osei's clinical presentation.

**B. California State Law and Regulatory Guidance:**

1.  **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This law explicitly prohibits health plans from conditioning approval of surgery on PT completion where:
    *   (a)(1) "The member's treating physician has documented... physical therapy... is unlikely to benefit the member." The physician documented that PT "was not addressing neurological progression." This satisfies this condition.
    *   (a)(3) "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address." Patricia Osei's progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and severe cord compression undeniably falls under this provision.
    *   (c) "A plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically." The denial's reliance on 5/6 weeks for myelopathy is a mechanical application directly prohibited by this statute.
    **Conclusion:** CIC § 10169.5 overrides the plan's PT requirement for this member.

2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This guidance is highly relevant:
    *   **Section II.1:** States that cervical myelopathy with cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI "constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated" and "Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent." This directly applies to Patricia Osei's condition.
    *   **Section II.2 (Conservative Treatment Exceptions):** Plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when (a) objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia, progressive cord injury), (b) the physician documents conservative treatment failure ("not addressing neurological progression"), and (c) surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. The presence of myelomalacia "is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements."
    *   **Section II.3:** Explicitly states that plans "shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice."
    **Conclusion:** DMHC APL 22-014 mandates the waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements for this member due to myelomalacia and progressive myelopathy, treating the condition as urgent.

3.  **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This section confirms that services meeting "generally accepted standards of medical practice" SHALL be approved, even if the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. This elevates clinical standards above plan criteria where a conflict exists.

**C. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

1.  **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy (Section 1.1):** Guidelines emphasize that myelopathy and radiculopathy are distinct, with different treatment algorithms. The evidence for conservative treatment, particularly PT duration, is primarily for radiculopathy. Applying radiculopathy-derived PT requirements to myelopathy is not supported.
2.  **Myelomalacia Significance (Section 1.2):** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) indicates existing cord injury, higher risk of permanent deficit, reduced recovery, and signifies surgical urgency. It is considered a strong indication for surgery and a contraindication to extended conservative trials. Patricia Osei has documented myelomalacia.
3.  **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy (Section 2):**
    *   For moderate-severe myelopathy, PT does "NOT produce meaningful functional improvement."
    *   "No high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy."
    *   "Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." (AO Spine Guidelines, 2021).
    *   Each month of surgical delay for moderate-severe myelopathy is associated with decreased likelihood of neurological recovery, and myelomalacia is the strongest predictor of incomplete recovery.
4.  **Surgical Indications (Section 3.1):** Patricia Osei's presentation (moderate-severe myelopathy, objective cord compression, myelomalacia, progressive neurological deficits) meets TIER 1 \u2014 STRONG INDICATION for surgery per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023).

**Conclusion:** Clinical standards strongly support immediate surgical intervention for Patricia Osei's progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and firmly reject mandatory, prolonged conservative treatment for this condition. The plan's 6-week PT requirement for this myelopathy case has no evidentiary basis in clinical guidelines and carries a risk of harm.

---

**IV. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND RATIONALE**

The denial for ACDF C4-C5 for Patricia Osei directly conflicts with HealthFirst's own myelopathy exception, California state law, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice. Following the established hierarchy:

1.  **Plan's Internal Criteria:** The denial failed to properly apply HealthFirst's own **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) \u2014 MYELOPATHY PATHWAY**. Under this exception, the member's 5 weeks of PT (meeting the reduced 4-week minimum) and documented contraindication to interventional treatment (pacemaker) would result in the conservative treatment requirements being satisfied. This constitutes an internal error in the denial's reasoning.

2.  **California State Law (Higher Authority):**
    *   **CIC § 10169.5** prohibits requiring PT for conditions with structural neurological compromise (like myelomalacia), directly overriding the plan's 6-week PT rule. The physician's determination that PT was not addressing neurological progression also satisfies the waiver criteria.
    *   **DMHC APL 22-014** explicitly instructs plans to waive conservative treatment requirements for myelopathy with myelomalacia, categorizing such conditions as urgent and noting the risk of irreversible harm with delay. This strongly mandates approval for Patricia Osei.

3.  **Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (Higher Authority than Plan Criteria):** Current AANS/CNS, NASS, and AO Spine guidelines clearly establish that moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia is a strong indication for surgery and that delaying surgery for extended conservative treatment (especially PT) is not supported by evidence and may worsen outcomes. The plan's requirements are more restrictive and contrary to prudent clinical judgment for this time-sensitive condition.

Therefore, under all levels of the hierarchical review, the denial is insupportable.

---

**V. DETERMINATION**

Based on the foregoing review, the prior authorization denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C4-C5) for Patricia Osei is **OVERTURNED**.

**Warrant:** The member's condition of progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia (documented by MRI with T2 signal change, severe central cord compression, and UMN signs on neurological exam) is a Tier 1 strong surgical indication according to AANS/CNS (2023), NASS (2020), and AO Spine (2021) guidelines. These clinical standards explicitly state there is no evidence to support mandatory prolonged physical therapy for moderate-severe myelopathy and warn against surgical delay which can lead to irreversible neurological deterioration (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1, 2.2, 2.3).

Furthermore, California state law dictates that health plans cannot require PT completion where there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address (CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)), a condition clearly met by the member's myelomalacia and progressive cord injury. DMHC APL 22-014 specifically identifies myelopathy with myelomalacia as a condition warranting waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements due to urgency and risk of irreversible harm (DMHC APL 22-014, Sections II.1, II.2).

Finally, even under HealthFirst's own criteria, the denial was an error. The plan's **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) \u2014 MYELOPATHY PATHWAY** reduces the PT requirement to 4 weeks (member completed 5 weeks) and waives the interventional requirement due to the physician-documented contraindication (pacemaker for CESI), meaning the member *does* meet the plan's criteria when correctly applied.

Therefore, authorization for CPT 22551 is medically necessary and should be approved.
