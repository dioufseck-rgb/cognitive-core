**APPEAL DISPOSITION: OVERTURN DENIAL**

---

**WARRANT AND RATIONALE:**

This appeal concerns the prior authorization denial for an anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 for Patricia Osei, diagnosed with progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia. The initial denial was based on the member not meeting the conservative treatment requirements of HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), specifically regarding the duration of physical therapy (5 of 6 weeks completed) and the lack of a cervical epidural steroid injection (CESI).

Upon review of the submitted clinical record, HealthFirst's own criteria, California state law, and generally accepted clinical standards, the denial is determined to be incorrect and is therefore **OVERTURNED**.

The decision to overturn is based on the following findings, applying the hierarchy of authority: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria**.

1.  **Misapplication of HealthFirst's Own Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
    *   The member's primary diagnosis is documented as "Progressive cervical myelopathy C4-C5 with myelomalacia," supported by significant upper motor neuron signs (bilateral lower extremity spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor bilateral hands, Lhermitte sign positive) and MRI findings (severe central cord compression, T2 signal change/myelomalacia, progressive cord injury). This clearly meets criterion 2.1(b) for Cervical Myelopathy.
    *   HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 includes an **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY**, which specifically modifies the conservative treatment requirements for confirmed cervical myelopathy. Under this exception:
        *   The physical therapy requirement is reduced to a **4-week minimum**. The member completed 5 weeks of PT (15 sessions). Therefore, this requirement is **MET**.
        *   The interventional treatment requirement (e.g., CESI) is **WAIVED** if the treating physician documents that such treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration. The clinical record clearly documents that a cervical epidural steroid injection is **CONTRAINDICATED** due to the member's permanent pacemaker, as confirmed by an anesthesiology consultation (Dr. Rivera, 2024-09-10). Therefore, this requirement is **MET (waived)**.
        *   The pharmacotherapy requirement remains, and the member completed trials of Baclofen and Methylprednisolone, which is **MET**.
    *   **Conclusion:** The initial denial failed to apply HealthFirst's own myelopathy exception (Section 2.2A), which, if correctly applied, demonstrates that the member *does* meet the plan's conservative treatment requirements.
    *   *Source: HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria, CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2 and Exception 2.2(A).*

2.  **Violation of California State Law (DMHC APL 22-014):**
    *   The Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter 22-014, "Prior Authorization Requirements for Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions," dictates that plans **SHALL NOT** require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when:
        *   Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia, or progressive herniation on serial imaging). The member's MRI on 2024-08-20 confirmed T2 signal change (myelomalacia) and "progressive cord injury." This condition "constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated" and "Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent."
        *   The treating physician documents that conservative treatment has been attempted and failed to arrest progression. The physician's note explicitly states that PT was "not addressing neurological progression."
        *   Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines for the condition. (See point 4 below).
    *   DMHC APL 22-014 specifically states that the "presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements even where a full course has not been completed."
    *   **Conclusion:** State law mandates the waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements in this specific clinical scenario, overriding any more restrictive plan criteria.
    *   *Source: California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter APL 22-014, Sections II.1, II.2(a), II.2(b), II.2(c).*

3.  **Violation of California State Law (CIC § 10169.5):**
    *   California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a) explicitly prohibits health insurers from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where:
        *   (1) The treating physician has documented that physical therapy is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition. The physician's note states PT was "not addressing neurological progression."
        *   (2) The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau. The completion of 5 weeks of PT with documented neurological progression suggests a functional plateau for the underlying neurological condition.
        *   (3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. The diagnosis of progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and severe central cord compression, as seen on MRI, represents significant structural neurological compromise that PT cannot resolve.
    *   **Conclusion:** State law directly prohibits the requirement for additional physical therapy in this case.
    *   *Source: California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5, Sections (a)(1), (a)(2), (a)(3).*

4.  **Inconsistent with Generally Accepted Clinical Standards of Medical Practice:**
    *   Leading clinical guidelines, including those from the AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine (2023 Update) and AO Spine International (2021), classify cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia as a **TIER 1 strong indication for surgical intervention**.
    *   These guidelines critically distinguish myelopathy from radiculopathy, noting that physical therapy has **limited to no efficacy for moderate-severe myelopathy** and that **delaying surgery to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by evidence and may result in neurological deterioration.** Myelomalacia specifically indicates existing cord injury and greater urgency for surgical decompression.
    *   The IMR standard (CHSC § 1374.32(b)) specifies that a service meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice **SHALL** be approved, regardless of whether a plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.
    *   **Conclusion:** Requiring further conservative treatment for this progressive myelopathic condition with myelomalacia directly contradicts established, evidence-based clinical standards and carries a significant risk of irreversible neurological harm due to treatment delay.
    *   *Source: Clinical Reference Summary: AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), AO Spine Guidelines (2021), NASS Guidelines (2020), Cochrane Review (2021), Sections 1.1, 1.2, 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 3.1 (Tier 1), 3.2.*
    *   *Source: California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b).*

**Summary:**
The initial denial of ACDF at C4-C5 for Patricia Osei was erroneous. The member meets the criteria for surgery under HealthFirst's own myelopathy exception. Furthermore, state laws (DMHC APL 22-014 and CIC § 10169.5) explicitly mandate the waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements in this clinical scenario involving myelopathy with myelomalacia and documented contraindications/lack of benefit for conservative measures. Finally, generally accepted clinical standards unequivocally support surgical intervention for this urgent condition and caution against delaying surgery for extended conservative treatment.

Based on the compelling evidence from the clinical record, HealthFirst's own policy, California state law, and current clinical guidelines, the requested ACDF C4-C5 is medically necessary.
