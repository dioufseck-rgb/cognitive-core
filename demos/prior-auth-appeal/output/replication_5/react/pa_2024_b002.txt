**1. Summary of Prior Authorization Request and Denial:**
A prior authorization request was submitted by Dr. Karen Liu for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 for member Thomas Reed, diagnosed with persistent cervical radiculopathy C6-C7. The initial denial, issued on 2024-10-20, cited reason code MED-003, stating that the "Member does not meet criteria for ACDF. Imaging shows C6-C7 disc herniation with mild cord contact but no measurable compression. No myelopathy findings on exam. PT not yet completed to functional plateau."

**2. Appeal Basis:**
The appeal submitted by Dr. Liu asserted that the member had completed 7 weeks of physical therapy (PT) with documented failure to improve, a functional plateau was reached per the PT discharge summary, and the surgeon believes ACDF is indicated for persistent radiculopathy.

**3. Clinical Review and Determination:**

Upon comprehensive review of the member's clinical record, HealthFirst PPO Gold Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory framework (CIC, CHSC, DMHC APL), and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021), the following findings are noted:

**A. Clinical Presentation:**
The member's clinical presentation, including right C7 dermatomal numbness, diminished right tricep reflex, 7/10 pain at rest, and MRI findings of C6-C7 disc herniation with mild right C7 foraminal stenosis, is consistent with **cervical radiculopathy**. The neurological exam explicitly states "No bilateral findings. No upper motor neuron signs," and the MRI notes "No cord signal change. No myelomalacia," effectively ruling out cervical myelopathy as the primary diagnosis in this case. This distinction is critical as radiculopathy and myelopathy have different treatment algorithms and conservative treatment requirements.

**B. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

*   **2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** The member meets criteria 2.1(a) Cervical radiculopathy and 2.1(c) Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit, as documented by symptoms and MRI findings.
*   **2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:**
    *   **(a) Physical Therapy:** The plan requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. The member completed 7 weeks of PT (21 sessions). The denial stated "PT not yet completed to functional plateau." However, the PT discharge summary explicitly states, "functional plateau reached — patient has not improved over final 3 sessions." HealthFirst's own "Functional plateau" definition specifies "<10% improvement over 3 sessions OR therapist documents maximum benefit reached." The member's record directly satisfies the plan's definition of functional plateau. Therefore, the plan's assertion that PT was not completed to a functional plateau is **inaccurate based on its own criteria and the submitted documentation.**
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** The member completed a 4-month trial of Pregabalin and a 3-month trial of Meloxicam, both exceeding the 4-week minimum and fulfilling the requirement for two agents.
    *   **(c) Interventional Treatment:** The member received a right C7 epidural steroid injection, meeting this requirement.
    *   **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY:** This exception does not apply as myelopathy is not the primary diagnosis.
*   **2.3 Imaging Requirement:** Current MRI (2024-08-01) demonstrates structural pathology (C6-C7 disc herniation, mild right C7 foraminal stenosis) consistent with symptoms.
*   **Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The member *has* met all applicable plan criteria for conservative treatment, including the specific definition of functional plateau, making the plan's denial reason for PT invalid under its own policy.

**C. Application of California Regulatory Framework:**

*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This state law explicitly prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where "(2) The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." The clinical record unequivocally documents that Mr. Reed completed 7 weeks of PT and reached a functional plateau, with persistent radicular symptoms. The plan's denial based on the PT not reaching a functional plateau is in **direct violation of CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)**.
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** While this APL emphasizes reduced conservative treatment requirements for myelopathy with myelomalacia, it is not directly applicable here as the member has radiculopathy without myelomalacia or other signs of myelopathic urgency.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This provision mandates that services meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice SHALL be approved regardless of more restrictive plan internal criteria. This reinforces the importance of clinical standard review.

**D. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

*   For cervical radiculopathy (not myelopathy), generally accepted clinical guidelines (e.g., AANS/CNS 2023) categorize "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" as a **TIER 2 "Reasonable Indication" for surgical intervention**. Mr. Reed has completed 7 weeks of PT, 4 months of neuropathic agents, 3 months of NSAIDs, and an epidural injection, with documented persistent symptoms (Pain 7/10) and a functional plateau. This constitutes an adequate trial of conservative therapy under clinical standards.
*   The clinical standard definition of "functional plateau" also aligns with the documentation provided: "Less than 10% improvement in validated functional outcome measure... over 3 consecutive therapy sessions, OR therapist documentation that patient has reached maximum therapeutic benefit." The PT discharge summary explicitly meets this definition.
*   Delaying surgery for radiculopathy that has failed an adequate conservative trial to a functional plateau, particularly with persistent symptoms causing functional impairment, is not supported by current evidence.

**4. Conflict Resolution (Hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):**

The primary reason for the initial denial, "PT not yet completed to functional plateau," is directly contradicted by the clinical record and the plan's *own definition* of functional plateau. Furthermore, this denial basis is explicitly prohibited by **California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2)**. Additionally, under generally accepted clinical standards, the member has completed an appropriate and failed course of conservative treatment for cervical radiculopathy, reaching a functional plateau, thus meeting reasonable indications for surgical intervention.

**5. Disposition:**
**OVERTURN**

**6. Warrant:**
The denial for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) for member Thomas Reed is hereby **OVERTURNED**. This determination is based on the following:

1.  **Misapplication of HealthFirst Plan Criteria:** The initial denial incorrectly stated that physical therapy (PT) was not completed to a functional plateau. The clinical record clearly documents 7 weeks of PT with a discharge summary explicitly stating "functional plateau reached — patient has not improved over final 3 sessions," which directly meets the definition of functional plateau within the HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024). Therefore, the plan misapplied its own criteria when denying the request.
2.  **Violation of California State Law:** California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2) expressly prohibits a health insurer from denying a surgical procedure solely on the basis of incomplete physical therapy when the member has completed a course of PT and the treating physician/therapist documents that a functional plateau has been reached without adequate symptom resolution. The documentation in Mr. Reed's record satisfies this statutory requirement, making the denial's reliance on the PT reason impermissible under California law.
3.  **Consistency with Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:** For cervical radiculopathy, major spine society guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) consider surgical intervention a "reasonable indication" (Tier 2) when a patient experiences persistent symptoms after an adequate course of conservative treatment (typically 6-12 weeks) that has reached a functional plateau. Mr. Reed has completed 7 weeks of PT, 4 months of neuropathic agents, 3 months of NSAIDs, and an epidural, with persistent severe pain (7/10) and documented lack of improvement, meeting these clinical standards for surgical consideration.

Based on the hierarchy of authority (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria), the explicit provisions of California state law and the supporting clinical standards mandate the approval of the requested ACDF C6-C7.
