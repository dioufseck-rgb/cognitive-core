**1. INTRODUCTION**
This letter serves as the official determination for the appeal of the prior authorization denial for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C3-C4 for member Angela Morrison. The initial denial, dated 2024-10-01, cited "MED-003: Member meets conservative treatment requirements. However, imaging does not show sufficient pathology at C3-C4 to support surgical intervention. MRI shows mild degenerative changes. Procedure not medically necessary." This appeal was submitted with the basis that the member completed conservative treatment, the surgeon identified C3-C4 as symptomatic, and a CT myelogram confirmed compression not visible on MRI due to hardware artifact.

---

**2. CASE SUMMARY**
Ms. Angela Morrison, a 67-year-old member, is requesting an ACDF at C3-C4 due to adjacent segment disease following a prior C4-C5 fusion in 2019. She presents with left C4 radicular symptoms, including shoulder and lateral arm numbness, deltoid weakness (3+/5 left), and diminished bicep reflex, which are new since 2023. She has completed an extensive course of conservative treatment, including 10 weeks of physical therapy (with no improvement in symptoms), trials of Pregabalin and Duloxetine, and a left C4 epidural steroid injection with transient relief. An initial MRI was limited by hardware artifact. A subsequent CT myelogram confirmed moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression and significant foraminal stenosis at C3-C4, clarifying the pathology.

---

**3. REVIEW OF PLAN'S INITIAL DETERMINATION**
The initial denial by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, acknowledged that the member met conservative treatment requirements. However, the denial was based solely on the interpretation that "imaging does not show sufficient pathology at C3-C4 to support surgical intervention," specifically referencing "MRI shows mild degenerative changes." This suggests the reviewer relied primarily on the MRI findings without adequately incorporating the clarifying CT myelogram.

---

**4. ANALYSIS APPLYING HIERARCHY: STATE LAW > CLINICAL STANDARD > PLAN CRITERIA**

**4.1. Primary Clinical Presentation: Radiculopathy**
Based on the clinical record, Ms. Morrison's symptoms (unilateral dermatomal numbness, weakness, diminished reflex) are characteristic of **cervical radiculopathy**. There is no documentation of upper motor neuron signs (Hoffman, hyperreflexia), bilateral symptoms, or gait disturbance that would indicate cervical myelopathy. Therefore, the conservative treatment requirements and surgical indications for radiculopathy, rather than myelopathy, are the most relevant.

**4.2. Application of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**

*   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:**
    *   The member's CT myelogram confirms "moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression" and "Significant foraminal stenosis" at C3-C4, which correlates with her "Left C4 dermatomal distribution: shoulder and lateral arm numbness, deltoid weakness 3+/5 left, bicep reflex diminished left." This clearly satisfies **Criterion 2.1(a) Cervical radiculopathy** and/or **2.1(c) Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit**. (MET)

*   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **(a) Physical Therapy:** Member completed 10 weeks (exceeding the 6-week minimum) with "No improvement in left C4 radicular symptoms." (MET)
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** Member completed 6 months of Pregabalin (partial relief) and 3 months of Duloxetine (minimal benefit), meeting the "at least two... for minimum 4 weeks each" requirement. (MET)
    *   **(c) Interventional Treatment:** Member underwent a left C4 epidural steroid injection, satisfying the "one interventional treatment" requirement. (MET)
    *   The plan's initial denial explicitly stated, "Member meets conservative treatment requirements," which is consistent with our review.
    *   The **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY** is not applicable, as Ms. Morrison's primary diagnosis is radiculopathy, not myelopathy (as per Note under Section 2.2).

*   **2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:**
    *   The initial denial cited insufficient pathology based on the MRI. However, the MRI report itself noted "SIGNIFICANT ARTIFACT from C4-C5 hardware limiting evaluation. Mild degenerative signal changes visible. Cannot exclude moderate compression due to artifact."
    *   Crucially, a **CT myelogram (2024-09-10)** was performed *specifically due to MRI artifact limitation*. Its findings state: "C3-C4: moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression confirmed. Significant foraminal stenosis. MRI underestimated pathology due to hardware artifact from prior fusion."
    *   The CT myelogram clearly demonstrates structural pathology consistent with the member's symptoms at the proposed surgical level, thereby fulfilling **Criterion 2.3**. The initial reviewer's reliance on the limited MRI alone, in disregard of the clarifying CT myelogram, represents an incorrect application of the plan's own criteria. (MET)

*   **2.4 PHYSICIAN DOCUMENTATION:** The appeal basis and clinical record indicate that the treating physician has provided the necessary rationale and documentation. (ASSUMED MET)

**4.3. Application of California Regulatory Framework (State Law):**

*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):**
    *   Subdivision (a)(2) states that a plan cannot condition approval on completion of PT where "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." Ms. Morrison completed 10 weeks of PT, and her therapist documented "No improvement in left C4 radicular symptoms," clearly demonstrating a functional plateau.
    *   Subdivision (a)(3) states that PT completion cannot be required if "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The CT myelogram confirming "moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression" and "significant foraminal stenosis" constitutes such objective evidence.
    *   These provisions affirm that the member has satisfied, or indeed surpassed, any reasonable physical therapy requirements.

*   **DMHC APL 22-014 (II.3 — Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard):** "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for conditions identified in this guidance. Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs." While the specific myelopathy provisions of this APL are not directly applicable, the general principle applies. The plan's initial denial, by overlooking critical imaging (CT myelogram) that clarifies the underlying pathology, effectively applied its imaging criterion in a manner more restrictive than generally accepted standards.

*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (Standard of Review for IMR):** "The IMR standard does NOT require that the service be consistent with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the service meets the standard in subdivision (a), the reviewer SHALL recommend approval regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." This reinforces that even if the plan's criteria were *more* restrictive (which they weren't in this case, but rather incorrectly applied), generally accepted medical practice takes precedence.

**4.4. Application of Clinical Evidence Base (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

*   **SECTION 1 — DIAGNOSTIC CLASSIFICATION:** The clinical record aligns with the definition of cervical radiculopathy.
*   **SECTION 2 — EVIDENCE ON CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT FOR MYELOPATHY:** As established, this section is less directly applicable due to the diagnosis of radiculopathy, not myelopathy. However, the general principles of functional plateau are supported.
*   **SECTION 3 — SURGICAL INDICATIONS: CLINICAL STANDARD:**
    *   Per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), Ms. Morrison's case meets **TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION** for surgery, specifically:
        *   **(b) Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx:** Member has persistent C4 radiculopathy after 10 weeks of PT, 6+ months of pharmacotherapy, and an interventional procedure.
        *   **(c) Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care:** Member has documented deltoid weakness (3+/5) and numbness, which represents significant functional impairment that has not responded to conservative care.
    *   The presence of "moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression confirmed" and "Significant foraminal stenosis" on the CT myelogram provides objective structural evidence consistent with these clinical findings and supports the need for surgical intervention given conservative treatment failure.

**5. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS**
The core conflict lies between the plan's initial denial reason ("imaging does not show sufficient pathology") and the comprehensive clinical record, particularly the CT myelogram. The initial denial erroneously concluded insufficient pathology by seemingly disregarding the CT myelogram, which was explicitly performed to overcome the limitations of the MRI due to hardware artifact. This oversight led to an incorrect application of the plan's own imaging criteria.

When all clinical documentation, including the definitive CT myelogram, is considered:
1.  The member meets all HealthFirst Plan Criteria for diagnosis (radiculopathy) and conservative treatment (exceeded requirements, reached functional plateau).
2.  The imaging requirement (2.3) is met by the CT myelogram, which clearly shows "moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression confirmed."
3.  State law (CIC 10169.5) supports that the member has completed an adequate course of physical therapy, reaching a functional plateau with objective structural neurological compromise.
4.  Clinical standards (AANS/CNS) identify the member's persistent radiculopathy with objective neurological deficit after extensive failed conservative care as a "reasonable indication" for surgery.

Therefore, the plan's denial was based on a flawed assessment of the objective imaging evidence and is not supported by a full review of the clinical record against plan criteria, state law, or generally accepted clinical standards.

---

**6. DISPOSITION**
**OVERTURN**

---

**7. WARRANT FOR DISPOSITION**
The prior authorization denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C3-C4) for Angela Morrison is **OVERTURNED** for the following reasons:

1.  **Plan Criteria Met – Imaging:** The initial denial was based on HealthFirst Plan Criterion 3.3 (imaging does not demonstrate pathology) and cited the MRI. However, the subsequent **CT myelogram (2024-09-10)** explicitly documented "moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression confirmed" and "Significant foraminal stenosis" at C3-C4, clarifying that the MRI underestimated the pathology due to artifact. This directly fulfills HealthFirst Plan Criterion 2.3, which requires current imaging demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms. The initial reviewer's failure to adequately consider the clarifying CT myelogram rendered the imaging-based denial erroneous.
2.  **Plan Criteria Met – Diagnosis & Conservative Treatment:** The member meets HealthFirst Plan Criterion 2.1(a) for cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits (left C4 dermatomal numbness, 3+/5 deltoid weakness, diminished bicep reflex). The plan's initial denial correctly acknowledged that the member met conservative treatment requirements (Criterion 2.2), having completed 10 weeks of PT (exceeding the 6-week minimum), two pharmacotherapy agents for appropriate durations, and one interventional procedure.
3.  **State Law Support (CIC § 10169.5):** California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2) and (a)(3) prohibits conditioning surgical approval on further PT when a functional plateau has been reached (10 weeks PT with no improvement in symptoms) or when objective evidence of structural neurological compromise exists (moderate-severe nerve root compression on CT myelogram). This confirms that Ms. Morrison has satisfied the spirit and letter of conservative treatment requirements.
4.  **Clinical Standard Met (AANS/CNS Guidelines):** Per the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023), Ms. Morrison's presentation of persistent radiculopathy with objective neurological deficit (weakness 3+/5) after an adequate trial of conservative therapy, and with objective imaging confirming moderate-severe nerve root compression, constitutes a **TIER 2 "Reasonable Indication" for surgical intervention.** This aligns with generally accepted standards of medical practice.

Based on the hierarchy of authority (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria), the evidence strongly supports that the requested ACDF C3-C4 is medically necessary and meets all applicable criteria when thoroughly reviewed. The initial denial was based on an incomplete assessment of the imaging record.
