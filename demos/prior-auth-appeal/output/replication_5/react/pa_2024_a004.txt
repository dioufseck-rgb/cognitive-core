**I. BACKGROUND**

Robert Chang, a 38-year-old member, presented to the emergency room on 2024-11-10 with acute onset bilateral arm weakness and difficulty walking, progressing over 72 hours, with no prior cervical history. An emergency MRI revealed severe C5-C6 central cord compression with extensive T2 hyperintensity (myelomalacia), consistent with acute cord injury, leading to an emergent neurosurgery consultation. Neurological examination confirmed an incomplete spinal cord syndrome with significant motor weakness, hyperreflexia, and bilateral Babinski signs.

Dr. Elena Marchetti, Neurosurgery, requested prior authorization for an ACDF C5-C6, declaring it an emergency surgical situation. She explicitly documented that physical therapy was absolutely contraindicated due to extensive myelomalacia and the risk of converting an incomplete cord syndrome to a complete cord syndrome, and that delaying surgery risked permanent complete paralysis. The patient received IV methylprednisolone for 24 hours per acute spinal cord injury protocol. No physical therapy or other interventional procedures were initiated due to the acute nature and physician's contraindication.

The initial prior authorization request was denied by HealthFirst on 2024-11-18, citing a lack of documented conservative treatment, specifically 0 weeks of physical therapy, no pharmacotherapy (referring to sustained oral agents), and no interventional procedures, as required under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2.

The appeal argues that the member presented with acute myelopathy and myelomalacia, making physical therapy contraindicated and the situation an emergency, citing California Insurance Code (CIC) 10169.5(a)(3).

---

**II. REVIEW FRAMEWORK**

This appeal determination is made by applying a three-source hierarchy:
1.  **State Law and Regulations:** California Insurance Code (CIC), California Health & Safety Code (CHSC), and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letters (APLs).
2.  **Clinical Standards and Guidelines:** Generally accepted standards of medical practice from authoritative bodies such as AANS/CNS, NASS, and AO Spine.
3.  **Health Plan Criteria:** HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024.

Where these sources conflict, the higher authority governs.

---

**III. ANALYSIS**

**1. Primary Clinical Presentation:**
The clinical record unequivocally establishes the member's diagnosis as **Acute Cervical Myelopathy with rapidly progressive deficits** and objective evidence of severe cord compression and extensive myelomalacia on MRI. This is a critical distinction from cervical radiculopathy. (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.1)

**2. Health Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024) Application:**
*   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** The member meets criteria 2.1(b) for Cervical Myelopathy, with confirmed cord compression and myelopathic symptoms (bilateral weakness, hyperreflexia, gait disturbance).
*   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT (Denial Basis):**
    *   The plan denied based on failure to meet 2.2(a) (6 weeks PT), 2.2(b) (two 4-week pharmacotherapy agents), and 2.2(c) (one interventional treatment).
    *   **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY:** The plan's myelopathy exception reduces PT to a 4-week minimum and waives interventional treatment if the physician documents risk. The pharmacotherapy requirement remains in full.
        *   While the interventional requirement could arguably be waived based on the physician's documented urgency and risk of neurological deterioration from delay, the 4-week PT and full pharmacotherapy requirements (two agents for 4+ weeks each) were not met.
        *   The physician explicitly stated PT was never started because it would cause immediate harm. The patient received 24 hours of IV methylprednisolone, which is standard for acute spinal cord injury, but does not meet the plan's criteria for two *oral* pharmacotherapy agents for 4+ weeks each.
*   **Conclusion regarding Plan Criteria:** Based *solely* on the explicit text of HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024, the conservative treatment requirements, even with the myelopathy exception, were not fully met.

**3. California Regulatory Requirements (State Law Override):**

*   **CIC § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite (Effective Jan 1, 2023):** This state law directly addresses and limits a plan's ability to require physical therapy prior to surgery.
    *   **§ 10169.5(a)(1):** A plan cannot condition approval on PT where the "physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition."
        *   **Application:** Dr. Marchetti explicitly documented that "PT is absolutely contraindicated and would be dangerous" and that "Initiating PT would risk converting incomplete cord syndrome to complete cord syndrome." This fully satisfies CIC § 10169.5(a)(1), overriding the plan's 4-week or 6-week PT requirement.
    *   **§ 10169.5(a)(3):** A plan cannot condition approval on PT where "the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
        *   **Application:** The MRI shows "Severe C5-C6 central cord compression" and "Extensive T2 hyperintensity throughout cord (extensive myelomalacia)," which is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise and active cord injury. Physical therapy cannot address or reverse direct spinal cord compression or myelomalacia. This fully satisfies CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), providing a second basis for overriding the plan's PT requirement.
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 — Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions (Issued Sep 2022):**
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** This guidance states that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent."
        *   **Application:** The member's condition perfectly matches this description and should be treated as urgent.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** This section states that for conditions where delay risks irreversible harm (like this case), plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when: (a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia)... AND (b) Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression [or is contraindicated for urgency]... AND (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines."
        *   **Application:** All three conditions are met. The MRI shows myelomalacia (progressive pathology). Dr. Marchetti documented urgency and contraindication to delay. Clinical guidelines (see below) strongly indicate surgery. This provision directly instructs plans to *not* require a *full course* of conservative treatment, which includes pharmacotherapy and interventional procedures, when urgency and myelomalacia are present.
    *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** "Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs." This reinforces that if the plan's criteria are more restrictive than clinical standards for this urgent condition, the clinical standard takes precedence.

**4. Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) Override:**

*   **Myelomalacia Significance:** The clinical evidence base highlights myelomalacia as indicating "existing spinal cord injury/damage," "higher risk of permanent deficit with continued compression," and "surgical urgency." It is considered a "strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials." (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2)
*   **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** For moderate-severe myelopathy, physical therapy "does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement." Critically, the AO Spine Guidelines (2021) state, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1)
*   **Conservative Treatment Duration:** For myelopathy, "No randomized controlled trials support a defined minimum duration of conservative treatment before surgery." The common 6-week PT requirement derives from radiculopathy evidence, not myelopathy. Applying it to myelopathy is a "category error." (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.2)
*   **Surgical Indications (TIER 1):** The member's presentation — acute severe myelopathy with objective cord compression, extensive myelomalacia, and rapidly progressive neurological deficits — meets multiple TIER 1 strong surgical indications. (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.1)
*   **Conclusion on Clinical Standard:** The plan's conservative treatment requirements (especially for PT and a prolonged pharmacotherapy trial) are directly contradicted by generally accepted clinical standards for acute, severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia. Requiring these treatments would be inconsistent with prudent clinical judgment and could risk permanent neurological harm.

**5. Denial Notice Adequacy:**
The denial notice cited CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 and provided specific reasons (lack of PT, pharmacotherapy, interventional). This meets the procedural requirements of CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2) and is not procedurally defective under § 1374.31(b). The issue lies in the substantive application of the criteria in light of state law and clinical standards.

---

**IV. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND CONCLUSION**

The HealthFirst denial was based on the member not meeting conservative treatment requirements in CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. While the member did not meet the plan's specific requirements, this denial is overridden by both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.

1.  **California State Law (CIC § 10169.5) explicitly prohibits conditioning surgery on physical therapy when a physician documents it as contraindicated or when objective structural neurological compromise exists that PT cannot address.** Both conditions are met in this case.
2.  **California Regulatory Guidance (DMHC APL 22-014) mandates that plans treat myelopathy with myelomalacia as urgent and waive full conservative treatment requirements when urgency, progressive pathology (myelomalacia), and surgical indication by guidelines are present.** All these conditions are met.
3.  **Leading Clinical Guidelines overwhelmingly support immediate surgical intervention for acute, moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and progressive deficits, and specifically state that delaying surgery for conservative treatment trials (including PT) is not evidence-based and may be harmful.**

Therefore, the plan's internal criteria regarding conservative treatment duration are more restrictive than state law and generally accepted standards of medical practice for this urgent clinical presentation. Under the established hierarchy, state law and clinical standards supersede the plan's criteria.

---

**V. DISPOSITION**

The prior authorization denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level) for Robert Chang is **OVERTURNED**.

**Warrant:**
The denial of CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) for Robert Chang is overturned because the requested service meets medical necessity criteria when applying the hierarchy of authority: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.

1.  **California State Law Override:** The plan's requirement for a minimum duration of physical therapy is directly superseded by California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3). The treating neurosurgeon documented that physical therapy is "absolutely contraindicated and would be dangerous" and that "initiating PT would risk converting incomplete cord syndrome to complete cord syndrome." Furthermore, the MRI demonstrates "severe C5-C6 central cord compression" with "extensive T2 hyperintensity throughout cord (extensive myelomalacia)," which constitutes objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address.
2.  **California Regulatory Guidance Override:** DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 explicitly directs health plans to treat cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia as an urgent condition with potential for irreversible harm. For such conditions, the APL mandates that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" where objective imaging evidence shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia), the treating physician documents urgency (as in this case), and surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines. The presence of myelomalacia is specifically identified as supporting the waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements.
3.  **Clinical Standard Override:** Generally accepted standards of medical practice from AANS/CNS (2023), NASS (2020), and AO Spine (2021) classify acute, moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and progressive neurological deficits as a TIER 1 strong surgical indication. These guidelines explicitly state that there is no high-quality evidence supporting mandatory physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for this condition, and that delaying surgery to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by evidence and may result in neurological deterioration. Myelomalacia on MRI is considered a strong indicator for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials. The acute administration of IV methylprednisolone was appropriate as emergency pharmacotherapy for acute spinal cord injury, and the expectation of a prolonged course of oral pharmacotherapy is not supported for this acute, rapidly progressive myelopathy.

The plan's denial, based on conservative treatment requirements outlined in CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, is in direct conflict with both California state law and prevailing clinical standards for this urgent and severe neurological condition. Therefore, the denial must be overturned to ensure the member receives medically necessary care consistent with the highest authority in the review hierarchy.
