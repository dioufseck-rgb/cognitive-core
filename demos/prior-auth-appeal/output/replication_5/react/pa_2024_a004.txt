### REVIEWER'S DETERMINATION: **OVERTURNED**

The denial for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) is **OVERTURNED**. The requested procedure is deemed medically necessary and appropriate for approval, as the original denial was inconsistent with both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, which supersede HealthFirst's internal criteria in this specific case.

---

### WARRANT / RATIONALE FOR DISPOSITION:

This appeal was reviewed applying the three-source hierarchy: **California State Law > Clinical Standard > HealthFirst Plan Criteria.**

#### 1. Clinical Presentation and Diagnosis:
The member, Robert Chang, presented with an **acute incomplete cervical spinal cord syndrome** consistent with severe cervical myelopathy. Key findings include:
*   **Acute onset:** Bilateral arm weakness and difficulty walking over 72 hours, with no prior cervical history.
*   **Neurological Deficits:** Bilateral arm weakness (2/5), bilateral leg weakness (3/5), hyperreflexia, bilateral Babinski sign, and urinary urgency. These are classic upper motor neuron signs of myelopathy.
*   **Imaging (MRI):** Urgent findings of "Severe C5-C6 central cord compression" and "Extensive T2 hyperintensity throughout cord (extensive myelomalacia)." Myelomalacia signifies existing spinal cord injury.
*   **Treating Physician's Assessment:** Dr. Marchetti explicitly declared this an "acute cervical myelopathy with rapidly progressive deficits – surgical emergency" and "acute incomplete cervical cord syndrome with extensive myelomalacia."

These findings unequivocally establish a diagnosis of moderate-to-severe acute cervical myelopathy with objective neurological compromise and cord damage (myelomalacia). This condition carries a high risk of permanent, irreversible neurological deficit if not treated expeditiously.

#### 2. Review Against HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):
The initial denial cited HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, requiring completion of 6 weeks of PT, 2 pharmacotherapy agents for 4+ weeks each, and 1 interventional procedure.

*   **Plan's Myelopathy Exception:** HealthFirst's own policy (Section 2.2(A)) includes a "MYELOPATHY PATHWAY" exception. This exception reduces the PT requirement to 4 weeks and waives the interventional treatment if the physician documents risk. The initial denial *failed to apply this specific myelopathy exception*, instead applying the standard requirements. Even if the exception had been applied, it would still require 4 weeks of PT and full pharmacotherapy.
*   **Conservative Treatment in this Case:** The clinical record clearly shows **0 weeks of PT** and **no interventional treatment**, and only 24 hours of IV methylprednisolone (acute protocol, not a 4-week trial). This is the basis of the plan's denial.

#### 3. Review Against California State Law (Hierarchy: Higher Authority):

California state law directly addresses the conditions under which conservative treatment, particularly physical therapy, cannot be mandated.

*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This statute prohibits health plans from conditioning surgical approval on PT completion where:
    *   **(a)(1) Physician documents PT contraindicated or unlikely to benefit:** Dr. Marchetti explicitly documented that "PT is absolutely contraindicated and would be dangerous. Initiating PT would risk converting incomplete cord syndrome to complete cord syndrome." This meets the criteria for waiver.
    *   **(a)(3) Objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address:** The MRI demonstrates "Severe C5-C6 central cord compression" and "Extensive T2 hyperintensity throughout cord (extensive myelomalacia)." The clinical presentation includes "incomplete spinal cord syndrome." Myelomalacia is objective evidence of cord damage that PT cannot reverse or address, and for which delay is detrimental. This also meets the criteria for waiver.
    *   **Conclusion for PT:** The plan's requirement for PT (whether 6 weeks or the reduced 4 weeks under its myelopathy exception) is directly overridden by CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3).

*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 — Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions:** This Department of Managed Health Care guidance explicitly addresses myelopathy cases:
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** The APL states that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent." This precisely describes the member's condition.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** For such urgent conditions, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment... when: (a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia...), AND (b) Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression [or is contraindicated], AND (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines."
    *   The APL specifically notes: "The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements even where a full course has not been completed, when the treating physician documents urgency."
    *   **Conclusion for ALL Conservative Treatment:** DMHC APL 22-014 directly mandates the waiver of *all* conservative treatment requirements for this member given the presence of myelomalacia, acute progressive deficits, and Dr. Marchetti's documentation of urgency and contraindication to conservative delay. The plan's denial, requiring any conservative treatment, conflicts with this state regulation.

*   **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) — Standard of Review:** This section mandates that for IMR (and by extension, internal appeals governed by the same principles of generally accepted medical practice), approval "SHALL be recommended regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive" if the service meets generally accepted standards.

#### 4. Review Against Clinical Standard (Hierarchy: Higher Authority):

Leading clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021, Cochrane Review 2021) strongly support surgical intervention for this presentation and contraindicate delaying for conservative treatment.

*   **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines emphasize that myelopathy and radiculopathy are distinct conditions requiring different treatment algorithms. The evidence for conservative treatment, particularly PT, is primarily for radiculopathy, not moderate-severe myelopathy.
*   **Myelomalacia:** The presence of myelomalacia (T2 signal change) on MRI indicates existing spinal cord injury, increased risk of permanent deficits, reduced recovery, and signifies **surgical urgency**. It is considered a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials.
*   **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:**
    *   AO Spine Guidelines (2021) explicitly state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." This directly supports Dr. Marchetti's clinical judgment and contradicts the plan's denial reason.
    *   For moderate-severe myelopathy, PT "does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement."
*   **Risk of Delay:** Clinical evidence consistently shows that delaying surgery for moderate-to-severe myelopathy, especially with myelomalacia, decreases the likelihood of neurological recovery and increases the risk of permanent, complete deficits.
*   **Surgical Indication:** The member meets multiple **TIER 1 STRONG INDICATION** criteria for surgery per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023):
    *   Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI.
    *   Myelomalacia on MRI.
    *   Acute severe myelopathy with risk of permanent deficit.
*   **Conclusion:** Generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice strongly recommend surgical decompression for acute, moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and progressive neurological deficits, and specifically advise against delaying surgery for a trial of conservative therapies, including physical therapy, as this can be detrimental to the patient.

#### 5. Resolution of Conflicts and Final Determination:
The initial denial was based on HealthFirst's internal criteria requiring conservative treatment (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2). This denial is in direct conflict with higher authorities:
1.  **California State Law (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014):** These state regulations specifically permit or mandate the waiver of physical therapy and other conservative treatment requirements in cases of myelopathy with objective neurological compromise (like myelomalacia) and physician-documented urgency or contraindication.
2.  **Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:** Major clinical guidelines unequivocally support immediate surgical intervention and contraindicate delaying surgery for conservative treatment in this clinical scenario, identifying such delay as potentially harmful.

Applying the hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), the requirements for conservative treatment in HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 are overridden by both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice for this specific, urgent myelopathy presentation. The member's condition meets strong criteria for immediate surgical intervention.

Therefore, the requested ACDF C5-C6 procedure is medically necessary and warranted without prior completion of conservative treatment.
