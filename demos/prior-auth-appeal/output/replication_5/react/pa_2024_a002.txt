**Disposition:** **OVERTURNED**

**Warrant:**
The prior authorization denial for an anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 for Mr. Marcus Webb is hereby **OVERTURNED**. The denial was based on HealthFirst's plan criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) regarding the minimum physical therapy duration and a restrictive interpretation of the myelopathy exception, which are superseded by California state law and generally accepted clinical standards for the member's diagnosed condition.

**Basis for Overturn:**

1.  **Clinical Diagnosis of Myelopathy Overrides Billing Discrepancy:**
    *   The denial stated the myelopathy exception (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A)) did not apply because "treating physician's most recent note lists 'cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy' as primary diagnosis." This relies on a billing code (ICD-10 M47.812) rather than a comprehensive review of the clinical record.
    *   The clinical record explicitly notes a "BILLING DISCREPANCY," confirming that while an ICD-10 code for radiculopathy was used administratively, the treating physician's narrative notes consistently describe cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia on MRI.
    *   Mr. Webb's initial presentation included classic symptoms and signs of cervical myelopathy: bilateral hand weakness (3/5), coordination problems, positive Hoffman sign bilaterally, bilateral hyperreflexia, and impaired tandem gait. MRI findings from July 10, 2024, confirm C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate cord compression and critically, **T2 signal change within the cord consistent with early myelomalacia.**
    *   **Domain Knowledge and Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021, Section 1.1):** Myelopathy is a distinct and more severe condition than radiculopathy, characterized by spinal cord compression and upper motor neuron signs. The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is a strong indicator of existing spinal cord injury. To deny the myelopathy exception based on an administrative billing code, when all clinical and imaging evidence points to myelopathy, is medically inappropriate and ignores the true clinical picture. Mr. Webb clearly meets the plan's own diagnostic criteria for cervical myelopathy (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(b)).

2.  **State Law Mandates Waiver of Conservative Treatment Requirements:**
    *   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a):** This state law explicitly prohibits a health insurer from conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy completion where:
        *   **(1) Physician documents PT contraindicated or unlikely to benefit:** Mr. Webb completed 4 weeks of PT (12 sessions). The treating physician halted PT after myelomalacia was confirmed on MRI and provided a letter stating further PT was contraindicated. This provision directly applies.
        *   **(3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address:** The MRI finding of "T2 signal change within cord consistent with early myelomalacia" directly constitutes objective evidence of structural neurological compromise. Myelomalacia signifies existing cord damage that physical therapy cannot reverse or treat, rendering further PT unnecessary and potentially harmful due to delay. This provision also applies.
    *   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Section II.2):** For conditions where delay of treatment risks irreversible harm, plans **SHALL NOT** require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents conservative treatment failure, and surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. All these conditions are met in Mr. Webb's case:
        *   Objective imaging shows myelomalacia.
        *   Treating physician documented PT halted and other conservative treatments failed to improve neurological symptoms.
        *   Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted guidelines for myelopathy with myelomalacia (see point 3 below).
    *   **Conclusion:** The plan's requirement for a 6-week minimum of physical therapy is directly overridden by California state law.

3.  **Clinical Standard Opposes Mandatory PT for Myelopathy with Myelomalacia:**
    *   **Hierarchy:** Clinical standard governs where it conflicts with plan criteria (CHSC § 1374.32(b), DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3).
    *   **AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021 (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2 & 2.1):** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) indicates existing spinal cord injury, a higher risk of permanent deficit with continued compression, and is a strong indication for surgical intervention. It is considered a **contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials**.
    *   **AO Spine Guidelines (2021, Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1):** "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." Mr. Webb presents with moderate-severe myelopathy (bilateral weakness, UMN signs, gait impairment, myelomalacia).
    *   **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023, Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.1 & 3.2):** Myelopathy with objective cord compression, myelomalacia, and progressive neurological deficit meets TIER 1 – STRONG INDICATION for surgery. The clinical standard explicitly states it does NOT require a minimum course of physical therapy for this presentation.
    *   **Risk of Delay (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.3):** Myelomalacia on preoperative MRI is the strongest predictor of incomplete neurological recovery postoperatively, and delaying surgery for moderate-severe myelopathy is associated with decreased likelihood of recovery.
    *   **Conclusion:** The plan's 6-week PT requirement is a "category error" when applied to cervical myelopathy, particularly with myelomalacia, and is inconsistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice.

**Conclusion:**
The HealthFirst denial failed to appropriately apply its own myelopathy exception by relying on a billing code rather than comprehensive clinical and imaging evidence. More importantly, the denial's reliance on a 6-week physical therapy requirement is in direct conflict with explicit mandates from California state law (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) and leading clinical guidelines for the management of cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia. The clinical evidence strongly supports surgical intervention as medically necessary and urgent in this case, and further delay for additional conservative treatment is contraindicated and risks irreversible neurological harm.

Therefore, the requested ACDF C6-C7 is medically necessary and should be authorized.

**Action:** Authorize CPT 22551 (ACDF C6-C7, one level) for Marcus Webb.
