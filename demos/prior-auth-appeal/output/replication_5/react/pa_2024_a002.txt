**Disposition:** **OVERTURNED**

**Warrant:**

The initial denial for Marcus Webb's requested ACDF C6-C7 is hereby OVERTURNED. The denial was based on HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, specifically Section 2.2(a) (failure to complete 6 weeks of physical therapy) and a misapplication of the Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A) (assertion that myelopathy was not the primary diagnosis). Upon a comprehensive review of the clinical record, relevant state law, and generally accepted clinical standards, it is determined that the service is medically necessary and the plan's denial criteria are overridden.

**Clinical Presentation Analysis:**

The clinical record unequivocally supports a diagnosis of **cervical spondylotic myelopathy C6-C7**.
*   **Symptoms:** Mr. Webb presents with bilateral hand weakness (3/5 grip), coordination problems, and impaired tandem gait, which are classic myelopathic symptoms.
*   **Neurological Examination:** The presence of positive bilateral Hoffman signs and bilateral hyperreflexia are definitive upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, indicative of spinal cord compression.
*   **Imaging:** MRI dated 2024-07-10 shows C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate cord compression and, critically, **T2 signal change within the cord consistent with early myelomalacia.**
*   **Diagnosis Discrepancy:** While the initial ICD-10 billing code on PT referrals was M47.812 ("cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy"), the treating physician's narrative notes consistently describe cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia. The physician explicitly clarified this as a "billing discrepancy" and an "administrative artifact." For medical necessity review, clinical documentation and objective findings supersede administrative billing codes, especially when the discrepancy is explained.

**Application of HealthFirst's Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

1.  **Diagnosis Requirement (2.1):** Mr. Webb meets criterion 2.1(b) (Cervical myelopathy) and 2.1(c) (Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit), supported by MRI.
2.  **Conservative Treatment Requirement (2.2):**
    *   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2(b)) and Interventional (2.2(c)):** Mr. Webb completed a 3-month trial of Gabapentin and a 2-month trial of Naproxen (meeting 2.2(b)). He also underwent a cervical epidural injection (meeting 2.2(c)). These aspects of the conservative treatment pathway were met.
    *   **Physical Therapy (2.2(a)):** Mr. Webb completed 4 weeks of PT (12 sessions). The plan's standard pathway requires 6 weeks.
    *   **Myelopathy Exception (2.2(A) Note):** The plan's denial hinges on the assertion that myelopathy was not the "PRIMARY diagnosis." This is a misapplication of the plan's own criteria. Given the extensive clinical documentation of myelopathic symptoms, UMN signs, and myelomalacia on MRI, cervical myelopathy is undeniably the primary *clinical* diagnosis, despite an administrative billing code discrepancy. Therefore, Mr. Webb *does* qualify for the myelopathy exception, which reduces the PT requirement to a 4-week minimum. Having completed 4 weeks, he *does* meet the plan's *modified* PT requirement under its own exception.

**Application of California Regulatory Requirements (State Law Override):**

Even if the plan's interpretation of its own criteria were upheld (which it is not), relevant California state laws provide direct overrides for this clinical scenario.

1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):**
    *   This law explicitly prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of surgery on PT completion where:
        *   **(a)(1):** The treating physician documented PT as contraindicated or unlikely to benefit. In this case, the treating physician halted PT after myelomalacia was confirmed and explicitly stated further PT was contraindicated.
        *   **(a)(3):** The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. Mr. Webb's MRI showing **myelomalacia with moderate cord compression** is definitive objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot reverse.
    *   Therefore, the plan's requirement for a minimum duration of physical therapy is directly overridden by state law.

2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):**
    *   This APL instructs plans that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and **myelomalacia on MRI constitutes an urgent condition** with potential for irreversible harm if untreated (II.1).
    *   It mandates that plans **SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment** when:
        *   **(a):** Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (e.g., myelomalacia). Mr. Webb's MRI shows myelomalacia.
        *   **(b):** The treating physician documents conservative treatment has been attempted and failed to arrest progression. Mr. Webb's physician halted PT, documented partial pain relief from pharmacotherapy but no improvement in weakness, and minimal response to interventional treatment, confirming failure to arrest progression.
        *   **(c):** Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines (as confirmed below).
    *   The APL specifically identifies myelomalacia as a finding that supports waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements. This directly overrides HealthFirst's PT duration requirement.

3.  **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard of Review):** This section confirms that services meeting "generally accepted standards of medical practice" SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. This sets the stage for the clinical standard to override plan criteria.

**Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021 Override):**

1.  **Myelomalacia Significance:** Major spine societies (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021) consider myelomalacia (T2 signal change) on MRI to be a strong indicator of existing spinal cord injury, increased risk of permanent deficits, and a **strong indication for surgical intervention, often constituting a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials.** (Clinical Reference, Section 1.2).
2.  **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:**
    *   Clinical guidelines (NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021, Cochrane Review 2021) state that physical therapy for moderate-severe myelopathy does **NOT** produce meaningful functional improvement.
    *   The AO Spine Guidelines (2021) specifically state, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. **Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration.**" (Clinical Reference, Section 2.1).
    *   The plan's 6-week (or even 4-week) PT requirement is primarily derived from radiculopathy evidence and represents a **category error** when applied to myelopathy, especially with myelomalacia (Clinical Reference, Section 2.2).
3.  **Surgical Indications:** Mr. Webb's presentation meets **TIER 1 (STRONG INDICATION)** for surgery per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) due to:
    *   Moderate-severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI.
    *   Myelomalacia on MRI (cord signal change).
    *   Objective neurological deficits (bilateral weakness, UMN signs, gait disturbance).
    *   The clinical standard does not require a minimum course of physical therapy as a prerequisite for surgery in this high-risk myelopathic presentation (Clinical Reference, Section 3.2).

**Conclusion:**

The plan's denial is based on an incorrect interpretation of Mr. Webb's primary diagnosis and the mechanical application of a physical therapy duration requirement that is not supported by clinical evidence for myelopathy, particularly in the presence of myelomalacia. Both California state law (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014) and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021) directly override the plan's criteria in this case. Delaying surgical intervention for Mr. Webb, given his diagnosis of cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and objective neurological deficits, carries a significant risk of irreversible neurological deterioration, as documented in the clinical evidence.

Therefore, the requested ACDF C6-C7 is medically necessary and should be approved.

**Decision:** The denial of prior authorization for CPT 22551 for Marcus Webb is **OVERTURNED**. Authorization for the procedure is granted.
