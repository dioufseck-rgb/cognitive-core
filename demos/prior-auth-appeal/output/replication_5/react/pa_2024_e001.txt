**I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

This appeal reviews the denial of a prior authorization request for a two-level ACDF at C5-C6 and C6-C7 for Mr. Victor Patel. The initial denial cited that while C5-C6 demonstrated pathology consistent with surgical criteria, C6-C7 showed only mild changes and did not meet criteria, thus not approving the two-level fusion.

Upon comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst Plan Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory framework (CIC, CHSC, DMHC APL 22-014), and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021), applying the hierarchy of State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria, the denial of the **two-level ACDF (C5-C6 and C6-C7) is UPHELD.**

While the C5-C6 level clearly meets medical necessity criteria for surgical intervention, the C6-C7 level does not demonstrate objective evidence of nerve root compression or definitive radiculopathy, failing to meet criteria under both the HealthFirst plan policy and generally accepted clinical standards.

---

**II. CASE BACKGROUND**

Mr. Victor Patel, a 60-year-old member, was diagnosed with two-level cervical disc disease at C5-C6 and C6-C7, with asymmetric severity. Dr. Lisa Park requested prior authorization for a two-level ACDF. The request was denied by HealthFirst, stating C5-C6 met criteria but C6-C7 did not, thus precluding approval for a two-level procedure.

The appeal argues that both levels are symptomatic, adjacent levels should be addressed simultaneously to prevent accelerated degeneration, and the member meets full criteria for both levels.

**IV. APPLICATION OF HEALTHFIRST PLAN CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)**

1.  **Denial Adequacy:** The initial denial notice identified the reason (C6-C7 not meeting criteria) and cited a reason code. This appears procedurally adequate per CHSC § 1374.31(b).

2.  **Assessment of C5-C6 Level:**
    *   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** Met. The MRI shows a large disc herniation with moderate-severe right C6 nerve root compression, and the neurological exam documents right C6 weakness, absent reflex, and dermatomal numbness/tingling, consistent with cervical radiculopathy (2.1a) and objective neurological deficit (2.1c).
    *   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:** Met for radiculopathy.
        *   **Physical Therapy (2.2a):** 8 weeks of PT completed exceeds the 6-week minimum. C6 symptoms persisted.
        *   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2b):** Two agents (Pregabalin, Naproxen) used for >4 weeks each.
        *   **Interventional (2.2c):** A right C6 epidural steroid injection was performed.
    *   **2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:** Met. MRI shows pathology at C5-C6 consistent with symptoms.
    *   **Myelopathy Exception (2.2A):** Not applicable, as this is a radiculopathy case, not primary myelopathy.

    **Conclusion for C5-C6:** This level clearly meets HealthFirst's medical necessity criteria for surgical intervention.

3.  **Assessment of C6-C7 Level:**
    *   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** **NOT MET.**
        *   The MRI explicitly states "no nerve root compression identifiable, no cord contact." This fails to demonstrate structural pathology consistent with radiculopathy (2.1a) or myelopathy (2.1b).
        *   The neurological exam showed "No definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms" and only "right tricep reflex slightly diminished," which does not constitute an "objective neurological deficit" clearly attributable to C7 compression (2.1c) given the imaging findings. Physician notes regarding C7 symptoms are qualified as "mild" and "possibly present but not clearly documented."
    *   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:** Although PT was completed for 8 weeks and pharmacotherapy met, the interventional treatment was C6-specific. More importantly, the *primary failure* for C6-C7 is the lack of objective diagnosis and imaging findings, not a failure of conservative treatment trials.
    *   **2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:** **NOT MET.** The MRI does not demonstrate structural pathology (nerve root compression or cord contact) at C6-C7 consistent with surgical indication.

    **Conclusion for C6-C7:** This level does **not** meet HealthFirst's medical necessity criteria for surgical intervention due to lack of objective evidence of nerve root compression or definitive neurological deficit.

**V. APPLICATION OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK**

1.  **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Prerequisite):**
    *   This provision prevents plans from requiring PT where it's contraindicated, a functional plateau is reached, or objective structural neurological compromise exists that PT cannot address.
    *   In this case, 8 weeks of PT was completed, exceeding the plan's 6-week minimum. For C5-C6, the symptoms persisted, suggesting a functional plateau was reached for that specific radiculopathy. For C6-C7, symptoms were "minimal and possibly resolving," suggesting PT was either effective or the symptoms were not significant. This regulation does not override the denial for C6-C7 because the denial is based on a lack of objective pathology, not a failure to complete conservative treatment for that level. No objective structural neurological compromise is identified for C6-C7.

2.  **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):**
    *   This APL specifically addresses myelopathy cases and allows for waiver of conservative treatment requirements for time-sensitive neurological conditions with progressive pathology like myelomalacia.
    *   **This APL is NOT APPLICABLE** as the member's clinical record does not provide any objective evidence of cervical myelopathy or myelomalacia. This case is clearly a radiculopathy presentation.

3.  **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard is NOT Plan Criteria):**
    *   This critical provision mandates that services meeting "generally accepted standards of medical practice" SHALL be approved regardless of more restrictive plan criteria. Therefore, I must evaluate the request against clinical standards.

---

**VI. APPLICATION OF CLINICAL STANDARD KNOWLEDGE (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)**

1.  **Distinction: Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** The clinical standards emphasize the fundamental difference. This case is definitively a radiculopathy at C5-C6, with no evidence of myelopathy at either level.

2.  **Conservative Treatment for Radiculopathy:** For radiculopathy, 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment (PT, pharmacotherapy, injections) is generally recommended before surgery (NASS 2020). Mr. Patel completed 8 weeks of PT, 3-4 months of pharmacotherapy, and a C6-specific epidural injection. This largely aligns with standard conservative treatment recommendations for radiculopathy.

3.  **Surgical Indications for ACDF (AANS/CNS 2023):**
    *   **C5-C6 Level:** Meets TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION (3.1(b)): "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and 3.1(c) "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care." The objective neurological deficit (weakness, absent reflex) and persistent symptoms after adequate conservative treatment clearly support surgical intervention at C5-C6.
    *   **C6-C7 Level:** Falls under TIER 3 — SURGERY NOT INDICATED (3.1(b)): "Imaging findings without correlating symptoms." The MRI shows "no nerve root compression identifiable, no cord contact" at C6-C7, and the clinical exam shows "No definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms." Clinical standards do not support surgical intervention for mild imaging changes without clear correlating objective symptoms and nerve root compression.
    *   **Argument for simultaneous treatment of adjacent levels:** While sometimes considered, the clinical standard does not support prophylactic fusion of an asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic level lacking objective pathology (e.g., nerve root compression or myelopathy) simply because it is adjacent to a symptomatic level. The risk of future degeneration in an asymptomatic level does not outweigh the immediate surgical risks without a clear medical necessity.

---

**VII. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND FINAL DETERMINATION**

1.  **C5-C6 Level:** Both HealthFirst Plan Criteria and generally accepted Clinical Standards concur that the C5-C6 level meets medical necessity for ACDF due to objective C6 radiculopathy and failure of conservative treatment. No state law overrides this finding.

2.  **C6-C7 Level:** Both HealthFirst Plan Criteria and generally accepted Clinical Standards concur that the C6-C7 level does **not** meet medical necessity for ACDF.
    *   **Plan Criteria:** Fails sections 2.1 (Diagnosis) and 2.3 (Imaging) due to lack of objective nerve root compression or definitive C7 radicular symptoms.
    *   **Clinical Standards:** Falls under AANS/CNS Tier 3(b) (Imaging findings without correlating symptoms). There is no high-quality evidence or guideline supporting fusion of a cervical level with "no nerve root compression identifiable" and "no definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms."
    *   **State Law:** No California state law or regulatory guidance overrides the need for objective clinical and imaging evidence of pathology to justify spinal surgery, particularly for radiculopathy where myelopathy is not present.

3.  **Two-Level Request:** The original denial correctly stated that a "Two-level fusion not approved when one level meets criteria and one does not." Since C6-C7 does not meet medical necessity for fusion under any applicable standard (State Law, Clinical Standard, or Plan Criteria), the request for a *two-level* ACDF, as submitted, cannot be approved.

**DISPOSITION: UPHELD**

**WARRANT:** The denial of the two-level ACDF (C5-C6 and C6-C7) is upheld because the C6-C7 level does not demonstrate medical necessity for surgical intervention. The clinical record, including MRI findings ("no nerve root compression identifiable, no cord contact") and neurological examination ("No definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms"), fails to meet the diagnostic and imaging requirements of both HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Sections 2.1 and 2.3) and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, Tier 3(b)). While the C5-C6 level clearly meets criteria for ACDF, the absence of medical necessity for the C6-C7 level prevents approval of the requested two-level procedure. The arguments regarding simultaneous adjacent level treatment are not supported by evidence for asymptomatic or non-radiculopathic levels.

---

**Next Steps:** Mr. Patel's treating physician may submit a new prior authorization request for a single-level ACDF at C5-C6, which appears to meet all medical necessity criteria.
