**DISPOSITION: OVERTURN**

**WARRANT:**

The denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) for member Jessica Turner is **OVERTURNED**. This determination is based on a critical procedural defect in the denial process, which violates California state law, and further supported by the overriding authority of state law and clinical standards regarding conservative treatment requirements.

**I. Procedural Defect in Denial Notice (State Law > Plan Criteria):**

The denial issued on 2024-10-15 was communicated verbally during a peer-to-peer call, and the plan failed to provide a written denial notice that specified the clinical criteria applied, the specific clinical reason for the denial, or notice of Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights. The case record explicitly notes "Verbal only — peer-to-peer call" for the written denial basis and "imr_notice_included": false.

California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a)(2) mandates that a plan SHALL include in its denial notice: "the specific criteria applied, the specific clinical reason for the denial, and notice of IMR rights." Furthermore, CHSC § 1374.31(b) explicitly states: "A denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is DEFECTIVE and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."

Given that the denial was verbal-only, lacked specific written criteria, and did not include notice of IMR rights, it is procedurally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b). This fundamental violation of state law renders the denial invalid, requiring it to be overturned. The plan is obligated to issue a compliant denial notice or approve the service.

**II. Override of Conservative Treatment Criteria by State Law (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):**

Even if the procedural defect were overlooked, the plan's denial based on unmet conservative treatment criteria is superseded by California state law. The plan's criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2) require a minimum of 6 weeks of physical therapy, two pharmacotherapy agents for 4+ weeks each, and one interventional procedure. The member completed 5 weeks of PT, 3 months of Meloxicam (partial relief), and 2 weeks of Prednisone (temporary relief), but no interventional procedure.

*   **Physical Therapy Requirement:**
    California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a) explicitly prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where:
    *   (2) "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." In this case, the member completed 5 weeks of PT, and the treating physician *declared a plateau in the appeal letter*. While "not formally documented" by the therapist, the treating physician's documentation is sufficient under this section of state law.
    *   (3) "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The MRI findings of C6-C7 disc herniation and right C7 nerve root compression, combined with neurological exam findings of right C7 dermatomal numbness/tingling and diminished tricep reflex, constitute objective structural neurological compromise (cervical radiculopathy). Physical therapy, while beneficial for symptom management, does not resolve such structural compression.
    Therefore, per CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) and (a)(3), the plan cannot legitimately deny the requested surgery based on the member not completing the full 6 weeks of physical therapy.

*   **Interventional Treatment Requirement:**
    While the member did not undergo an interventional procedure as required by HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(c), the generally accepted clinical standards for radiculopathy do not always mandate a specific sequence or type of conservative treatment for surgery to be indicated. The AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), Section 3.1, TIER 2(b) state that surgery is a "reasonable option" for "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx." The member has undergone 5 weeks of PT, 3 months of an NSAID, and a steroid taper, with persistent symptoms and a physician-declared plateau for PT. This constitutes a substantial "conservative tx" trial. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) clarifies that "The IMR standard does NOT require that the service be consistent with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the service meets the standard in subdivision (a), the reviewer SHALL recommend approval regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." This principle applies broadly in appeal review. The plan's requirement for a specific interventional procedure after a sufficient trial of other conservative modalities (PT and pharmacotherapy) and where PT has plateaued or is structurally ineffective may be considered more restrictive than generally accepted clinical standards.

**III. Clinical Presentation and Standards (Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):**

The member's clinical presentation of cervical radiculopathy C6-C7, confirmed by MRI showing C6-C7 disc herniation with right C7 nerve root compression and moderate foraminal stenosis, and neurological findings (right C7 dermatomal numbness/tingling, diminished tricep reflex), aligns with an appropriate diagnosis for ACDF. The AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), Section 3.1, TIER 2(b) supports surgery as a "reasonable option" for radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment. Given the conservative treatments attempted (5 weeks PT, Meloxicam for 3 months, Prednisone taper for 2 weeks) and the physician's documented plateau, the member has undergone a reasonable trial of conservative care with persistent symptoms.

**Conclusion:**

The denial is fundamentally flawed due to its non-compliance with CHSC § 1374.31(b) regarding written denial notices. Furthermore, the plan's specific conservative treatment requirements, particularly for physical therapy, are superseded by CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) and (a)(3). Finally, the clinical presentation and extent of conservative treatment align with generally accepted standards for surgical intervention for cervical radiculopathy.

Therefore, the denial is **OVERTURNED**. The requested procedure is to be authorized.
