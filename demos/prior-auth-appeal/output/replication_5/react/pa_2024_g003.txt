**I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

The prior authorization request for an ACDF C5-C6 for Mr. Bernard Achebe was denied based on incomplete conservative treatment, specifically insufficient physical therapy, pharmacotherapy, and lack of interventional procedures.

Upon review, the plan's denial based on the incomplete physical therapy requirement directly conflicts with California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3). The clinical record demonstrates objective structural neurological compromise (moderate central cord compression with bilateral Hoffman signs and hyperreflexia) that physical therapy cannot address. Therefore, conditioning surgical approval on further physical therapy is prohibited by state law.

While other conservative treatment requirements (pharmacotherapy, interventional procedures) may still be applicable based on the clinical presentation of mild, stable myelopathy without myelomalacia, the presence of an invalid reason for denial necessitates overturning the original denial. The plan must re-evaluate the request, excluding the invalidated physical therapy requirement.

**DISPOSITION: OVERTURNED**

---

**II. DETAILED REVIEW AND ANALYSIS**

**A. Clinical Presentation and Diagnosis:**
Mr. Achebe is a 61-year-old male diagnosed with mild cervical myelopathy at C5-C6.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-10-01):** Shows C5-C6 moderate central cord compression (canal diameter 8mm). Notably, "No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia." and "Cord contact present without compression" are also reported. While some findings are mildly contradictory, the diagnosis of "mild cervical myelopathy" and objective neurological signs confirm spinal cord involvement.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-10-05):** Reveals bilateral Hoffman signs and mild bilateral hyperreflexia, which are definitive upper motor neuron signs consistent with myelopathy. Hand grip strength is 4+/5 bilateral, with no frank weakness, intact tandem gait, and fully independent ADLs. The mJOA score is 15/18, indicating mild myelopathy.
*   **Treating Neurologist's Declaration:** Dr. Osei emphasizes "surgical urgency" due to "severe and progressive" cord compression, stating "further delay is medically contraindicated." However, this declaration of "progressive" and "severe" is contradicted by the functional assessment noting "6 months, stable" symptoms and the absence of myelomalacia or acute progression.

**B. Denial Basis and Appeal Basis:**
The denial was based on unmet conservative treatment requirements:
1.  Physical therapy: 3 weeks completed vs. 6 weeks required.
2.  Pharmacotherapy: One agent (Baclofen) completed vs. two agents required.
3.  Interventional procedures: None on record vs. one required.

The appeal argues surgical urgency, confirmed myelopathy, and failure of conservative treatment to arrest neurological progression.

**C. Application of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
1.  **Diagnosis (2.1):** Member meets criteria for Cervical Myelopathy (2.1(b)) due to spinal cord compression and myelopathic symptoms (Hoffman sign, hyperreflexia).
2.  **Conservative Treatment (2.2):**
    *   **Physical Therapy (2.2(a)):** 3 weeks completed, falling short of the standard 6 weeks or the myelopathy exception's 4-week minimum. **(NOT MET)**
    *   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2(b)):** One agent (Baclofen) used for 6 weeks, falling short of the "at least two... for minimum 4 weeks each." **(NOT MET)**
    *   **Interventional Treatment (2.2(c)):** None on record. **(NOT MET)**
3.  **Myelopathy Exception (2.2(A)):**
    *   **PT:** Reduced to 4 weeks minimum. Member only completed 3 weeks. **(STILL NOT MET)**
    *   **Interventional:** Waived "if treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses risk of neurological deterioration." Dr. Osei's letter states a risk from *further delay* to surgery, not specifically from an *interventional procedure itself*. This condition for waiver is not met. **(NOT MET)**
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Requirement remains in full. **(NOT MET)**

*Conclusion on Plan Criteria:* Based solely on HealthFirst's plan criteria, the conservative treatment requirements (even with the myelopathy exception for PT and interventional waiver) are not met.

**D. Application of California Regulatory Framework:**
1.  **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This state law explicitly states a health insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... [t]he condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
    *   **Analysis:** Mr. Achebe has objective moderate central cord compression on MRI and objective upper motor neuron signs (bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia). Physical therapy cannot decompress the spinal cord or reverse these neurological signs. The clinical reference summary (SOURCE DOCUMENT 3, Section 5) also clarifies that neurological symptoms in myelopathy are not expected to respond to PT regardless of duration.
    *   **Finding:** This condition of state law is met. Therefore, the plan's denial based on incomplete physical therapy is invalid.

2.  **DMHC APL 22-014 — Conservative Treatment Exceptions for Urgent Conditions:** This APL provides for waiver of full conservative treatment where:
    *   (a) Objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia, or progressive herniation on serial imaging).
        *   **Analysis:** The MRI explicitly states "No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia." No serial imaging is provided to demonstrate progression. The mJOA functional assessment states "6 months, stable." While Dr. Osei declares the condition "progressive," this is contradicted by objective findings in the record. Therefore, this specific waiver condition (progressive pathology/myelomalacia) is **NOT MET**.
    *   (b) Treating physician documents conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression.
        *   **Analysis:** Dr. Osei's letter states, "Conservative treatment has failed to arrest neurological progression." This condition is met based on physician documentation.
    *   (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines.
        *   **Analysis:** To be assessed under Clinical Standard (Section E below).
    *   **Finding:** While Dr. Osei's documentation satisfies (b), the absence of objective progressive pathology or myelomalacia (DMHC APL 22-014 II.2(a)) means the APL's strongest waiver provisions for *all* conservative treatment requirements for urgent conditions are not definitively triggered in this case.

3.  **CHSC § 1374.32(b) — IMR Standard of Review:** This code states that services meeting "generally accepted standards of medical practice" SHALL be approved regardless of the plan's more restrictive internal criteria. This principle allows for overriding plan criteria when they are inconsistent with clinical standards.

**E. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
1.  **Myelopathy Distinction:** The member has confirmed cervical myelopathy, not radiculopathy. The clinical standards emphasize different treatment algorithms and evidence for these conditions (SOURCE DOCUMENT 3, Section 1.1).
2.  **Myelomalacia Significance:** The absence of myelomalacia in Mr. Achebe's MRI (per the clinical record and AANS/CNS interpretation) indicates a lower level of urgency compared to cases where myelomalacia is present, and supports the mJOA interpretation of "mild myelopathy" rather than "severe and progressive."
3.  **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy (SOURCE DOCUMENT 3, Section 2.1):**
    *   "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy."
    *   For *mild* myelopathy, PT may provide "modest short-term pain reduction."
    *   Crucially, neurological symptoms like Hoffman's sign and hyperreflexia are "not expected to respond to PT regardless of duration." This reinforces the application of CIC 10169.5(a)(3).
4.  **Surgical Indications (AANS/CNS 2023, SOURCE DOCUMENT 3, Section 3.1):**
    *   Mr. Achebe's case presents as "mild myelopathy with stable symptoms" (mJOA 15/18, stable for 6 months), with objective cord compression and UMN signs but *no myelomalacia or acute progression*.
    *   This aligns more closely with **TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION:** "(a) Mild myelopathy with stable symptoms after adequate conservative trial." It does not fully meet TIER 1 criteria, which require moderate-to-severe myelopathy, progressive deficit, myelomalacia, or acute severe myelopathy.
    *   For a TIER 2 indication, an "adequate conservative trial" is generally considered reasonable. Clinical standards question the *duration* of PT for myelopathy, but do not necessarily waive *all* conservative treatment (pharmacotherapy, interventional) for a mild, stable presentation without myelomalacia.

**F. Conflict Resolution:**
The plan denied based on three conservative treatment requirements.
1.  **Physical Therapy:** The plan's requirement for 4-6 weeks of PT directly conflicts with CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) because Mr. Achebe has objective structural neurological compromise (cord compression with UMN signs) that PT cannot address. State law overrides plan criteria.
2.  **Pharmacotherapy:** The plan requires two agents; Mr. Achebe used one. While the clinical standard criticizes PT duration for myelopathy, it does not explicitly waive pharmacotherapy for *mild, stable* myelopathy (Tier 2 AANS/CNS). The DMHC APL's stronger waiver for *all* conservative care is not fully met due to the absence of myelomalacia or documented progression. Therefore, the plan's requirement for a second pharmacotherapy agent, in the context of a mild, stable myelopathy, is not directly overridden by higher authority.
3.  **Interventional Procedure:** The plan requires one, and Mr. Achebe had none. The plan's waiver for myelopathy (physician documents risk of interventional treatment) was not met. Similar to pharmacotherapy, for a mild, stable myelopathy without myelomalacia or clear progression, there isn't a strong basis in state law or clinical standards to *mandate* waiver of an interventional procedure trial.

**G. Conclusion on Denial Validity:**
The denial issued by HealthFirst relied, in part, on the member not having completed the required duration of physical therapy. This specific reason for denial is invalid under California state law (CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)). Since the original denial is based on an invalid reason, the denial itself must be overturned. The plan must then re-evaluate the prior authorization request, excluding the invalidated physical therapy requirement. The plan may still consider the unmet pharmacotherapy and interventional requirements as part of a new review, but cannot continue to condition approval on further physical therapy.

---

**III. DISPOSITION**

The denial of prior authorization for CPT 22551 for Mr. Bernard Achebe is **OVERTURNED**.

**WARRANT:**
The plan's denial was based, in part, on the member's failure to complete the required duration of physical therapy. This requirement is in direct conflict with California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3). The clinical record demonstrates objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (moderate central cord compression with bilateral Hoffman signs and bilateral hyperreflexia) that physical therapy cannot address. Under this state law, a health plan cannot condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy in such circumstances.

Because a significant reason for the denial is invalid under state law, the entire denial decision must be overturned. The HealthFirst PPO Gold plan is directed to re-evaluate the prior authorization request for ACDF C5-C6 without requiring further physical therapy.
