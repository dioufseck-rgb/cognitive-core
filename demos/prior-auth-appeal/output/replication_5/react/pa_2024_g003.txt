**APPEAL DETERMINATION: HealthFirst PPO Gold**

**Case ID:** PA-2024-G003
**Member:** Bernard Achebe, M-G003, Age 61
**Procedure:** ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551)
**Date of Denial:** 2024-11-05
**Level of Review:** First-Level Appeal

---

**1. CASE SUMMARY:**

Bernard Achebe, a 61-year-old member, requested prior authorization for an ACDF at C5-C6 for a diagnosis of mild cervical myelopathy. The initial request was denied by HealthFirst Utilization Management (Dr. Alan Foster) on November 5, 2024, citing incomplete conservative treatment: 3 weeks of physical therapy (PT) completed out of 6 required, only one pharmacotherapy agent used, and no interventional procedure on record. The treating neurologist, Dr. Kwame Osei, appealed this denial, citing surgical urgency, confirmed myelopathy with neurological progression, and failure of conservative treatment.

**2. CLINICAL RECORD REVIEW:**

*   **Diagnosis:** Mild cervical myelopathy at C5-C6.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-10-01):** Moderate central cord compression at C5-C6. **Crucially, no T2 signal change (no myelomalacia)**. Canal diameter 8mm. Cord contact present without compression.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-10-05):** Bilateral Hoffman signs, mild bilateral hyperreflexia. Tandem gait intact. Hand grip strength 4+/5 bilateral. No frank weakness. No dermatomal deficit.
*   **Functional Status:** mJOA score 15/18 (interpreting as mild myelopathy). Activities of daily living (ADLs) fully intact. Symptom duration 6 months, stable.
*   **Treating Neurologist Declaration (2024-11-01):** Dr. Osei states "immediate surgical intervention," "cervical cord compression is severe and progressive," "further delay is medically contraindicated," and "risks irreversible neurological deterioration."
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 3 weeks (9 sessions) completed. Halted by Dr. Osei due to "myelopathy concern."
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Baclofen 5mg BID for 6 weeks. Response: "Mild spasticity improvement." (One agent only).
    *   **Interventional:** None on record.

**3. HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY:**

State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria

**4. ANALYSIS:**

**A. Plan Criteria Review (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**

*   **2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** Met. Confirmed cervical myelopathy (2.1(b)) with supporting MRI.
*   **2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement (Standard Pathway):** Not met.
    *   PT: 3 weeks completed, 6 required.
    *   Pharmacotherapy: One agent used, two required.
    *   Interventional: None completed, one required.
*   **2.2(A) Myelopathy Exception Pathway:**
    *   Applies as myelopathy is the primary diagnosis.
    *   **Physical Therapy:** Requirement reduced to 4 weeks minimum. Member completed 3 weeks. **Still not met by 1 week.**
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Requirement remains in full (two agents, 4+ weeks each). Member used one agent. **Not met.**
    *   **Interventional Treatment:** Waived *if treating physician documents risk of neurological deterioration*. Dr. Osei's letter states general urgency and risk of deterioration with *delay*, but does not specifically document that *interventional treatment* itself poses risk. **Not explicitly met for waiver under plan criteria.**
*   **Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The member does not meet the conservative treatment requirements, even under the plan's myelopathy exception, due to uncompleted physical therapy (by 1 week) and pharmacotherapy (only one agent used).

**B. California Regulatory Framework Review:**

*   **CHSC § 1374.31(b) (Denial Notice Adequacy):** The initial denial notice specified the reason (incomplete conservative treatment and specific shortfalls in PT, pharmacotherapy, and interventional procedures). It is considered procedurally adequate.
*   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This guidance allows waiver of full conservative treatment requirements when "objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia, or progressive herniation)" AND "treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression."
    *   **Critically, the MRI states "No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia."** This is a key exclusion for the specific urgency identified by DMHC for waiving conservative care. Furthermore, the clinical record notes "symptom duration: 6 months, stable," which contradicts Dr. Osei's declaration of "progressive" pathology and failure to arrest progression. Therefore, APL 22-014's specific waiver conditions are not met for this case.
*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This state law prohibits requiring PT completion where:
    *   **(a)(1) Physician documents PT contraindicated or unlikely to benefit:** Dr. Osei halted PT citing "myelopathy concern." Clinical standard knowledge (Cochrane, NASS, AO Spine) emphasizes that PT has "LIMITED efficacy" for established myelopathy and "does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement" for moderate-severe myelopathy. It explicitly states that "Neurological symptoms in myelopathy (weakness, hyperreflexia) are not expected to respond to PT regardless of duration." For these reasons, it is reasonable to conclude that further PT for the *myelopathy's neurological symptoms* is "unlikely to benefit."
    *   **Conclusion on PT:** Based on CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) and supporting clinical evidence, the plan cannot mandate completion of the remaining 1 week of physical therapy. This aspect of the denial is overridden by state law.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This states that IMR decisions are based on "generally accepted standards of medical practice" and "SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." This provision is the ultimate override if clinical standards clearly support surgery without further conservative treatment.

**C. Clinical Standard Knowledge Review (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

*   **Myelomalacia:** The absence of myelomalacia is a critical factor. Myelomalacia is a strong indicator for surgical urgency and a contraindication to extended conservative trials (AANS/CNS TIER 1). Its absence here means the highest level of surgical urgency is not present.
*   **Surgical Indications for ACDF (AANS/CNS TIERs):**
    *   **TIER 1 (Strong Indication):** This category includes moderate-severe myelopathy, progressive neurological deficit, myelomalacia, or acute severe myelopathy. Bernard Achebe has *mild* myelopathy (mJOA 15/18), *stable* symptoms for 6 months, and *no myelomalacia*. His objective findings do not meet TIER 1 criteria, despite Dr. Osei's declaration of severity and progression, which is not clinically supported by the records.
    *   **TIER 2 (Reasonable Indication):** This includes "Mild myelopathy with stable symptoms after adequate conservative trial." This is the relevant category for the member. The question then becomes whether an "adequate conservative trial" has been completed.
*   **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy:**
    *   Clinical guidelines state that for *mild cervical myelopathy*, "conservative treatment may be considered... as initial management, with close monitoring for progression" (NASS 2020).
    *   "No high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with *moderate to severe* cervical myelopathy" (AO Spine 2021). This is less relevant for *mild* myelopathy, where conservative care is still an option.
    *   While PT is of limited utility for myelopathy's neurological symptoms, the plan's requirement for *two* pharmacotherapy agents for mild myelopathy is consistent with an "adequate conservative trial" as permitted by NASS guidelines for mild, stable cases.

**D. Conflict Resolution and Determination:**

1.  **PT Requirement:** The plan's requirement for 4 weeks of PT (under its myelopathy exception) is overridden by California state law (CIC § 10169.5(a)(1)). Dr. Osei's documentation of "myelopathy concern" and the clinical evidence that PT is unlikely to benefit the neurological symptoms of myelopathy make further PT unwarranted.
2.  **Interventional Procedure Requirement:** The plan's myelopathy exception waives this if risk is documented. While Dr. Osei documents general urgency, specific risk from interventional procedures is not explicitly stated. However, given the limited utility of such procedures for primary myelopathy (cord compression), this is less of a barrier.
3.  **Pharmacotherapy Requirement:** The plan's myelopathy exception still requires a trial of *two* pharmacotherapy agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The member has only completed a trial of one agent (Baclofen) for 6 weeks.
    *   The member has *mild, stable myelopathy* without the high-risk feature of myelomalacia. In such cases, clinical guidelines (NASS TIER 2) state that surgery is a "reasonable option" *after an adequate conservative trial*. An adequate conservative trial for mild, stable myelopathy typically includes a trial of appropriate pharmacotherapy agents. Trying a second pharmacotherapy agent, as required by the plan, is a reasonable and generally accepted step in conservative management for a stable, mild condition.
    *   Dr. Osei's strong declaration of "immediate surgical intervention" and "severe and progressive" disease is not supported by the objective clinical evidence (mJOA 15/18, stable symptoms, no myelomalacia, no frank weakness).

Therefore, despite the PT requirement being overridden by state law, the member has not completed the full pharmacotherapy component of an "adequate conservative trial" for *mild, stable cervical myelopathy without myelomalacia*. This unmet requirement for pharmacotherapy, combined with the lack of objective indicators for urgent surgical intervention (e.g., myelomalacia, acute progression, moderate-severe functional deficit), means that authorization for surgery is not yet medically necessary under generally accepted clinical standards.

**5. DISPOSITION:**

**UPHELD.**

**6. WARRANT:**

The denial is upheld. While the HealthFirst plan's requirement for an additional week of physical therapy is overridden by California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(1) in conjunction with the treating physician's documentation of "myelopathy concern" and the clinical evidence indicating limited benefit of PT for myelopathy's neurological symptoms, the member has not fulfilled the pharmacotherapy requirement of HealthFirst's own myelopathy exception (Section 2.2(A)). The plan requires a trial of at least two pharmacotherapy agents, but the member has only tried one (Baclofen).

The member presents with mild cervical myelopathy (mJOA 15/18) with symptoms stable for 6 months. Crucially, imaging reveals no T2 signal change or myelomalacia. The absence of myelomalacia and objective progression, coupled with the mild and stable nature of the condition, indicates that the case does not meet the strong surgical indications (TIER 1) outlined in AANS/CNS guidelines. While cervical myelopathy warrants careful consideration, for *mild, stable* presentations without myelomalacia, an "adequate conservative trial" (AANS/CNS TIER 2) remains an appropriate initial management strategy, which includes an attempt at multiple pharmacotherapy agents. The treating physician's strong declaration of urgency is not fully supported by the objective clinical findings provided. Therefore, the conservative treatment remains incomplete under generally accepted standards for a mild, stable myelopathy, and thus, surgery is not yet medically necessary.

The member is encouraged to complete a trial of a second pharmacotherapy agent to meet the plan's criteria for an adequate conservative trial, after which a new prior authorization request may be submitted.
