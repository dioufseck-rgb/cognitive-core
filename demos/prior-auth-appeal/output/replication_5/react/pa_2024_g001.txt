### I. Case Summary and Original Denial Basis

Mr. Marco Reyes, a 49-year-old member under HealthFirst PPO Gold, requested prior authorization for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 for persistent right-sided cervical radiculopathy. Imaging revealed a large disc herniation with moderate right C7 nerve root compression, but explicitly noted no cord contact and no myelomalacia. Neurological exam findings were consistent with C7 radiculopathy, with no bilateral findings or upper motor neuron (UMN) signs.

The initial authorization request was denied by HealthFirst Utilization Management (Dr. Alan Foster, MD) on 2024-11-01, citing reason code MED-003. The denial stated that the member "does not meet conservative treatment requirements under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. Physical therapy: member has completed only 3 weeks of physical therapy. Standard pathway requires 6 weeks minimum. Functional plateau not documented. All other criteria remain unmet."

The member appealed, asserting that the denial miscounted the physical therapy (PT) duration (totaling 9 weeks across two providers), that PT records documenting a functional plateau were submitted with the original request, and that all other conservative treatment criteria were met.

### II. Determination of Primary Clinical Presentation

Based on the clinical record, the primary diagnosis is **Cervical Radiculopathy C6-C7**.
*   **Imaging:** MRI findings of C6-C7 large disc herniation with *moderate right C7 nerve root compression* are consistent with radiculopathy. Critically, the MRI explicitly states "No cord contact" and "No myelomalacia."
*   **Neurological Exam:** Findings include "Right C7 dermatomal numbness," "Tricep reflex diminished right," and "Grip strength 4/5 right," which are consistent with radicular symptoms. The exam explicitly states "No bilateral findings" and "No UMN signs."
*   **Domain Knowledge:** Cervical myelopathy is characterized by spinal cord compression (often with myelomalacia), bilateral symptoms, gait disturbance, and UMN signs. These are specifically absent in Mr. Reyes' presentation.

This distinction is crucial, as the myelopathy-specific exceptions in plan criteria and state regulations (DMHC APL 22-014, CIC 10169.5(a)(3)) regarding reduced conservative treatment requirements or waiver of PT for myelomalacia/cord compression **do not apply** to this case.

### III. Review Against Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)

The HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 policy (Source Document 1) requires the following conservative treatment for cervical radiculopathy (Section 2.2):
*   **2.2(a) Physical therapy:** Minimum 6 weeks with documented functional outcomes.
    *   **Denial Claim:** "member has completed only 3 weeks of physical therapy."
    *   **Clinical Record:** Mr. Reyes completed 3 weeks at Valley Physical Therapy (2024-06-01 to 2024-06-21) and 6 weeks at Capital Physical Therapy (2024-07-08 to 2024-08-16), totaling **9 weeks** of physical therapy. Both providers' records were submitted with the original PA request.
    *   **Denial Claim:** "Functional plateau not documented."
    *   **Clinical Record:** The Capital PT discharge summary explicitly states: "'Maximum therapeutic benefit achieved. No further improvement expected from continued PT.'" This directly meets the plan's definition of functional plateau ("therapist documents maximum benefit reached").
    *   **Finding:** The denial's factual claims regarding PT duration and functional plateau documentation are **incorrect**. Mr. Reyes exceeded the 6-week minimum and achieved a documented functional plateau.
*   **2.2(b) Pharmacotherapy:** Trial of at least two agents for minimum 4 weeks each.
    *   **Clinical Record:** Gabapentin 600mg TID for 4 months; Naproxen 500mg BID for 3 months.
    *   **Finding:** **Met.**
*   **2.2(c) Interventional treatments:** One completed.
    *   **Clinical Record:** Right C7 epidural steroid injection on 2024-09-15.
    *   **Finding:** **Met.**

**Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The initial denial was based on incorrect factual assertions regarding the member's completion of conservative treatment, specifically physical therapy duration and documentation of functional plateau. Based on the submitted clinical record, Mr. Reyes **did meet** all conservative treatment requirements of HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 for cervical radiculopathy.

### IV. Review Against California Regulatory Framework

*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite) (Source Document 2, Part III):** This state law prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where:
    *   **(a)(2):** "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
    *   **Application:** Mr. Reyes completed 9 weeks of PT, and the Capital PT discharge summary explicitly documented that "Maximum therapeutic benefit achieved. No further improvement expected from continued PT." This directly satisfies subdivision (a)(2).
    *   **Finding:** Per state law, the plan cannot deny based on PT duration as a functional plateau was reached.
*   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions) (Source Document 2, Part II):** This All-Plan Letter specifically addresses myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia, allowing for waivers of conservative treatment in such urgent cases.
    *   **Application:** As established in Section II, Mr. Reyes has cervical radiculopathy, not myelopathy, and his MRI explicitly shows "No cord contact. No myelomalacia."
    *   **Finding:** This APL, and its provisions for waiving conservative treatment, **do not apply** to Mr. Reyes' case.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard is NOT plan criteria) (Source Document 2, Part I):** This provision dictates that services meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice shall be approved regardless of more restrictive plan criteria.
    *   **Application:** This is a general principle, and we will cross-reference with clinical standards in the next section.
    *   **Finding:** While not directly triggered by a *conflict* with plan criteria in this specific instance (since the plan's criteria were *met* and misapplied), it serves as an overarching principle.

**Conclusion on Regulatory Requirements:** California state law (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)) directly supports overturning the denial, as the member reached a documented functional plateau after completing a course of physical therapy.

### V. Review Against Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)

*   **Diagnostic Classification (Source Document 3, Section 1.1):** The clinical record's findings are consistent with Cervical Radiculopathy, not Myelopathy. The guidelines emphasize the critical distinction, noting different treatment algorithms.
*   **Conservative Treatment Duration for Radiculopathy (Source Document 3, Section 4, Summary Table):** For radiculopathy, physical therapy duration is typically 6-12 weeks. Mr. Reyes completed 9 weeks, exceeding the lower end of this range.
*   **Surgical Indications (Source Document 3, Section 3.1):**
    *   TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION: "(b) Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx [treatment]."
    *   **Application:** Mr. Reyes has persistent right-sided C6-C7 radiculopathy after a documented 9-week course of physical therapy (with documented plateau), 4 months of neuropathic agents, 3 months of NSAIDs, and a C7 epidural injection which provided only temporary, partial relief. This clearly aligns with the clinical standard for a reasonable surgical indication for radiculopathy.
*   **Myelomalacia Significance (Source Document 3, Section 1.2):** Myelomalacia is a strong surgical indication.
    *   **Application:** Mr. Reyes' MRI explicitly states "No myelomalacia." Thus, this urgent indication for immediate surgery does not apply to his case.
*   **Functional Plateau (Source Document 3, Section 5):** The clinical standard defines functional plateau as "<10% improvement over 3 sessions OR therapist documentation that patient has reached maximum therapeutic benefit."
    *   **Application:** The Capital PT discharge summary ("Maximum therapeutic benefit achieved") clearly meets this clinical standard.

**Conclusion on Clinical Standard:** The clinical standard for cervical radiculopathy supports surgical intervention after a documented trial of 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment, particularly when a functional plateau has been reached and symptoms persist. Mr. Reyes' clinical presentation and failed conservative trial meet this standard.

### VI. Conflict Resolution (Hierarchy: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria)

1.  **Plan Criteria Application:** The denial was based on a fundamental factual error in assessing the conservative treatment completed. The member *did* complete 9 weeks of PT, exceeding the plan's 6-week minimum, and a functional plateau was explicitly documented, meeting HealthFirst's own criteria. Therefore, the plan's denial was based on a misapplication of its own policy due to an incorrect factual assessment.
2.  **State Law Override:** Even if there were ambiguity regarding the 6-week minimum, California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2) explicitly states that a plan cannot require PT completion where a member has completed a course of PT and reached a functional plateau. This state law takes precedence over any plan criteria that might be interpreted to deny based on PT duration after a plateau is reached.
3.  **Clinical Standard Alignment:** The clinical standard (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) supports surgery for radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks of failed conservative treatment and documented functional plateau, which Mr. Reyes clearly demonstrates.

There are no conflicts where a higher authority would justify the denial. Instead, the denial is contradicted by both the accurate application of the plan's own criteria and by California state law.

### VII. Disposition

The prior authorization denial for ACDF C6-C7 for Mr. Marco Reyes is **OVERTURNED**.

### VIII. Warrant

The initial denial by HealthFirst Utilization Management was based on factual inaccuracies regarding the duration of physical therapy completed and the documentation of a functional plateau. The clinical record clearly demonstrates that Mr. Reyes completed 9 weeks of physical therapy across two providers, exceeding the 6-week minimum required by HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a). Furthermore, the Capital Physical Therapy discharge summary explicitly documented that "Maximum therapeutic benefit achieved. No further improvement expected from continued PT," which satisfies both the plan's internal definition of a functional plateau and the clinical standard (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a), Clinical Reference Summary Section 5).

Additionally, California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2) prohibits health plans from conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy completion where a member has completed a course of therapy and reached a functional plateau. This state law takes precedence over any conflicting plan criteria.

Mr. Reyes also met all other conservative treatment requirements, including trials of two pharmacotherapy agents for adequate durations (Gabapentin for 4 months, Naproxen for 3 months) and completion of one interventional procedure (Right C7 epidural steroid injection), as required by HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b) and 2.2(c).

The member's persistent cervical radiculopathy with objective findings and failure of an extensive and documented conservative treatment trial aligns with Tier 2 surgical indications as per the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Section 3.1, supporting the medical necessity of the requested procedure.

Therefore, the denial was in error and contrary to the member's clinical record, HealthFirst's own criteria when accurately applied, and California state law.
