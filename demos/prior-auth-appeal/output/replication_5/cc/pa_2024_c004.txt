DETERMINATION: UPHOLD DENIAL

Dear Mr. Flores,

HealthFirst has completed its clinical appeal review for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of your clinical records, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, the decision to deny prior authorization for this procedure is UPHELD.

CLINICAL SUMMARY
You are a 54-year-old male employed as a construction worker operating heavy equipment. You present with axial neck pain, rated 5/10. 
- Imaging: A cervical spine MRI dated September 15, 2024, demonstrates severe central canal stenosis at C5-C6 with a canal diameter of 6 mm (normal is >13 mm). The MRI shows spinal cord contact without compression, no T2 signal change, and no evidence of myelomalacia.
- Neurological Examination: A physical and neurological examination performed on September 20, 2024, was completely normal. There was no evidence of radiculopathy, no signs of myelopathy, no upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, and no objective neurological deficits.
- Conservative Treatment: Your clinical record documents that you completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of physical therapy for axial neck pain, resulting in modest pain improvement (from 7/10 to 5/10). Pharmacotherapy included Naproxen 500mg BID for 3 months (moderate relief) and Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks (sleep improvement only). Additionally, a C5-C6 epidural steroid injection performed on October 10, 2024, provided 60% pain relief lasting 6 weeks.

PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS
Under the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery — Cervical Procedures (Document: CC-SPINE-2024), the requested procedure does not meet the requirements for medical necessity:
1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): This section requires a confirmed diagnosis of either (a) cervical radiculopathy with nerve root compression and radicular symptoms, (b) cervical myelopathy with spinal cord compression and myelopathic symptoms, or (c) cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit. Your clinical record documents a completely normal neurological examination with no radiculopathy, no myelopathy, and no objective neurological deficits.
2. Section 3.1 (Exclusions): This section explicitly states that authorization will be denied when "Surgery is requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy." Because your primary clinical complaint is axial neck pain and you lack any documented radiculopathy or myelopathy, the request is excluded from coverage.

REGULATORY ANALYSIS (CALIFORNIA LAW)
Under California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, the standard of review for medical necessity requires that a service be in accordance with "generally accepted standards of medical practice" and "clinically appropriate, in terms of type, frequency, extent, site, and duration."
- While your treating surgeon, Dr. Karen Davis, argues that prophylactic surgery is indicated due to the risk of catastrophic spinal cord injury from potential trauma in your high-risk occupation, generally accepted standards of medical practice do not support prophylactic surgical decompression for asymptomatic or axial-pain-only patients, even in the presence of severe canal stenosis.
- Furthermore, Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 addresses time-sensitive neurological conditions, specifically identifying cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI as a condition where conservative treatment may be waived. However, you do not have clinical myelopathy, and your MRI explicitly notes "no T2 signal change" and "no myelomalacia." Therefore, the urgent exceptions under APL 22-014 do not apply.
- California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 regarding physical therapy prerequisites is satisfied, as you completed 8 weeks of physical therapy. However, completing conservative treatment does not override the fundamental requirement for a qualifying clinical diagnosis under the plan's criteria and generally accepted medical standards.

CLINICAL EVIDENCE ANALYSIS
Generally accepted clinical guidelines do not support surgical intervention for your clinical presentation:
- The AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020) classify surgical indications into tiers. 
- Your presentation falls under Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated), which specifically includes "axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy" and "imaging findings without correlating symptoms." 
- Clinical evidence demonstrates that the risk of prophylactic surgery in an asymptomatic or axial-pain-only patient outweighs the potential benefits, as there are no neurological deficits to correct, and conservative management (including activity modification and clinical monitoring) is the standard of care.

RESPONSE TO APPEAL ARGUMENTS
The appeal emphasizes that you are a construction worker operating heavy equipment, placing you at an elevated risk for cervical trauma that could cause acute spinal cord injury given your 6 mm canal diameter. While the clinical concern raised by Dr. Davis is acknowledged, clinical evidence and regulatory standards do not support performing an invasive spinal fusion (ACDF) prophylactically based on occupational risk alone when no clinical neurological deficits are present. The standard clinical recommendation for this presentation is counseling on activity modification to reduce trauma risk, combined with close clinical monitoring for the development of any new neurological symptoms.

INDEPENDENT MEDICAL REVIEW (IMR) RIGHTS
If you disagree with this appeal decision, you have the right to request an Independent Medical Review (IMR) from the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) under California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.30.
- An IMR is an independent review conducted by medical professionals who are not affiliated with HealthFirst.
- You can apply for an IMR if the plan has denied, modified, or delayed a health care service based on a decision that the service is not medically necessary.
- To request an IMR, you may submit an application to the DMHC. The DMHC can be reached at 1-888-466-2219 or online at www.dmhc.ca.gov. There is no cost to you for an IMR.
