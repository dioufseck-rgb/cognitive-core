Dear Mr. Flores,

HealthFirst has completed its clinical review of the first-level appeal submitted on your behalf by Dr. Karen Davis, MD, regarding the prior authorization denial for a prophylactic anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551). Based on a comprehensive evaluation of your clinical records, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, the decision to deny prior authorization has been UPHELD.

I. CLINICAL SUMMARY
The clinical documentation submitted with your request and appeal has been thoroughly reviewed:
- Imaging: A cervical spine MRI dated September 15, 2024, demonstrates severe central canal stenosis at the C5-C6 level with a canal diameter of 6mm (normal is greater than 13mm). The radiologist noted spinal cord contact but explicitly documented "no compression," "no T2 signal change," and "no myelomalacia."
- Clinical Examination: A neurological examination performed on September 20, 2024, was completely normal. There was no evidence of cervical radiculopathy (no dermatomal pain, numbness, or weakness) and no signs of cervical myelopathy (no gait disturbance, no bilateral upper extremity weakness, no hyperreflexia, no positive Hoffman sign, and no upper motor neuron signs). Your primary clinical symptom is axial neck pain, rated at 5/10.
- Conservative Treatment: You have completed 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy (24 sessions) for axial pain, which resulted in modest pain improvement (from 7/10 to 5/10) but addressed no neurological symptoms. Your pharmacotherapy trial included Naproxen 500mg BID for 3 months (moderate pain relief) and Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks (sleep improvement only). You also received a C5-C6 epidural steroid injection on October 10, 2024, which provided 60% pain relief lasting 6 weeks.
- Surgeon's Assessment: Dr. Davis requested prophylactic decompression, noting that your critically narrow canal diameter (6mm) puts you at high risk for acute spinal cord injury from minor trauma, particularly given your occupation as a construction worker operating heavy equipment.

II. PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS
Your request was evaluated under the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria document: Spinal Surgery — Cervical Procedures (CC-SPINE-2024, Effective January 1, 2024).

1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1): To qualify for surgical authorization, a member must have a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (nerve root compression with dermatomal symptoms), cervical myelopathy (spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms), or cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit. Your clinical record dated September 20, 2024, explicitly documents a "completely normal neurological examination" with no radiculopathy, no myelopathy, and no upper motor neuron signs. Therefore, you do not meet the diagnostic requirements of Section 2.1.

2. Exclusions (Section 3.1): Section 3.1 explicitly states that authorization will be denied when "surgery is requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy." Because your primary clinical complaint is axial neck pain (5/10) and there is no documented radiculopathy or myelopathy, the requested procedure is excluded from coverage under Section 3.1.

III. REGULATORY AND CLINICAL STANDARDS ANALYSIS
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, the standard of review for medical necessity requires that the requested service be in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice and clinically appropriate for the patient's illness, injury, or disease. CHSC § 1374.32(b) specifies that the plan's internal criteria are not the sole or determinative basis if they are more restrictive than generally accepted standards.

To resolve this, we evaluated your clinical presentation against generally accepted clinical guidelines, including the AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine Guidelines for Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020):
- Tier 3 Classification (Surgery Not Indicated): Under Section 3.1 of the Clinical Reference Summary (derived from AANS/CNS 2023 and NASS 2020 guidelines), surgical intervention is classified as "Tier 3 — Surgery Not Indicated" for "axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy" (Tier 3(a)) and "imaging findings without correlating symptoms" (Tier 3(b)). Generally accepted standards of medical practice do not support prophylactic surgical decompression (ACDF) for severe cervical stenosis in patients who are completely asymptomatic neurologically and present only with axial neck pain.
- Risk-Benefit Ratio: Clinical evidence indicates that the risks of major spinal surgery (including CPT 22551) outweigh the theoretical benefit of preventing a future traumatic spinal cord injury in an asymptomatic patient, even one in a high-risk occupation. There is no high-quality clinical evidence or guideline supporting prophylactic ACDF based on occupational risk alone.

We also evaluated the Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter APL 22-014, which addresses prior authorization requirements for time-sensitive neurological conditions:
- Under APL 22-014 Part II.1, cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI is classified as an urgent condition where standard conservative treatment requirements may be waived. 
- However, your MRI dated September 15, 2024, explicitly noted "no T2 signal change" and "no myelomalacia," and your clinical exam showed no myelopathy. Therefore, the urgent exceptions under APL 22-014 do not apply to your case.

Because generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020) align with the plan's criteria in excluding surgery for axial pain without neurological deficits, there is no conflict between the plan's criteria and the clinical standard of care. The requested prophylactic surgery does not meet the standard for medical necessity under CHSC § 1374.32.

IV. CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS
Because you do not meet the diagnostic requirements of HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1, are subject to the exclusion in Section 3.1, and do not meet the clinical standard for medical necessity under CHSC § 1374.32 or generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020), the denial of prior authorization is UPHELD.

We recommend that you continue to be monitored closely by your physician for any developing neurological symptoms (such as numbness, tingling, weakness in your arms or legs, or changes in your gait). You should also discuss activity modifications with your physician to mitigate the risk of cervical trauma in your occupation.
