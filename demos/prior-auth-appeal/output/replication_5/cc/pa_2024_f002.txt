DISPOSITION: UPHOLD THE DENIAL OF PRIOR AUTHORIZATION

Dear Mr. Clark and Dr. Nguyen,

The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical documentation submitted, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, we have determined that the initial denial of prior authorization must be UPHELD.

While the documentation submitted on appeal successfully resolves the physical therapy discrepancy cited in the initial denial, the clinical record fails to satisfy the conservative pharmacotherapy requirements set forth in the plan's clinical coverage guidelines. Specifically, the patient has not completed a trial of at least two distinct classes of conservative pharmacotherapy as required by Section 2.2(b) of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024.

CLINICAL SUMMARY
Mr. Clark is a 46-year-old male with a primary diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy at C5-C6 with functional impairment. 
- Imaging: A cervical spine MRI dated August 15, 2024, demonstrates a large C5-C6 disc herniation with severe right C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis.
- Neurological Examination: An exam dated August 20, 2024, reveals objective neurological deficits, including right C6 dermatomal numbness, bicep weakness (graded 4/5 on the right), an absent right bicep reflex, and a positive right Spurling's sign.
- Conservative Treatment:
  1. Physical Therapy: The patient completed 6 weeks (18 sessions) of supervised physical therapy at Riverside Physical Therapy from April 1, 2024, to May 10, 2024, where a functional plateau was documented. He completed an additional 6 weeks (18 sessions) of physical therapy at Coastal Physical Therapy from June 1, 2024, to July 12, 2024, also reaching a functional plateau.
  2. Interventional Treatment: The patient underwent a right C6 cervical epidural steroid injection on August 28, 2024, which provided 60% temporary relief before symptoms returned to baseline after 3 weeks.
  3. Pharmacotherapy: The patient trialed Gabapentin 600mg TID for 5 months (resulting in partial relief) and Duloxetine 60mg daily for 3 months (resulting in partial neuropathic benefit).

PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 (Spinal Surgery — Cervical Procedures), prior authorization for cervical spinal surgery (CPT 22551) requires that all criteria in Section 2 be satisfied.

1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): MET. The patient has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with objective nerve root compression and correlating symptoms (pain, numbness, and bicep weakness in a right C6 dermatomal distribution), supported by an MRI within 6 months.
2. Section 2.2(a) (Physical Therapy): MET. The plan's initial denial on October 10, 2024, cited conflicting physical therapy documentation between Riverside PT and Coastal PT. However, Section 2.2(a) requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. The undisputed records from Riverside Physical Therapy independently document a complete 6-week course (18 sessions) from April 1, 2024, to May 10, 2024, with a documented functional plateau. Therefore, the physical therapy requirement was fully satisfied on the undisputed record alone, rendering the documentation dispute regarding Coastal Physical Therapy irrelevant to the clinical determination.
3. Section 2.2(b) (Pharmacotherapy): NOT MET. Section 2.2(b) requires a trial of at least two of the following bulleted drug classes for a minimum of 4 weeks each:
   - NSAIDs or COX-2 inhibitors
   - Muscle relaxants
   - Neuropathic agents (gabapentin, pregabalin, duloxetine)
   - Oral corticosteroids
   The clinical record documents that the patient trialed Gabapentin (5 months) and Duloxetine (3 months). Both Gabapentin and Duloxetine are explicitly grouped under the single bulleted class of "Neuropathic agents." The patient has not trialed an agent from a second distinct class (such as an NSAID/COX-2 inhibitor, a muscle relaxant, or an oral corticosteroid). Consequently, the patient has failed to complete a trial of at least two distinct classes of conservative pharmacotherapy.
4. Section 2.2(c) (Interventional Treatment): MET. The patient underwent a right C6 epidural steroid injection on August 28, 2024, satisfying the requirement for at least one interventional treatment.

Because the conservative pharmacotherapy requirement under Section 2.2(b) has not been met, the request does not satisfy the plan's clinical coverage criteria. Under Section 3.2 of CC-SPINE-2024, prior authorization must be denied when conservative treatment requirements have not been met.

CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK EVALUATION
We evaluated whether any California statutory provisions or regulatory mandates override the plan's pharmacotherapy criteria in this case:

1. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Myelopathy Exceptions): Under APL 22-014, health plans are prohibited from requiring a full course of conservative treatment for time-sensitive neurological conditions, specifically cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia. However, this regulatory waiver does not apply to Mr. Clark's case. The clinical record establishes that Mr. Clark's primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, and there is no clinical or imaging evidence of cervical myelopathy or spinal cord signal change (myelomalacia). Under Section 2.2 of CC-SPINE-2024 and APL 22-014, standard conservative treatment requirements remain fully applicable to radiculopathy presentations.
2. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite): CIC § 10169.5 prohibits plans from mechanically applying physical therapy duration requirements when a patient has documented a functional plateau. While this statute protects the patient's completed physical therapy course (which is deemed fully compliant due to the documented functional plateau at Riverside PT), it does not waive or modify the separate, non-discriminatory requirement for conservative pharmacotherapy trials.

CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARDS EVALUATION
Under generally accepted standards of medical practice, including the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020) and the joint guidelines of the American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS, 2023):
- Cervical radiculopathy is a condition that typically resolves with non-operative management in 70% to 80% of cases.
- Comprehensive conservative management is strongly recommended before proceeding to invasive surgical intervention such as an ACDF.
- A robust conservative trial includes multi-modal pharmacotherapy targeting different physiological pathways (e.g., combining a neuropathic agent with an anti-inflammatory NSAID or a muscle relaxant) to address both the inflammatory and neuropathic components of radicular pain.
- Because the patient has only trialed agents within a single class (neuropathic), a complete and structured trial of multi-modal pharmacotherapy has not been exhausted. Proceeding to surgery without a trial of a second distinct class of pharmacotherapy is inconsistent with both the plan's clinical criteria and conservative clinical standards for stable cervical radiculopathy.

CONCLUSION
The prior authorization request for a single-level ACDF at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is UPHELD. To meet clinical criteria for approval, the patient must complete a documented trial of at least one additional class of pharmacotherapy (such as an NSAID/COX-2 inhibitor, muscle relaxant, or oral corticosteroid) for a minimum of 4 weeks, unless clinically contraindicated by the treating physician.
