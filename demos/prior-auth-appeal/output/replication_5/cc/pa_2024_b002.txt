Dear Mr. Reed and Dr. Liu,

The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C6-C7 level (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has OVERTURNED the initial denial. The requested surgical procedure is APPROVED.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & CORRECTION OF FACTUAL ERROR IN INITIAL DENIAL

The initial prior authorization denial issued on October 20, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, cited two primary grounds for denial: (1) that physical therapy (PT) had not yet been completed to a functional plateau, and (2) that the member's imaging did not demonstrate measurable spinal cord compression or myelopathy findings.

Upon review of the complete clinical record, the plan finds that the initial denial was based on a clear factual error regarding the member's conservative treatment history. The physical therapy discharge summary explicitly documents that Mr. Reed completed 7 weeks (21 sessions) of supervised physical therapy and reached a documented "functional plateau," showing no clinical improvement over his final 3 sessions. 

Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2), a health insurer is strictly prohibited from conditioning the approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of further physical therapy once a member has completed a course of PT and the treating provider documents that the member has reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms. Because Mr. Reed completed 7 weeks of PT (exceeding the 6-week requirement in Section 2.2(a) of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024) and reached a documented functional plateau, the physical therapy requirement is fully satisfied. The initial denial's objection on these grounds is legally and factually invalid under CIC § 10169.5.

II. CLINICAL-IMAGING CORRELATION AND RESOLUTION OF DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA

The initial denial and subsequent peer reviews raised concerns regarding whether the member met the diagnostic criteria under Section 2.1(a) of Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, which requires "nerve root compression with radicular symptoms," and whether the case was excluded under Section 3.3, which excludes surgery when imaging does not demonstrate pathology consistent with the clinical presentation. Specifically, the member's cervical MRI (dated August 1, 2024) was interpreted as showing "C6-C7 disc herniation," "mild right C7 foraminal stenosis," and "mild cord contact without measurable compression," with no cord signal change or myelomalacia.

While the radiologist described the structural findings as "mild," a rigorous clinical-imaging correlation demonstrates a perfect anatomical match with the member's objective neurological deficits:
1. Neurological Exam Findings (August 5, 2024): Documented right C7 dermatomal distribution numbness, a diminished right tricep reflex, and severe 7/10 pain at rest.
2. Anatomical Correlation: The C7 nerve root exits the spinal column at the C6-C7 level and directly subserves the triceps reflex and the C7 dermatome. 
3. Resolution of Stenosis Severity: In clinical practice, "mild" radiographic foraminal stenosis can cause severe, debilitating nerve root impingement and objective neurological deficits depending on the patient's unique foraminal anatomy. The presence of objective, unilateral neurological deficits (diminished reflex and dermatomal numbness) matching the exact level of the C6-C7 disc herniation and right C7 foraminal stenosis provides definitive clinical evidence of active nerve root compromise.

Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, health plans must determine medical necessity in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice and clinical appropriateness. CHSC § 1374.32(b) explicitly mandates that a plan's internal coverage criteria shall not be the sole or determinative basis for a decision if the requested service meets generally accepted clinical standards, overriding any hyper-restrictive plan interpretations (such as requiring explicit "severe compression" terminology on an MRI report when a perfect clinical-imaging correlation exists).

According to the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020) and the American Association of Neurological Surgeons / Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Joint Guidelines (2023), surgical decompression via ACDF is highly indicated and clinically appropriate (Tier 2 "Reasonable Indication") for patients with persistent cervical radiculopathy, objective neurological deficits, and correlating structural pathology who have failed an exhaustive course of conservative management. Therefore, the clinical-imaging correlation is fully established, satisfying Section 2.1(a) and Section 2.3, and rendering the Section 3.3 exclusion inapplicable.

III. COMPLIANCE WITH CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENTS

The clinical record confirms that Mr. Reed has exhausted all conservative treatment pathways required under Section 2.2 of CC-SPINE-2024:
1. Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): Completed 7 weeks (21 sessions) of supervised cervical physical therapy with a documented functional plateau (PT discharge summary).
2. Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): Completed a 4-month trial of Pregabalin (150mg BID, a neuropathic agent) and a 3-month trial of Meloxicam (15mg daily, an NSAID), satisfying the requirement for two agents trialed for at least 4 weeks each. Both failed to provide adequate symptom resolution.
3. Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): Underwent a right C7 cervical epidural steroid injection on September 20, 2024, which provided only temporary (30%) relief for 3 weeks before symptoms returned to baseline.

IV. REGULATORY OVERRIDES AND GOVERNING AUTHORITY

Pursuant to California regulatory standards, the plan's internal clinical guidelines (CC-SPINE-2024) are subordinate to state law:
1. Physical Therapy Plateau Override (CIC § 10169.5): The plan cannot deny or delay surgical authorization based on a demand for further physical therapy when a functional plateau has been documented by the treating therapist.
2. Medical Necessity Standard Override (CHSC § 1374.32): The plan cannot apply internal criteria in a manner that is more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. Because Mr. Reed's clinical presentation of persistent C7 radiculopathy with correlating C6-C7 pathology and objective reflex/sensory deficits meets the clinical standard for ACDF, the procedure is medically necessary and must be approved.

V. AUTHORIZATION DETAILS

Based on the foregoing clinical and regulatory analysis, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED.
