FINAL DETERMINATION: OVERTURNED ON APPEAL

Dear Ms. Torres and Dr. Kim,

HealthFirst Health Plan’s Appeal Determination Services has completed its review of the appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical records, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California statutory and regulatory mandates, and generally accepted clinical practice guidelines, HealthFirst has OVERTURNED the initial prior authorization denial. 

The requested surgical procedure is APPROVED.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The initial prior authorization request was denied on November 1, 2024, citing a failure to meet the conservative treatment requirements under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria Document CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2. Specifically, the denial noted that the member completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy (instead of the standard 6 weeks or the modified 4 weeks under the myelopathy pathway), trialed only one pharmacotherapy agent, and did not undergo an interventional procedure.

On appeal, the clinical and regulatory evidence demonstrates that the plan's conservative treatment requirements are legally inapplicable and clinically contraindicated for this member. Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3), a health plan cannot condition surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy when the treating physician documents that physical therapy is contraindicated or when the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise. Furthermore, Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 prohibits plans from requiring conservative treatment for time-sensitive neurological conditions like cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia. Generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) establish that moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia is a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) surgical emergency where delaying surgery to perform physical therapy risks irreversible spinal cord injury. Therefore, the initial denial is overturned, and the procedure is authorized.

II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND HISTORY
According to the clinical records submitted:
1. Initial Presentation (August 10, 2024): Ms. Torres presented to Dr. James Kim with a 4-month history of progressive bilateral hand clumsiness and gait instability. 
2. Neurological Examination (August 10, 2024): Documented objective upper motor neuron signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia in both upper and lower extremities, positive bilateral Babinski signs, and gait ataxia.
3. Diagnostic Imaging (MRI, August 15, 2024): Demonstrated severe central cord compression at C5-C6 with T2 hyperintensity within the spinal cord, diagnostic of myelomalacia.
4. Conservative Treatment Trial:
   - Physical Therapy: Initiated at Valley PT on September 1, 2024. After 2 weeks (6 sessions), Dr. Kim issued a written order to discontinue therapy on September 14, 2024, because it caused severe symptom exacerbation. Dr. Kim documented that physical therapy was clinically contraindicated.
   - Pharmacotherapy: Trialed a 2-week course of a Methylprednisolone taper, which provided only temporary mild improvement with no sustained benefit.
   - Interventional Treatment: None performed.
5. Physician Declaration (October 15, 2024): Dr. Kim submitted a formal declaration stating that continuing physical therapy posed an imminent risk of irreversible spinal cord injury due to the documented myelomalacia. Dr. Kim declared physical therapy contraindicated, cited the statutory exception under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), and stated that surgical decompression was urgently indicated.

III. PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024:
- Section 2.1(b) requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy with supporting MRI evidence within 6 months. The member satisfies this criterion based on the MRI dated August 15, 2024, showing severe central cord compression and myelomalacia, and the neurological exam showing gait ataxia, hyperreflexia, and bilateral Hoffman signs.
- Section 2.2 requires a 6-week course of physical therapy, a trial of two pharmacotherapy agents for 4 weeks each, and one interventional treatment.
- Section 2.2(a) "Myelopathy Pathway Exception" modifies these requirements for primary myelopathy by reducing physical therapy to 4 weeks, waiving the interventional treatment requirement if documented as a risk, but keeping the pharmacotherapy requirement in full.
- Section 3.2 mandates denial if conservative treatment requirements are not met.

Because the member completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy and trialed only one pharmacotherapy agent (Methylprednisolone), the clinical record does not strictly satisfy the plan's internal criteria under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 or the modified Exception 2.2(a).

IV. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY AND STATUTORY ANALYSIS
While the member does not meet the plan's internal criteria, California law supersedes these criteria and renders them legally unenforceable in this case:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy Override:
   - CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) states that an insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member. Dr. Kim's written declaration dated October 15, 2024, explicitly documented that physical therapy was contraindicated.
   - CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) prohibits requiring physical therapy where the condition involves "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The member's MRI dated August 15, 2024, showing severe central cord compression and T2 hyperintensity (myelomalacia) constitutes objective, structural neurological compromise.
   - CIC § 10169.5(c) prohibits the mechanical application of minimum physical therapy duration requirements without reference to neurological urgency.

2. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 — Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions:
   - Section II.1 classifies cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI as a condition with "potential for irreversible harm if untreated" and requires plans to treat these cases as urgent.
   - Section II.2 states that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization" when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents urgency.
   - Section II.3 mandates that plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice.

3. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b) — Procedural Compliance:
   - The initial denial notice dated November 1, 2024, stated that the "Standard pathway requires minimum 6 weeks" of physical therapy. However, because the member had a primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, the plan's own criteria under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a) modified this requirement to 4 weeks. Citing the incorrect 6-week standard in the denial notice constitutes a procedural defect under CHSC § 1374.31(b).

V. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND GUIDELINES ANALYSIS
Generally accepted clinical standards strongly support immediate surgical intervention and reject conservative therapy for this clinical presentation:

1. AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023):
   - Classifies moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI as a Tier 1 — Strong Indication for surgical decompression.
   - States that conservative treatment is not indicated for patients with established upper motor neuron signs and cord signal changes.

2. AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):
   - States: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."

3. North American Spine Society (NASS) Guidelines (2020):
   - Recommends surgical decompression as the primary treatment for moderate-to-severe myelopathy. Physical therapy is only appropriate as an initial trial for mild, stable myelopathy. Ms. Torres's presentation (gait ataxia, bilateral hand clumsiness, and myelomalacia) represents moderate-to-severe, progressive disease.

4. Clinical Risk of Delay:
   - Preoperative myelomalacia (T2 signal change) is the strongest predictor of incomplete neurological recovery. Every month of delay in surgical decompression for moderate-to-severe myelopathy is associated with a decreased likelihood of neurological recovery and an increased risk of permanent, irreversible spinal cord damage.

VI. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS (HIERARCHY OF RULES)
In resolving conflicts between the plan's internal clinical criteria, state regulatory mandates, and clinical standards, HealthFirst applies the following hierarchy:
1. California Statutory and Regulatory Law (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) — Controlling Authority.
2. Generally Accepted Standards of Medical Practice (AANS/CNS, AO Spine, NASS Guidelines) — Governing Clinical Authority.
3. HealthFirst Internal Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) — Reference Authority.

Under this hierarchy, the plan's internal requirement for 4 to 6 weeks of physical therapy and multiple pharmacotherapy trials is legally superseded by CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014 due to the documented structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) and the treating physician's written contraindication. Clinically, applying the plan's conservative treatment criteria to a patient with progressive myelopathy and myelomalacia represents a category error (applying radiculopathy standards to myelopathy) that violates generally accepted standards of medical practice.

VII. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION
Based on the controlling statutory provisions of California Insurance Code § 10169.5, the regulatory mandates of DMHC APL 22-014, and the overwhelming clinical evidence supporting urgent decompression to prevent irreversible neurological deficit, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED.

The request for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is APPROVED.
