HealthFirst has completed its clinical and regulatory review of the first-level appeal submitted on November 15, 2024, regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). 

After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's internal coverage criteria, California statutory law, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has determined that the initial denial issued on November 1, 2024, must be OVERTURNED. The requested procedure is hereby authorized.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND HISTORY
According to the clinical records submitted with the appeal:
1. Presenting Diagnosis: The member is a 61-year-old female presenting with progressive cervical myelopathy at C5-C6 with myelomalacia (Clinical Record).
2. Clinical History: The member has a 4-month history of bilateral hand clumsiness and gait instability, with upper motor neuron signs documented at her initial presentation on August 10, 2024 (Clinical Record).
3. Neurological Examination: A physical exam on August 10, 2024, revealed objective neurological deficits, including bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia in both upper and lower extremities, bilateral positive Babinski signs, and documented gait ataxia (Clinical Record).
4. Objective Imaging: A cervical spine MRI performed on August 15, 2024, demonstrated severe central cord compression at C5-C6 and T2 hyperintensity within the spinal cord, confirming active myelomalacia (Clinical Record - Imaging).
5. Conservative Treatment History:
   - Physical Therapy: The member attempted physical therapy at Valley PT from September 1, 2024, to September 14, 2024 (completing 6 sessions over 2 weeks). This therapy was discontinued by written order of the treating physician because it caused severe symptom exacerbation (Clinical Record - Conservative Treatment).
   - Pharmacotherapy: The member completed a 2-week trial of a methylprednisolone taper, which provided only temporary mild improvement with no sustained benefit (Clinical Record - Pharmacotherapy).
6. Physician Declaration: In a formal letter dated October 15, 2024, Dr. James Kim, MD, declared that physical therapy is strictly contraindicated for this patient due to the presence of myelomalacia on the initial MRI. Dr. Kim noted that continuing physical therapy risks irreversible spinal cord injury and that surgical decompression is urgently indicated (Clinical Record - Physician Declaration).

II. INITIAL DENIAL RATIONALE AND PLAN CRITERIA
The initial prior authorization request was denied on November 1, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. The denial cited the following reasons:
1. The member completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy, whereas the standard pathway requires a minimum of 6 weeks (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a)).
2. The pharmacotherapy requirement was not met because only one agent (methylprednisolone) was trialed, whereas the criteria require a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b)).
3. The member did not meet the modified "Myelopathy Pathway" exception under Section 2.2(A), which reduces the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks but maintains the full pharmacotherapy requirement of two agents for 4 weeks each.

III. RESOLUTION OF CROSS-SOURCE CONFLICTS (HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY)
In determining this appeal, HealthFirst applies a strict hierarchy of authority: California State Law and Regulatory Mandates supersede generally accepted Clinical Standards, which in turn supersede the Plan's Internal Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024). 

While the member does not satisfy the literal conservative treatment requirements of CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 or Section 2.2(A), these plan requirements are legally unenforceable and clinically contraindicated in this case. The conflict is resolved as follows:

1. Statutory Override of Physical Therapy Requirements (California Insurance Code § 10169.5)
Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a), a health insurer is strictly prohibited from conditioning the approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy under the following circumstances:
- CIC § 10169.5(a)(1): When the member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition. Dr. Kim's written declaration dated October 15, 2024, explicitly states that physical therapy is contraindicated and poses a risk of irreversible spinal cord injury.
- CIC § 10169.5(a)(3): When the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. The member's MRI dated August 15, 2024, provides objective evidence of severe central cord compression and myelomalacia (structural neurological compromise).
- CIC § 10169.5(c): This section prohibits the mechanical application of a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy without reference to neurological urgency under subdivision (a)(3).
Consequently, the plan's 6-week (standard) and 4-week (modified myelopathy) physical therapy requirements are legally inapplicable to this member.

2. Regulatory Mandate on Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions (DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014)
The California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 establishes clear mandates for time-sensitive neurological conditions:
- APL 22-014 Section II.1: Cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated, and plans must treat such cases as urgent.
- APL 22-014 Section II.2: Plans shall not require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia), the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed or is contraindicated, and surgery is clinically indicated.
- APL 22-014 Section II.3: Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. Where a conflict exists, the clinical standard governs.
Applying the plan's conservative treatment criteria to deny this surgery directly violates APL 22-014.

3. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (Clinical Reference Summary)
Generally accepted clinical standards do not support requiring conservative treatment or physical therapy for patients with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia:
- AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Section 3.1: Surgical decompression (ACDF, CPT 22551) is a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) recommendation for patients presenting with moderate-to-severe myelopathy, objective cord compression, and myelomalacia on MRI.
- AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021) Section 2.1: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
- NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020): Surgical decompression is recommended as the primary treatment for moderate-to-severe myelopathy, as conservative treatment has limited efficacy and delaying surgery is associated with decreased likelihood of neurological recovery (Clinical Reference Summary Section 2.3).

IV. DETERMINATION
The clinical record demonstrates that Ms. Torres has severe, progressive cervical myelopathy with objective structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia and severe cord compression) and significant functional deficits (gait ataxia, hand clumsiness, hyperreflexia).

Under California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3), and DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014, the plan's physical therapy and conservative treatment requirements are legally superseded and cannot be applied to deny this request. Furthermore, generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine, NASS) establish that immediate surgical decompression is the medically necessary and appropriate standard of care, and that delaying surgery for conservative trials risks permanent, irreversible neurological damage.

Therefore, the prior authorization denial for ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED. The procedure is APPROVED.
