Dear Mr. Green and Dr. Kim,

The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the first-level appeal submitted on November 22, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial for a one-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). 

After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, we have determined that the original denial issued on November 8, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, must be OVERTURNED. The requested surgical procedure is approved.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The prior authorization request was originally denied under reason code MED-003, stating that the member did not meet plan criteria. However, our appeal review demonstrates that:
1. Clinically, the member fully satisfies all medical necessity criteria outlined in HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, including diagnostic, imaging, and conservative treatment requirements.
2. Procedurally, the original denial notice was legally defective under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31 because it failed to provide the referenced clinical criteria document and omitted the mandatory Independent Medical Review (IMR) notice.
3. Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5, the plan is prohibited from requiring further physical therapy as the member has already completed a 7-week course and reached a documented functional plateau.

II. CLINICAL COMPLIANCE WITH PLAN COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
A detailed review of the clinical record against HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 (Effective January 1, 2024) confirms full compliance with all authorization requirements:

1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1(a)):
The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits. 
- Clinical Evidence: Dr. Kim's neurological examination on September 25, 2024, documented left C6 dermatomal numbness, a diminished left bicep reflex, and left bicep weakness graded at 4/5. This represents a clear, objective dermatomal neurological deficit correlating with the C5-C6 level.

2. Imaging Requirement (Section 2.1 & 2.3):
- Clinical Evidence: An MRI of the cervical spine dated September 20, 2024 (within the required 6-month window), demonstrated a large disc herniation at C5-C6 causing moderate left C6 nerve root compression. This structural pathology directly correlates with the member's left-sided C6 radicular symptoms and objective neurological deficits.

3. Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2):
The member has completed a comprehensive, documented course of conservative therapy that meets and exceeds all plan requirements:
- Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): The criteria require a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. The clinical record documents that the member completed 7 weeks (total of 21 sessions) of supervised physical therapy. The physical therapy notes explicitly document that the member reached a functional plateau at week 6, with persistent radicular symptoms.
- Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): The criteria require a trial of at least two specified classes of medications for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The member trialed:
  - Gabapentin (Neuropathic agent) at 600mg TID for 4 months (16 weeks), which provided only partial relief.
  - Naproxen (NSAID) at 500mg BID for 3 months (12 weeks), which provided inadequate relief.
  Both trials far exceed the 4-week minimum duration.
- Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): The criteria require at least one interventional treatment. The member underwent a left C6 cervical epidural steroid injection on October 28, 2024, which provided 40% temporary relief before symptoms returned to baseline within 3 weeks.

4. Physician Documentation (Section 2.4):
The clinical record submitted by Dr. Sarah Kim, MD, contains the necessary clinical rationale, description of functional limitations, and documentation of failed conservative treatments, satisfying Section 2.4.

III. REGULATORY COMPLIANCE AND PROCEDURAL DEFECTS
Under California law, the original denial notice issued on November 8, 2024, contained critical procedural defects that independently invalidate the denial:

1. Failure to Provide Criteria and IMR Notice (CHSC § 1374.31):
- CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2) mandates that any medical necessity denial notice must include the specific clinical criteria applied and a notice of the member's Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights.
- The original denial notice referenced an attached criteria document, but no document was attached, and no IMR notice was included. Both the member and provider confirmed that no attachments were received.
- Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied is legally DEFECTIVE and must be treated as if no denial was issued for the purposes of appeal timelines.

2. Mechanical Application of Physical Therapy Requirements (CIC § 10169.5):
- CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) states that a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy if the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms.
- CIC § 10169.5(c) prohibits plans from mechanically applying a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy without reference to whether a functional plateau was achieved.
- Because the member completed 7 weeks of PT and reached a documented functional plateau at week 6, any denial based on a perceived failure to complete further physical therapy is a direct violation of California Insurance Code.

IV. ALIGNMENT WITH GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS
The requested ACDF at C5-C6 is highly aligned with generally accepted standards of medical practice:
- NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020) and AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) classify cervical radiculopathy with persistent symptoms and objective neurological deficits (such as bicep weakness and diminished reflexes) that have failed to respond to 6–12 weeks of conservative care as a Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) for surgical decompression.
- Delaying surgical intervention in a patient with objective, progressive motor weakness (4/5 bicep weakness) and a documented functional plateau in conservative therapy is clinically inappropriate and increases the risk of permanent neurological deficit.
- According to California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) Independent Medical Review (IMR) data from 2023, cervical spine denials are overturned at a rate of 72.3%, with the most common reason being that conservative treatment had already been completed (38% of overturns).

V. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION DETAILS
Because the member meets all clinical criteria under CC-SPINE-2024, and because the original denial notice was procedurally defective under California law (CHSC § 1374.31 and CIC § 10169.5), the prior authorization denial is hereby OVERTURNED.
