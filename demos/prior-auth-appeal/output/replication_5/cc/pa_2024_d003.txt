I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The Appeals and Grievances Division has completed its review of the appeal filed on behalf of Jessica Turner regarding the prior authorization denial for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551). The initial prior authorization request was denied on October 15, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, following a peer-to-peer discussion with the requesting physician, Dr. Robert Chen, MD, on 10/12/2024. The plan's written denial notice stated that clinical criteria were not met and that detailed criteria review was provided verbally during the peer-to-peer call. No written criteria or Independent Medical Review (IMR) notices were provided to the member.

Upon review of the clinical record, plan criteria, and California regulatory requirements, the Appeals and Grievances Division has determined that the initial denial is procedurally defective under California law. Specifically, California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31 requires that all medical necessity denials be issued in writing and include the specific clinical criteria applied, the specific clinical reasons for the denial, and a notice of IMR rights. Verbal communication during a peer-to-peer call cannot substitute for this statutory written notice requirement. 

Therefore, the prior authorization denial is REMANDED. HealthFirst Health Plan is ordered to immediately vacate the initial denial and reissue a compliant, written determination that fully details the specific clinical criteria applied, the specific clinical reasons for the decision, and includes the mandatory IMR notice, while also properly evaluating the treating physician's documentation of a functional plateau under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5.

II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION & RECORD REVIEW
The clinical record submitted for review contains the following clinical information:
1. Presenting Diagnosis: Cervical radiculopathy C6-C7 (Clinical Record, Presenting Diagnosis).
2. Objective Imaging: An MRI dated September 1, 2024, demonstrates a C6-C7 disc herniation, right C7 nerve root compression, and moderate foraminal stenosis (Clinical Record, Imaging).
3. Neurological Examination: A physical exam dated September 5, 2024, reveals right C7 dermatomal numbness and tingling, a diminished tricep reflex, and right hand grip strength of 4+/5 (Clinical Record, Neurological Exam).
4. Conservative Treatment History:
   - Physical Therapy: The member completed 5 weeks of supervised physical therapy (total of 15 sessions). While a functional plateau was not formally documented in the physical therapy records, the treating physician, Dr. Chen, explicitly declared in the appeal letter that the member reached a functional plateau (Clinical Record, Conservative Treatment).
   - Pharmacotherapy: The member trialed Meloxicam 15mg daily for 3 months, which provided partial relief, and a Prednisone taper for 2 weeks, which provided temporary improvement (Clinical Record, Pharmacotherapy).
   - Interventional Treatment: No interventional treatments (such as cervical epidural steroid injections, medial branch blocks, or trigger point injections) have been performed (Clinical Record, Interventional).

III. PLAN CLINICAL COVERAGE CRITERIA ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Health Plan Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 (Effective January 1, 2024), Section 2 outlines the medical necessity criteria for cervical spinal surgery (CPT 22551):
1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): The member satisfies this requirement as she has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with correlating MRI findings (C6-C7 disc herniation and right C7 nerve root compression) and dermatomal neurological deficits (C7 numbness, diminished tricep reflex, and 4+/5 grip strength).
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): The clinical record does not satisfy the standard conservative treatment requirements:
   - Section 2.2(a) (Physical Therapy): Requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. The member completed only 5 weeks.
   - Section 2.2(b) (Pharmacotherapy): Requires a trial of at least two specified agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. While the member trialed Meloxicam for 3 months (satisfying one agent), the Prednisone taper was only trialed for 2 weeks, failing the 4-week minimum duration for the second agent.
   - Section 2.2(c) (Interventional Treatment): Requires at least one interventional treatment (cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point injection series). The member has had no interventional treatments.
3. Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway): This exception does not apply because the member's primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, and there is no clinical or imaging evidence of cervical myelopathy (no spinal cord compression, no myelomalacia, and no upper motor neuron signs such as gait disturbance, hyperreflexia, or Hoffman sign).

IV. REGULATORY ANALYSIS & CALIFORNIA LAW COMPLIANCE
Under the governing hierarchy of authority, California state law and regulatory mandates supersede the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria. The initial denial of October 15, 2024, violates several key provisions of California law:

1. Defective Denial Notice (CHSC § 1374.31):
   - CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2) mandates that when a plan issues a denial based on medical necessity, the written denial notice must include "the specific criteria applied, the specific clinical reason for the denial, and notice of IMR rights."
   - CHSC § 1374.31(b) explicitly states: "A denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is DEFECTIVE and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."
   - The plan's written denial basis was "Verbal only — peer-to-peer call," and the written notice failed to cite any specific clinical criteria or clinical reasons, and failed to include the mandatory IMR notice. Verbal communication during a peer-to-peer call on 10/12/2024 does not satisfy the statutory requirement for written notice.

2. Mechanical Application of Physical Therapy Requirements (CIC § 10169.5):
   - CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy if "the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
   - CIC § 10169.5(c) states that a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy "shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau."
   - The plan's mechanical denial based on the member completing 5 weeks of physical therapy instead of 6 weeks, despite the treating physician's written declaration of a functional plateau in the appeal letter, violates CIC § 10169.5.

V. CLINICAL STANDARDS ANALYSIS
Generally accepted clinical standards, including the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Clinical Guidelines (2020), recommend a structured course of conservative management (typically 6 to 12 weeks of physical therapy and/or interventional injections) for cervical radiculopathy before surgical intervention is authorized, unless there is evidence of progressive neurological deficit or cervical myelopathy. 

The clinical record indicates that the member has a stable neurological deficit (grip strength 4+/5, diminished tricep reflex) and has not trialed interventional treatments or completed a full 6-week course of physical therapy. Therefore, from a purely clinical standpoint, the clinical record does not currently support immediate surgical authorization under generally accepted clinical standards.

VI. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS & JUSTIFICATION FOR REMAND
This case presents a conflict between substantive clinical criteria and statutory procedural requirements:
1. Substantive Clinical Shortfall: Under both the plan's criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) and generally accepted clinical standards, the member has not completed the necessary conservative treatment (shortfalls in physical therapy duration, pharmacotherapy duration, and lack of interventional treatment).
2. Procedural Statutory Defect: Under California law (CHSC § 1374.31), the plan's denial notice is legally defective because it failed to provide written criteria, written clinical reasons, and the mandatory IMR notice.

Applying the established hierarchy of authority (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria), the statutory requirements of California law are absolute and supersede clinical or plan-level arguments. A defective denial notice under CHSC § 1374.31(b) is treated as if no denial was issued, meaning the plan's initial decision cannot legally stand.

However, because the clinical record does not support immediate approval of the surgery due to the conservative treatment shortfalls, an outright reversal (OVERTURN) is not clinically justified. The appropriate legal and clinical remedy is a REMAND.

VII. ORDER OF REMAND & INSTRUCTIONS TO THE PLAN
HealthFirst Health Plan is hereby ordered to take the following actions within five (5) business days of this order:
1. Vacate the defective initial denial dated October 15, 2024.
2. Reissue a compliant, written initial determination to the member and the requesting physician, Dr. Robert Chen, MD.
3. The reissued written notice must:
   - Explicitly cite the specific clinical coverage criteria applied (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2).
   - Detail the specific clinical reasons for the decision (including the 5-week physical therapy duration, the 2-week prednisone duration, and the lack of interventional treatment).
   - Address and evaluate the treating physician's written declaration of a functional plateau in accordance with CIC § 10169.5, rather than mechanically applying the 6-week requirement.
   - Include the mandatory Independent Medical Review (IMR) application and notice of rights as required by CHSC § 1374.31(a).
