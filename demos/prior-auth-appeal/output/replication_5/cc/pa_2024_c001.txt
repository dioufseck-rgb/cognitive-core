HealthFirst Health Plan's Appeal Determination Services has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory statutes, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, the initial decision to deny coverage has been OVERTURNED. Prior authorization for CPT 22551 is hereby APPROVED.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND DIAGNOSIS OF CLINICAL MYELOPATHY
The member is a 58-year-old female presenting with progressive neurological symptoms. A neurological examination performed on September 5, 2024, by Dr. Robert Adams, MD, revealed multiple objective, bilateral upper motor neuron (UMN) signs:
1. Positive Hoffman sign bilaterally.
2. Hyperreflexia in the bilateral upper extremities.
3. Sustained clonus at the bilateral ankles (3 beats).
4. Positive finger escape sign.
5. Mildly wide-based gait.

A cervical spine MRI dated September 1, 2024, demonstrated a C5-C6 disc herniation with mild cord contact. The radiologist noted that while cord contact is present, it is not quantifiable as measurable compression on standard sequences, and there is no T2 signal change or myelomalacia.

The initial denial by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, on October 30, 2024, asserted that the plan's Myelopathy Pathway exception did not apply because the MRI showed "mild cord contact only" without measurable cord compression or cord signal change, and that UMN signs on exam are not conclusive for myelopathy in the absence of imaging-confirmed compression.

Under generally accepted standards of medical practice, specifically the Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine of the American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) 2023 Guidelines, cervical myelopathy is primarily a clinical diagnosis. It is established by the presence of objective UMN signs on physical examination (such as bilateral Hoffman, hyperreflexia, sustained clonus, and finger escape sign) with correlating imaging demonstrating structural pathology at the corresponding level (here, a C5-C6 disc herniation with mild cord contact). The clinical standard recognizes "imaging-negative" or "mild-contact" myelopathy as a distinct clinical entity where clinical signs of spinal cord dysfunction precede gross structural compression or signal changes on standard MRI sequences.

II. REGULATORY FRAMEWORK AND OVERRIDE OF RESTRICTIVE PLAN CRITERIA
The plan's initial denial relied on a strict interpretation of Section 2.1(b) of CC-SPINE-2024, which defines cervical myelopathy as "spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms," requiring measurable compression on imaging.

However, under California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(a), "medically necessary" services must be determined in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice. Furthermore, California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 Section II.3 explicitly prohibits health plans from applying internal coverage criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted clinical standards, the clinical standard must govern.

Because the plan's requirement for measurable cord compression or T2 signal change on MRI to confirm myelopathy is more restrictive than the AANS/CNS clinical guidelines—which establish myelopathy based on multiple positive UMN signs with correlating structural pathology—the clinical standard must override the plan's restrictive criteria. Accordingly, the member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy under the clinical standard, and the modified conservative treatment requirements of the Myelopathy Pathway (Section 2.2(A)) must be applied.

III. SATISFACTION OF THE PHYSICAL THERAPY REQUIREMENT
Under the plan's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway), the physical therapy requirement is modified and reduced from 6 weeks to a minimum of 4 weeks.

The clinical record demonstrates that the member completed 5 weeks of supervised physical therapy (totaling 15 sessions) directed at her cervical spine. The physical therapy summary notes that the member's neurological signs remained unchanged, and the treating physician halted further therapy due to concerns over myelopathic progression.

Because the member completed 5 weeks of physical therapy, she has fully satisfied the 4-week physical therapy requirement under the Myelopathy Pathway (Section 2.2(A)). Furthermore, under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(c), a plan's internal requirement for a minimum duration of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or whether the condition involves neurological urgency. The physical therapy records confirm the member reached a functional plateau with unchanged neurological signs, making further mechanical application of a longer PT trial legally and clinically impermissible.

IV. SATISFACTION OF THE PHARMACOTHERAPY REQUIREMENT
The plan's Myelopathy Pathway (Section 2.2(A)) states that the pharmacotherapy requirement of Section 2.2(b) "remains in full." Section 2.2(b) requires a trial of at least two qualifying agents (such as NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each.

The clinical record shows the member trialed:
1. Baclofen (muscle relaxant) 5mg TID for 2 months (minimal spasticity relief).
2. Methylprednisolone taper (oral corticosteroid) for 10 days (no improvement).

Strictly applied, the member did not complete a 4-week trial of a second agent, as the methylprednisolone taper was limited to 10 days. However, under CHSC § 1374.32(a)(2), a medically necessary service must be "clinically appropriate, in terms of type, frequency, extent, site, and duration."

Under generally accepted standards of medical practice, a 4-week continuous trial of oral corticosteroids is clinically inappropriate, highly unsafe, and contraindicated due to the severe risk of systemic side effects, including adrenal suppression, avascular necrosis (osteonecrosis) of the femoral head, severe immunosuppression, and metabolic derangement. A 10-day taper represents the standard, safe clinical duration for oral steroid administration in this clinical context.

Furthermore, because the member has established clinical myelopathy with progressive upper motor neuron signs (including sustained ankle clonus and a wide-based gait), requiring an additional 4-week trial of any other conservative pharmacotherapy is clinically contraindicated. According to the AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021) and the AANS/CNS Guidelines (2023), delaying surgical decompression in patients with active clinical myelopathy to complete prolonged conservative trials is not supported by clinical evidence and poses a severe, unacceptable risk of irreversible spinal cord injury and permanent neurological deficit.

Therefore, the member's 2-month trial of Baclofen combined with a clinically appropriate 10-day methylprednisolone taper satisfies the clinical standard of medical necessity under CHSC § 1374.32, and the pharmacotherapy requirement is deemed met.

V. CONCLUSION AND DISPOSITION
The member has established clinical cervical myelopathy at C5-C6 with multiple positive upper motor neuron signs. Under California law (CHSC § 1374.32 and DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3), the clinical standard of medical practice overrides the plan's restrictive imaging requirements. The member has completed 5 weeks of physical therapy, satisfying the 4-week requirement under the Myelopathy Pathway (Section 2.2(A)). The pharmacotherapy requirement is satisfied under clinical standards, as a 4-week oral steroid trial is clinically inappropriate and unsafe, and further conservative delays are contraindicated.

Accordingly, the prior authorization denial for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED. The procedure is APPROVED.
