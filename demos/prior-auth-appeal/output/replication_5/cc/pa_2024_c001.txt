I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The prior authorization denial for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED. 

We explicitly acknowledge that the member does not meet HealthFirst's internal clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) under either the standard pathway or the Myelopathy Pathway (Exception 2.2(A)). Specifically, the member completed 5 weeks of physical therapy instead of the standard 6 weeks, and failed to complete the mandatory 4-week trial of a second pharmacotherapy agent (the Methylprednisolone trial was only 10 days, failing the 4-week requirement under Section 2.2(b), which remains in full force under Exception 2.2(A)). Furthermore, we acknowledge that California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 does not mandate a waiver of conservative treatment in this case, as the member's MRI report dated September 1, 2024, explicitly states "no measurable cord compression, no T2 signal change, no myelomalacia," thereby lacking the specific high-risk imaging findings (such as myelomalacia or progressive cord signal change) required to trigger the regulatory waiver under APL 22-014 Section II.1 and II.2.

However, under California law, the plan's internal criteria are superseded by statutory mandates. This overturn is based solely on the statutory overrides provided by California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) (regarding physical therapy requirements in cases of structural neurological compromise) and California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 (requiring medical necessity determinations to align with generally accepted standards of medical practice).

II. CLINICAL EVIDENCE & FINDINGS OF RECORD
The clinical record contains the following objective findings:
1. Imaging (MRI dated September 1, 2024): Demonstrates a C5-C6 disc herniation with mild cord contact. The report notes "no measurable cord compression, no T2 signal change, no myelomalacia," and adds that "cord contact is present but not quantifiable as compression on standard sequences."
2. Neurological Examination (dated September 5, 2024): Documented by Dr. Robert Adams, MD, demonstrating five objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs:
   - Positive bilateral Hoffman sign
   - Bilateral upper extremity hyperreflexia
   - Sustained clonus at bilateral ankles (3 beats)
   - Positive finger escape sign
   - Mildly wide-based gait
3. Conservative Treatment History:
   - Physical Therapy: Completed 5 weeks (15 sessions) under a myelopathy protocol. The physical therapy was halted by the treating physician due to safety concerns regarding the member's myelopathic presentation.
   - Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 2-month trial of Baclofen 5mg TID (muscle relaxant) with minimal spasticity relief. Completed a 10-day trial of a Methylprednisolone taper (oral corticosteroid) with no improvement in UMN signs.

III. REGULATORY ANALYSIS & STATUTORY OVERRIDES

1. Physical Therapy Requirement Override under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)
Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where:
"(3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(c) mandates that "A plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or whether the condition involves neurological urgency under subdivision (a)(3)."

In this case, the member's clinical record contains clear, objective evidence of structural neurological compromise: a C5-C6 disc herniation with documented "mild cord contact" on MRI (dated September 1, 2024) that correlates directly with multiple objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs on physical examination (dated September 5, 2024), including bilateral Hoffman's sign, bilateral upper extremity hyperreflexia, sustained ankle clonus, and a positive finger escape sign. Physical therapy is a mechanical modality that cannot address, reverse, or relieve this structural mechanical contact on the spinal cord. 

Therefore, the plan's mechanical application of the 6-week physical therapy requirement (denying the request because the member completed 5 weeks instead of 6) is a direct violation of CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) and (c). The physical therapy requirement is legally waived.

2. Medical Necessity Standard and Pharmacotherapy Override under CHSC § 1374.32
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(a), "medically necessary" means health care services that a physician or other provider, exercising prudent clinical judgment, would provide to a patient for the purpose of preventing, evaluating, diagnosing, or treating an illness, injury, disease, or its symptoms, and that are:
"(1) In accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice; [and]
(2) Clinically appropriate, in terms of type, frequency, extent, site, and duration, and considered effective for the patient's illness, injury, or disease..."

Crucially, CHSC § 1374.32(b) states: "The IMR standard does NOT require that the service be consistent with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the service meets the standard in subdivision (a), the reviewer SHALL recommend approval regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." Under CHSC § 1374.32(c), the plan's internal medical necessity criteria may be used as a reference but "SHALL NOT be the sole or determinative basis" for a decision.

Under generally accepted standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines for Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy 2023; NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines 2020; AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guideline 2021):
- A clinical diagnosis of cervical myelopathy is established by the presence of multiple objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs on physical examination (such as bilateral Hoffman's, hyperreflexia, clonus, finger escape, and gait disturbance) with correlating structural pathology (such as C5-C6 disc herniation with mild cord contact). Imaging-negative or mild-contact myelopathy is a recognized clinical entity where clinical signs precede advanced MRI changes (such as myelomalacia or T2 signal change).
- These clinical standards explicitly state that there is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy or extended pharmacotherapy (such as a 4-week steroid trial) prior to surgical decompression for patients with cervical myelopathy and objective neurological deficits.
- Delaying surgery to complete an arbitrary 4-week trial of a second pharmacotherapy agent (such as oral steroids, which are not indicated for long-term use in myelopathy and carry significant risks) is clinically inappropriate, is not supported by evidence, and poses a risk of irreversible neurological deterioration.
- The AO Spine Guidelines (2021) state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."

Because the plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b)) require a 4-week trial of at least two pharmacotherapy agents, and because the plan's internal Myelopathy Pathway (Exception 2.2(A)) does not waive this requirement, the plan's criteria are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. Under CHSC § 1374.32, generally accepted standards of medical practice govern, and the plan's restrictive pharmacotherapy requirements are legally overridden.

IV. CONCLUSION
The requested Anterior Cervical Decompression and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is medically necessary under California law and generally accepted clinical standards. The member has objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (C5-C6 disc herniation with mild cord contact and multiple positive UMN signs) that physical therapy cannot address, triggering a statutory waiver of the physical therapy requirement under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3). Furthermore, the plan's internal pharmacotherapy requirements are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice and are overridden under CHSC § 1374.32.

The prior authorization denial is hereby OVERTURNED, and the procedure is APPROVED.
