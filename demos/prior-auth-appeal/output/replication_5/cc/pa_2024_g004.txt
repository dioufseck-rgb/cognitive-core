FINAL APPEAL DETERMINATION: UPHOLD DENIAL

Dear Ms. Oduya and Dr. Walsh:

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its formal first-level internal appeal review of the prior authorization request for a one-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT code 22551) at UCLA Medical Center. After a comprehensive review of the clinical documentation, relevant state regulations, plan coverage criteria, and national clinical guidelines, HealthFirst has determined that the initial denial of coverage must be UPHELD. 

This determination is based on the fact that the clinical presentation does not meet the medical necessity requirements for surgical intervention under either the plan's clinical coverage criteria or generally accepted standards of medical practice. Below is a detailed explanation of the clinical and regulatory basis for this decision.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND CLASSIFICATION

Based on the clinical records submitted, Ms. Oduya's primary diagnosis is right-sided C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy. 
1. Clinical Examination (September 15, 2024): Documents right shoulder and neck pain (6/10) and mild right C6 dermatomal tingling. Crucially, the neurological examination reveals no objective deficits: bilateral symmetric bicep reflexes (2+), normal grip strength (5/5) bilaterally, no motor weakness, and no upper motor neuron (UMN) signs (such as Hoffman's sign, clonus, or gait disturbance). The functional assessment notes that the member is working full-time with no activity limitations.
2. Objective Imaging (MRI dated September 10, 2024): Demonstrates a C5-C6 mild-to-moderate disc herniation with right C6 nerve root contact, but specifically notes "without measurable compression." There is no spinal cord contact, no spinal cord compression, no T2 signal change, and no myelomalacia. Mild right foraminal narrowing is present.

Based on these findings, the clinical presentation is classified as primary cervical radiculopathy without myelopathy. There is no evidence of cervical myelopathy (which would require spinal cord compression, myelomalacia, and corresponding clinical symptoms such as hyperreflexia, gait disturbance, or bilateral weakness).

II. REGULATORY COMPLIANCE: CALIFORNIA INSURANCE CODE § 10169.5

Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(c), a health plan is prohibited from mechanically applying a minimum duration of physical therapy (PT) without evaluating whether the patient meets specific statutory exceptions. HealthFirst has conducted an individualized clinical assessment of Ms. Oduya's record against the statutory exceptions outlined in CIC § 10169.5(a) and determined that no exceptions are met:

1. Contraindication or Lack of Benefit (§ 10169.5(a)(1)): There is no documentation from the treating physician or physical therapist stating that physical therapy is clinically contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member. To the contrary, the physical therapy summary notes "mild improvement in pain" over the course of the 12 sessions completed.
2. Functional Plateau (§ 10169.5(a)(2)): Under generally accepted clinical standards, a "functional plateau" is defined as a failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement (less than 10% improvement over three consecutive sessions) or therapist documentation of maximum therapeutic benefit. Ms. Oduya completed 4 weeks (12 sessions) of PT. The physical therapy records do not document a clinical plateau; rather, the records indicate that the treating surgeon, Dr. Walsh, elected to halt PT prematurely to seek surgical authorization. A voluntary, physician-directed cessation of therapy prior to the completion of an adequate trial does not constitute a documented functional plateau under § 10169.5(b).
3. Structural Neurological Compromise (§ 10169.5(a)(3)): This exception applies when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address, or where there is neurological urgency. Ms. Oduya's MRI documents right C6 nerve root "contact" without measurable compression, and explicitly notes no spinal cord compression or cord contact. Furthermore, her neurological examination is entirely normal, with no motor weakness, normal reflexes, and full functional capacity. There is no objective structural neurological compromise or progressive neurological deficit that would render physical therapy futile or unsafe.

Because none of the statutory exceptions under CIC § 10169.5(a) are satisfied, the requirement for a complete course of conservative physical therapy remains clinically and legally appropriate and is not being applied mechanically.

III. REGULATORY COMPLIANCE: CALIFORNIA HEALTH & SAFETY CODE § 1374.32 & DMHC APL 22-014

California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 prohibit health plans from utilizing internal coverage criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. Where a conflict exists, the broader clinical standard must govern.

1. Evaluation of Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) vs. Clinical Standard:
HealthFirst's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2) require a minimum of 6 weeks of physical therapy, a trial of two separate pharmacotherapy agents for 4 weeks each, and at least one interventional treatment (such as an epidural steroid injection). 

Generally accepted standards of medical practice, specifically the American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) 2023 Guidelines, state that surgical intervention (ACDF) for cervical radiculopathy is a reasonable option (Tier 2) only for patients with "persistent symptoms after 6 to 12 weeks of conservative treatment." The clinical standard defines conservative treatment as physical therapy and oral medications, and does not mandate invasive interventional treatments (such as epidural injections) or multiple concurrent pharmacotherapy agents as absolute prerequisites for surgery.

2. Resolution of Conflict and Waiver of Restrictive Criteria:
In strict compliance with CHSC § 1374.32 and DMHC APL 22-014, HealthFirst acknowledges that the plan's internal requirements for an invasive interventional treatment (epidural steroid injection) and a second pharmacotherapy agent are more restrictive than generally accepted standards. Therefore, HealthFirst hereby WAIVES the following requirements for Ms. Oduya's request:
- The requirement for an interventional treatment (Section 2.2(c))
- The requirement for a second pharmacotherapy agent (Section 2.2(b))

3. Application of the Governing Clinical Standard:
With the restrictive criteria waived, the request is evaluated solely under the governing clinical standard (AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines), which requires a minimum of 6 to 12 weeks of conservative treatment (including physical therapy) for radiculopathy prior to surgical consideration. 

Ms. Oduya's conservative treatment history consists of:
- Pharmacotherapy: 8 weeks of Naproxen 500mg BID (completed, with moderate pain relief).
- Physical Therapy: 4 weeks (12 sessions) of supervised physical therapy.

Because Ms. Oduya has completed only 4 weeks of physical therapy, she has not met the minimum 6-week conservative treatment threshold required under generally accepted standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS Guidelines). Consequently, the requested surgical intervention is premature and does not meet the standard of medical necessity at this time.

IV. RESPONSE TO THE TREATING SURGEON'S STATEMENT

Dr. Walsh's extensive experience of 25 years and his direct physical examination of the patient are fully acknowledged and respected. However, under both plan criteria and independent clinical guidelines, professional credentials and subjective clinical assertions cannot override the objective clinical evidence documented in the medical record. 

Dr. Walsh's assertion that the patient faces permanent disability without immediate surgery is not supported by the objective clinical findings. The MRI shows only mild-to-moderate herniation with nerve root contact and no spinal cord compression. The neurological exam confirms that Ms. Oduya has no motor weakness, normal reflexes, and full functional capacity, allowing her to work full-time. There is no clinical evidence of progressive neurological decline or structural urgency. Under these clinical circumstances, completing the remaining 2 weeks of conservative physical therapy is the safest and most appropriate clinical pathway, and is highly supported by clinical evidence showing a 70-80% resolution rate for radiculopathy with conservative care.

CONCLUSION

Because the member has not completed the minimum 6 weeks of conservative physical therapy required under generally accepted standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS Guidelines), and because no statutory exceptions under CIC § 10169.5 apply to waive this requirement, the prior authorization request for ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is UPHELD as not medically necessary at this time.

To proceed, it is recommended that Ms. Oduya complete the remaining 2 weeks of supervised physical therapy. If her symptoms remain persistent and unresolved after a full 6-week course, a resubmission of the surgical request with updated physical therapy clinical notes and outcomes will be promptly reviewed.
