HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its clinical and regulatory review of the appeal submitted on December 10, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial for a three-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C3-C4, C4-C5, and C5-C6 (CPT 22551 x3). 

Based on a comprehensive review of the clinical record, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has issued a PARTIAL OVERTURN of the initial denial. The level-by-level determinations are as follows:

1. C4-C5 Level: OVERTURNED (Approved for surgery)
2. C5-C6 Level: OVERTURNED (Approved for surgery)
3. C3-C4 Level: UPHELD (Denied for surgery)

A detailed clinical and regulatory rationale for each level is provided below.

---

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION SUMMARY
The member is a 53-year-old female presenting with multi-level cervical spondylosis with myeloradiculopathy. 
- Cervical MRI (dated October 1, 2024) demonstrates:
  - C3-C4: Mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing.
  - C4-C5: Large disc herniation, severe spinal cord compression, and T2 signal change within the spinal cord (myelomalacia).
  - C5-C6: Moderate disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression, and foraminal stenosis.
- Neurological Examination (dated October 5, 2024) reveals:
  - Myelopathic findings: Bilateral hyperreflexia, bilateral Hoffman sign, and a wide-based gait.
  - C5 level: Bilateral deltoid weakness (4/5).
  - C6 level: Left bicep weakness (4-/5) and diminished left bicep reflex.
  - C4 level: Unclear clinical correlation; neck pain is present with C3-C4 palpation, but there is no specific radicular pattern.
- Conservative Treatment History:
  - Physical Therapy (PT): Completed 9 weeks (27 sessions). The physical therapist documented that myelopathic symptoms remained stable but unimproved, C5-C6 radicular symptoms persisted, and the C3-C4 contribution remained unclear.
  - Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 3-month trial of Baclofen 10mg TID (minimal spasticity relief) and a 4-month trial of Pregabalin 300mg BID (partial pain relief).
  - Interventional: Cervical epidural steroid injection on October 20, 2024, resulting in modest improvement in radicular symptoms but no change in myelopathic signs.

---

II. LEVEL-BY-LEVEL CLINICAL AND REGULATORY ANALYSIS

1. LEVEL C4-C5: OVERTURNED (APPROVED)
- Plan Criteria Alignment: The clinical record satisfies HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(b) (Cervical Myelopathy), as the member exhibits objective spinal cord compression on MRI and multiple myelopathic symptoms (gait disturbance, hyperreflexia, bilateral Hoffman sign). Under Section 2.2 Exception (Myelopathy Pathway), the conservative treatment requirement is modified, reducing the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks (the member completed 9 weeks) and waiving the interventional treatment requirement if documented as a risk (though the member completed an epidural regardless).
- Regulatory Requirements: Under California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, Section II.1, cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm. Section II.2 mandates that plans shall not require a full course of conservative treatment when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia) and the clinical standard indicates surgery. Furthermore, California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) prohibits conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy where there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address.
- Clinical Standards: Under the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines for Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023) and AO Spine International Guidelines (2021), the presence of moderate-to-severe myelopathy with myelomalacia (T2 signal change) is a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) for surgical decompression. Delaying surgery in this setting is associated with permanent neurological deficits.
- Conclusion: The denial of the C4-C5 level is overturned. Surgical intervention is approved.

2. LEVEL C5-C6: OVERTURNED (APPROVED)
- Plan Criteria Alignment: The clinical record satisfies CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(a) (Cervical Radiculopathy) and Section 2.1(c) (Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit). The member has a moderate disc herniation at C5-C6 with moderate left C6 nerve root compression, corresponding to left bicep weakness (4-/5) and a diminished left bicep reflex. The member fully completed the conservative treatment requirements under Section 2.2: 9 weeks of PT (exceeding the 6-week requirement in 2.2(a)), two neuropathic/muscle relaxant medications for over 4 weeks each (exceeding 2.2(b)), and a cervical epidural steroid injection on October 20, 2024 (satisfying 2.2(c)).
- Regulatory Requirements: Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), a health plan cannot condition surgical approval on physical therapy where the member has completed a course of physical therapy and reached a functional plateau. The physical therapy summary explicitly notes that after 9 weeks, C5-C6 radicular symptoms persisted and a functional plateau was reached.
- Clinical Standards: Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) and NASS Guidelines (2020), surgical decompression is a Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) for radiculopathy with persistent symptoms and objective neurological deficits (weakness and reflex changes) that have failed to respond to 6-12 weeks of conservative care.
- Conclusion: The initial denial stated that C5-C6 did not demonstrate pathology of sufficient severity. This finding is contradicted by the objective MRI and neurological exam. Because the member met all plan criteria and clinical standards, the denial of the C5-C6 level is overturned. Surgical intervention is approved.

3. LEVEL C3-C4: UPHELD (DENIED)
- Plan Criteria Alignment: Under CC-SPINE-2024, Section 3.1, surgical authorization is excluded when requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy. Section 3.3 further excludes coverage when imaging does not demonstrate pathology consistent with the clinical presentation. The MRI shows only a mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing at C3-C4. The neurological exam notes that the clinical contribution of this level is "unclear," with neck pain on palpation but no specific radicular pattern. The physical therapy records also state that the C3-C4 contribution is unclear.
- Regulatory Requirements: There are no regulatory overrides or provisions under California law (including CIC § 10169.5 or DMHC APL 22-014) that mandate coverage for a surgical level lacking objective pathology or correlating clinical deficits.
- Clinical Standards: Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Tier 3, surgical intervention is not indicated for axial neck pain in the absence of correlating radiculopathy or myelopathy, or where imaging findings do not correlate with symptoms.
- Conclusion: Because there is insufficient clinical or radiographic evidence to support surgical intervention at C3-C4, the denial of this level is upheld.

III. PROCEDURAL DEFECT IN INITIAL DENIAL NOTICE
Pursuant to California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), a denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the denial. The initial denial notice dated November 25, 2024, issued by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, failed to provide a level-by-level clinical analysis or specify the criteria used to deny the C5-C6 level, which fully met the plan's medical necessity criteria. Under California law, a denial notice that fails to specify these elements is procedurally defective. HealthFirst has corrected this defect through this comprehensive appeal determination.

IV. INDEPENDENT MEDICAL REVIEW (IMR) RIGHTS (FOR UPHELD LEVEL C3-C4)
If you disagree with the decision to uphold the denial of the C3-C4 level, you have the right to request an Independent Medical Review (IMR) from the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC). 

Under California Health & Safety Code § 1374.30, you may submit an application for an IMR if your request for a health care service has been denied, modified, or delayed by the plan on the basis of medical necessity. 
- There is no cost to you for an IMR.
- The DMHC will make a decision within 30 days of receiving your completed application and supporting documentation (or within 3 days for urgent cases involving an imminent threat to your health).
- If the IMR decision is in your favor, HealthFirst is legally bound to accept the decision and authorize the service.

To apply for an IMR, please complete the Independent Medical Review Application Form enclosed with this letter or apply online at the DMHC website (www.dmhc.ca.gov). You may also contact the DMHC Help Center toll-free at 1-888-466-2219.
