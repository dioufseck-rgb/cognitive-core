SECTION 2 — PROCEDURAL CORRECTION REGARDING DISPOSITION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
An initial administrative review of this appeal proposed a "Remand" of the case to the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) for an Independent Medical Review (IMR) under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.30. This proposed action has been rejected as legally and procedurally impossible.

Under CHSC § 1367.01 and California Code of Regulations (CCR) Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1, a health plan's internal appeals department must issue a definitive, final decision (either Uphold or Overturn) within 30 days of receiving a standard appeal. A health plan has no legal authority to "remand" its own internal appeal to the DMHC or to initiate an IMR on its own behalf; the right to apply for an IMR belongs solely and exclusively to the enrollee (CHSC § 1374.30(a)). Furthermore, if a plan fails to issue a definitive decision within the statutory 30-day timeline, the timeline is breached, resulting in an automatic "Deemed Approval" of the requested service under Part IV.3 of the California Regulatory Framework. 

Accordingly, HealthFirst must and does issue this definitive Overturn of the initial denial, resolving the appeal in full compliance with California administrative timelines.

SECTION 3 — SATISFACTION OF PLAN'S INTERNAL CLINICAL COVERAGE CRITERIA
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
A clinical re-evaluation of the medical record demonstrates that the patient fully satisfies the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery (Document: CC-SPINE-2024), specifically under the Myelopathy Pathway Exception 2.2(A).

1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1(b)): 
The patient has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy. The clinical record dated October 05, 2024, documents objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia of the upper extremities, and a subtle balance impairment on tandem gait. 

2. Imaging Requirement (Section 2.3):
While the plan's initial reviewer (Dr. Alan Foster, MD) disputed the presence of myelomalacia, a second read of the October 01, 2024 MRI performed on October 15, 2024, by Radiologist B at the treating hospital (UCSF Medical Center) confirmed C4-C5 cord compression with early myelomalacia. Radiologist B documented that the T2 hyperintensity is reproducible on multiple sequences and represents early cord signal change rather than an artifact. Under Section 2.3, imaging must be interpreted by a "radiologist or treating specialist." The interpretation by the treating hospital's radiologist, combined with the treating neurosurgeon's clinical findings, satisfies the diagnostic and imaging requirements.

3. Satisfaction of Exception 2.2(A) — Myelopathy Pathway:
Under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, the standard conservative treatment pathway requires 6 weeks of physical therapy, 4 weeks of pharmacotherapy, and at least one interventional procedure (such as an epidural steroid injection). However, because the patient has a primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, Exception 2.2(A) applies, modifying these requirements as follows:
  - Physical Therapy: Reduced to a 4-week minimum. The patient completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy, exceeding this requirement.
  - Pharmacotherapy: Remains in full. The patient completed 3 months of Gabapentin (400mg TID) and 6 weeks of Cyclobenzaprine, satisfying the requirement for a trial of two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
  - Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): This requirement is explicitly WAIVED under Exception 2.2(A) if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration. Dr. James Park, MD, explicitly documented that he did not refer the patient for an epidural steroid injection because such injections are relatively contraindicated in the presence of severe cord compression and pose a direct risk of acute neurological decline. 

Because the patient met the modified physical therapy and pharmacotherapy requirements, and the interventional requirement was clinically waived, the patient fully satisfied the plan's internal criteria. The initial denial was a misapplication of the plan's own written guidelines.

SECTION 4 — OVERRIDING CALIFORNIA STATUTORY AND REGULATORY MANDATES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Even if the plan's internal criteria were interpreted to require an interventional procedure, California law overrides more restrictive plan criteria and renders the initial denial unlawful.

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3):
Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), a health insurer is strictly prohibited from conditioning the approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy or other conservative treatments when "the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy [or conservative treatment] cannot address." The patient's documented C4-C5 cord compression, early myelomalacia, and objective upper motor neuron signs (bilateral Hoffman signs and hyperreflexia) constitute clear, objective evidence of structural neurological compromise. Conditioning surgical decompression on further conservative or interventional trials directly violates this statute.

2. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:
APL 22-014 addresses prior authorization requirements for time-sensitive neurological conditions. The DMHC has determined that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with high potential for irreversible harm if untreated. APL 22-014 Section II.2 mandates that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization" when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology and the treating physician documents urgency. Furthermore, Section II.3 states that plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. 

By requiring an interventional procedure (epidural injection) in a patient with documented cord compression and myelomalacia, the plan violated both CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) and DMHC APL 22-014.

SECTION 5 — GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), clinical standards of medical practice govern over restrictive plan criteria. Generally accepted clinical guidelines support immediate surgical intervention for this patient:

- AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update): Classifies moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI as a Tier 1 — Strong Indication for surgical decompression. 
- AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021): Explicitly states, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
- Clinical Evidence Summary (Section 2.3): Preoperative myelomalacia is the strongest predictor of incomplete neurological recovery if surgery is delayed. Each month of delay in surgical decompression for progressive myelopathy is associated with a statistically significant decrease in the likelihood of long-term neurological recovery.

The interventional treatment requirement is waived under the plan's own Exception 2.2(A) and is legally barred from being enforced under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) and DMHC APL 22-014.
