Dear Helen Brooks,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of the first-level appeal submitted on your behalf by Dr. James Park, MD, regarding the prior authorization denial for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 (CPT 22551). 

After a comprehensive evaluation of your clinical records, the plan's coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, we have determined that a genuine clinical conflict exists regarding the interpretation of your diagnostic imaging. Consequently, the binary decision to uphold or overturn the denial cannot be resolved internally. This case is hereby REMANDED for Independent Medical Review (IMR) through the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) in accordance with California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.30.

I. CLINICAL BACKGROUND AND DISPUTED FINDINGS
On October 1, 2024, you underwent an MRI of the cervical spine at UCSF Medical Center. The interpretation of this imaging is subject to a critical conflict among qualified medical specialists:
1. Original Read (Radiologist A, 2024-10-01): Noted a C4-C5 disc herniation with cord contact and "possible early T2 signal change — equivocal, may represent artifact," recommending clinical correlation.
2. Second Read (Radiologist B, Treating Hospital, 2024-10-15): Confirmed C4-C5 cord compression with early myelomalacia. Radiologist B documented that the T2 hyperintensity is reproducible on multiple sequences and represents early cord signal change, not an artifact, which is highly consistent with early myelopathy.
3. Plan Radiologist Review (Plan Reviewer, 2024-11-05): Asserted that the T2 signal change is below the threshold for clinical significance, does not represent myelomalacia, and is likely an artifact or normal variant.

Your clinical neurological examination on October 5, 2024, demonstrated objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia of the upper extremities, and a subtle balance impairment on tandem gait. 

Your conservative treatment history includes:
- Physical Therapy: 8 weeks (24 sessions) completed, with documented "no improvement in UMN signs," indicating you have reached a functional plateau.
- Pharmacotherapy: Gabapentin 400mg TID for 3 months (partial benefit) and Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks (minimal benefit).
- Interventional Treatment: Not performed. Dr. James Park, MD, explicitly documented that he did not refer you for an epidural steroid injection due to myelopathy concerns, noting that epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated in the presence of spinal cord compression.

II. PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(b), cervical myelopathy is defined as spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms (including gait disturbance, hyperreflexia, and a positive Hoffman sign). Your clinical exam on October 5, 2024, objectively satisfies these diagnostic criteria.

Under Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway Exception), the conservative treatment requirements are modified as follows:
- Physical therapy is reduced to a 4-week minimum (satisfied; you completed 8 weeks).
- Pharmacotherapy requirements under Section 2.2(b) remain in full (satisfied; you trialed two agents for at least 4 weeks each).
- The interventional treatment requirement under Section 2.2(c) is WAIVED if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration.

Dr. Alan Foster, MD, denied the initial request on November 20, 2024, under Reason Code MED-003, asserting that the myelopathy exception did not apply because the MRI T2 signal change was minimal and represented an artifact. Because the exception was deemed inapplicable, the plan applied the standard pathway, which requires an interventional procedure (Section 2.2(c)). Because no interventional procedure was completed, the request was denied.

III. REGULATORY COMPLIANCE AND HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY
Under California regulatory standards, the hierarchy of authority dictates that California State Law and generally accepted clinical standards supersede a health plan's internal coverage criteria where a conflict exists.

1. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:
APL 22-014 establishes that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI is a time-sensitive neurological condition with potential for irreversible harm. Under Section II.2, plans shall not require a full course of conservative treatment (such as interventional injections) when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology and the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed or poses a risk. The presence of myelomalacia on MRI specifically supports the waiver of conservative treatment. By mechanically requiring an injection despite the treating physician's documented contraindication, the plan's initial denial violates APL 22-014.

2. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5:
Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Furthermore, under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), a physical therapy prerequisite is waived if the member has completed a course of therapy and reached a functional plateau. Your physical therapy records from October 2024 document a functional plateau with no improvement in upper motor neuron signs, making the mechanical application of further conservative requirements legally impermissible.

3. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32:
CHSC § 1374.32(a) mandates that medical necessity determinations be made in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice. Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the standard of review does not require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If a service is clinically appropriate and supported by clinical standards, it must be approved.

IV. CLINICAL EVIDENCE BASE AND RESOLUTION OF CONFLICT
According to the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023) and the AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021), surgical decompression (ACDF) is a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) recommendation for patients presenting with moderate-to-severe myelopathy, objective cord compression, and myelomalacia (T2 signal change) on MRI. 

These clinical guidelines emphasize that:
- There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy or interventional injections prior to surgery for patients with cervical myelopathy.
- Delaying surgery to complete conservative trials in patients with objective upper motor neuron signs and cord compression risks irreversible neurological deterioration.
- Epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated in patients with severe cord compression due to the risk of acute hematoma or volume expansion causing rapid neurological decline.

V. RATIONALE FOR REMAND
A direct conflict exists between the interpretation of the October 1, 2024 MRI by the treating hospital's radiologist (Radiologist B), who identified reproducible early myelomalacia, and the plan's reviewer, who dismissed the finding as an artifact. 

Under CHSC § 1374.32, the plan cannot resolve this conflict by unilaterally prioritizing its own reviewer's interpretation over that of the treating institution's radiologist, particularly when the clinical neurological exam independently confirms correlating upper motor neuron deficits (bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, and balance impairment). 

Because the resolution of this radiological conflict is determinative of whether the Myelopathy Pathway Exception (Section 2.2(A)) applies, and because generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine) strongly support surgical intervention without conservative delays for patients with clinical myelopathy, a binary internal determination is inappropriate. 

Therefore, this case is REMANDED to the Independent Medical Review (IMR) process under CHSC § 1374.30. An independent, external medical specialist will review the disputed MRI sequences and clinical records to issue a final, binding decision.
