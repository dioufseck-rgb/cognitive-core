Dear Dorothy James and Dr. Paul Adams,

The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals Department has completed its review of the prior authorization appeal for the requested single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551). Based on a comprehensive review of the complete clinical record, including physical therapy documentation newly submitted with this appeal, the initial prior authorization denial issued on November 02, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED. 

This determination is based on the member's full compliance with HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California statutory requirements under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.


I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION & IMAGING SUMMARY

Ms. Dorothy James is a 51-year-old female presenting with a diagnosis of bilateral C6-C7 cervical radiculopathy. 
- Neurological Examination (Dated September 05, 2024): Documents objective neurological deficits in a bilateral C7 dermatomal distribution, including bilateral tricep weakness graded at 4/5, diminished bilateral tricep reflexes, and bilateral finger tingling.
- Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) (Dated September 01, 2024): Demonstrates a large C6-C7 disc herniation, bilateral C7 nerve root compression, bilateral foraminal stenosis, and mild spinal cord contact without compression.


II. PLAN CRITERIA COMPLIANCE ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)

Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2, authorization for cervical spinal surgery (CPT 22551) is approved when all medical necessity criteria are satisfied. 

1. Section 2.1 — Diagnosis Requirement: MET. The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with correlating bilateral C7 nerve root compression and objective neurological deficits (tricep weakness 4/5, diminished reflexes) consistent with Section 2.1(a) and 2.1(c). The supporting MRI was performed on September 01, 2024, which is within the required 6-month window.

2. Section 2.2 — Conservative Treatment Requirement: MET. 
   - (a) Physical Therapy: MET. The plan's initial denial (MED-003) was based on the receipt of only 4 weeks of physical therapy records from Valley Physical Therapy. With this appeal, records from Mountain Physical Therapy have been submitted, documenting an additional 4 weeks of supervised physical therapy. 
     * Valley Physical Therapy: June 01, 2024, to June 28, 2024 (4 weeks, 12 sessions). Outcome: Moderate improvement followed by a documented plateau.
     * Mountain Physical Therapy: July 15, 2024, to August 09, 2024 (4 weeks, 12 sessions). Outcome: No improvement in neurological symptoms, with a functional plateau documented at discharge.
     * Total PT Duration: 8 weeks (24 sessions) completed across two providers, exceeding the plan's 6-week minimum requirement under Section 2.2(a).
     * Note on Continuity: The 17-day gap between the two physical therapy courses (June 28 to July 15) does not invalidate the treatment. HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a) specifies a cumulative duration ("minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy") and does not mandate uninterrupted continuity or penalize transitions between licensed providers.
   - (b) Pharmacotherapy: MET. The clinical record documents trials of two qualifying agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each: Pregabalin 150mg BID for 3 months (neuropathic agent, partial relief) and Naproxen 500mg BID for 2 months (NSAID, inadequate response), satisfying Section 2.2(b).
   - (c) Interventional Treatment: MET. The member underwent a bilateral C7 epidural steroid injection on September 20, 2024, which provided only temporary relief (50% left-sided, 30% right-sided) before symptoms returned to baseline, satisfying Section 2.2(c).

3. Section 2.3 — Imaging Requirement: MET. The MRI dated September 01, 2024, is within 6 months and demonstrates structural pathology (large C6-C7 disc herniation and bilateral C7 nerve root compression) directly correlating with the member's bilateral C7 dermatomal symptoms and the proposed surgical level.

4. Section 2.4 — Physician Documentation: MET. Dr. Paul Adams has provided the necessary clinical documentation, including conservative treatment outcomes, surgical rationale, functional limitations, and discussion of risks.


III. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY COMPLIANCE ANALYSIS

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:
   Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician or therapist documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms. 
   Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(c) mandates that a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau. 
   The initial denial on November 02, 2024, mechanically enforced the 6-week duration requirement despite Valley Physical Therapy records documenting a plateau on June 28, 2024. On appeal, the submission of the Mountain Physical Therapy records confirms a cumulative 8-week trial with a final documented functional plateau, fully satisfying both the statutory standard and the plan's internal guidelines.

2. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 — Standard of Medical Necessity:
   Under California law, medical necessity determinations must align with generally accepted standards of medical practice. The clinical record demonstrates that the member has persistent, objective neurological deficits that have failed to resolve despite exhaustive conservative management, making surgical decompression clinically appropriate and medically necessary.


IV. CLINICAL EVIDENCE & GUIDELINE ALIGNMENT

This determination is highly aligned with the clinical evidence base and major spine society guidelines:
- North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020): Recommend surgical intervention for patients with cervical radiculopathy who exhibit objective neurological deficits (such as tricep weakness and reflex changes) and who have failed to achieve adequate functional improvement after 6 to 12 weeks of conservative therapy.
- AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine Guidelines (2023): Classify radiculopathy with persistent dermatomal symptoms and objective neurological deficits after an appropriate conservative trial as a Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) for surgical decompression via ACDF.
- Clinical Definition of Functional Plateau: Ms. James's physical therapy records document a failure to achieve further meaningful functional recovery, particularly regarding her neurological symptoms (weakness and tingling). Because physical therapy cannot mechanically decompress a structurally compressed nerve root, continuing conservative therapy past this plateau poses a risk of prolonged nerve injury without clinical benefit.

Based on the resolution of the administrative record gap and the clinical findings detailed above, the requested procedure is approved.
