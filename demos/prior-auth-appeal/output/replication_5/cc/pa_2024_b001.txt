The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a two-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 and C6-C7 levels (CPT 22551 x2).

The requested two-level ACDF procedure is APPROVED for both the C5-C6 and C6-C7 levels.

I. PROCEDURAL ANALYSIS & DEEMED APPROVAL UNDER CALIFORNIA LAW

Under California law, procedural compliance in the issuance of prior authorization denials is strictly mandated. A review of the initial denial notice dated October 28, 2024, revealed a critical procedural defect:

1. Defective Denial Notice (CHSC § 1374.31(b)): California Health & Safety Code § 1374.31(b) states that a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is defective and "shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines." The initial denial notice issued on October 28, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, failed to specify or cite the clinical criteria applied (specifically, HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024). Consequently, this notice is legally defective and is treated as if no denial was issued.
2. Deemed Approval (CHSC § 1367.01 & CCR Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1): Under California Health & Safety Code § 1367.01, a health plan must issue a valid, legally compliant decision within five (5) business days of receipt of all necessary information for a standard prior authorization request. Because the plan's initial decision was procedurally defective and therefore legally void, the plan failed to issue a valid decision within the required 5-business-day timeframe. Under CHSC § 1367.01, the entire requested service (both C5-C6 and C6-C7 levels) is legally DEEMED APPROVED.

Because state law occupies the highest tier in the hierarchy of authority, this procedural defect and the resulting deemed approval supersede any internal plan criteria or clinical arguments. On this basis alone, the denial must be overturned in full.

II. CLINICAL MERITS ANALYSIS

To ensure a complete and robust record, HealthFirst also evaluated the clinical merits of the requested levels under both internal plan criteria and generally accepted clinical standards.

1. C5-C6 Level (Medically Necessary):
The clinical record demonstrates that the member fully meets the criteria for the C5-C6 level under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024:
- Diagnosis (Section 2.1(a)): The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy. The neurological exam dated September 5, 2024, documents right C6 dermatomal numbness and tingling, a diminished right bicep reflex, and right grip strength of 4/5.
- Imaging (Section 2.3): The MRI dated September 1, 2024, demonstrates a large disc herniation at C5-C6 with moderate-to-severe nerve root compression and moderate foraminal stenosis, which directly correlates with the right C6 radicular symptoms.
- Conservative Treatment (Section 2.2): The member completed 8 weeks of physical therapy (24 sessions) across two providers, exceeding the 6-week requirement in Section 2.2(a). She trialed two neuropathic/muscle relaxant agents (Gabapentin 600mg TID for 4 months and Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks), satisfying the pharmacotherapy requirement in Section 2.2(b). She also underwent a right C6 epidural steroid injection on October 1, 2024, which provided only 50% temporary relief, satisfying the interventional treatment requirement in Section 2.2(c).
Therefore, the C5-C6 level is medically necessary under both plan criteria and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023 Joint Guidelines, Tier 2).

2. C6-C7 Level (Prudent Clinical Judgment & IMR Standard):
The plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024 Sections 2.3 and 3.3) require correlating symptoms and imaging findings for each proposed surgical level. The MRI dated September 1, 2024, shows only a mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing at C6-C7 without nerve root compression, and there are no documented C7 radicular symptoms. 
However, under California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32(b), the Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard of review does not require consistency with the plan's internal criteria. Instead, it evaluates whether a provider exercising prudent clinical judgment would provide the service in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice.
- Prudent Clinical Judgment: Dr. James Wilson, MD, a board-certified spine surgeon at UCLA Medical Center, recommended a two-level fusion to address the active C5-C6 pathology and prophylactically stabilize the adjacent C6-C7 level to prevent adjacent segment disease (ASD).
- Clinical Evidence: Generally accepted clinical standards, including the AANS/CNS 2023 Joint Guidelines, recognize that adjacent segment disease has a significant 5-year incidence rate of approximately 25% following a single-level ACDF. Prophylactic stabilization of an adjacent degenerated level (even with mild imaging findings) is a recognized clinical strategy to prevent rapid adjacent segment deterioration and avoid a secondary, highly complex revision surgery.
- IMR Vulnerability: Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) data indicates a 72.3% overturn rate for cervical spine cases at IMR, with 21% of those overturns resulting from plan criteria being more restrictive than clinical standards. Relying solely on internal plan criteria to deny the C6-C7 level would be highly vulnerable to an IMR overturn.
In light of the prudent clinical judgment of the treating surgeon, the clinical evidence regarding adjacent segment disease, and the high likelihood of an IMR overturn, the C6-C7 level is approved.

III. CONCLUSION & DISPOSITION

Under the hierarchy of authority, California state law (CHSC §§ 1374.31(b) and 1367.01) is the supreme governing authority and mandates a full deemed approval of the requested service due to the procedural defect in the initial denial notice. Furthermore, the clinical record supports the medical necessity of the C5-C6 level, and prudent clinical judgment under the California IMR standard (CHSC § 1374.32) supports the C6-C7 level.

Accordingly, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED IN FULL. The request for a two-level ACDF at C5-C6 and C6-C7 (CPT 22551 x2) is APPROVED.
