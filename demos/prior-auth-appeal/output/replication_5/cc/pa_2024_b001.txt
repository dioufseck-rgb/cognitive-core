Dear Committee,

This clinical memorandum provides an objective review of the clinical evidence base regarding the medical necessity of prophylactic fusion for adjacent segment disease (ASD) and compares these findings to the plan's clinical coverage criteria. This review is conducted in reference to the prior authorization appeal for member Linda Park (Case ID: PA-2024-B001).

1. Clinical Evidence Base on Prophylactic Fusion for Adjacent Segment Disease
The clinical evidence base—including the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023), the North American Spine Society (NASS) Clinical Guidelines (2020), and the AO Spine International Guidelines (2021)—does not support prophylactic fusion of an asymptomatic cervical level to prevent adjacent segment disease.

Specifically:
- Symptom-Imaging Correlation: Generally accepted standards of medical practice require that each level of a proposed multi-level cervical fusion have correlating clinical symptoms and objective imaging findings demonstrating significant pathology (e.g., nerve root or spinal cord compression). Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Section 3.1, "Imaging findings without correlating symptoms" are classified as Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated).
- Lack of Evidence for Prophylaxis: While adjacent segment disease is a recognized clinical phenomenon with a documented incidence (approximately 2.9% per year or up to 25% over 10 years), there is no high-quality clinical evidence demonstrating that preemptively fusing a radiographically mild, asymptomatic adjacent level (such as C6-C7 in this patient) prevents future symptomatic ASD or improves long-term clinical outcomes.
- Risk-Benefit Profile: Prophylactic fusion unnecessarily increases surgical morbidity, including longer operative times, increased blood loss, higher rates of pseudarthrosis (non-union), and the transfer of adjacent segment stress further down the spinal column (e.g., to the C7-T1 level).

2. Comparison to Plan's Clinical Coverage Criteria
The HealthFirst Health Plan Clinical Coverage Criteria (Document: CC-SPINE-2024) are fully congruent with the established clinical evidence base:
- Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement) mandates that current imaging "must demonstrate structural pathology consistent with the member's symptoms and proposed surgical level."
- Section 3.3 (Exclusions) explicitly states that authorization will be denied when "Imaging does not demonstrate pathology at the proposed surgical level consistent with clinical presentation."

3. Synthesis and Comparison
Both the plan's internal criteria and generally accepted clinical guidelines require a strict level-by-level correlation between clinical symptoms and objective imaging findings. Neither the plan's criteria nor the clinical evidence base recognizes "prophylactic stabilization" or "prevention of adjacent segment disease" as a medically necessary indication for fusing an asymptomatic level that lacks significant structural pathology or nerve root/cord compression.
