On appeal, the prior authorization denial for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED IN FULL. 

Under California law, specifically California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5, a health plan is legally prohibited from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy when there is objective structural neurological compromise or when the treating physician documents that physical therapy is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit. Furthermore, under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), the standard of review for medical necessity is governed by generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersede a plan's restrictive internal coverage criteria.

Because the clinical record establishes that Mr. Achebe has symptomatic cervical myelopathy with objective structural cord compression, and because his treating neurologist has documented that physical therapy was halted due to myelopathy concerns, the plan's conservative treatment prerequisites are legally waived. Surgical decompression is medically necessary and is hereby authorized.

SECTION 3 — LEGAL & REGULATORY ANALYSIS (CALIFORNIA LAW)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
The plan's initial denial on November 5, 2024, mechanically enforced standard conservative treatment requirements (6 weeks of physical therapy, 2 pharmacotherapy agents, and an interventional procedure). This denial, and any attempt to remand or delay approval to enforce these requirements, violates California statutory law on two distinct grounds:

1. Statutory Waiver of Physical Therapy (California Insurance Code § 10169.5):
Under CIC § 10169.5(a), a health plan "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy" under any of the following circumstances:
  - § 10169.5(a)(3): "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Mr. Achebe's MRI demonstrates moderate central cord compression and an 8mm canal, and his clinical exam confirms upper motor neuron signs (bilateral Hoffman signs and hyperreflexia). Physical therapy cannot expand the spinal canal or relieve mechanical cord compression.
  - § 10169.5(a)(1): "The member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition." Dr. Osei explicitly halted physical therapy after 3 weeks citing myelopathy concerns and documented that further delay is medically contraindicated.
Consequently, the plan's physical therapy requirement is legally waived under California law.

2. Standard of Review for Medical Necessity (California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32):
Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the standard of review for medical necessity does NOT require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024). If a service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, the reviewer "shall recommend approval regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." Enforcing the plan's internal "Exception 2.2(A)" (which still requires 4 weeks of PT and 2 pharmacotherapy agents) violates this statutory standard by failing to align with the clinical standard of care.

3. DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014:
APL 22-014 dictates that plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for time-sensitive neurological conditions. Cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression is a progressive condition where delay risks irreversible neurological harm. Enforcing arbitrary conservative treatment durations in this clinical scenario constitutes an impermissible barrier to care.

SECTION 4 — CLINICAL STANDARD OF CARE & EVIDENCE-BASED ANALYSIS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Generally accepted standards of medical practice strongly support immediate surgical decompression for symptomatic cervical myelopathy, rejecting the clinical utility of conservative therapy in this population:

1. AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):
These guidelines state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with... cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." The guidelines recommend surgical decompression as the primary treatment for symptomatic myelopathy because conservative treatment has limited efficacy in arresting disease progression.

2. AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine Guidelines (2023 Update):
Under Section 2.1, surgical decompression is indicated for patients with symptomatic cervical myelopathy. While Mr. Achebe's presentation is classified as a Tier 2 (elective surgical candidate) rather than a Tier 1 (acute emergency) due to the absence of active myelomalacia or acute progressive deficits, the guidelines still establish that surgery is the definitive and appropriate treatment. The guidelines do not support delaying elective decompression in a patient with an 8mm canal and upper motor neuron signs to complete ineffective and potentially hazardous physical therapy.

3. Cochrane Review (Nikolaidis et al., 2021):
This systematic review confirms that physical therapy does not produce meaningful functional improvement for patients with established cervical myelopathy. Applying conservative treatment protocols derived from radiculopathy evidence to a patient with spinal cord compression is a clinical category error.
