HealthFirst Health Plan's Appeal Determination Services has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an emergency Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND TIMELINE CONTEXT
On November 10, 2024, the member, a 38-year-old male with no prior cervical history, presented to the Emergency Department at UCSF Medical Center with acute onset bilateral arm weakness and difficulty walking that had rapidly progressed over 72 hours. 

A neurological examination performed on November 10, 2024, revealed severe deficits consistent with an incomplete spinal cord syndrome:
- Bilateral upper extremity (arm) weakness graded at 2/5;
- Bilateral lower extremity (leg) weakness graded at 3/5;
- Hyperreflexia in all extremities;
- Bilateral positive Babinski signs;
- New-onset urinary urgency.

An emergency cervical spine MRI performed on November 10, 2024, demonstrated severe C5-C6 central cord compression with extensive T2 hyperintensity throughout the spinal cord, indicating extensive myelomalacia and acute spinal cord injury. Neurosurgery was emergently consulted, and the patient was immediately admitted and started on the standard acute spinal cord injury protocol, receiving a 24-hour inpatient intravenous (IV) methylprednisolone infusion. Dr. Elena Marchetti, MD, requested emergency prior authorization for a one-level ACDF at C5-C6 (CPT 22551).

On November 18, 2024, HealthFirst denied the request under clinical coverage criteria document CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, citing 0 weeks of documented physical therapy, no documented outpatient pharmacotherapy, and no interventional procedures. 

Timeline Gap Resolution:
The plan's initial denial on November 18, 2024, forced an administrative delay, resulting in the submission of this formal appeal on December 1, 2024. This delay was entirely administrative and does not diminish the acute clinical urgency of the patient's condition. The patient's progressive cervical myelopathy with extensive myelomalacia remains an active, acute neurological emergency. The clinical urgency is ongoing, and any further delay in surgical decompression poses an imminent, catastrophic risk of permanent, irreversible quadriplegia.

II. GOVERNING LEGAL AND REGULATORY ANALYSIS
The plan's initial denial relied mechanically on its internal clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024). However, under California law, internal plan criteria are subordinate to state statutory and regulatory standards.

1. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 — The Governing Standard of Medical Necessity
Under CHSC § 1374.32(a), "medically necessary" services are those that a prudent clinical provider would deliver to prevent, evaluate, diagnose, or treat an illness or injury in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice and clinical appropriateness. 
Crucially, CHSC § 1374.32(b) explicitly mandates that the medical necessity standard does NOT require a service to be consistent with the plan's internal coverage criteria. Under CHSC § 1374.32(c), the plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) may be used only as a reference and SHALL NOT be the sole or determinative basis for a medical necessity decision. Where a conflict exists between a plan's restrictive internal criteria and generally accepted clinical standards, the clinical standard of care governs.

2. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 — Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions
DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.1 establishes that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a time-sensitive condition with high potential for irreversible harm if untreated, requiring plans to treat these cases as urgent.
Section II.2 states that plans shall not require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia) and surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines.

Addressing the "Attempted and Failed" Conservative Treatment Requirement:
APL 22-014 Section II.2(b) notes that the treating physician should document that conservative treatment has been "attempted and has failed."
- First, the patient did undergo acute, inpatient pharmacotherapy: a 24-hour high-dose IV methylprednisolone protocol administered in the ER on November 10, 2024. This represents the only clinically appropriate, non-dangerous medical management for an acute spinal cord injury. This acute pharmacotherapy failed to decompress the spinal cord or arrest the underlying structural compression.
- Second, APL 22-014 Section II.2 explicitly notes that "the presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements even where a full course has not been completed, when the treating physician documents urgency."
- Third, under generally accepted standards of medical practice, "attempting" a treatment that is absolutely contraindicated is a violation of the clinical standard of care. Dr. Marchetti documented that physical therapy is absolutely contraindicated because initiating mechanical neck therapy in a patient with severe cord compression, 2/5 arm weakness, and active myelomalacia risks converting an incomplete spinal cord injury into a complete, permanent spinal cord injury (paralysis). Because further conservative treatment (such as outpatient physical therapy or a 4-week trial of oral NSAIDs/neuropathic agents) is clinically contraindicated and dangerous, the requirement to attempt it is waived under emergency medical necessity standards.

3. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy Restrictions
CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) explicitly prohibits a health plan from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. 
The patient's emergency MRI on November 10, 2024, provides objective, indisputable evidence of severe structural neurological compromise (severe central cord compression and extensive myelomalacia). Physical therapy cannot decompress a mechanically compressed spinal cord. Therefore, the plan's insistence on physical therapy as a prerequisite to surgery is a direct violation of CIC § 10169.5.

III. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND STANDARD OF CARE
The plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 and Exception 2.2(A)) require a 4-week trial of at least two pharmacotherapy agents and a physical therapy trial. Applying these requirements to an acute, progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia is a clinical category error.

According to generally accepted standards of medical practice:
- AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update) & NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020): Surgical decompression is a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) recommendation for patients presenting with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy, objective cord compression, and myelomalacia on MRI. Conservative treatment is not recommended as a primary treatment in this clinical cohort.
- AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021): "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
- Clinical Risk of Delay: Preoperative myelomalacia (T2 cord signal change) is the strongest predictor of incomplete neurological recovery. Every week of delay in surgical decompression for progressive myelopathy is associated with a decreased likelihood of neurological recovery and an increased risk of permanent, irreversible deficit.

IV. CONCLUSION AND DIRECTIVE
The clinical record establishes that Robert Chang has an acute, progressive cervical myelopathy with severe cord compression and extensive myelomalacia, representing an incomplete spinal cord syndrome. 
- Under CHSC § 1374.32, the clinical standard of care governs, and immediate surgical decompression is the only medically necessary and clinically appropriate treatment.
- Under DMHC APL 22-014, the standard conservative treatment requirements are waived due to the presence of myelomalacia and the acute clinical urgency, with the ER administration of IV methylprednisolone satisfying the acute pharmacotherapy attempt.
- Under CIC § 10169.5, the physical therapy requirement is legally barred due to objective structural neurological compromise.

Accordingly, the prior authorization denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level) is OVERTURNED. HealthFirst Health Plan is directed to issue an immediate, expedited authorization for this procedure.
