Dear Mr. Santos and Dr. Wong,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its clinical and regulatory review of the first-level appeal submitted on October 20, 2024, regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C6-C7 level (CPT 22551). 

After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California statutory and regulatory mandates, and generally accepted clinical standards of care, HealthFirst has determined that the original denial issued on October 05, 2024, was in error. Accordingly, the denial is OVERTURNED, and the requested surgical procedure is fully APPROVED.

I. CLINICAL BACKGROUND AND PRESENTATION
Mr. David Santos is a 63-year-old male with a documented clinical presentation of mixed cervical myelopathy and radiculopathy. 
- Imaging: A cervical spine MRI performed on September 01, 2024 (within the required 6-month window) demonstrated moderate cord compression at the C6-C7 level, accompanied by T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord. Additionally, right-sided C7 foraminal stenosis was noted to be more prominent than the left.
- Neurological Examination: A clinical evaluation on September 05, 2024, revealed a mixed presentation of radicular and myelopathic findings:
  * Radicular findings: Right tricep weakness (4/5), right finger numbness, and severe right-sided pain rated at 8/10, primarily in a C7 dermatomal distribution.
  * Myelopathic findings: Bilateral hyperreflexia (right greater than left), a positive right Hoffman sign, an equivocal left Hoffman sign, and a mildly wide-based gait.
- Conservative Treatment History:
  * Physical Therapy: Completed 6 weeks (18 sessions) of supervised physical therapy utilizing a myelopathy protocol. The physical therapist documented no improvement in either the bilateral neurological signs or the right-sided radicular symptoms.
  * Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 3-month trial of Pregabalin (150mg BID) with only partial right-sided pain relief, and a 2-month trial of Naproxen (500mg BID) with inadequate response.
  * Interventional Treatment: Received a right C7 cervical epidural steroid injection on September 28, 2024, which provided 50% temporary relief of right-sided pain that fully returned within 3 weeks, with no change in bilateral hyperreflexia.

II. ANALYSIS OF PLAN CRITERIA COMPLIANCE (CC-SPINE-2024)
The initial denial on October 05, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, cited Reason Code MED-003, stating that the "Myelopathy exception applied but criteria not met" because the member's symptoms were primarily right-sided (radicular) and the primary diagnosis remained radiculopathy. The reviewer argued that the myelopathy exception under Section 2.2 requires myelopathy to be the primary diagnosis.

A rigorous review of the plan's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) reveals that the initial denial was internally inconsistent and misapplied the plan's own guidelines:
1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1): The member has a confirmed diagnosis of both cervical radiculopathy (Section 2.1(a)) and cervical myelopathy (Section 2.1(b)), supported by an MRI within 6 months demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms (Section 2.3).
2. Standard Conservative Treatment (Section 2.2): Even if the plan evaluated the request under the standard radiculopathy pathway (rather than the myelopathy exception pathway), the member fully satisfied ALL standard conservative treatment requirements:
   - Section 2.2(a): Completed 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy (6 weeks, 18 sessions documented).
   - Section 2.2(b): Completed trials of two qualifying medications (Pregabalin for 3 months, Naproxen for 2 months), both exceeding the 4-week minimum.
   - Section 2.2(c): Completed one interventional treatment (right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 28, 2024).
Therefore, because the member met the standard conservative treatment criteria, the plan's denial based on the "primary diagnosis" restriction of the myelopathy exception was a clinical and procedural error. The member qualified for surgical authorization under the standard criteria, rendering the exception's restrictions irrelevant.

III. REGULATORY OVERRIDES AND CALIFORNIA LAW
Even if the member had not completed the standard conservative treatment requirements, California law and regulatory mandates strictly prohibit the plan from denying this request:
1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3): This statute mandates that a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the condition involves "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The member's MRI objectively documents moderate cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord, which is a severe structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot resolve. Conditioning surgical approval on further physical therapy or denying based on physical therapy requirements is a direct violation of CIC § 10169.5.
2. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014: The California Department of Managed Health Care has explicitly determined that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Under APL 22-014, plans are prohibited from requiring a full course of conservative treatment when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as cord signal change/myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression. The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically designated as a finding supporting the waiver of standard conservative treatment.
3. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b): Under California's Independent Medical Review (IMR) standards, medical necessity is determined based on generally accepted standards of medical practice, and the plan's internal coverage criteria "shall not be the sole or determinative basis" for a decision if they are more restrictive than the clinical standard. The plan's restriction requiring myelopathy to be the "primary" diagnosis to bypass conservative treatment is more restrictive than California law and generally accepted clinical standards, and is therefore overridden.

IV. GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS OF CARE
Under CHSC § 1374.32(a) and DMHC APL 22-014, generally accepted clinical standards of care govern this case and strongly support immediate surgical intervention:
1. AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023): These guidelines classify cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia (T2 signal change) on MRI as a Tier 1 — Strong Indication for surgical decompression. Immediate surgery is recommended because myelomalacia indicates active spinal cord injury, and delaying surgery risks permanent, irreversible neurological deficits.
2. NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020) & AO Spine International Guidelines (2021): Both authoritative bodies state that there is no high-quality clinical evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy or objective cord signal changes. Applying radiculopathy-derived physical therapy requirements to patients with spinal cord compression and myelomalacia is a clinical category error that exposes the patient to significant risk of neurological deterioration.

V. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS
In accordance with standard utilization review hierarchy, California statutory law (CIC § 10169.5) and regulatory mandates (DMHC APL 22-014) supersede any restrictive clauses within the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024). Furthermore, generally accepted clinical standards of care (AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines) override the plan's "primary diagnosis" restriction. 

Because the member has documented myelomalacia and cord compression, the plan cannot legally or clinically condition surgical approval on further conservative trials or deny authorization based on which diagnosis is labeled "primary." Additionally, because the member actually completed the full standard conservative treatment course, the plan's denial lacks any factual or clinical basis.

VI. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION DETAILS
The prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED. The request for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C6-C7, one level (CPT 22551) is APPROVED.
