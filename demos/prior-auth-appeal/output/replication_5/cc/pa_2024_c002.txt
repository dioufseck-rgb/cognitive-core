Dear Mr. Santos and Dr. Wong,

HealthFirst has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center. Based on a comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial denial issued on October 05, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED. The requested procedure is APPROVED.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The initial denial was based on the plan's internal "Myelopathy Exception" clause (Section 2.2, Exception 2.2(A)), which requires cervical myelopathy to be the "primary" diagnosis to modify conservative treatment requirements. The initial reviewer determined that because the member's symptoms were predominantly right-sided (radicular), radiculopathy was the primary diagnosis, and therefore the myelopathy exception did not apply. 

This appeal determination reverses that decision on three independent grounds:
1. Compliance with Standard Plan Criteria: The clinical record demonstrates that the member fully satisfied the plan's standard conservative treatment requirements (Section 2.2), rendering the "Myelopathy Exception" dispute moot.
2. California Regulatory Mandates: Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, a health plan cannot condition surgical approval on physical therapy when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (specifically, myelomalacia on MRI).
3. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards: Under the 2023 AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines and 2021 AO Spine Guidelines, the presence of myelomalacia (T2 signal change) on MRI represents a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) for surgical decompression, and delaying surgery for conservative treatment is clinically contraindicated due to the risk of irreversible neurological harm.

II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND OBJECTIVE FINDINGS
The clinical record documents a mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy presentation:
- Symptoms: The member presents with severe right-sided pain (8/10), right tricep weakness (4/5), and right finger numbness, consistent with C7 radiculopathy. Concurrently, the member exhibits myelopathic symptoms, including bilateral hyperreflexia (right greater than left), a positive right Hoffman sign, an equivocal left Hoffman sign, and a mildly wide-based gait.
- Imaging: A cervical MRI dated September 01, 2024, demonstrates moderate cord compression at C6-C7 with T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord, as well as prominent right C7 foraminal stenosis.

III. ANALYSIS OF PLAN CRITERIA (HEALTHFIRST CC-SPINE-2024)
The initial denial misapplied the plan's clinical coverage criteria by evaluating the request solely under the "Myelopathy Exception" pathway and failing to assess whether the standard criteria were met.
1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): The member has a confirmed diagnosis of both cervical radiculopathy (Section 2.1(a)) and cervical myelopathy (Section 2.1(b)), supported by an MRI within 6 months demonstrating structural pathology consistent with these symptoms.
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): The member completed the standard conservative treatment protocol:
   - Section 2.2(a) (Physical Therapy): The member completed 6 weeks (18 sessions) of supervised physical therapy directed at the cervical spine. The physical therapy summary notes a "functional plateau" with no improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms.
   - Section 2.2(b) (Pharmacotherapy): The member completed trials of two qualifying agents: Pregabalin (150mg BID for 3 months, neuropathic agent) and Naproxen (500mg BID for 2 months, NSAID).
   - Section 2.2(c) (Interventional Treatment): The member underwent a right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 28, 2024, which provided only temporary (50% relief for 3 weeks) relief of radicular pain and no change in bilateral hyperreflexia.
Because the member fully satisfied the standard conservative treatment requirements under Section 2.2, the plan's denial based on the "primary diagnosis" restriction of the myelopathy exception (Section 2.2, Exception 2.2(A)) represents a procedural misapplication of its own criteria.

IV. ANALYSIS OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK
Under California law, which governs this plan, the plan's internal criteria and denial are legally non-compliant:
1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3): A health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. The member's MRI of September 01, 2024, objectively documents moderate cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia). This constitutes structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address, overriding any plan-imposed physical therapy requirements.
2. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2): Approval cannot be conditioned on physical therapy when the member has completed a course of physical therapy and reached a "functional plateau." The treating physical therapist documented that the member reached a functional plateau after 6 weeks (18 sessions) with no improvement in neurological signs.
3. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014: The Department of Managed Health Care has determined that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a time-sensitive neurological condition with potential for irreversible harm. Under Section II.2, plans shall not require a full course of conservative treatment when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as cord signal change) and the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression. Furthermore, Section II.3 prohibits plans from applying internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice.

V. ANALYSIS OF CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARDS
Generally accepted clinical standards strongly support immediate surgical decompression for this member:
1. AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023): The presence of cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia (T2 signal change) on MRI meets Tier 1 (Strong Indication) criteria for surgical decompression.
2. AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021): There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery to complete physical therapy is not supported by clinical evidence and risks permanent, irreversible neurological deterioration.
3. Cochrane Review (2021): Physical therapy has limited to no efficacy for moderate-to-severe myelopathy and does not produce meaningful functional improvement when structural cord damage (myelomalacia) is present.

VI. RESOLUTION OF CROSS-SOURCE CONFLICTS (HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY)
To the extent that the plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) require myelopathy to be the "primary" diagnosis to waive or modify conservative treatment, a direct conflict exists with California law and clinical standards. 
Applying the established hierarchy of authority:
   California State Law (CIC § 10169.5 / DMHC APL 22-014) 
   > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS / AO Spine Guidelines) 
   > HealthFirst Internal Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)

The plan's "primary diagnosis" restriction is subordinate to California statutory prohibitions. Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), the presence of objective structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) legally invalidates any plan requirement conditioning surgery on further physical therapy, regardless of whether radiculopathy or myelopathy is designated as the "primary" diagnosis. Clinically, the distinction is a category error; both diagnoses are concurrently present (mixed presentation), and the myelopathic component carries an urgent risk of irreversible cord injury that dictates the clinical pathway.

VII. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION
The prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED. Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C6-C7, one level (CPT 22551) is authorized for member David Santos, to be performed by Dr. Lisa Wong, MD, at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center. This authorization is valid for 90 days from the date of this letter, expiring on January 22, 2025.
