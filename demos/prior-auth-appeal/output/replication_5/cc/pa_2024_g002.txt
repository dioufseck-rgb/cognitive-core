The HealthFirst Appellate Review Division has completed its review of the appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551).

DISPOSITION: The initial denial issued on October 25, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED. Prior authorization for the requested ACDF C5-C6 procedure is APPROVED.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION & DIAGNOSTIC FINDINGS
The clinical record establishes a primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy at C5-C6, supported by objective clinical and imaging evidence:
1. Imaging: A cervical spine MRI dated September 1, 2024, demonstrates moderate central cord compression at C5-C6 and bilateral foraminal stenosis.
2. Neurological Examination: A physical exam on September 5, 2024, reveals pathognomonic upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia of the upper extremities, mild impairment of tandem gait, and documented bilateral hand clumsiness. No lower extremity findings were noted.
3. Specialist Consultation: A neurologist consultation on September 12, 2024, confirmed that the clinical picture is consistent with cervical myelopathy, noting that the bilateral UMN signs are pathognomonic. The neurologist documented the primary diagnosis as cervical myelopathy (noted in the record with ICD-10: G35.89).

II. INITIAL DENIAL & PLAN CRITERIA PATHWAY ERROR
The initial denial on October 25, 2024, was based on Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 (Standard Pathway), which requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. The reviewer denied the request because the member had completed only 5 weeks of physical therapy.

This was a criteria-application error. Because the member has a confirmed primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (meeting Criterion 2.1(b)), the request must be evaluated under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway) rather than the standard radiculopathy pathway.

Under the correct Myelopathy Pathway (Section 2.2(A)):
1. Physical Therapy: The minimum duration is reduced to 4 weeks. The member completed 5 weeks (15 sessions) of supervised physical therapy under a myelopathy protocol, which exceeds the plan's 4-week requirement.
2. Interventional Treatment: The requirement for interventional treatment (Section 2.2(c)) is waived if the treating physician documents that such treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration. Dr. Chen's letter dated October 2024 explicitly provided this documentation, stating: "Interventional procedure not indicated — cord compression with myelopathy requires surgical evaluation, not injection." This waiver requirement is fully met.

III. PHARMACOTHERAPY SHORTFALL ANALYSIS
Under Section 2.2(A), the pharmacotherapy requirement of Section 2.2(b) "remains in full." Section 2.2(b) requires a trial of at least two of the listed agents (NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a "minimum 4 weeks each."

The clinical record documents the following pharmacotherapy trials:
1. Baclofen (Muscle Relaxant): 10mg TID for 3 months, resulting in minimal spasticity relief. This trial satisfies the 4-week minimum duration.
2. Methylprednisolone (Oral Corticosteroid): Dose pack completed over 3 weeks, providing only temporary improvement.

We explicitly acknowledge a technical shortfall under the plan's strict criteria: the methylprednisolone trial lasted only 3 weeks, which is 1 week short of the 4-week minimum required by Section 2.2(b).

IV. RESOLUTION OF SHORTFALL VIA HIERARCHY OF STANDARDS
While the plan's internal criteria were not strictly met due to this 1-week pharmacotherapy shortfall, California regulatory mandates and generally accepted clinical standards override this internal plan requirement. Under the governing hierarchy of standards (State Law > Clinical Standards > Plan Criteria), the denial must be overturned:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 Override:
Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy or other conservative treatments where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that conservative treatment cannot address. The member's moderate central cord compression and progressive upper motor neuron deficits (bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, gait impairment, and hand clumsiness) constitute objective structural neurological compromise. Mechanically enforcing a conservative treatment duration in this clinical scenario violates California law.

2. DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 & CHSC § 1374.32:
Under DMHC APL 22-014, cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression is classified as a time-sensitive neurological condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans are prohibited from applying internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice, or mechanically enforcing conservative trials that delay necessary surgical decompression. Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, the standard for medical necessity is determined by clinical appropriateness and generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersede more restrictive internal plan guidelines.

3. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:
According to guidelines from the American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS, 2023), the North American Spine Society (NASS, 2020), and AO Spine International (2021), moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy is a progressive condition for which surgical decompression is the primary and definitive treatment.
- The AO Spine Guidelines state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy [or conservative treatment] prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
- Furthermore, requiring a 4-week trial of oral corticosteroids (methylprednisolone) is clinically inappropriate and dangerous. Standard methylprednisolone dose packs are designed for short-term administration (typically 1 to 3 weeks). Forcing a patient to extend oral steroid therapy to 4 weeks poses significant clinical risks, including adrenal suppression, metabolic dysfunction, and osteonecrosis, without any clinical evidence of benefit for mechanical spinal cord compression.

V. CONCLUSION
The member has met the physical therapy and interventional waiver requirements under the plan's correct Myelopathy Pathway (Section 2.2(A)). The technical 1-week shortfall in the oral corticosteroid trial is clinically justified and legally overridden by CIC § 10169.5, CHSC § 1374.32, and DMHC APL 22-014, as well as national clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine). Delaying surgical decompression to satisfy an arbitrary and clinically hazardous 4-week steroid trial is medically contraindicated and poses an imminent risk of irreversible neurological harm.

Therefore, the prior authorization request for a single-level ACDF at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is APPROVED.
