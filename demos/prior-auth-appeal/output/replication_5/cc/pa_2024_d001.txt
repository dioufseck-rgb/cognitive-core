The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the appeal filed on October 18, 2024, regarding the denial of prior authorization for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551).

This determination is based on clear statutory mandates under California law, which supersede the plan's internal clinical criteria, as well as the clinical merits of your presentation.

I. PROCEDURAL DEFECTS AND STATUTORY DEEMED APPROVAL

Under the governing hierarchy of authority, California state law supersedes internal health plan coverage criteria. The initial denial notice issued by the plan on October 5, 2024, was procedurally defective under California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a) on two independent grounds:
1. Failure to Specify Criteria: The notice provided only a generic reference to plan criteria without citing the specific section numbers or clinical criteria applied, violating CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2).
2. Failure to Include IMR Notice: The notice failed to include the mandatory Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights notice, violating CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2).

Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is legally defective and "shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines." Because the initial denial is legally nullified, the plan has failed to issue a valid decision within the mandatory 5-business-day standard prior authorization timeline required by CHSC § 1367.01 (Part IV.1).

Pursuant to CHSC § 1367.01 and California Code of Regulations (CCR) Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1 (Part IV.3), if a plan fails to issue a timely, legally compliant decision, the request is legally "DEEMED APPROVED" for the period of the delay. 

Furthermore, we explicitly acknowledge that a "remand" to allow the plan to reissue a compliant denial notice is operationally and legally invalid for an internal appeal. Attempting to remand and retroactively deny the request would violate CHSC § 1367.01 and CHSC § 1374.31(b), exposing the plan to administrative penalties and an automatic administrative overturn by the Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC). Therefore, the procedural defects alone require an immediate and final OVERTURN of the denial.

II. CLINICAL-LEGAL MERITS AND PHYSICAL THERAPY WAIVER

The initial clinical denial was based on HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2, which requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy (PT) prior to surgical authorization. However, California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) prohibits health plans from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy where "the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."

Your clinical record contains clear, objective evidence of structural neurological compromise:
1. Imaging: A cervical MRI dated August 15, 2024, demonstrates a C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate right C7 nerve root compression.
2. Neurological Examination: A physical examination dated August 20, 2024, documents objective neurological deficits, specifically right C7 dermatomal numbness, tricep weakness graded at 4/5 on the right, and a diminished right tricep reflex.

Because tricep weakness (4/5) and a diminished reflex constitute objective structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address, the plan's 6-week PT requirement is legally waived under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3). The plan's internal requirement cannot be applied mechanically to deny coverage in the presence of these objective neurological deficits (CIC § 10169.5(c)).

Additionally, you completed 4 weeks of physical therapy (12 sessions) and reached a functional plateau. Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), the PT requirement is also waived when a member has completed a course of therapy and the treating physician documents a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms.

III. ALIGNMENT WITH GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS

Generally accepted clinical standards, which govern over restrictive plan criteria under DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, support surgical intervention for your presentation. According to the American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) 2023 Joint Guidelines for the Management of Cervical Spine Disorders, surgical decompression (ACDF) is indicated under Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) for patients with cervical radiculopathy, persistent symptoms, and objective neurological deficits (such as 4/5 motor weakness and diminished reflexes) who have not responded to initial conservative measures.

Your clinical presentation meets these clinical standards. Forcing an additional 2 weeks of physical therapy in the presence of active motor weakness (4/5 triceps) is clinically inappropriate, is not supported by medical evidence, and poses a risk of progressive, irreversible neurological deterioration.

IV. AUTHORIZATION DETAILS

Based on the statutory deemed approval under CHSC § 1367.01, the physical therapy waiver under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), and alignment with AANS/CNS clinical guidelines, the denial is OVERTURNED.
