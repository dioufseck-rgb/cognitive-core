The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C4-C5 level (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory mandates, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial denial issued on October 15, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED. 

Surgical authorization is APPROVED for CPT 22551 at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center, to be performed by Dr. Michael Torres, MD. This authorization is valid for 90 days from the date of this letter, expiring on January 27, 2025 (per Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 3.5).

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND RECORD REVIEW
The clinical record demonstrates that the member is a 55-year-old female presenting with progressive cervical myelopathy at the C4-C5 level with myelomalacia. 
1. Clinical Symptoms and Neurological Exam: A neurological examination dated August 25, 2024, documented bilateral lower extremity spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor skills in both hands, and a positive Lhermitte sign. These findings are diagnostic of cervical myelopathy under Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(b).
2. Objective Imaging: An MRI of the cervical spine dated August 20, 2024 (performed using a pacemaker-compatible protocol) revealed severe central cord compression at C4-C5 and confirmed T2 signal change (myelomalacia), indicating progressive spinal cord injury.
3. Conservative Treatment History:
   - Physical Therapy: The member completed 5 weeks (15 sessions) of supervised physical therapy. Therapy was discontinued due to pacemaker precautions limiting certain modalities and a clinical determination by the treating physician that further physical therapy was not addressing the patient's progressive neurological deterioration.
   - Pharmacotherapy: The member was treated with Baclofen 10mg TID for 3 months, which provided partial spasticity relief. She also underwent a methylprednisolone taper of "10 days x2" (20 days total), which provided only temporary improvement.
   - Interventional Treatment: No cervical epidural steroid injection or other interventional treatment was performed. A written consultation from anesthesiology (Dr. Rivera, dated September 10, 2024) documented that a fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injection is medically contraindicated due to the patient's permanent pacemaker (implanted in 2019, Boston Scientific).

II. ANALYSIS OF PLAN'S INTERNAL CLINICAL COVERAGE CRITERIA
Under HealthFirst Health Plan's Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery — Cervical Procedures (Document: CC-SPINE-2024), Section 2.2 requires a minimum of 6 weeks of physical therapy, a trial of two pharmacotherapy agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each, and at least one interventional treatment (such as a cervical epidural steroid injection). 

Under the Myelopathy Pathway Exception 2.2(A), the physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks, and the interventional treatment requirement is waived "if treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses risk of neurological deterioration." However, Exception 2.2(A) explicitly states that the pharmacotherapy requirement (Section 2.2(b)) "remains in full."

A strict application of the plan's internal criteria reveals that the member did NOT satisfy the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria for the following reasons:
1. Pharmacotherapy Duration Deficit: Section 2.2(b) requires a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks (28 days) each. While the member's Baclofen trial lasted 3 months, her methylprednisolone trial was only "10 days x2" (20 days total), failing the 4-week minimum duration.
2. Interventional Waiver Inapplicability: The plan's internal criteria under Exception 2.2(A) waive the interventional requirement only if the physician documents a "risk of neurological deterioration." The documented contraindication in the clinical record is a permanent pacemaker, which is a cardiac device and does not pose a "risk of neurological deterioration." Furthermore, there is no established clinical or physiological mechanism by which a permanent pacemaker contraindicates a fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injection.

Therefore, under a strict reading of CC-SPINE-2024, the member does not meet the plan's internal criteria. However, because the member resides in California, the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria are legally superseded by California regulatory mandates and generally accepted clinical standards, which require an overturn of the denial.

III. REGULATORY OVERRIDES UNDER CALIFORNIA LAW
The plan's internal clinical coverage criteria are more restrictive than California law and are therefore legally superseded. The denial is overturned based on the following statutory and regulatory provisions:

1. DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Prior Authorization Requirements for Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions):
   - Section II.1 of APL 22-014 determines that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI "constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated."
   - Section II.2 mandates that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization" when there is objective imaging evidence of progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia) and surgical intervention is indicated under clinical guidelines.
   - Section II.3 prohibits plans from applying internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. Because the member has confirmed progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia, the plan cannot legally condition surgical approval on the completion of the remaining physical therapy, pharmacotherapy, or interventional trials.

2. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy and Conservative Treatment Prerequisites):
   - CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) states that an insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on physical therapy or other conservative treatments where the treating physician has documented in writing that the treatment is contraindicated. The written anesthesiology consultation by Dr. Rivera on September 10, 2024, documenting that the cervical epidural steroid injection is contraindicated due to the permanent pacemaker, legally prohibits the plan from conditioning surgical approval on that treatment. This statutory protection applies to any written physician-documented contraindication (including device-related contraindications) and supersedes the plan's more restrictive internal requirement of a "neurological deterioration" risk.
   - CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) prohibits conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy where the condition "involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The member's severe central cord compression and myelomalacia constitute severe structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address, rendering the plan's physical therapy requirement legally unenforceable.
   - CIC § 10169.5(c) prohibits the mechanical application of a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or has neurological urgency. The member's physical therapy was stopped after 5 weeks because she reached a functional plateau and was experiencing progressive neurological decline, making the mechanical enforcement of a 6-week trial unlawful.

IV. CLINICAL STANDARDS AND EVIDENCE BASIS
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 and DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3, where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted clinical standards, the clinical standard governs. 

Generally accepted clinical standards for cervical myelopathy are established by the American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine (2023 Update), the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), and the AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021).
1. Surgical Indications: Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), surgical decompression is a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) recommendation for patients with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI. The member's clinical presentation (bilateral lower extremity spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor skills, and positive Lhermitte sign) combined with MRI-confirmed myelomalacia satisfies these Tier 1 criteria.
2. Inefficacy and Risk of Conservative Treatment: The AO Spine Guidelines (2021) state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." Furthermore, the Cochrane Review (2021) confirms that conservative treatment has limited efficacy for established moderate-to-severe myelopathy and does not produce meaningful functional improvement.
3. Risk of Delay: Clinical evidence demonstrates that preoperative myelomalacia is the strongest predictor of incomplete neurological recovery, and each month of delay in surgical decompression for progressive myelopathy is associated with a decreased likelihood of neurological recovery. Conditioning surgical approval on additional conservative trials is clinically contraindicated.

V. CONCLUSION
While the member did not strictly satisfy the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria under CC-SPINE-2024 (due to a 20-day methylprednisolone trial instead of 28 days, and a pacemaker contraindication that does not meet the plan's internal "neurological deterioration" waiver standard), these internal criteria are more restrictive than California law and generally accepted clinical standards. 

Under DMHC APL 22-014 and CIC § 10169.5, the plan is legally prohibited from requiring further physical therapy, pharmacotherapy, or interventional injections for a patient with progressive cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia. Generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) dictate that immediate surgical decompression is the standard of care and that delaying surgery for conservative treatment poses a high risk of irreversible neurological harm.

Accordingly, the initial denial is OVERTURNED, and the request for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C4-C5 (CPT 22551) is APPROVED.
