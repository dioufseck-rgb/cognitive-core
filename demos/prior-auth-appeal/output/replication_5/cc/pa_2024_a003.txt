Dear Ms. Osei and Dr. Torres,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its clinical and regulatory review of the first-level appeal submitted on October 28, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 (CPT 22551). 

After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California statutory mandates, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial denial issued on October 15, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED IN FULL. The requested surgical procedure is APPROVED.

Below is the detailed clinical, regulatory, and evidence-based rationale for this determination.

1. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND CASE SUMMARY
The member is a 55-year-old female with a primary diagnosis of progressive cervical myelopathy at C4-C5 with confirmed myelomalacia. Her comorbidities include a permanent pacemaker (implanted in 2019, Boston Scientific), Type 2 diabetes, and hypertension. 

- Neurological Examination (dated August 25, 2024): Demonstrates objective upper motor neuron signs, including bilateral lower extremity spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor skills in both hands, and a positive Lhermitte sign.
- Diagnostic Imaging (MRI dated August 20, 2024, performed under pacemaker-compatible protocol): Confirms severe central cord compression at C4-C5 with T2 signal change (myelomalacia), indicating progressive spinal cord injury.
- Conservative Treatment History:
  - Physical Therapy: Completed 5 weeks (15 sessions). Therapy was discontinued due to pacemaker precautions limiting specific modalities and a clinical determination by the treating physician that further therapy was futile and failed to address her progressive neurological decline.
  - Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 3-month trial of Baclofen 10mg TID (partial spasticity relief) and two 10-day courses of oral Methylprednisolone taper (temporary improvement only).
  - Interventional Treatment: Not performed. A formal anesthesiology consultation by Dr. Rivera on September 10, 2024, documented that a fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injection is strictly contraindicated due to the patient's permanent pacemaker.

2. ANALYSIS UNDER PLAN CLINICAL COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
The initial denial by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, cited Section 2.2 of the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), stating the member failed to complete 6 weeks of physical therapy and had no cervical epidural steroid injection on record. This application of the standard criteria was clinically and procedurally incorrect for the following reasons:

- Diagnosis Confirmation (Section 2.1): The member's clinical presentation of spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor skills, and positive Lhermitte sign, combined with MRI evidence of severe central cord compression, fully satisfies the diagnostic criteria for Cervical Myelopathy under Section 2.1(b).
- Application of the Myelopathy Pathway (Exception 2.2(A)): Because cervical myelopathy is the member's primary diagnosis, she qualifies for the "EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY." Under this pathway:
  1. Physical Therapy: The minimum duration is reduced from 6 weeks to 4 weeks. The member completed 5 weeks of supervised physical therapy, thereby fully satisfying this modified requirement.
  2. Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): The requirement for an epidural steroid injection is explicitly WAIVED under Exception 2.2(A) because the treating physician and consulting anesthesiologist (Dr. Rivera, 2024-09-10) documented in writing that the procedure is medically contraindicated due to her permanent pacemaker and poses a risk of neurological deterioration.
  3. Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): The member completed a 3-month trial of Baclofen (muscle relaxant) and oral corticosteroids (Methylprednisolone taper), satisfying the requirement for a trial of at least two agents.

Consequently, the member fully satisfies all applicable medical necessity criteria under HealthFirst Document CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2, when the Myelopathy Pathway exception is correctly applied.

3. ANALYSIS UNDER CALIFORNIA REGULATORY MANDATES (GOVERNING LAW)
Even if the member had not met the plan's internal criteria, California law prohibits the enforcement of standard conservative treatment prerequisites in this clinical scenario:

- California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Prerequisites): Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. The member's MRI demonstrates severe central cord compression and myelomalacia (T2 signal change), representing severe structural neurological compromise. Furthermore, under CIC § 10169.5(a)(1), the physical therapy requirement is waived because the treating physician documented in writing that further physical therapy was unlikely to benefit the member and was stopped due to pacemaker precautions.
- DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Prior Authorization for Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions): Under APL 22-014, Part II.1, cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI is classified as a time-sensitive condition with potential for irreversible harm. Under Part II.2, plans are prohibited from requiring a full course of conservative treatment when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (myelomalacia), the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression, and surgery is indicated under clinical guidelines. All three conditions are met here.
- California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 (Standard of Review): Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), medical necessity determinations must align with generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersede any more restrictive internal plan criteria.

4. ANALYSIS UNDER GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS
The clinical evidence base strongly supports immediate surgical decompression for this patient and contraindicates further conservative delays:

- AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update): The member's presentation of progressive cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI meets Tier 1 — Strong Indication criteria, where surgical decompression is the recommended primary treatment.
- AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021): These guidelines state that there is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by clinical evidence and risks irreversible neurological deterioration.
- NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020): Recommend surgical decompression as the primary treatment for moderate-to-severe myelopathy, noting that conservative treatment has limited efficacy and is only appropriate for mild, stable cases.
- Cochrane Review (Nikolaidis et al., 2021): Confirms that physical therapy does not produce meaningful functional improvement in patients with moderate-to-severe myelopathy, and that preoperative myelomalacia is the strongest predictor of incomplete neurological recovery if surgery is delayed.

5. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS AND CONCLUSION
In accordance with California regulatory hierarchy, state statutory mandates (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014) and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS and AO Spine Guidelines) take precedence over any restrictive or mechanical application of internal plan criteria. 

The initial reviewer, Dr. Alan Foster, MD, applied standard radiculopathy criteria (6 weeks of PT and an epidural injection) to a patient with progressive cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia. This constitutes a critical clinical and procedural error. The member meets the plan's own Myelopathy Pathway criteria, is legally exempt from further conservative treatment under California law, and meets the highest clinical standard for surgical intervention.

Therefore, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED. The request for ACDF C4-C5 (CPT 22551) at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center is APPROVED.
