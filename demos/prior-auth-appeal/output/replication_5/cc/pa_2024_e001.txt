FINAL DETERMINATION: PARTIAL OVERTURN (MODIFICATION)
- C5-C6 Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (CPT 22551): OVERTURNED (Surgical intervention is APPROVED)
- C6-C7 Cervical Fusion, adjacent level (CPT 22552): UPHELD (Surgical intervention is DENIED without prejudice)

This decision is a final modification of the initial denial issued on November 1, 2024. Below is the detailed clinical and regulatory rationale for this determination.

I. CLINICAL RATIONALE FOR C5-C6 LEVEL (CPT 22551) — OVERTURNED (APPROVED)

The initial denial of the C5-C6 surgical level is overturned because the clinical record demonstrates that the member meets all medical necessity criteria under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 and generally accepted clinical standards.

1. Diagnosis and Imaging Requirements (CC-SPINE-2024 Sections 2.1 & 2.3):
   - The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy. 
   - Current MRI (dated September 15, 2024) demonstrates a large disc herniation at C5-C6 causing moderate-to-severe right C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis.
   - Neurological examination (dated September 20, 2024) reveals objective correlating deficits: right bicep weakness graded at 3+/5, an absent right bicep reflex, and right C6 dermatomal numbness and tingling.

2. Conservative Treatment Requirements (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2):
   - Physical Therapy: The member completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy. Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2), the member reached a documented functional plateau with persistent, severe C6 radicular symptoms.
   - Pharmacotherapy: The member completed trials of two qualifying agents for at least 4 weeks each, including Pregabalin 150mg BID (4 months) and Naproxen 500mg BID (3 months), satisfying Section 2.2(b).
   - Interventional Treatment: The member underwent a right C6 epidural steroid injection on October 15, 2024, which provided 70% temporary relief but symptoms fully returned within 4 weeks, satisfying Section 2.2(c).

Because the clinical findings at C5-C6 demonstrate severe structural pathology, correlating objective neurological deficits, and a documented failure of comprehensive conservative therapy, surgical decompression and fusion at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is medically necessary under CHSC § 1374.32 and is hereby approved.

II. CLINICAL RATIONALE FOR C6-C7 LEVEL (CPT 22552) — UPHELD (DENIED)

The denial of the adjacent C6-C7 level (CPT 22552) is upheld because the clinical record does not support the medical necessity of surgical fusion at this level under CC-SPINE-2024 or generally accepted clinical standards.

1. Lack of Correlating Pathology and Symptoms (CC-SPINE-2024 Sections 2.3 & 3.3):
   - Imaging Findings: The MRI dated September 15, 2024, shows only a small disc bulge and mild foraminal narrowing at C6-C7. There is no identifiable nerve root compression and no spinal cord contact.
   - Clinical Exam Findings: The neurological exam dated September 20, 2024, notes only a "slightly diminished" right tricep reflex. There are no definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms (pain, numbness, or weakness in the C7 distribution). The treating physician's notes indicate that mild C7 symptoms were "possibly present but not clearly documented."
   - Conservative Treatment Response: Physical therapy records at week 8 explicitly document that C7-related symptoms were "minimal and possibly resolving."

2. Addressing the Treating Surgeon's Argument on Adjacent Segment Degeneration (ASD):
   - Dr. Park argued in the appeal that both levels should be addressed simultaneously to prevent accelerated adjacent segment degeneration. 
   - Response and Clinical Evidence: Under generally accepted clinical standards, including the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020) and the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023), prophylactic fusion of adjacent levels with mild, asymptomatic, or resolving pathology is not clinically supported. 
   - Clinical evidence demonstrates that performing a prophylactic fusion at an adjacent level (C6-C7) with mild imaging findings and no correlating radiculopathy does not improve long-term clinical outcomes. Instead, fusions at asymptomatic levels unnecessarily increase surgical morbidity, including prolonged operative time, increased blood loss, and an elevated risk of pseudarthrosis (non-union). 
   - Fusing a level without correlating clinical and imaging pathology is explicitly excluded under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 3.3.

Therefore, the request for C6-C7 fusion (CPT 22552) is denied. This denial is issued without prejudice to a future request should the member experience objective clinical deterioration, progressive neurological deficits, or new correlating imaging findings at the C6-C7 level.

III. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY ALIGNMENT AND HIERARCHY

This determination has been structured in strict compliance with California regulatory requirements:
1. Definitive Determination (CHSC § 1367.01): To prevent administrative delays and avoid a "deemed approval" of unsupported services under CHSC § 1367.01, this plan issues a definitive Partial Overturn (Modification) rather than an administrative remand.
2. Medical Necessity Standard (CHSC § 1374.32): The plan's initial blanket denial of the entire procedure was modified because fusions must be evaluated on a level-by-level basis. Fusing C5-C6 is supported by generally accepted clinical standards, whereas fusing C6-C7 is not.
3. Physical Therapy Protections (CIC § 10169.5): The plan acknowledges that the member has fully satisfied the physical therapy requirements for the symptomatic C5-C6 level by reaching a functional plateau.

IV. NOTICE OF INDEPENDENT MEDICAL REVIEW (IMR) RIGHTS

If you disagree with the portion of this decision that upholds the denial of the C6-C7 level (CPT 22552), you have the right to request an Independent Medical Review (IMR) from the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC).

An IMR is an intensive review of your case by independent, board-certified specialist physicians who are not associated with HealthFirst. If the IMR reviewer decides that the C6-C7 fusion is medically necessary under generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst must authorize the service, regardless of our internal plan criteria.

How to Apply for IMR:
- You will find an IMR Application Form enclosed with this letter.
- You must submit your completed application to the DMHC within 180 days of receiving this final determination.
- There is no fee or cost to you or your physician for an IMR.
- For urgent cases involving an imminent and serious threat to your health, you may request an expedited IMR, which is decided within 3 days.

For questions regarding your IMR rights or to request assistance, you may contact the DMHC Help Center at 1-888-466-2219 or visit their website at www.dmhc.ca.gov.
