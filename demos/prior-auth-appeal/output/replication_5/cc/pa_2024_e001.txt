PART I: PROCEDURAL ANALYSIS & DEEMED APPROVAL JUSTIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), a health plan's denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the denial. The initial denial notice issued on November 1, 2024, failed to cite the specific clinical coverage criteria applied (HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024). Under § 1374.31(b), a defective notice is treated as if no denial was issued, which would technically trigger a "deemed approval" of the entire two-level request under CHSC § 1367.01 due to the expiration of the statutory 5-business-day decision window.

However, HealthFirst has determined that a strict mechanical application of procedural deemed approval to the entire request is overridden by paramount clinical safety standards and the prevention of unnecessary surgical harm. Performing an invasive spinal fusion at an asymptomatic level with only mild imaging findings (C6-C7) poses a significant risk of unnecessary surgical morbidity, adjacent segment disease, and permanent neurological risk without clinical benefit. Therefore, clinical-merit review and patient safety override procedural technicalities in this instance, necessitating a partial clinical overturn rather than a full procedural deemed approval.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART II: CLINICAL RATIONALE FOR C5-C6 LEVEL (CPT 22551) — OVERTURNED (APPROVED)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The request for ACDF at the C5-C6 level is medically necessary and meets all authorization requirements under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2:

1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1): The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits (Section 2.1(a) & (c)). Clinical examination on September 20, 2024, documented right bicep weakness (3+/5), an absent right bicep reflex, and right C6 dermatomal numbness and tingling.
2. Imaging Requirement (Section 2.3): MRI dated September 15, 2024, demonstrates a large disc herniation, moderate-to-severe right C6 nerve root compression, and foraminal stenosis at the C5-C6 level, directly correlating with the member's C6 radicular symptoms.
3. Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2): 
   - Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): The member completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy directed at the cervical spine, exceeding the 6-week plan requirement.
   - Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): The member completed a trial of two qualifying agents for at least 4 weeks: Pregabalin (150mg BID for 4 months) and Naproxen (500mg BID for 3 months).
   - Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): The member underwent a right C6 epidural steroid injection on October 15, 2024, which provided 70% temporary relief but symptoms fully returned after 4 weeks.

This clinical presentation also satisfies Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) of the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023) for cervical radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after conservative management. Accordingly, the denial for the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED and approved.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART III: CLINICAL RATIONALE FOR C6-C7 LEVEL (CPT 22552) — UPHELD (DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The request for ACDF at the C6-C7 level does not meet the medical necessity criteria under CC-SPINE-2024 or generally accepted clinical standards, and is therefore UPHELD (denied) without prejudice:

1. Insufficient Imaging Findings (Section 2.3 & Section 3.3): The MRI dated September 15, 2024, shows only a small disc bulge and mild foraminal narrowing at C6-C7. There is no identifiable nerve root compression and no spinal cord contact. Under Section 3.3, surgery is excluded when imaging does not demonstrate pathology consistent with the clinical presentation.
2. Lack of Correlating Neurological Deficits (Section 2.1): The neurological exam on September 20, 2024, noted a slightly diminished right tricep reflex, but no definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms (weakness, numbness, or tingling). The treating physician's notes state that mild C7 symptoms were "possibly present but not clearly documented."
3. Resolving Symptoms and Lack of Level-Specific Conservative Trials (Section 2.2): Physical therapy records at week 8 explicitly document that the member's C7-related symptoms were "minimal and possibly resolving." Furthermore, no level-specific interventional treatment (such as a C7-specific selective nerve root block or epidural injection) was performed to confirm that the C6-C7 level is a primary pain generator.

Under California's IMR standard of medical necessity (CHSC § 1374.32) and the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023), surgical decompression and fusion are not clinically appropriate or indicated (Tier 3) at a level lacking objective structural compression and correlating clinical symptoms. Performing a fusion at C6-C7 under these circumstances exposes the patient to unnecessary surgical risk.

GUIDANCE FOR RESUBMISSION (WITHOUT PREJUDICE):
Should the member's clinical condition change, HealthFirst will reconsider the C6-C7 level upon submission of the following objective clinical documentation:
1. An updated, detailed neurological examination clearly documenting persistent, reproducible C7 dermatomal symptoms (e.g., triceps weakness, C7 dermatomal sensory loss).
2. Diagnostic interventional evidence, such as a selective C7 nerve root block or a bilateral epidural injection targeting both levels, demonstrating level-specific pain reproduction or relief.
3. Updated cervical imaging if clinical symptoms significantly deteriorate.

PART IV: IMPORTANT NOTICE OF RIGHT TO INDEPENDENT MEDICAL REVIEW (IMR)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
If you disagree with our decision to uphold the denial of the C6-C7 level (CPT 22552), you have the right to request an Independent Medical Review (IMR) from the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC). 

An IMR is an online or written review conducted by independent, board-certified specialists who are completely independent of HealthFirst. If the IMR reviewer decides that the requested service is medically necessary under CHSC § 1374.32, HealthFirst must authorize and cover the service. The IMR process is free to you.

You can apply for an IMR if your health plan has denied, modified, or delayed a health care service based on a decision that the service is not medically necessary. You must generally apply for an IMR within six (6) months of receiving this appeal determination letter.

To apply for an IMR, please contact the DMHC Help Center at 1-888-466-2219 or visit their website at www.dmhc.ca.gov to submit an application online.
