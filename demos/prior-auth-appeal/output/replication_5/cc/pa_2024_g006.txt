Dear Dr. Osei and Ms. Kamau,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) at UCSF Medical Center. After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial denial is hereby OVERTURNED. Authorization is granted for the requested procedure.

Below is the detailed clinical and legal rationale for this determination.

1. PROCEDURAL DEFECT OF THE INITIAL DENIAL (CHSC § 1374.31)
The initial denial issued on November 20, 2024, was based on the member's "prior authorization denial history" (specifically, a denial of a three-level fusion in May 2024). The denial letter stated that the current request did not demonstrate a sufficient change in clinical status to warrant reconsideration.

Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), a denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reason for the denial. A notice that fails to do so is procedurally defective. "Prior PA history" is not an authorized clinical criterion under HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery (CC-SPINE-2024). Each prior authorization request must be evaluated independently on its own merits based on the member's current clinical status. 

Furthermore, the prior denial in May 2024 was for a different, far more invasive procedure (a three-level fusion at C4-C5, C5-C6, and C6-C7). In that prior review, the medical director explicitly noted that a single-level C5-C6 fusion would have been approved at that time because the pathology was localized to that level. The current request is precisely for the single-level C5-C6 ACDF that was previously endorsed. Denying the current request based on a prior denial for a different procedure is procedurally and clinically invalid.

2. EVALUATION OF PLAN CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024) AND ACKNOWLEDGMENT OF GAPS
Under CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2, authorization for cervical spinal surgery requires the satisfaction of specific diagnostic, conservative treatment, and imaging criteria.

A. Diagnosis and Imaging Requirements (Sections 2.1 and 2.3):
The member has a confirmed diagnosis of progressive cervical radiculopathy C5-C6 (Section 2.1(a)). Current imaging (MRI dated October 15, 2024) demonstrates a large C5-C6 disc herniation with moderate-to-severe right C6 nerve root compression and a documented interval increase in size from the prior MRI dated April 20, 2024, along with new foraminal stenosis at C5-C6. This satisfies the diagnostic and imaging requirements of Sections 2.1 and 2.3.

B. Physical Therapy Requirement (Section 2.2(a)):
The clinical record contains a minor typographical discrepancy: the "clinical_change_since_prior_denial" field notes that a new 8-week PT course was completed in "October 2024," whereas the detailed "physical_therapy_summary" states it was completed "July-September 2024." We clarify that the 8-week PT course (consisting of 24 supervised sessions) was completed in September 2024, and a functional plateau was explicitly documented ("no further improvement expected, neurological deficit persistent and worsening"). This fully satisfies the 6-week minimum supervised physical therapy requirement under Section 2.2(a).

C. Interventional Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2(c)):
The member completed a right C6 epidural steroid injection on November 5, 2024, which provided 50% temporary relief for 2 weeks before symptoms returned to baseline. This satisfies Section 2.2(c).

D. Pharmacotherapy Requirement (Section 2.2(b)) — Acknowledged Gap:
Section 2.2(b) of CC-SPINE-2024 requires a trial of at least two pharmacotherapy agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The clinical record shows the member trialed Gabapentin (600mg TID) for 6 months, which satisfies the requirement for the first agent. However, the second agent trialed was Methylprednisolone, administered as "2 courses." Because a standard oral corticosteroid dose pack is a 6-day course, two courses total approximately 12 days. This falls short of the plan's 4-week minimum duration requirement. No other second agent was trialed for 4 weeks. Therefore, the member did not technically satisfy the plan's internal pharmacotherapy criteria under Section 2.2(b).

3. CLINICAL STANDARDS OVERRIDING PLAN CRITERIA (CHSC § 1374.32 & CIC § 10169.5)
While the plan's internal pharmacotherapy criteria were not technically met, California law dictates that health plan criteria cannot be applied mechanically when doing so violates generally accepted standards of medical practice or poses a risk of irreversible harm.

Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1) and (3), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of conservative treatment (such as physical therapy or extended drug trials) where the treating physician documents that such treatment is unlikely to benefit the member, or where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise. Furthermore, CHSC § 1374.32(b) establishes that the standard for medical necessity is governed by generally accepted standards of medical practice, which override a plan's internal criteria if those criteria are more restrictive or clinically inappropriate for the patient's specific presentation.

The member's neurological examination on October 20, 2024, revealed significant and progressive neurological deficits:
- Right C6 dermatomal numbness
- Absent right bicep reflex
- Right bicep motor weakness of 3+/5, representing an interval worsening from the prior examination.

According to the American Association of Neurological Surgeons and the Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine Guidelines (2023 Update) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Clinical Guidelines (2020), surgical decompression is strongly indicated (Tier 1 - Strong Indication) for patients presenting with progressive motor deficits and correlating structural nerve root compression on MRI. 

Delaying necessary surgical decompression to force the member to undergo an additional 2.5 weeks of a second pharmacotherapy agent (to satisfy the plan's arbitrary 4-week trial requirement) is highly clinically contraindicated. In the presence of active, progressive motor weakness (3+/5 bicep strength), further delay of surgical decompression violates the standard of care and exposes the member to an unacceptable risk of permanent, irreversible neurological damage and long-term functional disability.

Therefore, under CHSC § 1374.32 and CIC § 10169.5, generally accepted clinical standards override the plan's restrictive pharmacotherapy duration requirement.

4. FINAL DETERMINATION
Because the initial denial was procedurally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b), and because forcing further conservative pharmacotherapy trials in the face of progressive motor deficits is clinically contraindicated and legally unauthorized under CIC § 10169.5 and CHSC § 1374.32, the denial of single-level ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED.

HealthFirst Health Plan hereby authorizes CPT 22551 for member Theresa Kamau, to be performed by Dr. Victor Osei, MD, at UCSF Medical Center. This authorization is valid for 90 days from the date of this letter (expiring March 5, 2025).
