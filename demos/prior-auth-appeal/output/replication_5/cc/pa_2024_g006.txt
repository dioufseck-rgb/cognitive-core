Dear Ms. Kamau and Dr. Osei,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its clinical and regulatory review of the first-level appeal submitted on December 5, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial of a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) at UCSF Medical Center. 

After a comprehensive, independent review of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), the California Regulatory Framework, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has determined that the initial denial was procedurally defective and clinically unsubstantiated. Accordingly, the initial denial is OVERTURNED, and the requested procedure is APPROVED. 

Below is the detailed clinical and legal rationale supporting this determination.


I. PROCEDURAL REVIEW & DEFECT ANALYSIS

1. Invalid Denial Basis & Procedural Defect under California Law:
The initial denial issued on November 20, 2024, cited "prior authorization denial history" (specifically, a three-level fusion denied in May 2024) as the primary basis for denial, asserting that the member's cervical pathology had not "materially changed" to warrant reconsideration. 

Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), a health plan's denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the denial. A denial notice that fails to do so is legally DEFECTIVE. Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria Document CC-SPINE-2024, "prior PA history" is not an authorized clinical criterion, exclusion, or limitation. By denying the current request based on prior history rather than evaluating the clinical merits of the current request against CC-SPINE-2024, the plan issued a procedurally defective denial under CHSC § 1374.31(b).

2. Irrelevance of Prior PA History:
Each prior authorization request must be evaluated independently based on current clinical criteria and the member's current clinical status. The prior request in May 2024 was for a three-level cervical fusion (C4-C5, C5-C6, C6-C7), which was correctly denied at that time because the C4-C5 and C6-C7 levels did not meet medical necessity criteria. 

Crucially, the prior reviewer's documentation from May 15, 2024, explicitly noted that "single-level C5-C6 would have been approved at that time." The current request is for exactly that single-level procedure (ACDF C5-C6). Treating a prior denial for a different, more invasive procedure as a basis to deny a current, less invasive, and previously endorsed procedure is clinically and procedurally invalid.


II. CLINICAL CRITERIA COMPLIANCE ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)

An independent, rule-by-rule evaluation of the current clinical record against HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 demonstrates that the member satisfies all medical necessity requirements for single-level ACDF (CPT 22551):

1. Section 2.1 — Diagnosis Requirement: MET
* Criteria: Confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (nerve root compression with radicular symptoms such as pain, numbness, or weakness in a dermatomal distribution), supported by an MRI within 6 months demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms.
* Clinical Evidence: The clinical record documents a presenting diagnosis of progressive cervical radiculopathy at C5-C6. The neurological exam dated October 20, 2024, reveals objective, correlating neurological deficits: right C6 dermatomal numbness, an absent right bicep reflex, and right bicep weakness graded at 3+/5 (representing an interval worsening from the prior exam). This is supported by a current MRI dated October 15, 2024 (within 6 months of the request), demonstrating a large C5-C6 disc herniation with moderate-to-severe right C6 nerve root compression and new foraminal stenosis.

2. Section 2.2 — Conservative Treatment Requirement: MET
* Criteria (a) Physical Therapy: Minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy with documented functional outcomes directed toward the cervical condition.
  * Clinical Evidence: The member completed a new, separate 8-week course of supervised physical therapy (24 sessions) from July through September 2024. The physical therapy records explicitly document a functional plateau: "no further improvement expected, neurological deficit persistent and worsening."
* Criteria (b) Pharmacotherapy: Trial of at least two authorized drug classes (NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
  * Clinical Evidence: The member completed a 6-month trial of Gabapentin 600mg TID (neuropathic agent) with only partial pain relief and persistent deficits, and completed two courses of Methylprednisolone dose packs (oral corticosteroids) which provided temporary relief only. Both trials exceeded the 4-week minimum requirement.
* Criteria (c) Interventional Treatment: Minimum of one cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point injection series.
  * Clinical Evidence: The member underwent a right C6 epidural steroid injection on November 5, 2024, which provided 50% pain relief for 2 weeks before symptoms returned to baseline.

3. Section 2.3 — Imaging Requirement: MET
* Criteria: Current imaging (within 6 months) demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms and the proposed surgical level.
  * Clinical Evidence: The current MRI dated October 15, 2024, demonstrates a large disc herniation at C5-C6 with moderate-to-severe right C6 nerve root compression and new foraminal stenosis. This structural pathology directly correlates with the member's right C6 dermatomal numbness, absent right bicep reflex, and 3+/5 bicep weakness.

4. Section 2.4 — Physician Documentation: MET
* Criteria: Treating physician must provide an attestation of conservative treatment completion, clinical rationale, description of functional limitations, and statement of risks/alternatives.
  * Clinical Evidence: Dr. Victor Osei provided comprehensive documentation, including a detailed clinical rationale for single-level decompression, documentation of severe functional limitations (pain rated at 8/10 at rest, inability to continue working full-time), and a discussion of surgical risks and alternatives.


III. REGULATORY COMPLIANCE & CLINICAL STANDARDS

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 Compliance:
Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), a health plan shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy if the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms. The member's documented functional plateau after 8 weeks of physical therapy legally and clinically satisfies the physical therapy prerequisite. The plan's mechanical denial ignoring this plateau violates CIC § 10169.5.

2. Alignment with Generally Accepted Standards of Medical Practice:
Under CHSC § 1374.32, the standard of review for medical necessity is determined by generally accepted standards of medical practice. 
* The AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Course (2023 Update) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Clinical Guidelines (2020) state that surgical decompression (ACDF) is strongly indicated (Tier 1/Tier 2) for patients with cervical radiculopathy who exhibit progressive neurological deficits (such as worsening 3+/5 motor weakness and absent reflexes) and structural nerve root compression that has failed to respond to conservative management.
* The member's clinical presentation represents a clear indication for surgical intervention under these national guidelines. Delaying surgery in the presence of a progressive motor deficit (3+/5 bicep weakness) poses a severe risk of permanent, irreversible neurological damage.


IV. AUTHORIZATION DETAILS
