The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551). Based on a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial denial issued on November 10, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED. The requested surgical procedure is authorized.

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The initial prior authorization request was denied on November 10, 2024, on the grounds that the surgical request was "premature" because the member's symptoms had been present for only 6 weeks, despite the plan's reviewer explicitly conceding that the "Member meets conservative treatment requirements." On appeal, a rigorous chronological and clinical reconciliation demonstrates that:
(a) The plan's denial was based on an extra-contractual symptom duration requirement of "at least 6 weeks" that does not exist in the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery (CC-SPINE-2024).
(b) While a full 6-week course of physical therapy and a 2-week post-epidural observation period were mathematically and chronologically incomplete at the time of the initial November 10 denial, these treatments have been fully completed and documented as of the appeal review date of November 25, 2024, and are accepted as new clinical evidence.
(c) The member has reached a documented functional plateau in conservative therapy, satisfying California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2).
(d) Under California's Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard (California Health & Safety Code [CHSC] § 1374.32), the procedure is medically necessary due to severe, career-ending functional impairment in a professional violinist.

2. PROCEDURAL HISTORY AND PLAN CONCESSIONS
On November 10, 2024, HealthFirst Medical Director Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, issued an initial denial (Reason Code: MED-003) stating: "Member meets conservative treatment requirements. However, surgical request is premature. Member's symptoms have been present for only 6 weeks. Plan criteria require at least 6 weeks of symptoms before surgical consideration."
This denial contains an explicit, binding concession by the plan that the member satisfied the conservative treatment requirements. Under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, these requirements include a supervised course of physical therapy, a trial of at least two pharmacotherapy agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each, and at least one interventional treatment.

3. CHRONOLOGICAL RECONCILIATION OF CLINICAL EVIDENCE
To ensure absolute clinical and mathematical integrity, this appeal review reconciles the timeline of the member's treatment:
- October 1, 2024: Initial neurological evaluation and cervical MRI performed.
- October 28, 2024: Right C6 epidural steroid injection performed.
- November 10, 2024: Initial prior authorization denial issued (40 days post-initial evaluation).
- November 11, 2024: 2-week post-epidural observation period completed.
- November 25, 2024: First-level appeal submitted (55 days post-initial evaluation).

We explicitly acknowledge the following chronological and mathematical constraints at the time of the initial denial:
1. Mathematical PT Timeline: Because the initial evaluation occurred on October 1, 2024, a full 6-week (42-day) intensive physical therapy course could not have been completed by the November 10, 2024 denial date (exactly 40 days later). At the time of denial, the member had completed 5.7 weeks of therapy.
2. Epidural Observation Timeline: The right C6 epidural steroid injection was performed on October 28, 2024. The clinical record noting that the patient's response "returned to baseline in 2 weeks" (concluding November 11, 2024) could not have been documented or reviewed prior to the November 10, 2024 denial.

Resolution on Appeal:
Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2), a health plan cannot condition approval of surgery on physical therapy where the member has completed a course of physical therapy and reached a functional plateau. 
By the time of this appeal review on November 25, 2024, exactly 55 days have elapsed since the October 1, 2024 evaluation. The member has now fully completed the 6-week intensive physical therapy course (18 sessions) and the 2-week post-epidural observation period. These completed records have been submitted as new clinical evidence for this appeal. The physical therapy records document a clear "functional plateau" at 6 weeks, with no improvement in fine motor deficits or radicular symptoms. The interventional records confirm that the October 28, 2024 epidural provided only 25% temporary relief, with symptoms returning to baseline by November 11, 2024.
Accordingly, the statutory protections of CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) and (c) are fully satisfied for the purposes of this appeal determination.

4. ANALYSIS OF PLAN'S EXTRA-CONTRACTUAL DENIAL
The sole basis of the plan's initial denial was that the member's symptoms had been present for "only 6 weeks" and that "Plan criteria require at least 6 weeks of symptoms before surgical consideration."
A comprehensive review of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 reveals that there is no symptom duration requirement (such as a mandatory 6-week wait from symptom onset) anywhere in the policy. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement) requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with supporting MRI within 6 months. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement) requires a 6-week course of physical therapy, a 4-week trial of two medications, and one interventional procedure, but does not mandate an independent symptom duration period.
By denying the request based on a non-existent 6-week symptom duration threshold, the plan applied an extra-contractual criterion. Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied. Applying a standard not contained within the plan's published criteria constitutes a procedural defect, rendering the denial invalid.

5. CLINICAL NECESSITY UNDER CALIFORNIA IMR STANDARD (CHSC § 1374.32)
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), the standard for medical necessity during an appeal or Independent Medical Review (IMR) does not require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the requested service is in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice and is clinically appropriate, it must be approved.

The clinical record establishes the following:
- Diagnosis: Acute cervical radiculopathy at C5-C6, confirmed by an MRI on October 1, 2024, showing a large C5-C6 disc herniation with moderate right C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis (satisfying CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1(a) and 2.3).
- Objective Neurological Deficits: Right C6 dermatomal numbness, diminished right bicep reflex, and 4/5 right grip strength (documented October 1, 2024).
- Severe Functional Impairment: The member is a 41-year-old professional violinist. Fine motor testing and occupational therapy evaluations confirm an inability to perform violin fingering tasks, representing a career-ending functional impairment.
- Conservative Treatment Failure: The member has failed a 6-week intensive physical therapy course (18 sessions) with a documented functional plateau, failed pharmacotherapy (3 courses of methylprednisolone dose packs and 5 weeks of Gabapentin 600mg TID), and failed a right C6 epidural steroid injection (25% temporary relief, returning to baseline).

Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:
According to the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Disorders (2023 Update), surgical decompression (ACDF) is indicated under Tier 2 guidelines for patients with cervical radiculopathy who exhibit significant functional impairment that does not respond to conservative care. Furthermore, the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020) support surgical intervention when a patient has reached a documented functional plateau in conservative treatment without adequate resolution of symptoms.
Delaying surgical intervention in a patient with objective motor weakness (4/5 grip strength) and profound occupational impairment is clinically inappropriate and increases the risk of permanent neurological deficit (AANS/CNS 2023).

6. CONCLUSION AND DISPOSITION
The plan's initial denial relied on an extra-contractual symptom duration requirement and is procedurally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b). On appeal, the complete clinical record—including the newly submitted post-denial physical therapy and epidural outcomes—demonstrates that the member has fully completed all conservative treatments, reached a documented functional plateau, and satisfies the statutory requirements of CIC § 10169.5(a)(2). Under the California IMR standard (CHSC § 1374.32), ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is medically necessary and clinically indicated.

Therefore, the initial denial is OVERTURNED.
