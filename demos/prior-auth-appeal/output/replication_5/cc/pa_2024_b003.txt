Dear Angela Morrison and Dr. Paul Nguyen,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of the first-level clinical appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C3-C4 level (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial denial issued on October 1, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED. The requested procedure is APPROVED.

I. CLINICAL HISTORY AND PRESENTING PRESENTATION
The member is a 67-year-old female with a history of a successful C4-C5 ACDF performed in 2019. She presents with adjacent segment disease at the C3-C4 level, manifesting as new-onset (since 2023) left C4 radiculopathy. A neurological examination dated August 20, 2024, documents objective neurological deficits in the left C4 dermatomal distribution, including shoulder and lateral arm numbness, significant left deltoid weakness graded at 3+/5, and a diminished left bicep reflex.

The member has completed an exhaustive course of conservative treatment, which includes:
1. Physical Therapy: 10 weeks (30 sessions) of supervised physical therapy with no improvement in left C4 radicular symptoms.
2. Pharmacotherapy: Pregabalin 300mg BID for 6 months (providing only partial relief) and Duloxetine 60mg for 3 months (providing minimal benefit).
3. Interventional Treatment: A left C4 epidural steroid injection on September 25, 2024, which provided 60% initial relief but returned to baseline by week 3.

II. RESOLUTION OF IMAGING CONFLICT
The initial denial on October 1, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, was based on the finding that "imaging does not show sufficient pathology at C3-C4 to support surgical intervention," citing an MRI dated August 15, 2024, which showed only "mild degenerative changes."

However, a critical review of the imaging reports reveals a significant clinical conflict that the initial reviewer failed to resolve:
1. MRI (August 15, 2024): The radiologist explicitly noted "SIGNIFICANT ARTIFACT from C4-C5 hardware limiting evaluation" and stated that they "cannot exclude moderate compression due to artifact."
2. CT Myelogram (September 10, 2024): Because of the hardware artifact on the MRI, Dr. Nguyen appropriately ordered a CT myelogram. This definitive study confirmed "moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression" and "significant foraminal stenosis" at C3-C4. The radiologist explicitly noted that the "MRI underestimated pathology due to hardware artifact from prior fusion."

Under clinical and regulatory standards, when a standard imaging study (MRI) is compromised by hardware artifact, a secondary definitive study (CT myelogram) must be utilized to assess pathology. Relying solely on the compromised MRI while ignoring the definitive CT myelogram constitutes a clear clinical and procedural error.

III. PLAN CRITERIA COMPLIANCE ANALYSIS
The request was evaluated against HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria: Spinal Surgery — Cervical Procedures (Document: CC-SPINE-2024).

1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): Satisfied. The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy, characterized by left C4 nerve root compression with corresponding radicular symptoms (shoulder/arm numbness and 3+/5 deltoid weakness). Section 2.1 explicitly accepts either an "MRI or CT myelogram within 6 months of request demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms." The CT myelogram dated September 10, 2024, satisfies this requirement.
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): Satisfied. The member completed 10 weeks of PT (exceeding the 6-week requirement in 2.2(a)), trialed two neuropathic agents for over 4 weeks each (satisfying 2.2(b)), and underwent a left C4 epidural steroid injection (satisfying 2.2(c)). Furthermore, the initial denial notice explicitly conceded that the "Member meets conservative treatment requirements."
3. Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement): Satisfied. The CT myelogram dated September 10, 2024 (within 6 months of the request) demonstrates moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression and significant foraminal stenosis, which is directly consistent with the member's left C4 radicular symptoms and the proposed surgical level (C3-C4).
4. Section 2.4 (Physician Documentation): Satisfied. Dr. Nguyen provided the required clinical rationale, description of functional limitations, and surgical risk discussion.

IV. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY COMPLIANCE ANALYSIS
Because the member is a resident of California, this appeal is subject to the California Regulatory Framework:

1. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b): Under California law, a denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the denial. The initial denial notice was procedurally defective because it ignored the definitive CT myelogram in the medical record and relied entirely on an MRI report that was explicitly documented as unreliable due to hardware artifact.
2. CHSC § 1374.32 (Standard of Review for Independent Medical Review): Under California's statutory standard, "medically necessary" means services that a prudent physician would provide to treat an illness or injury in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice and clinically appropriate in terms of type and site. Dr. Nguyen's recommendation for ACDF C3-C4 is clinically appropriate and meets this standard. CHSC § 1374.32(b) mandates that the clinical standard overrides any restrictive plan criteria.
3. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5: This statute prohibits plans from mechanically applying physical therapy requirements when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. The member's 3+/5 deltoid weakness and moderate-to-severe nerve root compression represent objective structural compromise, further supporting the immediate necessity of surgical decompression.

V. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND GUIDELINES COMPLIANCE
The requested procedure is highly supported by generally accepted standards of medical practice:
1. AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023 Update): The member meets Tier 2 surgical indications for cervical radiculopathy due to persistent radicular symptoms, objective neurological deficits (3+/5 motor weakness), and failure to respond to comprehensive conservative management (PT, oral medications, and epidural injection).
2. NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020): Decompression and fusion are strongly recommended for patients with symptomatic cervical radiculopathy from foraminal stenosis who have failed conservative therapy and exhibit progressive or persistent neurological deficits.
3. Clinical Consensus on Hardware Artifact: It is a universally accepted clinical standard that MRI findings are frequently underestimated in the presence of metallic spinal hardware. In such cases, a CT myelogram is the gold standard for evaluating nerve root and spinal cord compression. Relying on the MRI in this context is contrary to standard neurosurgical and radiological practice.

VI. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION
Based on the clinical evidence, the CT myelogram dated September 10, 2024, demonstrates severe structural pathology at C3-C4 that correlates perfectly with the member's left C4 radiculopathy and objective motor weakness. The member has exhausted all conservative treatment options.

Therefore, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED.
