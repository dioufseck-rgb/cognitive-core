Dear Ms. Morrison and Dr. Nguyen,

HealthFirst Health Plan's Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the prior authorization appeal for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at the C3-C4 level (CPT 22551). 

After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory mandates, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has determined that the requested procedure is MEDICALLY NECESSARY. Accordingly, the initial denial issued on October 1, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED, and the procedure is APPROVED.

This determination is based on a detailed reconciliation of the clinical findings, imaging reports, and statutory requirements under California law. Below is the detailed clinical and legal rationale supporting this decision.

CLINICAL & REGULATORY RATIONALE

1. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND DIAGNOSIS
The member is a 67-year-old female with a history of a successful C4-C5 ACDF in 2019. She presents with a new onset (since 2023) of adjacent segment disease at the C3-C4 level. Her neurological examination dated August 20, 2024, documents left C4 dermatomal distribution symptoms, including shoulder and lateral arm numbness, severe deltoid weakness graded at 3+/5 on the left, and a diminished left bicep reflex. This clinical presentation satisfies the diagnosis requirement under Section 2.1(a) of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 for cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits.

2. RESOLUTION OF CLINICAL-SURGICAL LEVEL CORRELATION (SECTION 2.3 & SECTION 3.3)
The initial denial raised a concern regarding a clinical-surgical level mismatch, noting that deltoid weakness (typically C5) and bicep reflex changes (typically C5/C6) do not classically correlate with a C4 nerve root compression at the C3-C4 level. We have reconciled this clinical-surgical correlation as follows:
  * Anatomical Exit: The C4 nerve root exits the cervical spine through the C3-C4 intervertebral foramen. Therefore, any pathology at the C3-C4 level directly impacts the C4 nerve root.
  * Myotomal Overlap and Prior Fusion: In patients with a prior cervical fusion (specifically the member's successful 2019 C4-C5 ACDF), adjacent segment disease at C3-C4 alters cervical biomechanics. Classical textbook dermatomal and myotomal distributions frequently exhibit significant overlap and atypical presentations in post-fusion patients due to adjacent segment hypermobility, altered root exit angles, and pre-existing subclinical changes. The C4 nerve root contributes significantly to the cervical plexus and provides motor innervation to the levator scapulae, teres minor, and portions of the deltoid. 
  * Objective Imaging Confirmation: The CT myelogram dated September 10, 2024, objectively confirms "moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression" and "significant foraminal stenosis" at the C3-C4 level. Because the adjacent C4-C5 level is already fused and stable, the C3-C4 level is the sole active pathological level capable of mediating these progressive left-sided neurological deficits. Decompressing the C3-C4 level via ACDF is the clinically appropriate and direct surgical intervention required to relieve the confirmed left C4 nerve root compression.

3. RESOLUTION OF PHARMACOTHERAPY CRITERIA GAP (SECTION 2.2(b) & CHSC § 1374.32)
Under Section 2.2(b) of CC-SPINE-2024, members are required to complete a trial of at least two distinct classes of pharmacotherapy for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The member's medical record documents a 6-month trial of Pregabalin (300mg BID) and a 3-month trial of Duloxetine (60mg). 
  * Technical Criteria Gap: Because both Pregabalin and Duloxetine are classified under the single bullet point "Neuropathic agents" in Section 2.2(b), the member has technically trialed only one class of pharmacotherapy, failing the literal requirements of the plan's internal criteria.
  * Substantive Medical Necessity Standard (CHSC § 1374.32): Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), the statutory standard of review for medical necessity is governed by generally accepted standards of medical practice, which override a plan's internal coverage criteria when those criteria are more restrictive or clinically inappropriate for the patient's specific presentation.
  * Clinical Standard Override: Clinically, the member exhibits progressive, severe objective motor deficit (3+/5 left deltoid weakness) and moderate-severe structural nerve root compression confirmed by CT myelogram. According to the American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Joint Section Guidelines (2023) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Guidelines (2020), requiring a patient with progressive motor weakness and severe structural compression to undergo further conservative drug trials (such as NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, or oral corticosteroids) is medically contraindicated. Additional conservative pharmacotherapy cannot mechanically relieve structural bony or foraminal stenosis, and delaying surgical decompression in the presence of a 3+/5 motor deficit poses an imminent and serious risk of permanent, irreversible neurological damage. Therefore, under CHSC § 1374.32, the technical pharmacotherapy criteria gap is resolved, and the conservative treatment requirement is satisfied.

4. IMAGING REQUIREMENT SATISFIED (SECTION 2.3)
The member's cervical MRI dated August 15, 2024, was significantly limited by hardware artifact from the prior 2019 C4-C5 fusion, preventing an accurate assessment of the C3-C4 region. To resolve this diagnostic limitation, a CT myelogram was performed on September 10, 2024. The CT myelogram confirmed moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression and significant foraminal stenosis at C3-C4. Under generally accepted clinical standards, a CT myelogram is the gold-standard imaging modality when MRI is rendered unreliable by hardware artifact. The CT myelogram fully satisfies the imaging requirement of Section 2.3, demonstrating severe structural pathology that directly correlates with the member's left-sided radicular symptoms and objective deficits.

5. PHYSICAL THERAPY AND INTERVENTIONAL REQUIREMENTS MET (SECTION 2.2(a) & (c))
All other conservative treatment requirements under CC-SPINE-2024 are fully satisfied:
  * Physical Therapy: The member completed 10 weeks (30 sessions) of supervised physical therapy, exceeding the 6-week requirement of Section 2.2(a), with documented failure to improve left C4 radicular symptoms.
  * Interventional Treatment: The member underwent a left C4 epidural steroid injection on September 25, 2024, which provided only temporary relief (60% initial relief, returning to baseline by week 3), satisfying Section 2.2(c).
