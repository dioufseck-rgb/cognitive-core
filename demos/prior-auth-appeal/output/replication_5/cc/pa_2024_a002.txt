Dear Mr. Webb,

The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the prior authorization appeal submitted on your behalf by Dr. Sarah Chen, MD, regarding the denial of coverage for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C6-C7 level (CPT 22551) at UCSF Medical Center.

After a comprehensive review of your clinical records, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California statutory and regulatory mandates, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has determined that the initial prior authorization denial must be OVERTURNED. The requested surgical procedure is approved.

Below is a detailed explanation of the clinical, regulatory, and legal bases for this determination.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND BACKGROUND
Mr. Marcus Webb is a 47-year-old male who presented on July 1, 2024, with a clinical history of bilateral hand weakness, coordination problems, and upper motor neuron (UMN) signs. A comprehensive neurological examination on July 1, 2024, documented:
- Bilateral grip weakness graded at 3/5;
- Bilateral positive Hoffman sign;
- Bilateral upper extremity hyperreflexia;
- Impaired tandem gait.

An MRI of the cervical spine performed on July 10, 2024, revealed a C6-C7 disc herniation causing moderate spinal cord compression, accompanied by T2 signal changes within the spinal cord consistent with early myelomalacia.

Conservative treatment attempted prior to the surgical request included:
1. Physical Therapy: Completed 4 weeks (12 sessions). Dr. Chen halted further physical therapy after the MRI confirmed myelomalacia, documenting in writing that further physical therapy was contraindicated due to the risk of neurological deterioration.
2. Pharmacotherapy: Gabapentin 300mg TID for 3 months (resulting in partial pain relief but no improvement in weakness) and Naproxen 500mg BID for 2 months (inadequate response).
3. Interventional Treatment: A cervical epidural steroid injection performed on September 15, 2024, which yielded minimal improvement and no change in motor deficits.

The initial prior authorization request was denied on November 5, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, citing Reason Code MED-003. The denial was based on the member completing only 4 weeks of physical therapy (standard pathway requires 6 weeks minimum) and failing to qualify for the plan's "Myelopathy Exception" because the primary diagnosis code listed on the billing and referral forms was "cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy" (ICD-10 M47.812) rather than cervical myelopathy.

II. ANALYSIS OF PLAN CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024:
- Section 2.1(b) defines Cervical Myelopathy as spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms, including gait disturbance, bilateral upper extremity weakness, hyperreflexia, and a positive Hoffman sign. Mr. Webb's clinical presentation on July 1, 2024, and MRI findings on July 10, 2024, fully satisfy this diagnostic definition.
- Section 2.2 requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy, a trial of two neuropathic/NSAID agents for 4 weeks each, and at least one interventional treatment.
- Section 2.2 Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway) reduces the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks and waives the interventional treatment requirement if the physician documents that it poses a risk of neurological deterioration. However, a "Note" in Section 2.2 states that this exception applies "only when myelopathy is the PRIMARY diagnosis" and that cases where radiculopathy is listed as the primary complaint are subject to standard conservative treatment requirements.

Because the administrative billing code utilized on the physical therapy referrals was M47.812 (cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy), the initial reviewer mechanically applied the standard 6-week physical therapy requirement and denied the request because only 4 weeks were completed.

III. ANALYSIS OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY MANDATES (STATE LAW)
Under California law, which supersedes any conflicting internal health plan coverage criteria, the plan's mechanical application of the 6-week physical therapy requirement is legally invalid:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3):
This statute mandates that a health insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... [t]he condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Mr. Webb's MRI on July 10, 2024, provides objective evidence of moderate spinal cord compression and early myelomalacia (T2 signal change within the cord). This structural neurological compromise cannot be addressed or reversed by physical therapy.

2. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1):
This statute prohibits conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy when the "treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition." Dr. Chen explicitly documented that further physical therapy was contraindicated after the MRI confirmed myelomalacia.

3. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(c):
This section states that a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy "shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or whether the condition involves neurological urgency under subdivision (a)(3)." The plan's mechanical denial based on the 4-week duration violates this provision.

4. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:
APL 22-014 states that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated" and requires plans to treat such cases as urgent. Section II.2 of the APL mandates that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization" when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as cord signal change/myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression. The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that supports the waiver of standard conservative treatment.

IV. ANALYSIS OF GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS
Generally accepted clinical standards strongly support immediate surgical decompression for patients presenting with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy and objective MRI evidence of myelomalacia:

1. AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023):
Under these guidelines, surgical intervention for cervical myelopathy is classified under Tier 1 — Strong Indication (surgery recommended) when there is moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI, progressive neurological deficit, or myelomalacia (cord signal change) on MRI. Mr. Webb meets multiple Tier 1 criteria.

2. AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):
The guidelines state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." 

3. Cochrane Review (2021):
The evidence demonstrates that physical therapy does not produce meaningful functional improvement for moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy (mJOA score 8-12) or severe myelopathy (mJOA < 8), and conservative treatment is not recommended as a primary treatment in these populations.

V. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS (HIERARCHY OF RULES)
In resolving the conflict between the plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), generally accepted clinical standards, and California state law, the following hierarchy of authority is applied:
California State Law (CIC & DMHC Regulations) > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Plan Criteria.

1. Billing Code vs. Clinical Reality:
The plan's initial denial anchored on the administrative billing code M47.812 (radiculopathy) to deny the myelopathy exception. However, California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) and DMHC APL 22-014 establish that medical necessity determinations must be based on the clinical standard of care and objective clinical findings, not administrative billing artifacts. The clinical narrative, physical examination (3/5 grip weakness, hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman, impaired gait), and objective MRI findings (cord compression, myelomalacia) consistently and indisputably establish a primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy.

2. Physical Therapy Duration:
While the plan's internal criteria require 6 weeks of physical therapy (or 4 weeks under the myelopathy exception if myelopathy is the primary billing diagnosis), CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) and DMHC APL 22-014 legally override this requirement. Because there is objective MRI evidence of structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) and the treating physician documented that further physical therapy is contraindicated (CIC § 10169.5(a)(1)), the plan cannot legally condition surgical approval on the completion of additional physical therapy.

3. Restrictiveness of Plan Criteria:
Under CHSC § 1374.32(b) and DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3, plans are prohibited from applying internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. Requiring a patient with active myelomalacia and progressive upper motor neuron deficits to undergo 6 weeks of physical therapy is clinically contraindicated, unsupported by medical evidence, and violates the clinical standard of care.

VI. CONCLUSION AND DISPOSITION
Based on the clinical evidence, California statutory mandates, and established clinical guidelines, the prior authorization denial for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED.

The procedure is hereby AUTHORIZED.
