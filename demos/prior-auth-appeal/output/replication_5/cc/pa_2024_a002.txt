Dear Marcus Webb and Dr. Sarah Chen,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its clinical and regulatory review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) at UCSF Medical Center. 

After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California statutory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has determined that the original prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED. The requested surgical procedure is approved as medically necessary. 


I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND SUMMARY OF EVIDENCE

Mr. Marcus Webb is a 47-year-old male presenting with progressive neurological deficits. The clinical record contains the following objective findings:
1. Clinical Presentation (2024-07-01): Bilateral hand weakness, coordination problems, and upper motor neuron (UMN) signs.
2. Neurological Examination (2024-07-01): Bilateral grip weakness (3/5), bilaterally positive Hoffman sign, bilateral upper extremity hyperreflexia, and impaired tandem gait.
3. Objective Imaging (MRI dated 2024-07-10): C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate spinal cord compression and T2 signal change within the cord consistent with early myelomalacia.
4. Conservative Treatment History:
   - Physical Therapy: Completed 4 weeks (12 sessions). Dr. Chen halted further physical therapy after the MRI confirmed myelomalacia, documenting that further physical therapy was contraindicated.
   - Pharmacotherapy: Gabapentin 300mg TID for 3 months (partial pain relief, no improvement in weakness) and Naproxen 500mg BID for 2 months (inadequate response).
   - Interventional: Cervical epidural steroid injection on September 15, 2024 (minimal pain improvement, no change in motor deficits).


II. PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)

The original denial dated November 5, 2024, cited Section 2.2 of the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), stating that the member completed only 4 weeks of physical therapy instead of the standard 6-week minimum. The reviewer noted that the "Myelopathy Exception" under Section 2.2(A) did not apply because the primary diagnosis code on the referral and most recent note was "cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy" (ICD-10 M47.812) rather than cervical myelopathy.

Upon appeal review, this mechanical application of the criteria is incorrect under the plan's own guidelines:
1. Diagnosis Verification: Under Section 2.1(b), a diagnosis of cervical myelopathy is confirmed by "spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms (gait disturbance, bilateral upper extremity weakness, hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign)." Mr. Webb's clinical record dated July 1, 2024, explicitly documents every single one of these criteria. 
2. Billing Discrepancy: The plan's reliance on the ICD-10 billing code M47.812 as a basis to deny the myelopathy pathway is an administrative artifact. The treating physician's narrative notes consistently and clearly describe cervical myelopathy and objective myelomalacia. Clinical substance must govern over administrative billing labels.
3. Myelopathy Pathway Exception Compliance: Under Section 2.2(A), members with confirmed cervical myelopathy qualify for a modified conservative treatment pathway. The physical therapy requirement is reduced to a 4-week minimum. Mr. Webb completed exactly 4 weeks (12 sessions) of supervised physical therapy, satisfying this modified requirement. His pharmacotherapy trial (Gabapentin and Naproxen) satisfied Section 2.2(b), and his interventional trial (cervical epidural on September 15, 2024) satisfied Section 2.2(c). Therefore, Mr. Webb fully satisfied the plan's internal criteria under the Section 2.2(A) Myelopathy Pathway.


III. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY OVERRIDES (CONTROLLING AUTHORITY)

Even if the plan's internal criteria did not support an exception, California law and regulatory mandates supersede internal plan guidelines and require an immediate overturn of the denial:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy Prerequisites:
   - Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), a health insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... [t]he condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
   - Mr. Webb's MRI demonstrates moderate spinal cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia). This constitutes severe, objective structural neurological compromise. Physical therapy cannot decompress the spinal cord or resolve myelomalacia. Conditioning surgery on an additional 2 weeks of physical therapy directly violates this statute.
   - Furthermore, under CIC § 10169.5(a)(1), a plan cannot require physical therapy when the treating physician documents in writing that it is contraindicated. Dr. Chen explicitly documented that further physical therapy was contraindicated after myelomalacia was identified.
   - Under CIC § 10169.5(c), the plan's mechanical application of a minimum-week physical therapy requirement without reference to neurological urgency is legally invalid.

2. DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 — Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions:
   - APL 22-014, Section II.1, establishes that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated."
   - Section II.2 mandates that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization" when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as cord signal change/myelomalacia), the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression, and surgery is indicated under clinical guidelines.
   - Mr. Webb meets all three prongs: his MRI shows myelomalacia, his 4 weeks of physical therapy and epidural injection failed to arrest his motor deficits, and surgery is strongly indicated. The plan's insistence on 6 weeks of physical therapy violates APL 22-014.


IV. GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS

Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, the standard for medical necessity must align with generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersede restrictive internal plan criteria:

1. Surgical Indications: According to the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Clinical Guidelines (2020), surgical decompression is the recommended primary treatment for moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy. Mr. Webb's presentation (3/5 grip weakness, impaired tandem gait, hyperreflexia) represents moderate-to-severe myelopathy, placing him in the "Tier 1 — Strong Indication" category where surgery is recommended.
2. Contraindication of Delay: The AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021) state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." 
3. Significance of Myelomalacia: T2 cord signal change (myelomalacia) represents active spinal cord injury. Continued compression risks permanent, irreversible neurological deficits. Delaying surgery to satisfy an arbitrary 6-week physical therapy timeline is clinically contraindicated and violates the clinical standard of care.


V. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION

The plan's initial denial was based on an administrative billing code discrepancy and a mechanical application of physical therapy timelines. This denial directly violated California Insurance Code § 10169.5, DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014, and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS and AO Spine guidelines).

Accordingly, the prior authorization denial for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED.
