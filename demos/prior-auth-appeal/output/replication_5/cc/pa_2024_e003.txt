Dear Mr. Harris and Dr. Williams,

The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the first-level clinical appeal submitted on behalf of George Harris regarding the prior authorization denial for a Revision Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT code 22551).

After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has determined that the prior authorization denial issued on October 30, 2024, must be OVERTURNED. Authorization for the requested revision surgery at UCSF Medical Center is hereby APPROVED.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The initial denial of prior authorization on October 30, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, was based on the rationale that "Revision surgery [is] not covered under standard ACDF criteria... Revision criteria require documentation of hardware failure, pseudarthrosis, or new pathology... Documentation submitted does not confirm hardware failure."

This appeal review has identified that the initial denial was clinically and procedurally flawed. The plan's reviewer failed to evaluate the appropriate imaging modality—the cervical CT scan dated September 15, 2024—which explicitly confirms pseudarthrosis (failed fusion) at C5-C6. Instead, the reviewer relied on an MRI dated September 1, 2024, which was explicitly noted by the radiologist to be limited by hardware artifact. Under the plan's own stated revision criteria, "pseudarthrosis" is an independent qualifying indication for revision surgery, separate from "hardware failure." Furthermore, the member has fully satisfied all standard medical necessity criteria under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, and the denial violates California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 regarding physical therapy prerequisites.

II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND HISTORY
Mr. George Harris is a 48-year-old male with a history of a prior C5-C6 ACDF performed in 2021, which initially yielded a good outcome. However, his symptoms recurred approximately 18 months postoperatively and have progressively worsened.
- Neurological Examination (September 20, 2024): Demonstrates a return of right C6 radicular symptoms, including 4/5 right bicep weakness, a diminished right bicep reflex, and C6 dermatomal numbness. These objective deficits are worse than his preoperative baseline from 2021.
- Imaging Studies:
  1. Cervical MRI (September 1, 2024): Demonstrated hardware artifact that limited the assessment. The radiologist noted a "possible non-union at C5-C6 but cannot confirm" and that "residual disc material or recurrent herniation cannot be excluded."
  2. Cervical CT Scan (September 15, 2024): Performed specifically to overcome the hardware artifact of the MRI. The CT scan confirmed "incomplete bridging bone formation" and "lucency around hardware suggesting pseudarthrosis." The radiologist concluded that the findings are "consistent with failed fusion/pseudarthrosis" and noted that the hardware itself appeared intact without fracture.
- Conservative Treatment:
  1. Physical Therapy: Completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy. The treating therapist and physician noted no improvement and documented that physical therapy cannot address structural hardware or fusion failure.
  2. Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 4-month trial of Naproxen 500mg BID (NSAID) and a 3-month trial of Gabapentin 300mg TID (neuropathic agent), both resulting in only partial, inadequate relief.
  3. Interventional Treatment: Received a cervical epidural steroid injection on October 10, 2024, which resulted in minimal improvement, as expected in the presence of structural fusion failure.

III. PLAN CRITERIA COMPLIANCE ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)
The requested procedure (CPT 22551) fully satisfies the medical necessity criteria outlined in HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2:
1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): Mr. Harris has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy, characterized by objective nerve root compression with radicular symptoms (right bicep weakness 4/5, diminished right bicep reflex, and C6 dermatomal numbness). This is supported by the CT scan dated September 15, 2024, demonstrating structural pathology (pseudarthrosis) consistent with his symptoms, satisfying the 6-month imaging window.
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement):
   - Section 2.2(a) (Physical Therapy): Mr. Harris completed 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy (exceeding the 6-week minimum).
   - Section 2.2(b) (Pharmacotherapy): Mr. Harris completed trials of two qualifying agents for more than 4 weeks each: Naproxen (NSAID) for 4 months and Gabapentin (neuropathic agent) for 3 months.
   - Section 2.2(c) (Interventional Treatment): Mr. Harris received a cervical epidural steroid injection on October 10, 2024.
3. Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement): The cervical CT scan dated September 15, 2024, is current (within 6 months) and demonstrates structural pathology (pseudarthrosis/failed fusion) at the proposed surgical level (C5-C6) consistent with his right C6 radiculopathy.
4. Section 2.4 (Physician Documentation): Dr. Karen Williams, MD, provided the required clinical documentation, including attestation of completed conservative treatment, clinical rationale for revision surgery, description of functional limitations, and discussion of surgical risks.

IV. RESOLUTION OF REVISION CRITERIA AND INITIAL DENIAL ERROR
The initial denial letter dated October 30, 2024, stated that "Revision criteria require documentation of hardware failure, pseudarthrosis, or new pathology... Documentation submitted does not confirm hardware failure."

This rationale contains a critical logical and clinical error:
1. Misapplication of Disjunctive Criteria: The plan's revision criteria, as quoted in the denial, are disjunctive ("hardware failure, pseudarthrosis, OR new pathology"). This means satisfying any one of these three conditions is sufficient. By denying the request solely because "hardware failure" was not confirmed, the plan reviewer ignored the other two criteria.
2. Failure to Review the Appropriate Imaging Modality: The cervical CT scan dated September 15, 2024, explicitly confirms "incomplete bridging bone formation" and "lucency around hardware," which are diagnostic of pseudarthrosis (failed fusion). The initial reviewer's reliance on the MRI report (which was limited by hardware artifact and could not confirm fusion status) to deny the request constitutes a serious clinical oversight. In the presence of spinal hardware, a CT scan is the clinically accepted gold standard for evaluating fusion status, as MRI is inherently limited by metallic artifact.

Because pseudarthrosis is documented on the CT scan, the member meets the plan's revision criteria.

V. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY COMPLIANCE ANALYSIS
Under California law, the plan's initial denial is non-compliant and must be overturned based on the following statutory provisions:
1. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b) (Defective Denial Notice): A denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reason for the denial. The plan's denial notice was procedurally defective because it misapplied the criteria by requiring "hardware failure" while ignoring the documented "pseudarthrosis," which is an independent qualifying criterion.
2. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite): Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), a health plan shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Mr. Harris has objective structural compromise (pseudarthrosis with 4/5 bicep weakness and diminished reflex) that physical therapy cannot structurally repair. Furthermore, under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), Mr. Harris completed 8 weeks of physical therapy and reached a documented functional plateau. Mechanically applying or extending physical therapy requirements in this clinical scenario violates California law.
3. CHSC § 1374.32 (Independent Medical Review Standard): Under California's IMR standard, "medically necessary" means services that a provider, exercising prudent clinical judgment, would provide in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice. CHSC § 1374.32(b) explicitly states that the IMR standard does not require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If this case were to proceed to an Independent Medical Review (IMR), the plan's denial would be overturned because revision surgery for symptomatic pseudarthrosis with objective neurological deficits is the standard of care.

VI. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND GUIDELINES ANALYSIS
Generally accepted clinical standards strongly support revision surgery for Mr. Harris's presentation:
1. AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023): Revision surgery is indicated for symptomatic pseudarthrosis (failed fusion) with persistent radiculopathy and objective neurological deficits (Tier 1/Tier 2 indication). Mr. Harris exhibits progressive right C6 radiculopathy with objective motor weakness (4/5 bicep strength) and reflex changes.
2. NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020): Support surgical intervention when conservative measures fail to address symptomatic non-union or pseudarthrosis. Mr. Harris has failed a comprehensive 8-week course of physical therapy, multiple pharmacotherapies, and an epidural steroid injection.
3. Imaging Standards: As outlined in the clinical reference summary, CT imaging is the appropriate and superior modality for assessing fusion status in patients with existing spinal hardware, as MRI is heavily limited by metallic artifact. The plan's reliance on the MRI to deny the request is contrary to standard radiological and neurosurgical practice.

VII. FINAL DETERMINATION
Based on the clinical record, HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, California statutory requirements (CHSC § 1374.31, CIC § 10169.5), and generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020), the prior authorization denial for Revision ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED.

The requested procedure is medically necessary and is hereby APPROVED.
