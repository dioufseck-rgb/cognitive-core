SUMMARY OF DETERMINATION:
HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its first-level clinical appeal review for the requested three-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF). Based on a comprehensive review of the clinical record, California statutory and regulatory mandates, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial denial is PARTIALLY OVERTURNED. 

Surgical intervention is medically necessary and authorized for the C4-C5 and C5-C6 levels. However, the clinical record does not support the medical necessity of surgical intervention at the C3-C4 level; therefore, the denial for the C3-C4 level is upheld.

CLINICAL HISTORY & PRESENTING EVIDENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Mrs. Catherine Mills is a 53-year-old female presenting with a diagnosis of multi-level cervical spondylosis with myeloradiculopathy. 

1. Objective Imaging (MRI, dated October 1, 2024):
   - C3-C4: Mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing.
   - C4-C5: Large disc herniation, severe spinal cord compression, and T2-weighted signal change within the spinal cord (myelomalacia).
   - C5-C6: Moderate disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression, and foraminal stenosis.

2. Neurological Examination (dated October 5, 2024):
   - Myelopathic Signs: Bilateral hyperreflexia, bilateral positive Hoffman sign, and a wide-based gait.
   - Motor Deficits (C5 level): Bilateral deltoid weakness graded at 4/5.
   - Motor Deficits (C6 level): Left bicep weakness graded at 4-/5, with a diminished left bicep reflex.
   - C4 level: Neck pain elicited upon palpation of the C3-C4 region, but no specific radicular pattern or objective neurological deficit is documented.

3. Conservative Treatment History:
   - Physical Therapy: Completed 9 weeks (27 sessions) of supervised physical therapy. The physical therapist's summary notes that myelopathic symptoms remained stable but unimproved, C5-C6 radicular symptoms persisted, and the clinical contribution of the C3-C4 level remained unclear.
   - Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 3-month trial of Baclofen 10mg TID (muscle relaxant) with minimal spasticity relief, and a 4-month trial of Pregabalin 300mg BID (neuropathic agent) with only partial pain relief.
   - Interventional Treatment: Received a cervical epidural steroid injection on October 20, 2024, which provided modest, temporary improvement in radicular symptoms but no change in objective myelopathic signs.

LEVEL-BY-LEVEL CLINICAL & REGULATORY ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LEVEL 1: C4-C5 — DISPOSITION: OVERTURN (APPROVED)

1. Plan Criteria Alignment (CC-SPINE-2024):
   - Diagnosis: The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy under Section 2.1(b), supported by severe spinal cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI (October 1, 2024) and objective clinical findings (bilateral hyperreflexia, bilateral Hoffman sign, wide-based gait, and bilateral deltoid weakness).
   - Conservative Treatment: Under Section 2.2, the member completed 9 weeks of physical therapy (exceeding the 6-week standard and the 4-week myelopathy pathway exception), completed two qualifying pharmacotherapy trials (Baclofen and Pregabalin) for over 4 weeks each, and underwent a cervical epidural steroid injection. Thus, all standard and modified conservative criteria are fully satisfied.

2. California Regulatory Mandates:
   - DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014: This regulatory directive establishes that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a time-sensitive neurological condition with potential for irreversible harm. Under Section II.2, standard conservative treatment requirements must be waived or modified when myelomalacia is present. 
   - California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3): This statute prohibits health plans from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy when the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (such as severe cord compression and myelomalacia) that physical therapy cannot address. 

3. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:
   - The AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023) and NASS Guidelines (2020) classify moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with cord signal change (myelomalacia) as a Tier 1 Strong Indication for prompt surgical decompression. Extended conservative trials are clinically contraindicated due to the high risk of permanent, irreversible neurological deterioration.

Conclusion for C4-C5: The clinical evidence, plan criteria, state regulations, and clinical guidelines unanimously support surgical decompression at this level. The initial denial is overturned.

***

LEVEL 2: C5-C6 — DISPOSITION: OVERTURN (APPROVED)

1. Plan Criteria Alignment (CC-SPINE-2024):
   - Diagnosis: The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy and cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit under Sections 2.1(a) and 2.1(c). This is supported by the October 1, 2024 MRI showing a moderate disc herniation with moderate left C6 nerve root compression, and the October 5, 2024 neurological exam documenting correlating left bicep weakness (4-/5) and a diminished left bicep reflex.
   - Conservative Treatment: The member completed 9 weeks of physical therapy (satisfying the 6-week requirement in Section 2.2(a)), completed trials of Baclofen and Pregabalin for >4 weeks each (satisfying Section 2.2(b)), and underwent a cervical epidural steroid injection on October 20, 2024 (satisfying Section 2.2(c)). 
   - The initial denial stated that C5-C6 did not demonstrate pathology of sufficient severity. This finding is clinically incorrect and directly contradicts the plan's own criteria, as the member has documented structural compression, a correlating focal neurological deficit, and has failed a full course of conservative management.

2. California Regulatory Mandates:
   - CIC § 10169.5(a)(2): This statute dictates that physical therapy prerequisites are satisfied when a member completes a course of therapy and the treating physician/therapist documents that the member reached a "functional plateau" without adequate resolution of symptoms. The physical therapy summary explicitly documents that C5-C6 radicular symptoms persisted despite 27 sessions, establishing a clinical functional plateau.
   - CHSC § 1374.32: Under the California Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard, a service must be approved if it is clinically appropriate and in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice, regardless of restrictive internal plan criteria.

3. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:
   - Under AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Section 3.1, surgical decompression is a Tier 2 Reasonable Indication for cervical radiculopathy with persistent, objective neurological deficits (motor weakness and reflex changes) that have failed to respond to 6 to 12 weeks of conservative care. 

Conclusion for C5-C6: The member meets all internal plan criteria and state statutory requirements for surgical intervention at this level. The initial denial is overturned.

***

LEVEL 3: C3-C4 — DISPOSITION: UPHOLD (DENIED)

1. Plan Criteria Alignment (CC-SPINE-2024):
   - Diagnosis: The member does not meet the diagnostic requirements of Section 2.1 for this level. The October 1, 2024 MRI demonstrates only a "mild disc bulge" and "minimal foraminal narrowing" at C3-C4. The neurological exam on October 5, 2024, notes that the clinical contribution of this level is "unclear," with neck pain on palpation but no specific radicular pattern or objective neurological deficit.
   - Exclusions: Section 3.1 explicitly excludes coverage for surgical procedures requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy. Section 3.3 excludes coverage when imaging does not demonstrate pathology at the proposed level consistent with the clinical presentation.

2. California Regulatory Mandates:
   - No regulatory overrides or statutory exceptions apply to the C3-C4 level. There is no objective evidence of structural neurological compromise, cord compression, or radiculopathy originating from this level to trigger the protections of DMHC APL 22-014 or CIC § 10169.5.

3. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:
   - Under the NASS Guidelines (2020) and AANS/CNS Guidelines (2023), surgical fusion is classified as Tier 3 (Not Indicated) for mild imaging findings and axial neck pain in the absence of correlating radiculopathy or myelopathy. Performing a fusion at an asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic level increases surgical morbidity without clinical benefit.

Conclusion for C3-C4: Because there is no correlating clinical pathology, objective neurological deficit, or failed conservative treatment specific to this level, surgical intervention at C3-C4 is not medically necessary. The initial denial is upheld.

PROCEDURAL COMPLIANCE REVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a)(2), health plans are legally required to provide a written denial notice that specifies the exact clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the denial. 

A review of the initial denial notice issued on November 25, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, reveals that the plan issued a blanket denial of the three-level procedure without providing a level-by-level clinical analysis or citing the specific sections of the Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) utilized. Most notably, the denial failed to explain why the C5-C6 level was denied despite the clinical record demonstrating a moderate herniation, a correlating C6 motor deficit, and a fully completed course of conservative therapy. 

Pursuant to CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reasons is procedurally DEFECTIVE. HealthFirst has corrected this procedural defect on appeal through this detailed, level-by-level determination.

What is an IMR?
An IMR is an independent review of your case conducted by board-certified medical specialists who are not associated with HealthFirst. If the IMR reviewer decides that the denied level (C3-C4) is medically necessary under generally accepted standards of medical practice, HealthFirst is legally bound to accept that decision and authorize the service.

How to Apply:

2. There is no fee or cost to you for requesting an IMR.

Under CHSC § 1374.30, your right to an IMR is independent of any other grievance process. Because this case involves a time-sensitive neurological condition (cervical myelopathy), you or your provider may request an EXPEDITED IMR, which must be decided within three (3) days of receipt by the Department.
