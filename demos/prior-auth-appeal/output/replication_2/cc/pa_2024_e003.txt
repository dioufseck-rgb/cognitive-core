DISPOSITION: OVERTURNED (The health plan's denial of prior authorization for CPT 22551 is reversed. The requested revision surgery is medically necessary.)

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The member is a 48-year-old male with a history of a prior C5-C6 ACDF in 2021 who presents with a recurrence of severe right C6 radiculopathy symptoms. A standard CT scan dated September 15, 2024, confirms pseudarthrosis (failed fusion) at C5-C6. The health plan denied coverage on October 30, 2024, citing a failure to document "hardware failure" under unlisted "Revision criteria."

This Independent Medical Review (IMR) determines that the health plan's denial must be OVERTURNED. Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), generally accepted standards of medical practice override restrictive internal plan criteria. The clinical evidence demonstrates that the member has a confirmed pseudarthrosis with correlating objective neurological deficits and has fully exhausted all contractually required conservative treatments.

II. PROCEDURAL DEFECTS IN PLAN DENIAL (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
The health plan's initial denial notice, reviewed by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, states: "Revision criteria require documentation of hardware failure, pseudarthrosis, or new pathology at previously treated level. Documentation submitted does not confirm hardware failure."

This denial is procedurally defective under California law for two reasons:
1. Citation of Non-Existent Criteria: The plan's clinical coverage criteria document, CC-SPINE-2024, contains no "Revision criteria" section or any requirement that a revision procedure must demonstrate "hardware failure."
2. Failure to Evaluate Documented Pathology: Even under the plan's own cited (but unlisted) revision criteria, "pseudarthrosis" is explicitly listed as an alternative basis for revision. The plan's reviewer failed to evaluate the submitted CT scan confirming pseudarthrosis, focusing solely on the absence of hardware fracture.
Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the actual clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is defective.

III. CLINICAL ANALYSIS & RESOLUTION OF IMAGING MODALITY CONFLICT (CHSC § 1374.32(b))
The plan's legal counsel may argue that under CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1, the supporting documentation must be an "MRI or CT myelogram within 6 months of request," and that a standard CT scan is not a contractually approved imaging modality.

We resolve this conflict in favor of the member under CHSC § 1374.32(b), which mandates that "medically necessary" services must be determined in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice, regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive:
1. MRI Limitations: The member's MRI dated September 1, 2024, was severely limited by metallic hardware artifact ("Hardware artifact limits MRI assessment. Possible non-union at C5-C6 but cannot confirm").
2. CT Scan as Gold Standard: A standard CT scan dated September 15, 2024, was performed without artifact limitation, demonstrating "incomplete bridging bone formation" and "lucency around hardware suggesting pseudarthrosis."
3. Clinical Standards: Generally accepted clinical standards, including the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) and NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), establish that a standard CT scan is the clinical gold standard for evaluating bony fusion and pseudarthrosis when MRI is non-diagnostic due to hardware artifact. Insisting on an MRI or CT myelogram when a standard CT has already clearly demonstrated pseudarthrosis is clinically inappropriate and inconsistent with medical standards. Therefore, the CT scan is accepted as contractually compliant under CHSC § 1374.32(b).

IV. RESOLUTION OF OBJECTIVE EVIDENCE OF NERVE ROOT COMPRESSION
The plan may further argue that because the clinical presentation is classified as "radiculopathy_primary," Section 2.1(a) requires objective imaging evidence of "nerve root compression," which is absent on the CT scan and unconfirmed on the MRI due to hardware artifact.

We explicitly acknowledge this factual gap: current imaging does not directly visualize soft-tissue nerve root compression due to the physical limitations of the imaging modalities in the presence of surgical hardware.

However, this gap is clinically neutralized by the following evidence:
1. Objective Neurological Deficits: The member's neurological exam dated September 20, 2024, documents objective, correlating right C6 radiculopathy deficits: right bicep weakness (4/5), a diminished right bicep reflex, and C6 dermatomal numbness. These findings represent a severe recurrence of symptoms worse than the preoperative baseline.
2. Clinical Correlation: Under generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023), a patient with a CT-confirmed pseudarthrosis (failed fusion) and correlating, objective radicular neurological deficits meets the clinical standard for revision surgery. The structural instability of the failed fusion (pseudarthrosis) is the pathological driver of the radiculopathy. Requiring direct visualization of nerve root compression in the presence of severe hardware artifact is clinically inappropriate and violates CHSC § 1374.32(b).

V. COMPLIANCE WITH CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT (CC-SPINE-2024, SECTION 2.2)
The member has fully completed and exhausted all conservative treatment requirements under Section 2.2 of CC-SPINE-2024:
1. Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): The member completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy. The treating physician documented that the member reached a functional plateau with no improvement, noting that physical therapy cannot address structural hardware/fusion failure. This satisfies Section 2.2(a) and California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2), which prohibits plans from mechanically enforcing PT duration when a functional plateau is reached.
2. Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): The member completed trials of two qualifying agents for at least 4 weeks each:
   - Naproxen 500mg BID (NSAID) for 4 months (partial pain relief only).
   - Gabapentin 300mg TID (neuropathic agent) for 3 months (partial neuropathic relief).
3. Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): The member underwent a cervical epidural steroid injection on October 10, 2024, which yielded minimal improvement, as expected in the setting of structural failed fusion.

VI. CONCLUSION
The member has a documented, symptomatic C5-C6 pseudarthrosis (failed fusion) following a 2021 ACDF, with objective correlating neurological deficits. He has fully exhausted conservative therapy. The health plan's denial relied on non-existent criteria and a defective review of the imaging. Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the requested Revision ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is medically necessary and is hereby APPROVED.
