DISPOSITION: OVERTURN (Prior Authorization Approved)

Dear Mr. Harris and Dr. Williams,

HealthFirst has completed its formal first-level internal appeal review of the prior authorization denial issued on October 30, 2024, for a Revision Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's internal coverage criteria, California regulatory statutes, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has determined that the initial denial was incorrect. 

Accordingly, the initial decision is OVERTURNED, and the requested revision surgery is APPROVED.


I. CLINICAL BACKGROUND AND HISTORY

Mr. Harris is a 48-year-old male with a history of a prior C5-C6 ACDF performed in 2021. While he initially experienced a good clinical outcome, his symptoms recurred approximately 18 months postoperatively and have progressively worsened. 

A neurological examination on September 20, 2024, documented a return of right C6 radiculopathy symptoms, including 4/5 right bicep weakness, a diminished right bicep reflex, and C6 dermatomal numbness. These objective neurological deficits represent a clinical decline that is worse than his preoperative baseline prior to the 2021 surgery.

Prior to requesting revision surgery, the member completed a comprehensive course of conservative treatment, which included:
1. Physical Therapy: 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy directed at the cervical spine, which resulted in no clinical improvement. The treating physician and physical therapist documented that the member reached a functional plateau because physical therapy cannot resolve structural hardware or fusion failure.
2. Pharmacotherapy: A 4-month trial of Naproxen 500mg BID (NSAID) and a 3-month trial of Gabapentin 300mg TID (neuropathic agent), both of which provided only partial, temporary relief.
3. Interventional Treatment: A cervical epidural steroid injection performed on October 10, 2024, which yielded minimal improvement.


II. SUBSTANTIVE CLINICAL ANALYSIS UNDER CALIFORNIA LAW (CHSC § 1374.32)

The initial denial was based on the assertion that the submitted documentation did not confirm "hardware failure." Under California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, health plans must evaluate medical necessity in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice. CHSC § 1374.32(b) explicitly mandates that a plan's internal coverage criteria shall not be the sole or determinative basis for a decision if those criteria are more restrictive than generally accepted clinical standards.

1. The Imaging Requirement Conflict:
HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1 requires a confirmed diagnosis supported by an "MRI or CT myelogram within 6 months of request." 

In this case, an MRI performed on September 1, 2024, was inconclusive due to severe metal artifact from the existing 2021 surgical hardware ("Hardware artifact limits MRI assessment. Possible non-union at C5-C6 but cannot confirm"). To resolve this ambiguity, a high-resolution standard CT scan was performed on September 15, 2024. The CT scan clearly demonstrated "incomplete bridging bone formation" and "lucency around hardware consistent with failed fusion/pseudarthrosis."

We acknowledge that a standard CT scan does not technically satisfy the literal letter of Section 2.1, which specifies an "MRI or CT myelogram." However, under generally accepted clinical standards (including the North American Spine Society [NASS] 2020 Guidelines and the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines 2023), a high-resolution standard CT scan is the established gold standard for evaluating bony fusion and pseudarthrosis in patients with existing spinal hardware. Requiring an invasive CT myelogram—which carries risks of spinal headache, infection, and radiation—when a standard CT scan has already clearly and safely confirmed pseudarthrosis is clinically inappropriate and more restrictive than generally accepted medical practice. Therefore, pursuant to CHSC § 1374.32, the standard CT scan dated September 15, 2024, is accepted as sufficient diagnostic evidence of structural pathology.

2. The Plan's Substantive Error in Criteria Application:
The initial denial letter dated October 30, 2024, stated that revision criteria require "documentation of hardware failure, pseudarthrosis, or new pathology," but concluded that the request was denied because documentation "does not confirm hardware failure."

This application of the criteria represents a substantive clinical error. While the CT scan confirmed that the 2021 hardware itself is intact (not fractured), it explicitly confirmed "pseudarthrosis" (failed bony fusion). Under both the plan's own stated revision rules and generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023), "hardware failure" and "pseudarthrosis" are independent, alternative clinical indications for revision surgery. The plan substantively erred by focusing exclusively on the lack of hardware fracture while completely ignoring the confirmed pseudarthrosis. 


III. CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT COMPLIANCE

The clinical record demonstrates full compliance with the conservative treatment requirements outlined in HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 and California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5. The member completed 8 weeks of physical therapy (exceeding the 6-week requirement), trialed two classes of first-line medications for at least 4 weeks each, and underwent a cervical epidural steroid injection. Because the treating physician documented that the member reached a functional plateau due to an underlying structural failure (pseudarthrosis), conditioning surgical approval on further conservative treatment is clinically contraindicated and prohibited under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2).


IV. CONCLUSION

Because the member has a documented, CT-confirmed diagnosis of C5-C6 pseudarthrosis associated with progressive, objective neurological deficits (4/5 bicep weakness, diminished reflex) that have failed to respond to a complete and compliant course of conservative therapy, the requested revision surgery is medically necessary. 

The prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED. Revision ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is approved at UCSF Medical Center, to be performed by Dr. Karen Williams, MD. This authorization is valid for 90 days from the date of this letter, expiring on February 16, 2025.
