The Appeals and Grievances Department at HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of the appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551), requested by Dr. Robert Adams, MD, at UCSF Medical Center. After a comprehensive review of the clinical documentation, plan coverage criteria, California statutory and regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial decision to deny coverage is UPHELD.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND DIAGNOSTIC CLASSIFICATION
The clinical record demonstrates that you have clinical signs of cervical myelopathy. A neurological examination on September 5, 2024, revealed:
- Positive bilateral Hoffman sign
- Hyperreflexia in the bilateral upper extremities
- Sustained clonus at the bilateral ankles (3 beats)
- Positive finger escape sign
- Mildly wide-based gait
- No lower extremity symptoms or bowel/bladder dysfunction

An MRI of the cervical spine dated September 1, 2024, demonstrated a C5-C6 disc herniation with mild cord contact. However, the radiologist's report explicitly noted: "No measurable cord compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia. NOTE: Cord contact is present but not quantifiable as compression on standard sequences."

Based on the combination of mild gait disturbance, the absence of hand weakness or lower extremity symptoms, and the lack of measurable cord compression or myelomalacia on imaging, your clinical presentation is classified as mild cervical myelopathy.

II. PLAN CLINICAL COVERAGE CRITERIA ANALYSIS
Under the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery — Cervical Procedures (Document: CC-SPINE-2024), Section 2.1(b) defines cervical myelopathy as "spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms" and requires supporting documentation (MRI or CT myelogram) demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms. Because your MRI explicitly states there is "no measurable cord compression," the diagnostic criteria for cervical myelopathy under Section 2.1(b) are not met.

Consequently, the Myelopathy Pathway Exception under Section 2.2(a)—which reduces the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks and waives the interventional treatment requirement—does not apply. Therefore, your request is subject to the standard conservative treatment requirements outlined in Section 2.2:

1. Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): Requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. Your records indicate you completed 5 weeks (15 sessions) of physical therapy before it was halted by your physician. This does not satisfy the 6-week minimum.
2. Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): Requires a trial of at least two eligible agents (NSAIDs/COX-2 inhibitors, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each. Your records show you trialed Baclofen (a muscle relaxant) for 2 months, which satisfies the requirement for one agent. However, your trial of a Methylprednisolone taper lasted only 10 days, failing the 4-week minimum. No other eligible agent was trialed for the required 4-week duration.
3. Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): Requires at least one cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point injection series. No interventional treatments have been performed.

Because these standard conservative treatment requirements have not been met, the request does not satisfy the plan's clinical coverage criteria.

III. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY AND STATUTORY ANALYSIS
We evaluated whether any California statutory or regulatory provisions override the plan's conservative treatment requirements for your specific clinical presentation:

1. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:
APL 22-014 Sections II.1 and II.2 mandate that health plans waive conservative treatment requirements only for cases with "objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI" or "progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia, or progressive herniation on serial imaging)." Because your MRI report explicitly states "No measurable cord compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia," the regulatory protections and automatic waivers under APL 22-014 do not apply to your clinical presentation.

2. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Waiver):
- Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), a physical therapy requirement is waived if there is "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise." In this case, the MRI findings of "mild cord contact" without measurable cord compression or myelomalacia do not meet the statutory threshold of structural neurological compromise of the spinal cord.
- Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(1), a waiver applies if the treating physician documents in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition. Although your clinical record notes that your physician halted physical therapy after 5 weeks citing "myelopathy concern," there is no documented clinical rationale explaining why continuing physical therapy for the remaining 1 week was contraindicated or unlikely to benefit you, particularly given that clinical standards support conservative management for mild myelopathy.

3. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 (Medical Necessity Standard):
Under CHSC § 1374.32, medical necessity determinations must align with generally accepted standards of medical practice. As detailed below, requiring conservative treatment for mild cervical myelopathy is fully consistent with these standards.

IV. GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS ANALYSIS
Generally accepted clinical guidelines support conservative management as the appropriate initial treatment pathway for mild cervical myelopathy:

1. North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020):
The NASS guidelines state (Grade B Evidence) that "Conservative treatment may be considered for mild cervical myelopathy as initial management, with close monitoring for progression."
2. AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):
While these guidelines recommend surgical decompression as the primary treatment for moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy, they recognize conservative management as a standard and reasonable initial pathway for mild cervical myelopathy.
3. Cochrane Systematic Review (Nikolaidis et al., 2021):
The evidence indicates that physical therapy produces modest short-term functional benefits in patients with mild cervical myelopathy (modified Japanese Orthopaedic Association score > 12).

Because your clinical presentation is classified as mild, requiring a structured course of conservative treatment—including physical therapy and an adequate pharmacotherapy trial—is consistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice.

V. RESOLUTION OF PHARMACOTHERAPY DEFICIT
We acknowledge that a 4-week trial of oral corticosteroids (such as Methylprednisolone) is clinically inappropriate and carries significant risks of systemic side effects, which would violate the clinical appropriateness standard under CHSC § 1374.32(a)(1). However, even when excluding oral corticosteroids from the duration requirement, you did not trial a second appropriate agent (such as NSAIDs, COX-2 inhibitors, or neuropathic agents like gabapentin, pregabalin, or duloxetine) for the required 4 weeks. Only Baclofen was trialed for the necessary duration. Therefore, the pharmacotherapy requirement remains unsatisfied under generally accepted clinical standards.

VI. CONCLUSION
The prior authorization request for ACDF at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is UPHELD because:
1. Your clinical presentation is classified as mild cervical myelopathy, for which conservative treatment is the clinically appropriate and standard initial management pathway.
2. The plan's conservative treatment requirements (6 weeks of physical therapy, a 4-week trial of two pharmacotherapy agents, and one interventional treatment) have not been completed.
3. The specific statutory and regulatory waivers under DMHC APL 22-014 and CIC § 10169.5 do not apply due to the absence of objective cord compression, myelomalacia, or documented clinical contraindications.

We recommend that you complete the remaining conservative treatment pathway, including completing physical therapy and trialing an alternative second pharmacotherapy agent (such as an NSAID or neuropathic agent) for 4 weeks, unless a specific clinical contraindication is documented by your treating physician.
