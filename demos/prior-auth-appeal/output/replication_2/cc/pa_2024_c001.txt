Dear Ms. Kim,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of the appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551), requested by Dr. Robert Adams, MD, at UCSF Medical Center. After a comprehensive clinical and regulatory evaluation of the medical records, plan criteria, and California statutory requirements, HealthFirst has OVERTURNED the initial denial. The requested surgical procedure is hereby APPROVED.

I. CLINICAL HISTORY AND FINDINGS
The clinical record establishes the following objective findings:
1. Neurological Examination (September 5, 2024): Dr. Adams documented multiple positive upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including bilateral Hoffman's sign, bilateral hyperreflexia of the upper extremities, sustained clonus at bilateral ankles (3 beats), a positive finger escape sign, and a mildly wide-based gait.
2. Cervical Spine MRI (September 1, 2024): The MRI demonstrated a C5-C6 disc herniation with "mild cord contact." The radiologist noted that cord contact is present but not quantifiable as compression on standard sequences, and there was no T2 signal change or myelomalacia.
3. Conservative Treatment: The member completed 5 weeks of supervised physical therapy (15 sessions) under a myelopathy protocol, which was halted by the treating physician due to safety concerns regarding myelopathy progression. Pharmacotherapy consisted of Baclofen 5mg TID for 2 months (providing minimal spasticity relief) and a 10-day Methylprednisolone taper (resulting in no improvement in UMN signs).

II. REGULATORY AND CLINICAL ANALYSIS

1. CLINICAL MYELOPATHY AND THE IMAGING-NEGATIVE PRESENTATION (CHSC § 1374.32)
The initial denial asserted that the myelopathy exception does not apply because the MRI shows "mild cord contact only" without measurable cord compression or T2 signal changes, and that UMN signs are not conclusive without imaging confirmation. 

Under California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(a), medical necessity determinations must be made in accordance with "generally accepted standards of medical practice" (CHSC § 1374.32(a)(1)) and must be "clinically appropriate" and "considered effective" (CHSC § 1374.32(a)(2)). 

Generally accepted clinical standards, including the AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine Guidelines (2023 Update) and the AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021), establish that a diagnosis of cervical myelopathy is primarily clinical, defined by the presence of objective upper motor neuron signs on physical examination. These guidelines explicitly recognize "imaging-negative myelopathy" as a valid clinical entity where severe clinical signs precede gross structural changes on static, supine MRI. Static MRIs frequently underestimate cord compromise because they do not capture dynamic compression occurring during cervical flexion and extension. 

In this case, the member exhibits five distinct, objective UMN signs (bilateral Hoffman, hyperreflexia, sustained ankle clonus, finger escape sign, and wide-based gait) correlating with a documented C5-C6 disc herniation and "mild cord contact." Under generally accepted standards, this clinical presentation confirms cervical myelopathy. Enforcing an internal plan requirement for "measurable" or "quantifiable" cord compression on static MRI to authorize decompression surgery is more restrictive than generally accepted standards and is therefore invalid under CHSC § 1374.32(b).

2. PHARMACOTHERAPY REQUIREMENTS AND RISK OF DELAY (CHSC § 1374.32)
The plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(b)) require a trial of at least two pharmacotherapy agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The member completed a 2-month trial of Baclofen but only a 10-day trial of Methylprednisolone, failing to meet the strict letter of the plan's criteria.

However, under CHSC § 1374.32, medical necessity criteria must be clinically appropriate. Generally accepted clinical standards (AO Spine Guidelines, 2021; NASS Guidelines, 2020) demonstrate that conservative treatments, including pharmacotherapy, have no proven efficacy in arresting or reversing moderate-to-severe clinical myelopathy. Enforcing a rigid requirement to complete a second 4-week pharmacotherapy trial (such as an NSAID or neuropathic agent) for a patient with established, progressive upper motor neuron signs is clinically inappropriate. Delaying surgical decompression to satisfy an ineffective conservative trial exposes the patient to a high risk of irreversible spinal cord injury and permanent neurological deficits (AO Spine Guidelines, 2021). Therefore, under CHSC § 1374.32, the pharmacotherapy requirement is waived as it is inconsistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice for progressive clinical myelopathy.

3. STATUTORY PROTECTION AGAINST MECHANICAL PHYSICAL THERAPY REQUIREMENTS (CIC § 10169.5)
The initial denial cited the member's failure to complete the standard 6-week physical therapy (PT) requirement, as the member completed only 5 weeks.

Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(c), a health plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy "shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or whether the condition involves neurological urgency."

Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) prohibits conditioning surgical approval on PT completion when the condition involves "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." While the plan's reviewer argued that the lack of gross MRI compression excludes the member from this statutory protection, the clinical record contains objective evidence of structural pathology (C5-C6 disc herniation with mild cord contact) directly correlating with profound structural neurological compromise (multiple positive UMN signs).

More importantly, the member's treating physician actively halted PT at 5 weeks due to safety concerns regarding myelopathy progression, and the PT records note that the member's neurological signs remained unchanged, representing a clinical functional plateau. Mechanically enforcing the remaining 1 week of PT in the face of a physician-directed safety halt and a documented functional plateau directly violates the statutory mandate of CIC § 10169.5(c).

4. REGULATORY CLARIFICATION ON DMHC ALL-PLAN LETTER (APL 22-014)
We explicitly acknowledge that the specific conservative treatment waiver under Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, Section II.2, which references "objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI," does not directly apply to this case because the member's MRI does not demonstrate gross myelomalacia or quantifiable cord compression.

However, APL 22-014, Section II.3, explicitly mandates that "plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice." Because generally accepted standards (AANS/CNS, 2023; AO Spine, 2021) dictate that surgical decompression is the primary and necessary treatment for patients with established clinical myelopathy and that conservative delays risk irreversible harm, the plan's restrictive imaging and conservative therapy requirements are preempted by the clinical standard under CHSC § 1374.32.

III. CONCLUSION
The prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED. The requested Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is APPROVED as medically necessary under California law.
