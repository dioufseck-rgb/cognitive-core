Dear Ms. Kamau and Dr. Osei,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of the first-level appeal submitted on December 5, 2024, regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) to be performed at UCSF Medical Center. 

After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has determined that the initial denial issued on November 20, 2024, was procedurally and clinically flawed. Accordingly, the initial decision is OVERTURNED, and full authorization is hereby GRANTED for the requested procedure.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. BACKGROUND AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
On November 20, 2024, HealthFirst Utilization Management issued an initial denial (Reason Code: MED-003) for a single-level ACDF C5-C6. The denial was based on the member's prior authorization history, specifically a denial in May 2024 for a three-level cervical fusion (C4-C7). The initial reviewer, Dr. Alan Foster, MD, asserted that the member's cervical pathology had not materially changed since the prior denial and that the current request did not demonstrate a sufficient change in clinical status to warrant reconsideration.

An appeal was filed on December 5, 2024, on the grounds that: (1) the prior denial was for a different, more extensive procedure (three-level fusion) whereas the current request is for a single-level fusion; (2) the member's clinical status has materially changed since May 2024, supported by a new MRI and a new conservative treatment course; (3) the current request independently meets all medical necessity criteria; and (4) using prior PA history as a denial basis is not authorized under the plan's clinical guidelines.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. PROCEDURAL AND REGULATORY ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Under California law and the plan's own guidelines, the initial denial is procedurally defective and cannot be sustained.

* California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b): This statute mandates that a health plan's denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reason for the denial. A denial notice that fails to apply valid clinical criteria is legally defective. The initial denial of November 20, 2024, relied on "prior PA history" as its primary basis. Review of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria Document CC-SPINE-2024 confirms that prior PA history is NOT an authorized criterion or basis for denial. Each prior authorization request must be evaluated independently on its own clinical merits.
* Distinction of Procedures: The prior denial in May 2024 was for a three-level fusion (C4-C5, C5-C6, C6-C7), which was correctly denied at that time because the C4-C5 and C6-C7 levels did not meet imaging criteria. However, the prior reviewer explicitly noted in the May 2024 record that a single-level C5-C6 request would have been approved at that time. The current request is for a single-level ACDF C5-C6 only. Denying a less invasive, clinically indicated procedure based on the prior denial of a different, more extensive procedure is a procedural error.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. CLINICAL EVALUATION UNDER PLAN CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
An independent evaluation of the current clinical record demonstrates that the member fully satisfies all medical necessity criteria outlined in HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria Document CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2:

* Section 2.1 — Diagnosis Requirement: The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy C5-C6 with progressive, persistent radicular symptoms. The neurological exam dated October 20, 2024, documents objective neurological deficits in a dermatomal distribution, including right C6 dermatomal numbness, an absent right bicep reflex, and right bicep weakness graded at 3+/5 (representing an interval worsening from the prior exam). This satisfies Section 2.1(a) and (c).
* Section 2.2 — Conservative Treatment Requirement:
  - (a) Physical Therapy: The member completed a NEW 8-week supervised physical therapy course (24 sessions) from July to September 2024 (separate from any prior PT). The physical therapist explicitly documented a functional plateau, noting "no further improvement expected, neurological deficit persistent and worsening." This satisfies Section 2.2(a) and complies with California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5, which prohibits plans from mechanically requiring additional physical therapy once a functional plateau is documented.
  - (b) Pharmacotherapy: The member completed a trial of two qualifying agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each: (1) Gabapentin 600mg TID (neuropathic agent) for 6 months, and (2) Methylprednisolone dose packs (oral corticosteroid), 2 courses. This satisfies Section 2.2(b).
  - (c) Interventional Treatment: The member underwent a right C6 epidural steroid injection on November 5, 2024, which provided 50% temporary relief for 2 weeks before symptoms returned to baseline. This satisfies Section 2.2(c).
* Section 2.3 — Imaging Requirement: A current MRI of the cervical spine dated October 15, 2024 (within 6 months of the request) demonstrates a large disc herniation at C5-C6 with moderate-to-severe right C6 nerve root compression and new foraminal stenosis. This represents a documented interval increase in herniation size compared to the prior MRI from April 20, 2024, and directly correlates with the member's right-sided C6 radicular symptoms and bicep weakness. This satisfies Section 2.3.
* Section 2.4 — Physician Documentation: Dr. Victor Osei, MD, has provided the required clinical documentation, including an attestation of completed conservative treatment, clinical rationale for single-level surgical intervention, documentation of severe functional limitations (pain 8/10 at rest, unable to continue working full-time), and a statement of surgical risks and alternatives. This satisfies Section 2.4.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARD EVALUATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Generally accepted standards of medical practice strongly support surgical intervention for this member, independent of internal plan criteria:

* AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update) & NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020): Under these guidelines, surgical decompression via ACDF is indicated as a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) or Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) intervention for patients with cervical radiculopathy who exhibit progressive neurological deficits (such as the member's worsening 3+/5 bicep weakness and absent reflex) or persistent radicular pain that has failed to respond to 6–12 weeks of conservative care. 
* Risk of Delay: The clinical evidence base (AANS/CNS 2023) demonstrates that delaying surgical decompression in the presence of progressive motor weakness (3+/5 bicep strength) increases the risk of permanent, irreversible neurological deficit. Conditioning approval on further conservative trials or prior denial history is contrary to medical standards.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS AND FINAL DETERMINATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Applying the conflict resolution hierarchy (California State Law > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Plan Criteria):

1. California Law (CHSC § 1374.31 and CIC § 10169.5) requires that the plan evaluate the current request on its independent clinical merits and prohibits the mechanical application of conservative treatment requirements when a functional plateau and objective neurological compromise are documented.
2. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS and NASS guidelines) dictate that single-level surgical decompression is strongly indicated for progressive motor deficits and persistent radiculopathy.
3. Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) are fully satisfied in all categories (diagnosis, conservative treatment, imaging, and physician documentation).

Because there is no conflict between these sources—all of which independently support the medical necessity of the requested single-level ACDF C5-C6—the initial denial based on prior PA history is unsubstantiated and must be overturned.
