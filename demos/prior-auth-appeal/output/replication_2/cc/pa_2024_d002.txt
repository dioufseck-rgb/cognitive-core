The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeal Determination Division has completed its de novo review of the prior authorization appeal for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551).

Based on a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, applicable plan coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), and California regulatory standards, the initial prior authorization denial issued on November 8, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED. The requested procedure is APPROVED on appeal.

This determination is based on the following clinical and regulatory findings:

1. RESOLUTION OF THE TEMPORAL PARADOX OF THE EPIDURAL STEROID INJECTION TRIAL
The initial denial issued on November 8, 2024, was technically compliant with Section 2.2(c) of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 at that specific point in time. The patient underwent a left C6 epidural steroid injection on October 28, 2024. As of the date of the initial denial (November 8, 2024), only 11 days had elapsed post-injection. Because the standard clinical observation period to evaluate the efficacy of an interventional trial is 3 weeks, the conservative treatment trial was active and incomplete on November 8, and a "failed" trial could not yet be clinically documented by the initial reviewer.

However, an appeal review is a de novo evaluation of medical necessity based on the clinical facts established at the time of the appeal (November 22, 2024). The clinical record now documents that as of November 18, 2024 (exactly 3 weeks post-injection), the patient's symptoms returned to baseline after providing only temporary, 40% relief. This completed 3-week observation period confirms the clinical failure of the interventional trial.

Because this critical clinical outcome matured prior to the appeal date, it is fully admissible and satisfies the interventional treatment requirement under Section 2.2(c). Combined with the patient's documented completion of 7 weeks of supervised physical therapy (exceeding the 6-week requirement in Section 2.2(a) and reaching a documented functional plateau at week 6) and completed pharmacotherapy trials (Gabapentin 600mg TID for 4 months and Naproxen 500mg BID for 3 months, satisfying Section 2.2(b)), the patient has fully satisfied all conservative treatment requirements under Section 2.2 of CC-SPINE-2024.

2. CLARIFICATION OF REGULATORY EFFECTS OF PROCEDURAL DEFECTS UNDER CHSC § 1374.31(b)
The initial denial notice failed to include the referenced clinical criteria document and the mandatory Independent Medical Review (IMR) notice, which constitutes a procedural defect under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b).

We clarify that under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a defective notice does not mandate an automatic substantive overturn of a medical necessity determination, nor does it void the plan's right to review clinical criteria. Rather, the statutory remedy for a defective notice is the tolling of appeal timelines and potential administrative penalties for plan non-compliance. Therefore, while these procedural defects are acknowledged as regulatory violations that subject the plan to administrative oversight, they are not the legal basis for the substantive approval of this surgery. The primary substantive basis for this overturn is the clinical evidence of conservative treatment failure, which was fully established and documented by the time of the appeal on November 22, 2024.

3. SATISFACTION OF SECTION 2.4 PHYSICIAN DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS
Section 2.4 of CC-SPINE-2024 requires the treating physician to provide: (a) an attestation of completed conservative treatment, (b) clinical rationale, (c) a description of functional limitations, and (d) a statement of surgical risks and alternatives.

The clinical record and Dr. Kim's surgical request, when evaluated in their entirety, satisfy the clinical intent of these requirements:
- Attestation of Conservative Treatment (Section 2.4(a)): Satisfied by the comprehensive clinical record detailing 7 weeks of physical therapy, 4 months of Gabapentin, 3 months of Naproxen, and the left C6 epidural steroid injection.
- Clinical Rationale (Section 2.4(b)): Satisfied by the direct correlation between the patient's persistent left C6 radiculopathy (left C6 dermatomal numbness, diminished left bicep reflex, and 4/5 left bicep weakness documented on September 25, 2024) and the MRI findings of a large C5-C6 disc herniation with moderate left C6 nerve root compression (MRI dated September 20, 2024).
- Functional Limitations (Section 2.4(c)): Documented in the physical therapy notes (plateau at week 6 with persistent radicular symptoms) and the neurological exam showing functional impairment (4/5 bicep weakness).
- Surgical Risks and Alternatives (Section 2.4(d)): Satisfied by the documented failure of extensive conservative alternatives (PT, multiple pharmacotherapies, and epidural injection), leaving surgical decompression and fusion as the only viable clinical option to prevent permanent neurological deficit.
