Dear Mr. Green and Dr. Kim,

The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the first-level appeal submitted on November 22, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT code 22551) at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center. 

After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California statutory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has determined that the original denial issued on November 08, 2024, must be OVERTURNED IN FULL. Prior authorization for CPT 22551 is hereby APPROVED.

This determination is based on two independent and sufficient grounds:
1. CLINICAL MERITS: The clinical record demonstrates that the member fully satisfies all medical necessity criteria under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 and California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5.
2. PROCEDURAL DEFECTS: The original denial notice dated November 08, 2024, was procedurally defective under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b) due to the failure to provide the referenced clinical criteria and the omission of mandatory Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights.

I. CLINICAL HISTORY AND EVIDENCE SUMMARY

The clinical record submitted for review establishes the following clinical history:
- Presenting Diagnosis: Cervical radiculopathy at C5-C6 with significant functional impairment.
- Objective Imaging: A cervical spine MRI dated September 20, 2024, demonstrates a large disc herniation at C5-C6 causing moderate left C6 nerve root compression. No spinal cord contact or myelopathy is noted.
- Neurological Examination: A physical and neurological examination dated September 25, 2024, documents objective neurological deficits correlating with the imaging, including left C6 dermatomal numbness, a diminished left bicep reflex, and left bicep motor weakness graded at 4/5.

II. PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery — Cervical Procedures (Document: CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2) outlines the requirements for authorization of CPT 22551. The member's clinical presentation satisfies every requirement:

1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): Requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (nerve root compression with radicular pain, numbness, or weakness in a dermatomal distribution) supported by an MRI within 6 months.
   - Status: MET. The member has a confirmed diagnosis of C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy. The MRI dated September 20, 2024 (within 6 months) shows moderate left C6 nerve root compression, and the neurological exam dated September 25, 2024, confirms left C6 dermatomal numbness and 4/5 bicep weakness.
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): Requires completion of a documented course of conservative treatment consisting of physical therapy, pharmacotherapy, and an interventional treatment.
   - Section 2.2(a) (Physical Therapy): Requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy.
     - Status: MET. The member completed 7 weeks (21 sessions) of supervised physical therapy.
   - Section 2.2(b) (Pharmacotherapy): Requires a trial of at least two listed classes (including NSAIDs and neuropathic agents) for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
     - Status: MET. The member trialed Gabapentin (neuropathic agent) for 4 months and Naproxen (NSAID) for 3 months.
   - Section 2.2(c) (Interventional Treatment): Requires at least one cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point injection series.
     - Status: MET. The member underwent a left C6 epidural steroid injection on October 28, 2024.
3. Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement): Requires current imaging (within 6 months) demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms and proposed surgical level.
   - Status: MET. The MRI dated September 20, 2024, demonstrates a large C5-C6 disc herniation with moderate left C6 nerve root compression, which directly correlates with the left C6 dermatomal numbness and left bicep weakness.
4. Section 2.4 (Physician Documentation): Requires treating physician attestation, clinical rationale, functional limitations, and surgical risks discussion.
   - Status: MET. Dr. Sarah Kim, MD, provided the necessary clinical documentation and surgical rationale.

Because the member met all clinical criteria under CC-SPINE-2024, the original denial on clinical grounds was incorrect.

III. REGULATORY COMPLIANCE ANALYSIS (CALIFORNIA LAW)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
The original denial notice issued on November 08, 2024, violated multiple provisions of the California regulatory framework, rendering the denial procedurally invalid:

1. Defective Denial Notice under CHSC § 1374.31:
   - Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a)(2), a plan must include the specific criteria applied and the specific clinical reason for the denial in its written notice.
   - Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason is legally DEFECTIVE and must be treated as if no denial was issued for the purposes of appeal timelines.
   - Analysis: The denial notice issued by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, stated: "Request denied. Member does not meet criteria for requested procedure under plan policy. See attached criteria document." However, no criteria document was attached, and the notice failed to specify which clinical criteria were not met or provide any specific clinical reasoning. This failure constitutes a major procedural violation under CHSC § 1374.31(b).
2. Omission of Independent Medical Review (IMR) Notice:
   - CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2) mandates that the plan include notice of IMR rights in any medical necessity denial.
   - Analysis: The plan failed to include the mandatory IMR notice, violating California statutory requirements.
3. Physical Therapy Mandate Restrictions under CIC § 10169.5:
   - California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2) prohibits an insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy if the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a "functional plateau" without adequate resolution of symptoms.
   - CIC § 10169.5(c) further prohibits plans from mechanically applying a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy without reference to whether a functional plateau was reached.
   - Analysis: The member completed 7 weeks of physical therapy and reached a documented functional plateau at week 6. Any denial based on a mechanical application of physical therapy requirements would directly violate CIC § 10169.5.

IV. CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARDS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Generally accepted standards of medical practice strongly support surgical intervention in this case. Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Radiculopathy (2023 Update) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), the member meets Tier 2 "Reasonable Indication" criteria for surgical decompression:
- The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with correlating MRI findings (large C5-C6 disc herniation with moderate left C6 nerve root compression).
- The member has persistent, objective neurological deficits (left C6 dermatomal numbness, diminished bicep reflex, and 4/5 bicep weakness) that have failed to resolve despite an exhaustive course of conservative therapy, including 7 weeks of physical therapy (with documented functional plateau), 4 months of neuropathic pharmacotherapy, and a targeted cervical epidural steroid injection.
- Delaying surgical intervention in the presence of progressive or persistent motor weakness (4/5 bicep weakness) poses a risk of permanent neurological deficit, making surgical decompression via ACDF clinically appropriate and medically necessary.

Accordingly, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED.
