FINAL APPEAL DETERMINATION: UPHOLD DENIAL

Dear Mr. Flores,

The Appeals and Grievances Department at HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of the appeal submitted on your behalf by Dr. Karen Davis, MD, regarding the prior authorization denial for an anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT code 22551). After a comprehensive review of your clinical records, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory standards, and generally accepted clinical guidelines, HealthFirst has determined that the initial denial must be UPHELD. 

Surgical intervention is not authorized at this time because the clinical evidence does not demonstrate current medical necessity for this procedure under your health plan's coverage criteria or under California law. Below is a detailed explanation of the clinical, contractual, and regulatory basis for this decision.

I. CLINICAL BACKGROUND & PRESENTING HISTORY
According to the clinical records submitted for review:
1. Clinical Presentation: You are a 54-year-old construction worker who operates heavy equipment. You present with axial neck pain, rated as 5/10.
2. Neurological Examination (dated September 20, 2024): Your neurological examination is completely normal. There is no evidence of cervical radiculopathy (no radiating pain, numbness, or weakness in a dermatomal distribution) and no evidence of cervical myelopathy (no gait disturbance, no bilateral upper extremity weakness, no hyperreflexia, no positive Hoffman sign, and no upper motor neuron signs).
3. Imaging Findings (MRI dated September 15, 2024): The MRI of your cervical spine demonstrates severe central canal stenosis at the C5-C6 level with a canal diameter of 6mm (normal is greater than 13mm). There is spinal cord contact, but no spinal cord compression, no T2 signal change, and no myelomalacia (spinal cord softening/damage).
4. Conservative Treatment History:
   - Physical Therapy: You completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy, which resulted in modest improvement in your axial neck pain (improving from 7/10 to 5/10) but addressed no neurological symptoms.
   - Pharmacotherapy: You completed a 3-month trial of Naproxen 500mg BID (providing moderate pain relief) and a 6-week trial of Cyclobenzaprine (providing sleep improvement only).
   - Interventional Treatment: You received a C5-C6 epidural steroid injection on October 10, 2024, which provided 60% pain relief lasting 6 weeks.
5. Surgeon's Assessment: Dr. Davis has requested prophylactic decompression, noting that your critically narrow canal diameter puts you at high risk for acute spinal cord injury in the event of cervical trauma, particularly given your high-risk occupation as a construction worker.

II. PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)
HealthFirst reviews requests for cervical spinal procedures under Clinical Coverage Criteria document CC-SPINE-2024. To obtain authorization for an ACDF (CPT 22551), a member must satisfy all criteria outlined in Section 2:

1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): This section requires a confirmed diagnosis of either (a) cervical radiculopathy with nerve root compression and correlating radicular symptoms, (b) cervical myelopathy with spinal cord compression and correlating myelopathic symptoms, or (c) cervical disc herniation with an objective neurological deficit. Your clinical record documents a completely normal neurological examination on September 20, 2024, with no radiculopathy, no myelopathy, and no objective neurological deficits. Therefore, Section 2.1 is not satisfied.
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): You have successfully completed the conservative treatment requirements, including 8 weeks of physical therapy (exceeding the 6-week minimum in Section 2.2(a)), two pharmacotherapy trials (exceeding the 4-week minimum in Section 2.2(b)), and one interventional epidural steroid injection (satisfying Section 2.2(c)). However, because the diagnostic criteria in Section 2.1 are not met, conservative treatment completion alone does not establish medical necessity for surgery.
3. Section 3.1 (Exclusions): This section explicitly states that authorization will be denied when "surgery is requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy." Because your primary symptom is axial neck pain (5/10) and you have no documented radiculopathy or myelopathy, your request is subject to this exclusion.

Consequently, under the terms of your health plan's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), the requested prophylactic surgery cannot be authorized.

III. CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARD ANALYSIS
Under California law, health plan criteria cannot be more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. Therefore, we evaluated your request against national clinical guidelines, including the American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Joint Section Guidelines (2023 Update) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020).

1. Surgical Indications: According to the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Section 3.1, surgical decompression (such as ACDF) is strongly indicated (Tier 1) for moderate-to-severe myelopathy with objective cord compression or myelomalacia on MRI. It is a reasonable option (Tier 2) for mild myelopathy or persistent radiculopathy that has failed conservative treatment. 
2. Surgery Not Indicated (Tier 3): The guidelines classify "axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy" and "imaging findings without correlating symptoms" under Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated). 
3. Prophylactic Surgery: Generally accepted clinical standards do not support prophylactic surgical decompression for asymptomatic cervical stenosis or stenosis presenting with axial neck pain alone, even when the canal diameter is severely reduced (6mm) and cord contact is present. Clinical studies demonstrate that the risk of complications from elective cervical fusion outweighs the theoretical risk of acute spinal cord injury from minor trauma in asymptomatic individuals. 

Therefore, the requested prophylactic ACDF is considered "Surgery Not Indicated" under generally accepted clinical standards, and there is no conflict between the plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) and national clinical guidelines.

IV. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK ANALYSIS
We evaluated your appeal under the relevant provisions of the California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) and California Insurance Code (CIC):

1. Medical Necessity Standard (CHSC § 1374.32): Under California law, "medically necessary" services are those that a physician exercising prudent clinical judgment would provide to prevent, evaluate, diagnose, or treat an illness, injury, or disease in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice. Because prophylactic decompression for asymptomatic stenosis is not supported by generally accepted standards of medical practice (as detailed in Section III), the requested procedure does not meet the statutory definition of medical necessity.
2. Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions (DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014): This regulation requires plans to waive conservative treatment and expedite reviews for urgent neurological conditions, specifically identifying "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI" as a condition risking irreversible harm. Your MRI on September 15, 2024, explicitly noted "no T2 signal change" and "no myelomalacia," and your physical exam was normal. Therefore, your condition does not qualify for the urgent exceptions under APL 22-014.
3. Physical Therapy Prerequisites (CIC § 10169.5): This statute prevents plans from mechanically requiring physical therapy if it is contraindicated or if the patient has reached a functional plateau. While you did complete physical therapy, this statute does not override the fundamental requirement that a patient must have an indicated diagnosis and correlating neurological deficit to undergo spinal surgery.

V. RESPONSE TO APPEAL ARGUMENTS
Dr. Davis argues that because you are a construction worker operating heavy equipment, you are at an elevated risk for cervical trauma, and prophylactic surgery is necessary to prevent catastrophic spinal cord injury. 

While we acknowledge the physical demands and potential trauma risks associated with your occupation, clinical evidence does not support performing a major spinal fusion (ACDF) prophylactically based on occupational risk alone when there are no current neurological deficits. Generally accepted medical practice for severe asymptomatic cervical stenosis involves:
- Activity modification to minimize the risk of high-impact cervical trauma.
- Close clinical monitoring with regular neurological examinations.
- Repeat imaging if new neurological symptoms (such as radiating pain, numbness, weakness, or gait instability) develop.

Performing an ACDF carries inherent surgical risks, including adjacent segment disease, pseudarthrosis, hardware failure, and dysphagia. In the absence of active radiculopathy or myelopathy, the clinical consensus is that the immediate risks of surgery exceed the potential preventive benefits.

VI. CONCLUSION & DISPOSITION
Based on the plan's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Sections 2.1 and 3.1), California statutory standards of medical necessity (CHSC § 1374.32), and national clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023), the prior authorization denial for a prophylactic ACDF at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is UPHELD. 

We recommend that you discuss activity modifications and a clinical monitoring plan with Dr. Davis to safely manage your cervical stenosis.

VII. NOTICE OF INDEPENDENT MEDICAL REVIEW (IMR) RIGHTS
If you disagree with this appeal decision, you have the right to request an Independent Medical Review (IMR) from the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) or the California Department of Insurance, depending on your plan type. 

Under CHSC § 1374.30, you may submit an application for an IMR if your request for a health care service has been denied on the basis of medical necessity. The IMR process is free to you and is conducted by independent medical specialists who are not affiliated with HealthFirst. The independent reviewer will determine whether the requested service is medically necessary under California's clinical standards (CHSC § 1374.32). If the IMR reviewer decides in your favor, HealthFirst is legally required to authorize the service.
