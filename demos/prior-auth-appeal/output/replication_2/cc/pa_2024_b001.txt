The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the first-level clinical appeal submitted on your behalf by Dr. James Wilson, MD, regarding the prior authorization denial for a two-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 and C6-C7 levels.

DETERMINATION: PARTIAL OVERTURN
After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, we have made the following determination:
1. APPROVED (OVERTURNED): The request for ACDF at the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551) is APPROVED. The prior denial for this level is OVERTURNED.
2. DENIED (UPHELD): The request for ACDF at the C6-C7 level (CPT 22551, second unit / CPT 22552) is DENIED. The prior denial for this level is UPHELD.

CLINICAL HISTORY AND PRESENTING SYMPTOMS
Ms. Linda Park is a 45-year-old female with a history of cervical radiculopathy. Clinical documentation from her neurological examination on September 5, 2024, reveals right C6 dermatomal numbness and tingling, a diminished right bicep reflex, and right grip strength of 4/5. No C7 dermatomal symptoms, C7 reflex changes, or C7-related motor deficits were documented. There were no bilateral findings or upper motor neuron signs.

An MRI of the cervical spine dated September 1, 2024, demonstrated:
- C5-C6: A large disc herniation with moderate-to-severe nerve root compression and moderate foraminal stenosis, correlating directly with the patient's right C6 radicular symptoms.
- C6-C7: A mild disc bulge with minimal foraminal narrowing, no spinal cord contact, and no identifiable nerve root compression.

Conservative treatments completed prior to the surgical request include:
- Physical Therapy: 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy (24 sessions total) across two providers, which failed to resolve her C6 radicular symptoms.
- Pharmacotherapy: Gabapentin 600mg TID for 4 months (providing partial relief) and Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks (providing minimal benefit).
- Interventional Treatment: A right C6 selective epidural steroid injection on October 1, 2024, which provided 50% temporary relief of right C6 symptoms, with symptoms fully returning by week 4.

LEVEL-BY-LEVEL MEDICAL NECESSITY ANALYSIS

1. C5-C6 Level (APPROVED / OVERTURNED)
The clinical record fully satisfies the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (Document: CC-SPINE-2024) for the C5-C6 level:
- Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits (diminished right bicep reflex, 4/5 grip strength) and an MRI within 6 months (September 1, 2024) demonstrating structural pathology (large disc herniation with moderate-to-severe nerve root compression) consistent with her C6 symptoms.
- Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): The member completed 8 weeks of physical therapy (exceeding the 6-week minimum in 2.2(a)), trialed two qualifying medications (Gabapentin and Cyclobenzaprine) for at least 4 weeks each (satisfying 2.2(b)), and underwent a cervical epidural steroid injection on October 1, 2024 (satisfying 2.2(c)).
- Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement): The MRI dated September 1, 2024, demonstrates structural pathology at C5-C6 correlating with the C6 symptoms.
- Section 2.4 (Physician Documentation): Dr. Wilson provided the necessary clinical rationale and documentation.

Furthermore, under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(a), surgical intervention at C5-C6 is "medically necessary" as it is in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice and is clinically appropriate. This is supported by the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Tier 2 criteria (reasonable indication for radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after conservative trial). Therefore, the denial for the C5-C6 level is overturned.

2. C6-C7 Level (DENIED / UPHELD)
The clinical record does not satisfy the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (Document: CC-SPINE-2024) or generally accepted clinical standards for the C6-C7 level:
- Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement) & Section 3.3 (Exclusions): The MRI dated September 1, 2024, shows only "mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing, no cord contact, no nerve root compression identifiable" at C6-C7. This does not constitute structural pathology consistent with the member's symptoms, as there are no documented C7 symptoms or deficits.
- Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): Conservative treatment was directed entirely at the C6 radicular symptoms. No conservative treatment was directed at or documented for C7 symptoms, as none existed.

Dr. Wilson's clinical rationale for fusing the C6-C7 level is prophylactic, aiming to prevent adjacent segment disease (noting a 25% 5-year incidence after single-level ACDF). However, under generally accepted clinical standards, including the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Tier 3 criteria, surgical fusion is "not indicated" for imaging findings without correlating clinical symptoms or deficits. Prophylactic fusion of an asymptomatic level with only mild degenerative changes does not meet the definition of medical necessity under CHSC § 1374.32(a) because it is not clinically appropriate or effective for treating an active illness, injury, or disease. Consequently, the denial for the C6-C7 level is upheld.

REGULATORY AND PROCEDURAL COMPLIANCE REVIEW
Under CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2), a health plan's denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reason for the denial. The initial denial notice issued on October 28, 2024, failed to cite the specific sections of the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) applied, rendering it procedurally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b).

In resolving this procedural defect, we have performed a de novo review of the entire clinical record. Under California's regulatory framework, while a procedural defect requires correction and de novo review, the substantive standard for authorization remains the statutory definition of medical necessity under CHSC § 1374.32(a). Because the clinical evidence does not support the medical necessity of the C6-C7 level, a full overturn of both levels based solely on a procedural defect is not legally or clinically warranted. The correct resolution under California law is a partial overturn, approving the medically necessary C5-C6 level and upholding the denial of the non-medically necessary C6-C7 level.

CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND GUIDELINES RESOLUTION
Generally accepted clinical standards, including the North American Spine Society (NASS) Clinical Guidelines (2020) and the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), require a direct correlation between clinical symptoms, physical examination findings, and advanced imaging pathology for each level of a proposed multi-level spinal fusion. Prophylactic stabilization of an adjacent segment with mild, asymptomatic degenerative changes is not supported by high-quality clinical evidence and is associated with unnecessary surgical risks (e.g., increased operative time, blood loss, and adjacent segment stress at higher levels) without proven therapeutic benefit. Therefore, clinical evidence standards align with the plan's criteria in denying the C6-C7 level.

SUMMARY OF AUTHORIZATION DECISION
- CPT Code 22551 (ACDF, single level — C5-C6): APPROVED.
- CPT Code 22551 (second unit) / 22552 (ACDF, additional level — C6-C7): DENIED.

CALIFORNIA INDEPENDENT MEDICAL REVIEW (IMR) RIGHTS
If you disagree with the portion of this decision that upholds the denial of the C6-C7 level, you have the right to request an Independent Medical Review (IMR) from the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC).

Under CHSC § 1374.30, you are entitled to an IMR if the health plan has denied, modified, or delayed a health care service based on a decision that the service is not medically necessary. The IMR process is free to you.

How to Apply:
1. You may submit an application for IMR to the DMHC.
2. An application form and addressed envelope are enclosed with this letter.
3. You may also apply online at the DMHC website (www.dmhc.ca.gov) or call the DMHC Help Center at 1-888-466-2219.

The DMHC will review your case, and if it qualifies, will refer it to an independent medical specialist who will make a binding decision. The standard timeframe for an IMR decision is 30 days from receipt of all required documentation (CHSC § 1374.32 / Part IV.2).
