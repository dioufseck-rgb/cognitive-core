APPEAL DISPOSITION: REFERRAL TO GATE (INDEPENDENT PHYSICIAN REVIEW)

Dear Mr. Achebe and Dr. Osei:

HealthFirst has completed its first-level internal appeal review of the prior authorization denial for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). 

To comply with California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1367.01 and California Code of Regulations (CCR) Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1, health plans must issue a definitive appeal determination within 30 days (or 72 hours for urgent cases). A "remand" is not a legally recognized appeal disposition under California law and would impermissibly exceed statutory timelines, triggering an automatic "deemed approval" under CHSC § 1367.01. 

Upon review of the clinical record, the plan's internal coverage criteria, and California statutory requirements, we have determined that this case presents a direct conflict between overly restrictive plan criteria and the patient's clinical presentation. Because this represents a borderline case, we are routing this request to GATE for an expedited Independent Physician Review by a board-certified specialist.

I. INITIAL DENIAL AND PLAN CRITERIA EVALUATION

The initial prior authorization request was denied on November 5, 2024, under Reason Code MED-003, citing incomplete conservative treatment. The initial reviewer evaluated the request under the standard pathway of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, which requires 6 weeks of physical therapy, trials of two pharmacotherapy agents, and an interventional procedure.

We note that because the patient has a primary diagnosis of mild cervical myelopathy, the request should have been evaluated under the Myelopathy Pathway Exception 2.2(A). Under Exception 2.2(A), the physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks, the interventional treatment requirement is waived, but the pharmacotherapy requirement (trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each) remains in full.

Even when evaluated under the correct Myelopathy Pathway Exception 2.2(A), the request remains non-compliant with the plan's internal criteria. The clinical record demonstrates that the patient has completed only 3 weeks of physical therapy (9 sessions) and has trialed only one pharmacotherapy agent (Baclofen 5mg BID for 6 weeks). No second pharmacotherapy agent has been trialed.

II. REGULATORY ANALYSIS AND PLAN CRITERIA NON-COMPLIANCE

Although the request does not meet the plan's internal criteria, California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) prohibits a health insurer from conditioning the approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy when "the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." 

The patient's MRI dated October 1, 2024, demonstrates moderate central cord compression at C5-C6 with a canal diameter of 8mm and cord contact. The neurological exam dated October 5, 2024, confirms objective upper motor neuron signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs and mild hyperreflexia. Because cervical myelopathy involves objective structural spinal cord compression that physical therapy cannot anatomically resolve, the plan's mechanical application of a minimum physical therapy duration (whether 6 weeks under the standard pathway or 4 weeks under the myelopathy pathway) is legally non-compliant under CIC § 10169.5.

III. CLINICAL URGENCY AND GUIDELINE ANALYSIS

In the appeal request, Dr. Osei declared immediate surgical urgency, stating in a letter dated November 1, 2024, that the patient's cord compression is "severe and progressive," that further delay is "medically contraindicated," and that any delay risks "irreversible neurological deterioration."

Under Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, plans must waive conservative treatment requirements for time-sensitive neurological conditions where delay risks irreversible harm. However, APL 22-014 requires objective imaging evidence of progressive pathology (such as cord signal change, myelomalacia, or progressive herniation on serial imaging) or acute progressive deficits to support such a waiver.

We must address the direct contradiction between Dr. Osei's subjective urgency declaration and the objective clinical findings in the record:
1. Imaging Findings: The MRI dated October 1, 2024, shows moderate central cord compression but explicitly notes "No T2 signal change" and "No myelomalacia."
2. Clinical Presentation: The neurological exam dated October 5, 2024, shows a modified Japanese Orthopaedic Association (mJOA) score of 15/18, which classifies the patient's condition as "mild myelopathy." Furthermore, the patient's tandem gait is intact, hand grip strength is 4+/5 bilaterally with no frank weakness, activities of daily living (ADLs) are fully intact and independent, and symptoms have been stable for 6 months.

Under the American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) 2023 Joint Section Guidelines (Section 2.1), a Tier 1 (Urgent) classification requires acute progressive neurological deficits or the presence of myelomalacia on MRI. Because the patient has mild, stable myelopathy (mJOA 15) and no myelomalacia, the presentation is classified as Tier 2 (Elective Surgical Candidate). Tier 2 status supports the clinical appropriateness of surgery as an elective option but does not support an emergency waiver of all conservative treatment requirements under DMHC APL 22-014.

IV. CONCLUSION AND ROUTING TO GATE

Because the plan's internal physical therapy requirements are legally non-compliant under CIC § 10169.5, but the clinical findings do not support a Tier 1 urgency that would completely waive conservative treatment under DMHC APL 22-014, this case represents a borderline clinical scenario. 

To resolve this tension and ensure a legally compliant, evidence-based decision, this case is hereby routed to GATE for Independent Physician Review. A board-certified specialist will review the clinical record to determine if the proposed elective ACDF is medically necessary under generally accepted standards of medical practice, notwithstanding the incomplete conservative trials. 

You will be notified of the independent physician's determination within the statutory timeframe.
