SECTION 1: CLINICAL PRESENTATION & EVIDENCE SUMMARY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1.1 Clinical History and Neurological Examination
Ms. Helen Brooks is a 49-year-old female presenting with early cervical myelopathy. A physical and neurological examination conducted on October 5, 2024, documented objective, severe upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including:
  - Bilateral Hoffman signs;
  - Bilateral hyperreflexia of the upper extremities;
  - Subtle balance impairment demonstrated during tandem gait testing.

1.2 Imaging Findings and Radiological Conflict Resolution
An MRI of the cervical spine dated October 1, 2024, demonstrated a C4-C5 disc herniation with direct spinal cord contact. The original radiologist (Radiologist A) noted a "possible early T2 signal change — equivocal, may represent artifact."

A subsequent second read of the same imaging on October 15, 2024, by Radiologist B at the treating hospital (UCSF Medical Center) confirmed "C4-C5 cord compression with early myelomalacia." Radiologist B explicitly documented that the T2 hyperintensity is reproducible on multiple sequences and represents early cord signal change, not an artifact, which is highly consistent with early myelopathy.

Although the plan's imaging reviewer, Dr. Alan Foster, MD, disputed this finding on November 5, 2024, claiming the T2 signal change was below the threshold for clinical significance, the clinical record and the reproducible radiological findings of the treating hospital's radiologist establish objective structural cord compression and early myelomalacia.

1.3 Conservative Treatment History
Ms. Brooks has completed an extensive course of conservative therapy, including:
  - Physical Therapy: 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy (24 sessions total) completed, with documented notes indicating "no improvement in UMN signs."
  - Pharmacotherapy: Over 3 months of Gabapentin (400mg TID) yielding only partial benefit, and 6 weeks of Cyclobenzaprine yielding minimal response.
  - Interventional Treatment: Not performed. Dr. James Park, MD, explicitly documented that he did not refer the patient for epidural steroid injections because they are relatively contraindicated in the presence of severe spinal cord compression due to the risk of acute neurological deterioration.

SECTION 2: COMPLIANCE WITH PLAN COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The initial denial on November 20, 2024, asserted that the "myelopathy exception does not apply" and denied coverage because "no interventional procedure [was] completed." This application of the plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) was clinically and procedurally incorrect.

2.1 Satisfaction of Section 2.1(b) (Diagnosis Requirement)
Under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1(b), cervical myelopathy is confirmed by "spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms (gait disturbance, bilateral upper extremity weakness, hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign, bowel/bladder dysfunction)." 
Ms. Brooks' clinical record objectively documents bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia, tandem gait impairment, and MRI-confirmed C4-C5 cord compression. Therefore, she fully satisfies the diagnostic criteria for cervical myelopathy under Section 2.1(b).

2.2 Satisfaction of Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway Exception)
Because Ms. Brooks has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, her request must be evaluated under the "EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY" of the plan's criteria. Under this pathway:
  - Physical Therapy: The requirement is reduced to a 4-week minimum. Ms. Brooks completed 8 weeks of physical therapy, exceeding this requirement.
  - Pharmacotherapy: The requirement for a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each remains in full. Ms. Brooks completed 3 months of Gabapentin and 6 weeks of Cyclobenzaprine, fully satisfying this requirement.
  - Interventional Treatment: Section 2.2(A) explicitly states that the interventional treatment requirement (Section 2.2(c)) "is WAIVED if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration."

Dr. Park explicitly documented that epidural steroid injections were not performed because they are contraindicated in cord compression and pose a risk of neurological deterioration. Consequently, the interventional requirement was fully waived under the plan's own written criteria. Denying the request for "no interventional procedure completed" represents a direct violation and misapplication of CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(A).

SECTION 3: CALIFORNIA STATUTORY AND REGULATORY COMPLIANCE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Even if the plan's internal criteria did not contain a waiver, California law and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) regulations supersede internal plan guidelines and mandate an immediate overturn of the denial.

3.1 California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(3) — Physical Therapy Restrictions
Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), a health plan is strictly prohibited from conditioning the approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy when "the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." 
Ms. Brooks' upper motor neuron signs (bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, gait impairment) and MRI-documented cord compression constitute objective structural neurological compromise. Mechanically requiring further conservative therapy or denying surgery based on conservative treatment thresholds violates California statutory law.

3.2 DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 — Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions
DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 dictates that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a time-sensitive condition with a high potential for irreversible neurological harm if untreated. 
APL 22-014 Section II.2 prohibits plans from requiring a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents urgency. The plan's mechanical denial of surgical decompression for a patient with documented myelomalacia and UMN signs directly violates APL 22-014.

3.3 California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32 — Clinical Standards of Care
Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the standard for medical necessity is governed by generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersede restrictive internal plan criteria. 
According to the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) and NASS Guidelines (2020), surgical decompression is the primary, strongly indicated treatment (Tier 1) for patients with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy or myelopathy with objective cord signal changes (myelomalacia). Delaying surgery to enforce conservative treatment trials is clinically contraindicated as it is associated with a decreased likelihood of neurological recovery and risks permanent, irreversible spinal cord damage.

3.4 Procedural Invalidity of "Remand" under CHSC § 1367.01
Under California Health & Safety Code § 1367.01 and California Code of Regulations (CCR) Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1, a health plan must issue a definitive "Uphold" or "Overturn" decision within 30 days of receiving a first-level internal appeal. 
Attempting to "remand" or "gate" the case back to the provider to resolve the radiological conflict between the plan's reviewer and the treating hospital's radiologist is legally impermissible. Such procedural delays violate mandatory regulatory timelines and would result in an automatic "deemed approved" status under CHSC § 1367.01 / CCR Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1, exposing the plan to administrative penalties and bad-faith litigation.
