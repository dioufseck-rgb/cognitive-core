The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the first-level internal appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a one-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California statutory and regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has OVERTURNED the initial denial. The requested surgical procedure is approved.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND EVIDENCE SUMMARY
The member is a 49-year-old female presenting with early cervical myelopathy. The clinical record contains the following objective evidence:
1. Neurological Examination (dated October 5, 2024): Documented objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia of the upper extremities, and subtle balance impairment on tandem gait.
2. Imaging Findings (MRI dated October 1, 2024):
   - Radiologist A (original read, October 1, 2024): C4-C5 disc herniation with cord contact. Possible early T2 signal change — equivocal, may represent artifact. Clinical correlation required.
   - Radiologist B (treating hospital second read, October 15, 2024): C4-C5 cord compression with early myelomalacia. T2 hyperintensity is reproducible on multiple sequences and represents early cord signal change, not artifact. Consistent with early myelopathy.
   - Plan Radiologist Review (November 5, 2024): T2 signal change is below threshold for clinical significance. Not consistent with myelomalacia. Represents artifact or normal variant.
3. Conservative Treatment History:
   - Physical Therapy: Completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy with no improvement in upper motor neuron signs.
   - Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 3-month trial of Gabapentin 400mg TID (partial benefit) and a 6-week trial of Cyclobenzaprine (minimal benefit).
   - Interventional Treatment: Not performed. The treating neurosurgeon, Dr. James Park, MD, explicitly documented that epidural steroid injections were not performed due to myelopathy concerns, as epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated in the presence of spinal cord compression.

II. RESOLUTION OF CROSS-SOURCE CONFLICTS & HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY
Under California law, a health plan's internal appeals department must issue a definitive binary decision (either UPHOLD or OVERTURN) within statutory timelines (30 days for standard appeals, 72 hours for expedited appeals) pursuant to California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1367.01 and § 1368. The plan has no legal authority to "remand" an appeal or delay a decision to resolve internal radiological disputes. Any such administrative delay or non-decision exceeds the statutory timeline, resulting in an automatic "deemed approval" (overturn) under CHSC § 1367.01 and California Code of Regulations (CCR) Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1, while exposing the plan to regulatory sanctions.

To resolve the conflict between the plan's internal reviewer and the treating hospital's radiologist, we apply the established hierarchy of authority: California Law > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Plan's Internal Criteria.

1. Application of Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)
The initial denial under reason code MED-003 asserted that the "Myelopathy exception does not apply" because the plan's internal reviewer (Dr. Alan Foster, MD) deemed the T2 signal change to be an artifact. This application of the criteria was incorrect and misapplied the plan's own rules:
- Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement) states that current imaging must demonstrate structural pathology consistent with symptoms and must be interpreted by "a radiologist or treating specialist."
- Radiologist B of the treating hospital (UCSF Medical Center) is a qualified radiologist who interpreted the MRI on October 15, 2024, confirming C4-C5 cord compression with early myelomalacia and noting that the T2 hyperintensity is reproducible on multiple sequences and represents early cord signal change, not artifact. This interpretation satisfies Section 2.3.
- The plan's criteria do not state that the plan's internal reviewer's reading overrides a qualified treating radiologist's reading. By ignoring Radiologist B's reading, the plan arbitrarily preferred its own reviewer's reading over a qualified hospital radiologist's reading, violating Section 2.3.
- Acknowledging Radiologist B's interpretation satisfies Section 2.3 and Section 2.1(b) (confirmed cervical myelopathy with spinal cord compression and myelopathic symptoms: gait disturbance, hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign).
- Consequently, the Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A) is triggered. Under Exception 2.2(A), the physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks (the member completed 8 weeks), the pharmacotherapy requirement is met (the member completed trials of gabapentin and cyclobenzaprine), and the interventional treatment requirement (2.2(c)) is WAIVED because the treating physician documented that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration (relative contraindication in cord compression). Therefore, the member fully satisfies the plan's clinical coverage criteria under the Myelopathy Pathway.

2. Application of California Regulatory Framework
Even if the plan's internal criteria were interpreted to deny coverage, California law overrides more restrictive plan criteria:
- CHSC § 1374.32(b) (Standard of Review for IMR) mandates that medical necessity determinations must be made in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice, and that the plan's internal criteria shall not be the sole or determinative basis for a decision if they are more restrictive than clinical standards.
- DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Part II.1 & II.2) establishes that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI is a time-sensitive neurological condition with potential for irreversible harm (such as permanent paralysis or quadriparesis) if untreated. Plans are prohibited from requiring a full course of conservative treatment (including interventional procedures) when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology and the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed or is contraindicated. Delaying surgery to resolve a radiological dispute violates DMHC guidelines and clinical standards.
- California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) prohibits conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (such as cord compression and myelomalacia) that physical therapy cannot address. Under CIC § 10169.5(c), a plan's internal requirement for physical therapy cannot be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or has neurological urgency. Here, the member completed 8 weeks of PT with a documented functional plateau (no improvement in upper motor neuron signs), satisfying CIC § 10169.5(a)(2).

3. Application of Generally Accepted Clinical Standards
Under the clinical standards of major spine societies, including the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023 Update) and the AO Spine Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):
- Cervical myelopathy with objective upper motor neuron signs (bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, tandem gait impairment) and early myelomalacia represents a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) for surgical decompression.
- There is no clinical evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy or interventional procedures (such as epidural steroid injections) prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate-to-severe or progressive cervical myelopathy.
- Delaying surgery to resolve a radiological dispute or to enforce conservative treatment trials violates the clinical standard of care, as each month of delay is associated with a decreased likelihood of neurological recovery and a high risk of irreversible neurological deterioration.

III. CONCLUSION
Because the member's clinical presentation of cervical myelopathy with early myelomalacia is confirmed by a qualified radiologist (Radiologist B), the plan's initial denial misapplied its own criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) and violated California law (CHSC § 1374.32, DMHC APL 22-014, and CIC § 10169.5) and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021). Recommending an administrative remand or further delay is legally and clinically impermissible.

Accordingly, the prior authorization denial for ACDF C4-C5 (CPT 22551) is hereby OVERTURNED, and the procedure is APPROVED.
