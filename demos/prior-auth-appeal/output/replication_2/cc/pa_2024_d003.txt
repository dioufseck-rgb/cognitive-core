I. SUMMARY OF DETERMINATION
The prior authorization denial for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is hereby REMANDED to HealthFirst Health Plan. 

A review of the clinical record indicates that the member does not currently satisfy the plan's substantive medical necessity criteria under Clinical Coverage Guideline CC-SPINE-2024 due to incomplete conservative therapy (specifically, completing 5 weeks of physical therapy instead of the required 6 weeks, and lacking a documented interventional trial). 

However, the plan's initial denial issued on October 15, 2024, contains critical procedural defects under California law. Specifically, the plan failed to provide a written denial notice detailing the clinical criteria applied, relying instead on verbal communication during a peer-to-peer call, and failed to include the mandatory Independent Medical Review (IMR) notice. Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria in writing is legally defective and must be treated as if no denial was issued. 

Therefore, the plan's denial cannot be upheld. The case is REMANDED to the plan with instructions to immediately issue a legally compliant, written determination that fully details the clinical criteria, provides the specific clinical reasons for the determination, and includes the mandatory California IMR rights notice.


II. CLINICAL BACKGROUND AND PRESENTING EVIDENCE
According to the clinical records submitted for review:
1. Presenting Diagnosis: Cervical radiculopathy C6-C7 (Clinical Record).
2. Objective Imaging: An MRI dated September 1, 2024, demonstrates a C6-C7 disc herniation, right C7 nerve root compression, and moderate foraminal stenosis (Clinical Record - Imaging).
3. Neurological Examination: A physical exam dated September 5, 2024, reveals right C7 dermatomal numbness and tingling, a diminished tricep reflex, and right hand grip strength of 4+/5 (Clinical Record - Neurological Exam).
4. Conservative Treatment History:
   - Physical Therapy: The member completed 5 weeks of supervised physical therapy (total of 15 sessions). While the treating physician declared a functional plateau in the appeal letter, a formal plateau was not documented within the physical therapy records (Clinical Record - Conservative Treatment).
   - Pharmacotherapy: The member trialed Meloxicam 15mg daily for 3 months (resulting in partial relief) and a Prednisone taper for 2 weeks (resulting in temporary improvement) (Clinical Record - Pharmacotherapy).
   - Interventional Treatment: No interventional treatments (such as cervical epidural steroid injections, medial branch blocks, or trigger point injections) have been performed (Clinical Record - Interventional).


III. PLAN CRITERIA EVALUATION (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2, authorization for cervical spinal surgery (CPT 22551) requires that ALL of the following criteria be satisfied:

1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): Satisfied. The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with correlating MRI findings within 6 months demonstrating C6-C7 disc herniation and right C7 nerve root compression.
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): NOT Satisfied. 
   - Section 2.2(a) requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. The member completed only 5 weeks.
   - Section 2.2(b) requires a trial of at least two specified pharmacotherapy agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. While Meloxicam was trialed for 3 months, the Prednisone taper was completed for only 2 weeks, representing a shortfall.
   - Section 2.2(c) requires at least one interventional treatment (epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point injection). The member has had none.
3. Section 2.2 Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway): Not Applicable. The member's primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, not cervical myelopathy. Therefore, standard conservative treatment requirements apply.

Consequently, under a strict clinical review of CC-SPINE-2024 Section 3.2, the clinical criteria for surgery were not met on the merits.


IV. REGULATORY COMPLIANCE AND PROCEDURAL DEFECTS
California law strictly regulates the manner in which health plans must issue prior authorization denials. The plan's initial review failed to comply with these statutory mandates in the following ways:

1. Failure to Provide Written Criteria (CHSC § 1374.31):
   - CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2) mandates that when a plan issues a denial based on medical necessity, the plan "shall... [i]nclude in the denial notice: the specific criteria applied, the specific clinical reason for the denial, and notice of IMR rights."
   - The plan's denial on October 15, 2024, was based on a peer-to-peer call between the plan medical director (Dr. Alan Foster) and the requesting physician. The plan's written denial basis was "Verbal only — peer-to-peer call" and noted that "detailed criteria review [was] provided verbally during peer-to-peer call" (Denial Info).
   - Verbal communication of clinical criteria during a peer-to-peer call does not satisfy the statutory requirement for written notice to both the provider and the member.
   - Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), "A denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is DEFECTIVE and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."

2. Failure to Include Mandatory IMR Notice (CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2)):
   - The plan failed to include the mandatory Independent Medical Review (IMR) notice in its communication (Denial Info, Clinical Record - Procedural Defects).

3. Mechanical Application of Physical Therapy Limits (CIC § 10169.5):
   - Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(c), a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy "shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau."
   - Although the member completed 5 weeks of PT instead of 6, the treating physician documented in the appeal letter that the member had reached a functional plateau. The plan's mechanical denial based on the 1-week shortfall, without evaluating the clinical plateau, violates the intent of CIC § 10169.5.


V. RESOLUTION OF CROSS-SOURCE CONFLICTS
When evaluating prior authorization appeals, a strict hierarchy of authority must be applied: California State Law > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Plan Internal Criteria.

- Conflict: The plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) and generally accepted clinical standards (which recommend 6–12 weeks of conservative care for stable radiculopathy) support a clinical denial. However, California statutory law (CHSC § 1374.31) dictates that a denial issued without written criteria and IMR notices is legally defective and "shall be treated as if no denial was issued."
- Resolution: State law supersedes both clinical standards and plan criteria. A plan cannot enforce its clinical criteria through a legally defective denial process. Because the initial denial is procedurally void under CHSC § 1374.31(b), the substantive clinical shortfalls cannot be used to uphold the denial on appeal. 
- Appropriate Remedy: The proper remedy for a critical procedural defect of this nature is a REMAND. This requires the plan to correct its statutory violation by issuing a legally compliant written notice, thereby preserving the member's right to a fair, transparent, and reviewable administrative record.


VI. REQUIRED ACTIONS ON REMAND
HealthFirst Health Plan is hereby ordered to take the following actions immediately:
1. Reissue a legally compliant, written determination regarding the prior authorization request for CPT 22551 within five (5) business days of this order, in accordance with CHSC § 1367.01.
2. The written notice must explicitly detail the specific clinical criteria applied (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2), the specific clinical reasons for the plan's decision (detailing the 5-week PT duration, the pharmacotherapy trial durations, and the lack of interventional treatment), and must include the mandatory California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) Independent Medical Review (IMR) application and notice of rights as required by CHSC § 1374.31(a).
3. The plan must evaluate the treating physician's declaration of a functional plateau in accordance with CIC § 10169.5 and document why a clinical plateau was or was not accepted, rather than mechanically enforcing the 6-week timeline.
