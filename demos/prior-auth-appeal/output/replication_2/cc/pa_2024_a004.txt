HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its expedited appeal review of the prior authorization denial for the emergency Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) requested at UCSF Medical Center. 

DETERMINATION
The initial denial issued on November 18, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED. Prior authorization for CPT 22551 is APPROVED as an emergency procedure. This determination is based on a comprehensive review of the clinical record, California statutory mandates, Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) regulations, and generally accepted clinical practice guidelines.

CLINICAL HISTORY AND PRESENTATION
According to the clinical records submitted:
1. On November 10, 2024, Mr. Chang presented to the Emergency Department at UCSF Medical Center with an acute onset of bilateral arm weakness (graded 2/5) and bilateral leg weakness (graded 3/5), hyperreflexia in all extremities, bilateral Babinski signs, and urinary urgency, developing over a 72-hour period (Clinical Record: Initial Presentation & Neurological Exam). This presentation is diagnostic of an acute incomplete spinal cord syndrome (Clinical Record: Neurological Exam).
2. An emergency MRI performed on November 10, 2024, revealed severe C5-C6 central cord compression with extensive T2 hyperintensity throughout the spinal cord, indicating extensive myelomalacia consistent with acute cord injury (Clinical Record: Imaging).
3. Inpatient emergency protocols were initiated, including a 24-hour course of IV methylprednisolone (Clinical Record: Pharmacotherapy).
4. Dr. Elena Marchetti (Neurosurgery) emergently consulted and declared that physical therapy (PT) was absolutely contraindicated, warning that initiating PT would risk converting Mr. Chang's incomplete cord syndrome into complete, permanent paralysis (Clinical Record: Physician Declaration). Dr. Marchetti requested emergency surgical authorization, noting that delaying surgery risked permanent complete paralysis (Clinical Record: Physician Declaration).

INITIAL DENIAL REASONING
On November 18, 2024, Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, denied the request under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, citing that the member had completed 0 weeks of physical therapy, had no documented outpatient pharmacotherapy, and had no interventional procedures (Denial Info).

EVALUATION OF PLAN CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1): Mr. Chang meets Section 2.1(b) for Cervical Myelopathy, as he has confirmed spinal cord compression on MRI with documented myelopathic symptoms (gait disturbance, bilateral upper extremity weakness, hyperreflexia, and bladder dysfunction).
2. Conservative Treatment (Section 2.2): Under a literal application of Section 2.2, a member must complete 6 weeks of PT, 4 weeks of pharmacotherapy, and an interventional procedure. Under the Myelopathy Pathway Exception 2.2(A), the PT requirement is reduced to 4 weeks, and the interventional requirement is waived if the physician documents a risk of neurological deterioration. However, the pharmacotherapy requirement remains.
3. Strict Plan Non-Compliance: Because Mr. Chang had completed 0 weeks of PT and no outpatient pharmacotherapy, the request did not strictly satisfy the plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 & Section 3.2).

REGULATORY OVERRIDES AND STATUTORY COMPLIANCE (CALIFORNIA LAW)
While the request does not meet the plan's internal conservative treatment criteria, California law and regulatory mandates strictly prohibit the application of these criteria to Mr. Chang's clinical presentation:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a) — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:
   - Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(1), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member. Dr. Marchetti explicitly documented that PT is absolutely contraindicated and dangerous (Clinical Record: Physician Declaration).
   - Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), a health insurer shall not condition approval on PT where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Mr. Chang's emergency MRI provides objective evidence of severe structural neurological compromise (severe central cord compression and extensive myelomalacia) (Clinical Record: Imaging).
   - Under CIC § 10169.5(c), the plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of PT cannot be applied mechanically without reference to neurological urgency.

2. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 — Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions:
   - Section II.1: Cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans must treat such cases as urgent.
   - Section II.2: Plans shall not require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents urgency.
   - Section II.3: Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. Where a conflict exists, the clinical standard governs.

CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND GENERALLY ACCEPTED STANDARDS OF MEDICAL PRACTICE
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), the standard for medical necessity is governed by generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersede restrictive internal plan criteria:

1. AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines for Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update): Surgical decompression is a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) for patients presenting with moderate-to-severe myelopathy, objective cord compression, and myelomalacia (cord signal change) on MRI, or acute severe myelopathy with a risk of permanent deficit (Clinical Evidence: Section 3.1 & 3.2).
2. AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guideline (2021): "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration" (Clinical Evidence: Section 2.1).
3. Cochrane Review (2021): Physical therapy does not produce meaningful functional improvement in patients with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy (Clinical Evidence: Section 2.1).

RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS
A direct conflict exists between HealthFirst's internal clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2), which requires conservative treatment, and California statutory law (CIC § 10169.5), DMHC regulations (APL 22-014), and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine). 

Applying the hierarchy of authority:
1. California statutory law (CIC § 10169.5) and DMHC regulations (APL 22-014) legally supersede internal plan guidelines. Conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy in this case is a violation of state law.
2. Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), generally accepted clinical standards govern medical necessity. Requiring a patient with acute, rapidly progressive incomplete spinal cord syndrome and active myelomalacia to undergo physical therapy—which is clinically contraindicated and carries a high risk of causing permanent quadriplegia—violates the clinical standard of care.

Therefore, the conservative treatment requirements of CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 are waived.
