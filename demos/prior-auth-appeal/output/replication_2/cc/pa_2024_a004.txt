Dear Mr. Chang,

The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the appeal filed on your behalf by your treating neurosurgeon, Dr. Elena Marchetti, MD, regarding the prior authorization denial for an emergency anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT code 22551) at UCSF Medical Center. 

After a comprehensive review of your clinical records, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California statutory law, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has determined that the initial denial issued on November 18, 2024, was in error. The prior authorization denial is hereby OVERTURNED, and the requested emergency surgical procedure is APPROVED.

Below is a detailed explanation of the clinical, legal, and regulatory basis for this determination.


SECTION 1: CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND EMERGENCY FINDINGS

According to the clinical records submitted from the emergency department at UCSF Medical Center dated November 10, 2024, you presented with an acute onset of bilateral arm weakness and difficulty walking that rapidly progressed over a 72-hour period. You had no prior history of cervical spine disease.

Your neurological examination on November 10, 2024, revealed severe, objective neurological deficits consistent with an incomplete spinal cord syndrome:
- Bilateral upper extremity (arm) weakness graded at 2/5;
- Bilateral lower extremity (leg) weakness graded at 3/5;
- Diffuse hyperreflexia in all extremities;
- Bilateral positive Babinski signs; and
- New-onset urinary urgency.

An emergency cervical spine MRI performed on November 10, 2024, demonstrated severe central cord compression at the C5-C6 level. Crucially, the MRI revealed extensive T2-weighted hyperintensity within the spinal cord itself, which is diagnostic of extensive myelomalacia (active, structural spinal cord injury/damage).

Your treating neurosurgeon, Dr. Marchetti, documented that you presented with an acute, rapidly progressive incomplete cervical spinal cord syndrome with extensive myelomalacia. Dr. Marchetti declared that physical therapy was absolutely contraindicated and highly dangerous, as any mechanical manipulation or delay in surgical decompression risked converting your incomplete cord injury into a complete spinal cord injury (permanent quadriplegia). Dr. Marchetti requested emergency prior authorization for immediate surgical decompression via ACDF.


SECTION 2: INITIAL DENIAL AND PLAN CRITERIA LIMITATIONS

On November 18, 2024, HealthFirst's initial reviewer, Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, denied the request under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria Document CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. The denial cited a failure to complete conservative treatment, noting that the clinical record documented 0 weeks of physical therapy, 0 weeks of outpatient pharmacotherapy, and no interventional procedures.

Under the strict, literal terms of CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, a member must complete a minimum of 6 weeks of physical therapy, a 4-week trial of at least two classes of medications, and at least one interventional procedure. Under the plan's "Myelopathy Pathway" (Exception 2.2(A)), the physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks and the interventional requirement is waived if documented as risky, but the 4-week multi-class pharmacotherapy trial remains mandatory. Because you had completed 0 weeks of outpatient conservative treatment, the initial reviewer mechanically applied these criteria to deny the request.

However, as detailed below, the mechanical application of these criteria to an acute, rapidly progressive surgical emergency is legally prohibited under California law and clinically contraindicated under generally accepted standards of medical practice.


SECTION 3: LEGAL OVERRIDE OF THE PHYSICAL THERAPY REQUIREMENT (CIC § 10169.5)

California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 establishes clear statutory limits on a health plan's ability to mandate physical therapy as a prerequisite for surgery. Under CIC § 10169.5(a), a health insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy" where either of the following independent statutory exceptions is met:

1. CIC § 10169.5(a)(1): The member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition.
2. CIC § 10169.5(a)(3): The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address.

Both independent statutory exceptions are fully satisfied in your case:
- Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(1), Dr. Marchetti explicitly documented in writing that physical therapy is absolutely contraindicated because initiating physical therapy in the presence of severe cord compression and active myelomalacia would risk causing permanent complete paralysis.
- Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), the emergency MRI dated November 10, 2024, provides objective, indisputable evidence of severe structural neurological compromise (severe C5-C6 central cord compression and extensive T2 cord signal change/myelomalacia) that physical therapy cannot address.

Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(c) explicitly prohibits plans from mechanically applying a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy without reference to whether the condition involves neurological urgency under subdivision (a)(3). Therefore, the plan's physical therapy requirements (whether the standard 6 weeks under Section 2.2(a) or the modified 4 weeks under Exception 2.2(A)) are legally overridden and cannot be applied to your request.


SECTION 4: CLINICAL STANDARD OVERRIDE OF PHARMACOTHERAPY AND INTERVENTIONAL REQUIREMENTS (CHSC § 1374.32 & DMHC APL 22-014)

While CIC § 10169.5 specifically addresses physical therapy, the plan's initial denial was also based on your failure to complete outpatient pharmacotherapy (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b)) and interventional procedures (Section 2.2(c)). These non-physical therapy conservative treatment requirements are overridden by the clinical standard of care under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014.

1. Acknowledgment of DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.2(b) Limitations:
We explicitly acknowledge that DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.2(b) requires that a "treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression" to qualify for the specific conservative treatment waiver outlined in Section II.2. Because you presented as an emergency with 0 weeks of prior outpatient conservative treatment, Section II.2(b) does not provide a complete waiver of conservative treatment in this case. However, APL 22-014 serves as critical regulatory evidence of the State of California's policy regarding the extreme urgency of myelopathy with myelomalacia, and the primary legal authority to override these requirements rests on CHSC § 1374.32 and APL 22-014 Section II.3.

2. The Statutory IMR Standard (CHSC § 1374.32):
Under CHSC § 1374.32(a), "medically necessary" health care services are defined as those that a prudent physician would provide in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice and that are clinically appropriate. Crucially, CHSC § 1374.32(b) mandates that the Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard "does NOT require that the service be consistent with the plan's internal coverage criteria." If the requested service meets generally accepted clinical standards, the reviewer must approve the service regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.

3. DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3:
APL 22-014 Section II.3 explicitly states: "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for conditions identified in this guidance [which includes cervical myelopathy with cord compression and myelomalacia]. Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs."

4. Generally Accepted Standards of Medical Practice:
Under generally accepted standards of medical practice—including the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update), the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), and the AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021)—immediate surgical decompression is the standard of care (Tier 1 Strong Indication) for patients presenting with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy, objective cord compression, and MRI evidence of myelomalacia. 

These guidelines establish that conservative treatments, including outpatient pharmacotherapy and interventional injections, are clinically inappropriate, ineffective, and strictly contraindicated for acute, rapidly progressive myelopathy with active spinal cord injury. Requiring a patient with rapidly progressive quadriparesis (2/5 arm weakness, 3/5 leg weakness) and urinary urgency to undergo a 4-week trial of outpatient medications or interventional injections would cause a catastrophic delay in surgical decompression. Clinical evidence demonstrates that each week of delay in this acute state is associated with a significantly decreased likelihood of neurological recovery and a high risk of irreversible, permanent neurological deterioration (complete paralysis).

Therefore, because the plan's internal requirements for outpatient pharmacotherapy and interventional procedures directly conflict with generally accepted standards of medical practice, the clinical standard of care overrides the plan's criteria under CHSC § 1374.32 and DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3. The only medically necessary and clinically appropriate treatment for your presentation is immediate surgical decompression.


SECTION 5: CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION

In summary, the plan's physical therapy requirement is legally void under CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3) due to documented medical contraindication and objective MRI evidence of structural neurological compromise. The plan's pharmacotherapy and interventional requirements are clinically overridden under CHSC § 1374.32 and DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3 because requiring conservative treatment for acute, rapidly progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia violates generally accepted standards of medical practice and poses an imminent threat of irreversible neurological harm.

Accordingly, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED. Emergency authorization is hereby granted for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level) to be performed by Dr. Elena Marchetti, MD, at UCSF Medical Center.
