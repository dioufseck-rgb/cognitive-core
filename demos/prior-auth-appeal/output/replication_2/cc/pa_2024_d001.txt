HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its formal appeal review of the prior authorization denial for the requested Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C6-C7 level (CPT 22551) to be performed at UCLA Medical Center. 

After a comprehensive clinical and regulatory review of the medical records, plan criteria, California insurance statutes, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial denial issued on October 5, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED. Full authorization is granted for CPT 22551.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION & MEDICAL RECORD SUMMARY
The clinical record establishes that the member, Sandra White, is a 44-year-old female presenting with a primary diagnosis of C6-C7 cervical radiculopathy. 
1. Diagnostic Imaging: A cervical spine MRI dated August 15, 2024, demonstrates a C6-C7 disc herniation causing moderate right C7 nerve root compression. No spinal cord contact or myelomalacia is present.
2. Neurological Examination: A physical and neurological examination performed by Dr. Paul Lee on August 20, 2024, reveals objective neurological deficits, including right C7 dermatomal numbness, significant tricep motor weakness graded at 4/5 on the right, and a diminished right tricep reflex.
3. Conservative Treatment: 
   - Physical Therapy: The member completed 4 weeks of supervised physical therapy (totaling 12 sessions). The physical therapy records indicate that the member reached a functional plateau with persistent, unresolved neurological symptoms.
   - Pharmacotherapy: The member completed a 6-week trial of Ibuprofen 800mg three times daily (TID), which provided only partial, temporary pain relief and failed to resolve the objective neurological deficits.
   - Interventional Treatment: No epidural steroid injections or other interventional procedures have been performed.

II. REGULATORY ANALYSIS & STATUTORY OVERRIDES
The initial denial of prior authorization was based on a mechanical application of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2, which requires a minimum of 6 weeks of physical therapy, trials of two distinct pharmacotherapy agents, and at least one interventional treatment. Under California law, this denial is legally invalid and must be overturned on both substantive and procedural grounds.

1. Substantive Override: California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Requirements)
Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), a health insurer is explicitly prohibited from conditioning the approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy when the member's condition involves "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
- Sandra White’s clinical record contains clear, objective evidence of structural neurological compromise, specifically a C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate right C7 nerve root compression on MRI, accompanied by 4/5 tricep motor weakness and a diminished tricep reflex on physical examination. Physical therapy cannot decompress a structurally compressed nerve root or reverse motor weakness caused by mechanical compression.
- Furthermore, under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), the member completed 4 weeks of physical therapy (12 sessions) and reached a documented functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms.
- Under CIC § 10169.5(c), a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy (such as the 6-week requirement in CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a)) "shall not be applied mechanically" without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or exhibits structural neurological compromise. The plan's mechanical denial based on the completion of only 4 weeks of physical therapy directly violates CIC § 10169.5.

2. Substantive Override: California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 (Independent Medical Review Standard)
Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the standard for determining medical necessity during an appeal or Independent Medical Review (IMR) does not require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If a requested service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, the reviewer must recommend approval regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive (CHSC § 1374.32(c); DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014).
- Generally accepted clinical standards, specifically the American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) 2023 Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Conditions, establish that surgical decompression (ACDF, CPT 22551) is a Tier 2 "Reasonable Indication" for patients presenting with cervical radiculopathy, correlating MRI evidence of nerve root compression, and persistent symptoms with objective motor deficits (4/5 weakness) after 6 weeks of conservative management (NSAID therapy).
- The plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), which mandate 6 weeks of physical therapy, two separate medications, and an invasive interventional injection, are significantly more restrictive than generally accepted clinical standards. Under CHSC § 1374.32 and DMHC APL 22-014, generally accepted clinical standards supersede the plan's restrictive internal criteria. Because the member meets the clinical standard for surgical intervention, the denial must be overturned.

3. Procedural Override: California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b) (Defective Denial and Deemed Approval)
The initial denial notice issued by Dr. Alan Foster on October 5, 2024, was procedurally defective under California law.
- CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2) mandates that a medical necessity denial notice must specify the exact clinical criteria applied, the specific clinical reasons for the denial, and include a notice of the member's right to an Independent Medical Review (IMR).
- The plan's denial notice failed to cite any specific section or criteria number from CC-SPINE-2024, provided only a generic reference to "criteria," and completely omitted the mandatory IMR rights notice.
- Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason is legally defective and "shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."
- Under CHSC § 1367.01 and California Code of Regulations (CCR) Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1, if a health plan fails to issue a valid, legally compliant decision within the required statutory timeframes, the requested service is "DEEMED APPROVED" for the period of the delay. Because the plan's initial notice was legally void, no valid denial was issued within the statutory timeframe, resulting in a statutory deemed approval of the requested surgery.

III. FINAL DETERMINATION & AUTHORIZATION
Based on the clear statutory mandates of California Insurance Code § 10169.5, California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32, and the deemed approval framework under CHSC § 1374.31(b), the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED.
