The HealthFirst Appeal Review Committee has completed its review of the appeal filed on October 18, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT code 22551) at UCLA Medical Center. 

After a comprehensive review of the clinical documentation, the plan's internal coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), and the governing California regulatory framework, the original denial of coverage is hereby OVERTURNED. Prior authorization for the requested surgical procedure is APPROVED.

I. PROCEDURAL ANALYSIS & STATUTORY DEEMED APPROVAL
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a)(2), when a health plan issues a denial based on medical necessity, it is mandated to provide a written notice that specifies the exact clinical criteria applied, the specific clinical reason for the denial, and a notice of the member's right to an Independent Medical Review (IMR). 

The original denial notice issued by HealthFirst on October 5, 2024, contained critical procedural defects:
1. It failed to include the mandatory IMR rights notice (imr_notice_included: false).
2. It failed to specify the exact clinical criteria applied, providing only a generic reference to plan criteria without citing any specific section or criterion numbers (criteria_cited: "Generic reference to plan criteria — no section number, no specific criterion").

Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is legally DEFECTIVE and "shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."

Furthermore, under CHSC § 1367.01 and California Code of Regulations (CCR) Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1, standard prior authorization decisions must be made and communicated within five (5) business days of receipt of all necessary information. Because the plan's October 5, 2024, denial notice was procedurally defective, California law dictates that it must be treated as if no denial was issued. Consequently, the plan failed to issue a valid, legally compliant decision within the mandatory 5-business-day statutory window. 

Under CHSC § 1367.01 and CCR Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1 (as referenced in Part IV.3 of the California Regulatory Framework), a plan's failure to timely issue a valid decision results in an automatic "deemed approval" of the requested service for the period of the delay. Therefore, the procedural defects in the denial notice trigger an automatic statutory approval, and a remand to allow the plan to reissue a compliant denial notice is legally impermissible.

II. SUBSTANTIVE CLINICAL ANALYSIS & CALIFORNIA INSURANCE CODE § 10169.5
In addition to the procedural grounds for approval, the denial must be overturned on its clinical merits under California law. The plan's original denial was based on the member's failure to complete the standard conservative treatment requirements outlined in HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2, which requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy (Section 2.2(a)), a trial of two pharmacotherapy agents (Section 2.2(b)), and an interventional treatment (Section 2.2(c)). The clinical record indicates the member completed 4 weeks (12 sessions) of physical therapy, trialed one pharmacotherapy agent (Ibuprofen 800mg TID for 6 weeks with partial relief), and had no interventional treatments.

However, the plan's mechanical application of these conservative treatment requirements directly violates California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5:

1. Objective Evidence of Structural Neurological Compromise (CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)):
   Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), a health plan or insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... [t]he condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
   The clinical record for Ms. White demonstrates severe, objective structural neurological compromise:
   - A neurological exam on August 20, 2024, documented right C7 dermatomal numbness, significant tricep motor weakness graded at 4/5 on the right, and a diminished tricep reflex.
   - An MRI of the cervical spine dated August 15, 2024, confirmed a C6-C7 disc herniation causing moderate right C7 nerve root compression.
   Because physical therapy cannot decompress a structurally compressed nerve root or reverse objective motor weakness (4/5 tricep weakness) and reflex deficits, conditioning surgical approval on further physical therapy is legally invalid under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3).

2. Prohibition on Mechanical Application of PT Durations (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) & (c)):
   Under CIC § 10169.5(c), a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy "shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau." Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), surgery cannot be conditioned on further PT if the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau.
   Ms. White completed a substantial 4-week course of physical therapy consisting of 12 supervised sessions. The clinical record notes that she reached a functional plateau with persistent, unresolved neurological symptoms. Applying the plan's 6-week requirement mechanically to deny the surgery violates the statutory protections of CIC § 10169.5.

III. CONCLUSION & AUTHORIZATION DETAILS
Because the plan's denial notice was procedurally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b), triggering a deemed approval under CHSC § 1367.01, and because the plan's physical therapy requirements are legally unenforceable under CIC § 10169.5 given the member's objective neurological deficits, the denial is OVERTURNED.

The request for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C6-C7, one level (CPT 22551) to be performed by Dr. Paul Lee, MD, at UCLA Medical Center is APPROVED.
