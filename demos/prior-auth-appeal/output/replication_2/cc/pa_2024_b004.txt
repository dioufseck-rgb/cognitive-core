Dear Mr. Watkins and Dr. Martinez,

The Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its de novo review of the appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California statutory and regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial decision to deny coverage has been OVERTURNED. 

HealthFirst will authorize coverage for the requested ACDF C5-C6 procedure at Stanford Medical Center.

I. PROCEDURAL DEFECTS AND STATUTORY VIOLATIONS IN THE INITIAL DENIAL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The initial denial issued on November 10, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, was procedurally defective and violated California law on two distinct grounds:

1. Citation of Non-Existent Plan Criteria (CHSC § 1374.31)
The denial notice stated that "Plan criteria require at least 6 weeks of symptoms before surgical consideration." However, a review of HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery (CC-SPINE-2024) reveals that there is no such requirement. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement) requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with supporting MRI evidence, but contains no minimum symptom duration threshold. Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the correct clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason is legally defective. Citing a non-existent 6-week symptom duration threshold as the sole basis for denial constitutes a procedural violation.

2. Mechanical Application of Physical Therapy Requirements (CIC § 10169.5)
The denial argued that the surgical request was "premature" because the member's symptoms had been present for only 6 weeks. This mechanical application of a duration requirement directly violates California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(c), which explicitly prohibits health plans from mechanically applying a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau. 
Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), a health insurer cannot condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy if the member has completed a course of therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms. As detailed below, the clinical record demonstrates a clear functional plateau.

II. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND RESOLUTION OF CHRONOLOGICAL TIMELINES ON APPEAL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

To ensure absolute clinical and legal defensibility, this de novo appeal review has reconciled the chronological timelines of the member's clinical course. We explicitly acknowledge and resolve the chronological gaps present at the time of the initial prior authorization request:

1. Physical Therapy Timeline and Functional Plateau
- Initial Request Status: The member's initial evaluation and MRI were dated October 1, 2024. When the prior authorization request was submitted in early November 2024, and subsequently denied on November 10, 2024 (40 days post-onset), a full 6-week (42-day) course of physical therapy was chronologically incomplete (the member had completed approximately 4.5 weeks of therapy).
- Appeal Status (Mootness of Duration): As of the date of this appeal review (November 25, 2024), exactly 55 days (7.8 weeks) have elapsed since the initial diagnosis on October 1, 2024. The member has now fully completed the 6-week intensive physical therapy course (consisting of 18 supervised sessions). 
- Documented Functional Plateau: The physical therapy and occupational therapy records confirm that the member reached a strict functional plateau. Despite 18 sessions, there was no improvement in his right C6 radicular symptoms or fine motor deficits. Occupational therapy evaluation confirmed a persistent, severe functional impairment: the member, a professional violinist, remains entirely unable to perform violin fingering tasks due to right hand weakness (grip strength 4/5) and C6 dermatomal numbness. This satisfies the "functional plateau" standard under CIC § 10169.5 and CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a).

2. Interventional Treatment Timeline
- Initial Request Status: The member received a right C6 epidural steroid injection on October 28, 2024. At the time of the initial request (submitted approximately November 3, 2024, 6 days post-injection), the clinical record noted "returned to baseline in 2 weeks," which was chronologically impossible to document at that time. At the time of the initial request, the patient had experienced only 25% temporary relief at 5 days post-injection, with no improvement in motor deficits.
- Appeal Status: The subsequent 2-week follow-up has been submitted as new clinical evidence on appeal. It is now chronologically documented that by November 11, 2024 (14 days post-injection), the member's symptoms had fully returned to baseline, confirming the failure of interventional therapy. This satisfies the interventional treatment requirement under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(c).

3. Non-Applicability of Myelopathy Pathway
We note that the clinical record attempted to argue for urgency under the Myelopathy Pathway. However, the member's MRI on October 1, 2024, explicitly states: "No cord contact. No myelomalacia." The primary diagnosis is "Acute cervical radiculopathy." Therefore, the member does not qualify for the Myelopathy Pathway (Exception 2.2(A) or DMHC APL 22-014). 
Nevertheless, because the member has reached a documented functional plateau under CIC § 10169.5, and because all conservative treatment requirements are now fully and indisputably completed as of this appeal date, the lack of a myelopathy diagnosis does not bar approval.

III. COMPLIANCE WITH CLINICAL STANDARDS (CHSC § 1374.32)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, the standard of review for medical necessity must align with generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersede more restrictive internal plan criteria. 

The requested ACDF C5-C6 procedure is highly indicated under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Spine Disorders (2023 Update):
- Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication): The guidelines recommend surgical decompression for patients with cervical radiculopathy who have persistent symptoms and significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care.
- The member has a large C5-C6 disc herniation with moderate right C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis, correlating perfectly with his objective neurological deficits (diminished right bicep reflex, 4/5 grip strength, and right C6 dermatomal numbness).
- The member's occupation as a professional violinist makes his right-hand fine motor deficit a career-ending, severe functional impairment. 
- Having failed a full course of physical therapy (18 sessions), oral corticosteroids (Methylprednisolone dose pack x3), neuropathic agents (Gabapentin 600mg TID for 5 weeks), and a cervical epidural steroid injection, further conservative delay is clinically inappropriate and poses a risk of permanent neurological deficit.

IV. CONCLUSION AND DISPOSITION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

At the de novo appeal stage, all clinical, statutory, and plan criteria are fully satisfied:
1. Diagnosis Requirement (CC-SPINE-2024 § 2.1): Confirmed C5-C6 disc herniation with moderate right C6 nerve root compression and correlating objective neurological deficits.
2. Conservative Treatment (CC-SPINE-2024 § 2.2): Completed 6 weeks of physical therapy to a documented functional plateau (§ 2.2(a)), failed two neuropathic/steroid pharmacotherapy agents (§ 2.2(b)), and failed one cervical epidural steroid injection (§ 2.2(c)).
3. Statutory Compliance: The mechanical denial of coverage violated CIC § 10169.5 and CHSC § 1374.31.
4. Clinical Standard: The procedure is medically necessary and indicated under AANS/CNS 2023 guidelines.

Therefore, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED. The single-level ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is APPROVED.
