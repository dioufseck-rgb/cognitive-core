HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its clinical and regulatory review of the first-level appeal submitted on November 5, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551). 

After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria, California statutory requirements, and national evidence-based clinical guidelines, HealthFirst has determined that the initial prior authorization denial must be OVERTURNED. --------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY OF DETERMINATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The initial denial issued on October 20, 2024, was based on two primary assertions: (1) that physical therapy (PT) had not been completed to a functional plateau, and (2) that imaging showed only mild cord contact without measurable compression or myelopathy findings. 

This appeal review has identified that the initial denial was based on a clear factual error and an incorrect application of clinical criteria:
1. Factual Error regarding PT: The clinical record demonstrates that the member completed 7 weeks (21 sessions) of supervised physical therapy, and the PT discharge summary explicitly documents that a "functional plateau" was reached. Denying authorization on this basis directly contradicts the clinical record and violates California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5.
2. Incorrect Pathway Application: The initial reviewer applied myelopathy-focused criteria (requiring cord compression and myelopathic findings) to a patient whose primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy. Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1(a), the member fully qualifies for the radiculopathy pathway, which does not require spinal cord compression or myelopathy.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CLINICAL HISTORY & FINDINGS OF RECORD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Presenting Diagnosis: Cervical radiculopathy C6-C7, persistent after conservative treatment.
- Subjective Symptoms: Severe, persistent right-sided radicular pain (rated 7/10 at rest) and numbness in a C7 dermatomal distribution.
- Objective Neurological Exam (2024-08-05): Documented right C7 dermatomal distribution numbness and a diminished right triceps reflex. No upper motor neuron signs or bilateral findings were present, confirming a localized, unilateral radiculopathy.
- Diagnostic Imaging (MRI dated 2024-08-01): Demonstrates a C6-C7 disc herniation with mild cord contact and mild right C7 foraminal stenosis. This structural pathology directly correlates with the patient's right-sided C7 radicular symptoms and neurological deficits.
- Conservative Treatment History:
  1. Physical Therapy: Completed 7 weeks (21 sessions) of supervised PT. The PT discharge summary explicitly states that a functional plateau was reached, noting that the patient failed to improve over the final 3 sessions, with radicular symptoms remaining unchanged.
  2. Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 4-month trial of Pregabalin (150mg BID) with only partial pain relief, and a 3-month trial of Meloxicam (15mg) with inadequate response.
  3. Interventional Treatment: Received a right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 20, 2024, which provided only 30% temporary pain improvement lasting 3 weeks before symptoms returned to baseline.

EVALUATION AGAINST CALIFORNIA REGULATORY REQUIREMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Under California law, health plans must adhere to strict standards regarding prior authorization and medical necessity reviews. The initial denial violated multiple state provisions:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):
   - Under § 10169.5(a)(2), an insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the member has completed a course of PT and the treating provider documents that the member reached a "functional plateau" without adequate resolution of symptoms.
   - Under § 10169.5(c), internal plan requirements for a minimum number of weeks of PT shall not be applied mechanically without reference to a documented functional plateau.
   - Application: The initial denial's claim that "PT [was] not yet completed to functional plateau" directly contradicts the physical therapist's discharge summary documenting a functional plateau. Denying authorization on this basis violates CIC § 10169.5.

2. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31 & § 1374.32 (Standard of Review):
   - Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the standard for medical necessity does not require that a service be consistent with the plan's internal coverage criteria if the service is in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice and is clinically appropriate.
   - Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that relies on incorrect clinical criteria or factual errors is procedurally defective.
   - Application: The initial reviewer's insistence on "measurable compression" and "myelopathy findings" represents an incorrect application of myelopathy criteria to a radiculopathy patient, creating a defective denial under California law.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EVALUATION AGAINST GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
According to generally accepted standards of medical practice, the requested ACDF is highly indicated:

- AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023): The member meets Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) criteria for surgical intervention. Surgery is recommended for patients with cervical radiculopathy who exhibit persistent, severe symptoms (pain 7/10) and objective neurological deficits (diminished reflex, sensory loss) that have failed to respond to an appropriate 6-to-12-week course of conservative management (including PT, NSAIDs, neuropathic medications, and epidural injections) in the presence of correlating MRI findings.
- NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020): Support surgical decompression when a patient has reached a functional plateau in conservative therapy and continues to experience significant functional limitations and radicular pain correlating with imaging.
- Clinical Definition of Functional Plateau: Defined as a failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement over 3 consecutive therapy sessions (Section 5 of the Clinical Reference Summary). The member's PT discharge summary explicitly notes no improvement over the final 3 sessions, satisfying this clinical standard.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS & FINAL ORDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In resolving the conflict between the initial denial's rationale and the clinical record, the hierarchy of authority is applied: California State Law > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Plan Criteria.

1. State Law (CIC § 10169.5) legally prohibits the plan from requiring additional physical therapy because a functional plateau was explicitly documented by the treating physical therapist.
2. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023) dictate that a patient with persistent, severe C7 radiculopathy, objective neurological deficits, and correlating C6-C7 disc herniation/stenosis who has failed comprehensive conservative therapy is an appropriate candidate for ACDF. The absence of spinal cord compression or myelopathy is irrelevant, as these are not requirements for a radiculopathy diagnosis.
3. Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) are fully satisfied under the cervical radiculopathy pathway.

Therefore, the initial denial is OVERTURNED. The request for a single-level ACDF at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is APPROVED.
