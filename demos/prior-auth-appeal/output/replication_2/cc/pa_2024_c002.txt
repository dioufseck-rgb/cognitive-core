Dear Mr. Santos and Dr. Wong,

The California Clinical Review Division has completed its independent medical necessity review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C6-C7 level (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the HealthFirst Health Plan clinical coverage criteria, the California regulatory framework, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, the initial denial issued on October 5, 2024, is OVERTURNED IN FULL. 

HealthFirst Health Plan is hereby ordered to approve and authorize the requested surgical procedure (CPT 22551) at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center, to be performed by Dr. Lisa Wong, MD.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The initial denial of prior authorization was based on HealthFirst Health Plan's internal clinical coverage criteria (Document: CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2, Exception 2.2(A)), which requires that cervical myelopathy be the "primary diagnosis" for the myelopathy conservative treatment exception to apply. The plan's reviewer, Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, determined that because the member's symptoms were primarily right-sided (radicular), radiculopathy was the primary diagnosis, and therefore the standard conservative treatment requirements applied and were allegedly not met.

This independent review overturns the denial on three independent and compounding grounds:
1. Full Compliance with Standard Plan Criteria: The clinical record demonstrates that the member actually completed the full, standard conservative treatment protocol under Section 2.2 of CC-SPINE-2024, rendering the plan's dispute over the "primary diagnosis" exception moot.
2. Statutory Regulatory Override: Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3), a health plan is strictly prohibited from conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (such as myelomalacia) that physical therapy cannot address.
3. Violation of DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL 22-014): The Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) mandates that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm. Under APL 22-014, plans cannot apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted clinical standards, which recommend immediate surgical decompression for this presentation.

II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND EVIDENCE REVIEWED
The clinical record contains objective, documented evidence of a mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy presentation at the C6-C7 level:
- Objective Imaging (MRI dated 2024-09-01): Demonstrates moderate spinal cord compression at C6-C7 with T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord. It also notes right C7 foraminal stenosis that is more prominent than the left.
- Neurological Examination (dated 2024-09-05): Confirms a mixed presentation. Radicular findings include right tricep weakness (4/5), right finger numbness, and severe right-sided pain (8/10). Myelopathic findings include bilateral hyperreflexia (right greater than left), a positive right Hoffman sign, an equivocal left Hoffman sign, and a mildly wide-based gait.
- Conservative Treatment History:
  - Physical Therapy: Completed 6 weeks (18 sessions) of supervised physical therapy utilizing a myelopathy protocol. The physical therapist documented no improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms.
  - Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 3-month trial of Pregabalin (150mg BID) with only partial right-sided pain relief, and a 2-month trial of Naproxen (500mg BID) with inadequate response.
  - Interventional Treatment: Received a right C7 cervical epidural steroid injection on 2024-09-28, which provided 50% temporary right-sided relief that fully returned within 3 weeks, with no change in bilateral hyperreflexia.

III. ANALYSIS OF PLAN CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
The plan's denial under reason code MED-003 misapplied its own clinical coverage criteria. 

First, the plan argued that the "Myelopathy Exception" (Exception 2.2(A)) did not apply because myelopathy was not the "primary diagnosis." However, the plan failed to evaluate whether the member met the standard, non-exception criteria under Section 2.2. A review of the clinical record shows that the member fully satisfied the standard conservative treatment requirements:
- Section 2.2(a) (Physical Therapy): Requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. The member completed 6 weeks (18 sessions) of physical therapy.
- Section 2.2(b) (Pharmacotherapy): Requires a trial of at least two listed agents (including NSAIDs and neuropathic agents) for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The member trialed Pregabalin (neuropathic agent) for 3 months and Naproxen (NSAID) for 2 months.
- Section 2.2(c) (Interventional Treatment): Requires at least one cervical epidural steroid injection. The member received a right C7 epidural steroid injection on 2024-09-28.

Because the member fully completed the standard conservative treatment protocol, the plan's denial based on the "primary diagnosis" restriction of the myelopathy exception is factually incorrect and clinically invalid. The member met the standard criteria in full.

IV. ANALYSIS OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY OVERRIDES
Even if the member had not completed the standard conservative treatment protocol, California law explicitly overrides the plan's restrictive internal criteria in this clinical scenario.

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) Override:
Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), a health insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... [t]he condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
The member's MRI on 2024-09-01 objectively documents moderate spinal cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord. Myelomalacia represents structural, irreversible damage to the spinal cord parenchyma. Physical therapy cannot decompress the spinal cord or resolve myelomalacia. Therefore, conditioning surgical approval on further physical therapy or denying the procedure based on physical therapy duration is a direct violation of CIC § 10169.5.

2. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL 22-014) Compliance:
APL 22-014 states that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated." The DMHC mandates that plans shall not require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as cord signal change/myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents the clinical necessity. 
Furthermore, APL 22-014, Section II.3, states: "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice... Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs." The plan's internal restriction requiring myelopathy to be the "primary diagnosis" to waive or modify conservative treatment is more restrictive than generally accepted clinical standards and is therefore invalid under California regulatory guidelines.

V. ANALYSIS OF GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, the standard of review for medical necessity is governed by generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersede restrictive plan criteria.

According to the AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine Guidelines (2023), the North American Spine Society (NASS) Clinical Guidelines (2020), and the AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):
- Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) on MRI is a critical indicator of existing spinal cord injury and represents a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) for surgical decompression.
- There is no high-quality clinical evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with cervical myelopathy and objective cord signal changes.
- Delaying surgical decompression in patients with documented myelomalacia to complete conservative treatment trials is clinically contraindicated, as it exposes the patient to a high risk of progressive, irreversible neurological deterioration (AO Spine Guidelines, 2021).
- The clinical standard does not distinguish between "primary" and "secondary" diagnoses when objective myelopathy and myelomalacia are present. The presence of upper motor neuron signs (bilateral hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign, gait disturbance) combined with MRI evidence of cord compression and T2 signal change establishes a diagnosis of cervical myelopathy that requires surgical decompression, regardless of co-existing radicular pain.

VI. CONCLUSION AND ORDER
The clinical record demonstrates that Mr. Santos has a severe, mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy with objective MRI evidence of spinal cord compression and myelomalacia. He has failed to respond to a comprehensive course of conservative therapy, including 6 weeks of physical therapy, multiple pharmacotherapy trials, and an epidural steroid injection.

The plan's denial under CC-SPINE-2024 was procedurally and clinically deficient because:
1. The member met the plan's standard conservative treatment criteria in full.
2. The plan's "primary diagnosis" restriction violates California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(3) and DMHC APL 22-014.
3. Generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) mandate surgical decompression for patients with myelomalacia and objective neurological deficits to prevent irreversible spinal cord damage.

Therefore, the prior authorization denial for ACDF C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED. HealthFirst Health Plan is directed to immediately authorize the procedure.
