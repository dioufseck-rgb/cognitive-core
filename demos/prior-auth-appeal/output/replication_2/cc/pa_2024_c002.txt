Dear Mr. Santos and Dr. Wong,

The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Division has completed its review of the medical necessity appeal for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has OVERTURNED the initial denial. Prior authorization for CPT 22551 is hereby APPROVED.

This determination is established via a dual-pathway framework: first, by demonstrating that the member fully satisfied the plan's standard conservative treatment criteria, rendering the initial denial's contractual restriction inapplicable; and second, by applying California statutory and clinical standards as an independent, reinforcing basis for approval.

PATHWAY 1: COMPLIANCE WITH STANDARD PLAN COVERAGE CRITERIA
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The initial denial dated October 05, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, asserted that because the member's symptoms are predominantly right-sided (radicular), radiculopathy is the primary diagnosis. The denial applied the "Note" under Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway) of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, which states that the myelopathy exception applies only when myelopathy is the primary diagnosis. 

However, a review of the clinical record demonstrates that the member fully satisfied the plan's standard conservative treatment requirements under Section 2.2. Because the standard requirements were met, the member does not require the Myelopathy Exception (Exception 2.2(A)) to qualify for surgery. Consequently, the "primary diagnosis" restriction within that exception is contractually irrelevant.

1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1):
   The member has a confirmed diagnosis of mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy. This is supported by a cervical MRI dated September 01, 2024 ("mri_1"), demonstrating moderate cord compression at C6-C7, T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord, and prominent right C7 foraminal stenosis. This satisfies both Section 2.1(a) (Cervical radiculopathy) and Section 2.1(b) (Cervical myelopathy).

2. Standard Conservative Treatment Requirements (Section 2.2):
   The clinical record confirms the member completed a full, standard course of conservative treatment prior to the surgical request:
   - (a) Physical Therapy: The member completed 6 weeks (18 sessions) of supervised physical therapy utilizing a myelopathy protocol (documented in the clinical record). The physical therapist noted "no improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms," establishing that the member reached a functional plateau.
   - (b) Pharmacotherapy: The member completed trials of two qualifying agents for at least 4 weeks each: Pregabalin 150mg BID for 3 months (neuropathic agent) and Naproxen 500mg BID for 2 months (NSAID). This fully satisfies Section 2.2(b).
   - (c) Interventional Treatment: The member underwent a right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 28, 2024, which provided 50% temporary relief but symptoms fully returned within 3 weeks, with no change in bilateral hyperreflexia. This satisfies the requirement for at least one interventional treatment under Section 2.2(c).

Because the member completed 6 weeks of physical therapy, two trials of pharmacotherapy, and one epidural injection, all standard conservative treatment requirements under Section 2.2 have been fully met. The member qualifies for surgical authorization under the standard criteria, and the initial denial's reliance on the "primary diagnosis" restriction to deny coverage was a misapplication of the plan's own policy.

PATHWAY 2: INDEPENDENT REGULATORY AND CLINICAL OVERRIDES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Even if the plan's medical director disputes the applicability of the completed conservative treatment (e.g., by arguing the physical therapy protocol was directed at myelopathy rather than radiculopathy, or that the epidural was too recent), California law and generally accepted clinical standards provide an independent, reinforcing basis that overrides the plan's internal restrictions.

1. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 (IMR Standard):
   Under California's Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard, a health plan's internal medical necessity criteria are not the sole or determinative basis for a decision. If a service is medically necessary under generally accepted standards of medical practice, the plan must approve it regardless of whether internal criteria are more restrictive. The plan's contractual restriction requiring myelopathy to be the "primary diagnosis" is more restrictive than clinical standards and is overridden by CHSC § 1374.32.

2. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023):
   According to the AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine Guidelines (2023 Update) and the AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021), surgical decompression is strongly recommended (Tier 1 Indication) for any patient with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy and objective cord compression with myelomalacia (T2 signal change) on MRI. 
   The clinical standard does not require myelopathy to be the "primary" diagnosis, nor does it require a patient to endure extended conservative treatment when myelomalacia is present. In fact, the AO Spine Guidelines state: "Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." The member's mixed presentation (8/10 right-sided pain, right tricep weakness 4/5, right finger numbness, bilateral hyperreflexia, positive right Hoffman, and mildly wide-based gait) combined with MRI-documented myelomalacia represents a high-risk clinical state that demands surgical intervention under clinical standards.

3. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:
   APL 22-014 Section II.1 establishes that cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. 
   - Acknowledge of Gap: We explicitly acknowledge that the strict criteria for an automatic waiver of conservative treatment under APL 22-014 Section II.2(a) are not met, as the clinical record contains only a single MRI from September 01, 2024, and lacks the serial imaging required to objectively demonstrate "progressive pathology." 
   - Regulatory Application: However, under APL 22-014 Section II.3, plans are prohibited from applying internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted clinical standards. Because generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS Guidelines) dictate that the presence of myelomalacia on a single MRI represents an urgent, high-risk state indicating surgery, the plan's restrictive "primary diagnosis" rule cannot be used to deny or delay authorization.

4. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3):
   To the extent that the plan might argue the completed physical therapy was insufficient or required further trials, CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where "the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The member's MRI-documented C6-C7 moderate cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia) constitute objective structural neurological compromise. Physical therapy cannot decompress the spinal cord or resolve myelomalacia; therefore, requiring further physical therapy is legally prohibited.
