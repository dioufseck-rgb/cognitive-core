FINAL DETERMINATION: OVERTURN THE PRIOR AUTHORIZATION DENIAL IN FULL

Dear Ms. Torres and Dr. Kim,

HealthFirst Health Plan's Appeal Determination Services has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's internal clinical coverage criteria, California statutory and regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial prior authorization denial issued on November 1, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED. 

The requested surgical procedure is approved. The authorization is valid for 90 days from the date of this letter, expiring on February 16, 2025, in accordance with CC-SPINE-2024 Section 3.5.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND HISTORY
The clinical record demonstrates that Ms. Diana Torres is a 61-year-old female with a presenting diagnosis of progressive cervical myelopathy at C5-C6 with myelomalacia. 
- Initial Presentation (August 10, 2024): The patient presented with a 4-month history of bilateral hand clumsiness and gait instability. 
- Neurological Examination (August 10, 2024): Documented objective upper motor neuron signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia in both upper and lower extremities, positive bilateral Babinski signs, and gait ataxia.
- Diagnostic Imaging (MRI, August 15, 2024): Demonstrated severe central cord compression at C5-C6 and T2 hyperintensity within the spinal cord, confirming myelomalacia.
- Conservative Treatment History:
  1. Physical Therapy: Initiated at Valley PT on September 1, 2024. Discontinued on September 14, 2024, after 2 weeks (6 sessions) because the treating physician, Dr. Kim, issued a written order to discontinue due to documented symptom exacerbation and clinical deterioration.
  2. Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 2-week trial of a methylprednisolone taper, which provided temporary mild improvement but no sustained benefit.
  3. Interventional Treatment: None performed.

II. INITIAL DENIAL AND PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS
The initial denial on November 1, 2024, cited HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria Document CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. The denial was based on the following findings:
1. The member completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy, whereas the standard pathway requires a minimum of 6 weeks (Section 2.2(a)).
2. The pharmacotherapy requirement was not met, as only one agent (methylprednisolone) was trialed, whereas Section 2.2(b) requires a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
3. No interventional treatment was performed, failing Section 2.2(c).

Under a strict, mechanical application of the plan's internal criteria, the clinical record does not satisfy CC-SPINE-2024. Even under the modified "Myelopathy Pathway" exception (Section 2.2(A)), which reduces the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks and waives the interventional treatment requirement, the member still fails to meet the criteria because she completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy and trialed only one pharmacotherapy agent.

III. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK AND LEGAL OVERRIDE
Under California law, a health plan's internal clinical criteria are legally subordinate to state statutory and regulatory mandates. In this case, multiple provisions of California law prohibit the application of the plan's conservative treatment requirements:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) (Physical Therapy Override):
   This statute explicitly mandates that a health insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... [t]he condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Ms. Torres's MRI on August 15, 2024, documented severe central cord compression and T2 hyperintensity (myelomalacia). This constitutes objective, structural neurological compromise. Therefore, under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), HealthFirst is legally prohibited from requiring physical therapy as a prerequisite for this surgery.

2. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1) (Contraindication Override):
   The statute also prohibits conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy where "[t]he member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition." Dr. Kim's written declaration dated October 15, 2024, explicitly stated that physical therapy is contraindicated due to the presence of myelomalacia and that continuing physical therapy risks irreversible spinal cord injury. This written documentation triggers the statutory override under CIC § 10169.5(a)(1).

3. Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter APL 22-014:
   APL 22-014 Section II.1 classifies cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI as a "time-sensitive neurological condition" with "potential for irreversible harm if untreated." Section II.2 mandates that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization" when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents urgency. Requiring Ms. Torres to complete additional weeks of physical therapy or trial a second pharmacotherapy agent directly violates this regulatory mandate.

IV. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND GENERALLY ACCEPTED STANDARDS OF MEDICAL PRACTICE
Generally accepted standards of medical practice, as compiled in the Clinical Reference Summary, strongly support immediate surgical decompression and contraindicate conservative therapy for this clinical presentation:

1. AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023):
   Under these guidelines, Ms. Torres's presentation meets Tier 1 — Strong Indication (surgery recommended) due to:
   - Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI (Section 3.1, Tier 1(a)).
   - Myelomalacia on MRI (cord signal change) (Section 3.1, Tier 1(c)).
   The guidelines state that for patients meeting Tier 1 criteria, immediate surgical intervention is indicated, and extended conservative treatment trials are contraindicated.

2. AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):
   The guidelines state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."

3. Cochrane Review (2021):
   Confirms that for moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy, physical therapy does not produce meaningful functional improvement, and conservative treatment is not recommended as a primary treatment.

4. Risk of Delay:
   Clinical evidence demonstrates that preoperative myelomalacia is the strongest predictor of incomplete neurological recovery, and each month of delay in surgical treatment for moderate-to-severe myelopathy is associated with a decreased likelihood of neurological recovery.

V. RESOLUTION OF CROSS-SOURCE CONFLICTS (HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY)
A direct conflict exists between HealthFirst's internal clinical criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), which require 4 to 6 weeks of physical therapy and two pharmacotherapy trials, and California state law and clinical standards, which prohibit these requirements.

To resolve this conflict, HealthFirst applies the following established Hierarchy of Authority:
1. Level 1: California State Law and Regulatory Mandates (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) — Controlling and legally binding.
2. Level 2: Generally Accepted Standards of Medical Practice (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021) — Governing clinical standard.
3. Level 3: HealthFirst Internal Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) — Subordinate reference.

Because California Insurance Code § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014 legally prohibit the application of conservative treatment requirements in the presence of structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) and physician-documented contraindication, the plan's internal criteria under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 and 2.2(A) are legally unenforceable and inapplicable to this case. Furthermore, under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), the standard of review does not require consistency with the plan's internal criteria when those criteria are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice.

VI. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION
The clinical record conclusively establishes that Ms. Torres has progressive cervical myelopathy with severe central cord compression and myelomalacia, representing a high risk of irreversible neurological damage if surgical decompression is delayed. 

Applying the controlling statutory overrides under CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3), and the regulatory mandates under DMHC APL 22-014, the physical therapy and pharmacotherapy requirements are waived. The requested procedure meets generally accepted standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS 2023 Tier 1 indication) and is medically necessary.

Therefore, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED, and the requested procedure is APPROVED.
