The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the prior authorization appeal submitted on behalf of Diana Torres regarding the requested single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT code 22551) to be performed at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center. 

After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California statutory and regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has determined that the initial prior authorization denial issued on November 1, 2024, must be OVERTURNED IN FULL. The requested surgical procedure is approved.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The initial prior authorization request was denied by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, on November 1, 2024, citing non-compliance with HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria Document CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement). Specifically, the denial noted that the member had completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy (instead of the standard 6 weeks) and had trialed only one pharmacotherapy agent (methylprednisolone taper).

On appeal, the clinical record demonstrates that the member has progressive cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of severe central cord compression and myelomalacia (T2 hyperintensity within the spinal cord) on MRI. Her treating neurosurgeon, Dr. James Kim, MD, documented that physical therapy was discontinued after 2 weeks because it caused symptom exacerbation and declared that continuing physical therapy was clinically contraindicated due to the risk of irreversible spinal cord injury.

Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3), a health plan is legally prohibited from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Furthermore, the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 mandates that plans shall not require conservative treatment trials for time-sensitive neurological conditions like cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia when the treating physician documents urgency and clinical contraindication. Because California law and generally accepted clinical standards supersede the plan's internal guidelines, the conservative treatment requirements are legally and clinically inapplicable to this case. Accordingly, the denial is overturned.

3. Neurological Examination: Objective upper motor neuron signs documented on August 10, 2024, include bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia in both upper and lower extremities, positive bilateral Babinski signs, and documented gait ataxia (Clinical Record, Neurological Exam).

   - Pharmacotherapy: Trial of a 2-week methylprednisolone taper, which provided temporary mild improvement but no sustained benefit (Clinical Record, Pharmacotherapy).
6. Physician Declaration: In a letter dated October 15, 2024, Dr. James Kim, MD, declared that physical therapy is strictly contraindicated in this patient due to the presence of myelomalacia on the initial MRI. Dr. Kim noted that continuing physical therapy risks irreversible spinal cord injury, that the statutory exception under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) applies, and that surgical decompression is urgently indicated (Clinical Record, Physician Declaration).

III. PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS
HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery (Document CC-SPINE-2024) requires the following for cervical surgical authorization:
- Section 2.1(b): Confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy supported by MRI demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms. The member satisfies this diagnostic requirement.
- Section 2.2: Completion of a conservative treatment course, including (a) minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy, (b) trial of at least two specified pharmacotherapy agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each, and (c) one interventional treatment.
- Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway Exception): For members with confirmed cervical myelopathy, the physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks, and the interventional treatment requirement is waived if the physician documents that it poses a risk of neurological deterioration. However, the pharmacotherapy requirement (trial of two agents) remains in full.

Under a literal application of CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 and Section 2.2(A), the member does not meet the plan's internal criteria because she completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy and trialed only one pharmacotherapy agent (methylprednisolone). However, as detailed below, these internal criteria are legally and clinically superseded by California regulatory mandates and generally accepted clinical standards.

IV. REGULATORY AND LEGAL OVERRIDE ANALYSIS
Because the member is a California resident enrolled in a California plan, the review is governed by California insurance laws and regulations, which strictly prohibit the mechanical application of the plan's conservative treatment criteria in this clinical scenario:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3): This statute explicitly states that a health insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... [t]he condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The member's MRI dated August 15, 2024, provides objective evidence of severe central cord compression and myelomalacia (T2 cord signal change), which constitutes structural neurological compromise. Therefore, under California law, the plan's physical therapy requirement is legally unenforceable.
2. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1): This provision prohibits requiring physical therapy when the treating physician documents in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member given the severity of the condition. Dr. Kim's written declaration dated October 15, 2024, explicitly states that physical therapy is contraindicated and poses a risk of irreversible cord injury.
3. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(c): This section mandates that a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy "shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or whether the condition involves neurological urgency."
4. California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, Section II.1 & II.2: The DMHC has determined that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a time-sensitive neurological condition with potential for irreversible harm. Under Section II.2, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization" when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed or is contraindicated. Under Section II.3, plans are prohibited from applying internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for these conditions.

V. CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARD ANALYSIS
Generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice strongly support immediate surgical intervention and reject mandatory conservative treatment trials for patients presenting with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia:

1. AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine Guidelines (2023 Update): Under these guidelines, patients with confirmed cervical myelopathy, objective cord compression, and myelomalacia on MRI meet Tier 1 (Strong Indication) criteria for surgical decompression. Immediate surgery is recommended, and extended conservative treatment is not indicated.
2. North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020): NASS guidelines state that while conservative treatment may be considered for mild myelopathy, surgical decompression is recommended as the primary treatment for moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy.
3. AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021): The guidelines state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
4. Clinical Significance of Myelomalacia: Myelomalacia (T2 signal change within the spinal cord) represents active, structural spinal cord injury. Clinical evidence demonstrates that each month of delay in surgical decompression for patients with myelomalacia is associated with a decreased likelihood of neurological recovery and an increased risk of permanent, irreversible neurological deficits (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.3).

VI. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS (HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY)
In resolving conflicts between conflicting sources of authority, HealthFirst applies the following established hierarchy:
   State Law & Regulatory Mandates (Highest Authority)
   ↳ Generally Accepted Clinical Standards of Medical Practice
     ↳ Plan-Specific Clinical Coverage Criteria (Lowest Authority)

In this case, a direct conflict exists:
- The Plan's Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2) require 4 to 6 weeks of physical therapy and a trial of two pharmacotherapy agents.
- State Law (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014) prohibits the plan from requiring physical therapy or a full course of conservative treatment due to the documented structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) and physician-documented contraindication.
- Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) state that delaying surgery for conservative treatment in a patient with moderate-to-severe myelopathy and myelomalacia is clinically contraindicated and below the standard of care.

Applying the hierarchy of authority, the statutory protections of CIC § 10169.5 and the regulatory mandates of DMHC APL 22-014 legally override the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria. Furthermore, generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice dictate that surgical decompression is the medically necessary and appropriate treatment. The plan's internal conservative treatment requirements are legally unenforceable and clinically inappropriate in this clinical scenario.

VII. DETERMINATION AND AUTHORIZATION
Based on the foregoing analysis, the prior authorization denial for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C5-C6, one level (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED. 

The procedure is hereby APPROVED.
