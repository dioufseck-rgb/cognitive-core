Dear Priya Nair and Dr. James Chen,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of the first-level internal appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's internal coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California statutory requirements, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has determined that the initial denial issued on October 25, 2024, must be OVERTURNED. The requested surgical procedure is approved.

I. CLINICAL HISTORY AND PRESENTING DIAGNOSIS

The member is a 56-year-old female with a primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy at C5-C6 (ICD-10: G35.89), which has been confirmed by both clinical neurological examination and objective imaging.

- Neurological Examination (September 5, 2024): The examination revealed bilateral upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs and bilateral hyperreflexia of the upper extremities. Additionally, the member exhibited a mildly impaired tandem gait and documented bilateral hand clumsiness. A neurologist consultation on September 12, 2024, confirmed that these pathognomonic bilateral UMN signs are clinically consistent with primary cervical myelopathy.
- Objective Imaging (MRI dated September 1, 2024): The MRI of the cervical spine demonstrated moderate central cord compression at C5-C6 and bilateral foraminal stenosis. The radiologist noted: "No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia."
- Conservative Treatment History: The member completed 5 weeks of supervised physical therapy (15 sessions) under a myelopathy protocol. The physical therapy was halted by the treating neurologist at 5 weeks due to a lack of improvement in the member's UMN signs. Regarding pharmacotherapy, the member trialed Baclofen 10mg TID for 3 months (resulting in minimal spasticity relief) and a Methylprednisolone dose pack for 3 weeks (resulting in temporary improvement only). Interventional treatments were waived by the requesting surgeon, Dr. Chen, who documented that interventional procedures were not indicated as cord compression with myelopathy requires surgical evaluation rather than injections.

II. PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS AND ACKNOWLEDGMENT OF CRITERIA GAP

The initial denial dated October 25, 2024, cited Section 2.2 of the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), stating that the member failed to meet the standard 6-week physical therapy requirement. This was a criteria-application error. Because the member has a confirmed primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, the request must be evaluated under Section 2.2A (Exception 2.2(A) — Myelopathy Pathway) rather than the standard radiculopathy pathway.

Under Section 2.2A, the conservative treatment requirements are modified as follows:
1. Physical Therapy: The minimum duration is reduced to 4 weeks. The member completed 5 weeks of physical therapy, thereby satisfying this specific requirement.
2. Interventional Treatment: This requirement is waived because the treating physician documented that interventional treatment was not indicated and posed a risk of neurological deterioration.
3. Pharmacotherapy: Section 2.2A explicitly states that the pharmacotherapy requirement under Section 2.2(b) "remains in full." Section 2.2(b) requires a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.

Factual Acknowledgment of Criteria Gap: The clinical record shows that while the member completed a 3-month trial of Baclofen, she only completed a 3-week trial of Methylprednisolone. Under the strict, mechanical letter of the plan's internal criteria, the member failed to meet the 4-week minimum duration for the second pharmacotherapy agent. Consequently, the member does not strictly satisfy all conservative treatment requirements under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2A.

III. REGULATORY OVERRIDE AND CLINICAL STANDARDS ANALYSIS

While the member does not meet the strict letter of the plan's internal pharmacotherapy criteria, California law and generally accepted clinical standards prohibit the mechanical application of conservative treatment trials in patients with objective structural neurological compromise.

1. Inapplicability of DMHC APL 22-014:
We explicitly note that the Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 is inapplicable to this case. APL 22-014 requires "objective imaging evidence [that] demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia...)" to support an automatic regulatory waiver of conservative treatment. Because the member's MRI report explicitly states "No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia," an automatic waiver under APL 22-014 cannot be legally sustained.

2. Statutory Override under California Insurance Code § 10169.5:
Instead, the denial is overturned pursuant to California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5, which governs physical therapy and conservative treatment prerequisites:
- CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) prohibits an insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy or conservative treatment when "the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The member's moderate central cord compression at C5-C6, combined with objective upper motor neuron signs (bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, hand clumsiness, and gait impairment), constitutes severe structural neurological compromise that conservative modalities cannot resolve.
- CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) further protects the member because she completed 5 weeks of physical therapy and her treating neurologist documented that she reached a "functional plateau" (no improvement in UMN signs), necessitating that therapy be halted to prevent neurological deterioration.
- CIC § 10169.5(c) mandates that a plan's internal requirements for conservative treatment "shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or whether the condition involves neurological urgency."

3. Clinical Standards Override under California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32:
Under California's Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard (CHSC § 1374.32(a)), medical necessity is defined by generally accepted standards of medical practice, not a plan's internal guidelines. CHSC § 1374.32(b) explicitly states that the clinical standard governs, and the reviewer shall recommend approval regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.

According to the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Clinical Guidelines (2020):
- Surgical decompression (ACDF) is a Tier 1 strong recommendation and the primary indicated treatment for patients presenting with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy and objective cord compression.
- There is no high-quality clinical evidence supporting a mandatory course of conservative treatment (including physical therapy or extended pharmacotherapy) for patients with objective upper motor neuron deficits. 
- Forcing a patient with moderate cord compression and progressive hand clumsiness and gait impairment to undergo an additional week of oral corticosteroids solely to satisfy an arbitrary 4-week plan threshold is clinically contraindicated. Delaying surgical decompression in the face of objective neurological deficits risks irreversible spinal cord injury and permanent neurological impairment.

IV. CONCLUSION

Although the member did not complete the 4-week minimum trial for her second pharmacotherapy agent under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2A, California Insurance Code § 10169.5 and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023) override the plan's internal conservative treatment requirements. The member has documented structural neurological compromise (moderate C5-C6 cord compression with bilateral upper motor neuron signs) and has reached a functional plateau in physical therapy. 

Therefore, the mechanical application of the plan's conservative treatment criteria is legally and clinically invalid. The prior authorization denial for ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is hereby OVERTURNED, and the procedure is APPROVED.
