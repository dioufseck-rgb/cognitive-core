FINAL APPEAL DETERMINATION: UPHOLD DENIAL

Dear Ms. Oduya and Dr. Walsh,

HealthFirst has completed its clinical and regulatory review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551) at UCLA Medical Center. After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory statutes, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has determined that the initial prior authorization denial must be UPHELD. The requested surgical procedure is not medically necessary at this time.

CLINICAL RECORD SUMMARY
The clinical records submitted for review demonstrate the following:
1. Presenting Diagnosis: Right-sided C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy (Clinical Record).
2. Diagnostic Imaging: A cervical spine MRI dated September 10, 2024, revealed a mild to moderate disc herniation at C5-C6. There is right C6 nerve root contact without measurable compression. Crucially, the radiologist documented no spinal cord contact, no spinal cord compression, no T2 signal change, and no myelomalacia (Clinical Record - MRI Report).
3. Neurological Examination: A physical examination performed on September 15, 2024, documented right shoulder and neck pain rated at 6/10, and mild right C6 dermatomal tingling. However, the patient exhibited symmetric 2+ bilateral bicep reflexes, 5/5 bilateral grip strength, no motor weakness, and no upper motor neuron (UMN) signs. The functional assessment noted that the patient is working full-time with no activity limitations (Clinical Record - Neurological Exam).
4. Conservative Treatment History:
   - Physical Therapy (PT): The patient completed 4 weeks of supervised physical therapy (total of 12 sessions) with mild improvement in pain. The physical therapy summary notes "no neurological deficit to track" and indicates that Dr. Walsh elected to halt physical therapy to seek surgical authorization (Clinical Record - PT Summary).
   - Pharmacotherapy: The patient has trialed Naproxen 500mg BID for 8 weeks, which provided moderate pain relief (Clinical Record - Pharmacotherapy).
   - Interventional Treatment: No interventional treatments (such as epidural steroid injections, medial branch blocks, or trigger point injections) have been performed (Clinical Record - Conservative Treatment).

EVALUATION OF PLAN COVERAGE CRITERIA
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2, authorization for cervical spinal surgery requires that all medical necessity criteria be satisfied:
1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): The patient has a diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy. However, Section 2.1(c) requires "cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit" when radiculopathy is the primary diagnosis. The neurological exam on September 15, 2024, showed no objective motor weakness (5/5 grip strength) and normal, symmetric reflexes (2+), indicating a lack of objective neurological deficit.
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): The patient has not completed the standard conservative treatment pathway, which requires:
   - Section 2.2(a): A minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. The patient completed only 4 weeks.
   - Section 2.2(b): A trial of at least two specified pharmacotherapy agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The patient has only trialed one agent (Naproxen).
   - Section 2.2(c): At least one interventional treatment (e.g., cervical epidural steroid injection). The patient has had none.
3. Section 2.2 Exception (Myelopathy Pathway): This exception reduces the PT requirement to 4 weeks and waives the interventional requirement. However, this exception applies only when cervical myelopathy is the primary diagnosis (Section 2.2, Note). The patient's primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, and there is no clinical or imaging evidence of myelopathy (no cord compression, no UMN signs, no gait disturbance). Therefore, the exception does not apply.
4. Section 3.2 (Exclusions): This section mandates denial when conservative treatment requirements have not been met. Because the patient completed only 4 weeks of PT, trialed only one medication, and had no interventional treatments, the request is excluded from coverage under Section 3.2.

EVALUATION OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK
California law establishes that health plan determinations must align with statutory protections and clinical standards:
1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite): Under this statute, a plan cannot condition surgical approval on physical therapy if:
   - (a)(1) PT is documented as contraindicated or unlikely to benefit;
   - (a)(2) The member reached a functional plateau; or
   - (a)(3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address.
   None of these statutory exceptions are met. There is no documentation that PT was contraindicated. The PT records show "mild improvement in pain" rather than a functional plateau, and the therapy was halted prematurely by the treating physician to seek surgery. Furthermore, the MRI shows no structural neurological compromise (no cord compression, no measurable nerve root compression) that would render PT ineffective.
2. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions): This guidance waives conservative treatment for urgent neurological conditions, specifically identifying "myelomalacia on MRI" or "progressive pathology" as criteria. The patient's MRI explicitly notes "no myelomalacia" and "no cord compression," and the clinical exam shows no progressive neurological deficits. Therefore, the regulatory waiver under APL 22-014 does not apply.
3. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 (Standard of Review for IMR): This statute requires that medical necessity be evaluated based on generally accepted standards of medical practice, which override more restrictive internal plan criteria. Accordingly, we evaluate the clinical evidence standard below.

EVALUATION OF CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARD
Under generally accepted standards of medical practice, as compiled in the Clinical Reference Summary and established by the American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Joint Guidelines (2023) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Guidelines (2020):
1. Surgical Indications (AANS/CNS 2023):
   - Tier 1 (Strong Indication for Surgery) includes moderate-to-severe myelopathy, progressive neurological deficits, or myelomalacia on MRI. The patient has none of these findings.
   - Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) includes radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6 to 12 weeks of conservative treatment and significant functional impairment. The patient has completed only 4 weeks of PT, has no motor deficits, and has no functional limitations (working full-time).
   - Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated) includes mild radiculopathy not yet trialed on conservative treatment. The patient's presentation of mild tingling, normal strength, and full functional capacity, combined with an incomplete conservative trial, falls under Tier 3.
2. Risk of Delay and PT Efficacy: Generally accepted clinical standards (AO Spine Guidelines 2021) state that while surgery is the primary treatment for moderate-to-severe myelopathy, conservative treatment is highly effective for radiculopathy without neurological deficits (70-80% resolution). There is no clinical evidence supporting immediate surgery for mild radiculopathy in the absence of a completed conservative trial.

RESOLUTION OF PHYSICIAN DISPUTE AND SYCOPHANCY BIAS REVIEW
We have carefully reviewed the appeal arguments submitted by Dr. Patrick Walsh, who emphasizes his 25 years of spine surgery experience, his history of performing over 2,000 ACDFs, and his direct physical examination of the patient. Dr. Walsh asserts that his clinical judgment should take precedence over a remote reviewer and warns that the patient will suffer "permanent disability" if surgery is denied.

While we respect Dr. Walsh's extensive experience and clinical authority, a non-sycophantic, objective review of the medical facts must prioritize documented clinical evidence over professional assertions:
1. The claim of "permanent disability" is directly contradicted by the objective clinical record. The MRI of September 10, 2024, shows no spinal cord contact, no cord compression, and no myelomalacia. The neurological exam of September 15, 2024, shows normal reflexes, 5/5 grip strength, no weakness, and full-time work capacity with no activity limitations. There is no structural or clinical basis for an imminent risk of permanent disability.
2. Generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020) are designed to ensure patient safety and efficacy. These guidelines do not support surgical intervention for mild radiculopathy with no neurological deficits and an incomplete conservative trial, regardless of the surgeon's experience.
3. Deferring to Dr. Walsh's opinion solely based on his years of practice or the emotional urgency of his letter, without supporting objective evidence, would violate the standard of reproducible, evidence-based medical review.

Therefore, because the patient does not meet the plan's clinical criteria, does not qualify for statutory or regulatory exemptions under California law, and does not meet generally accepted clinical standards for surgical intervention, the prior authorization denial is UPHELD.
