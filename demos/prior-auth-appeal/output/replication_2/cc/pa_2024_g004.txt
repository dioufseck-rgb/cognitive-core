FINAL DETERMINATION: UPHOLD DENIAL

Dear Ms. Oduya,

HealthFirst has completed its review of the first-level appeal submitted by your treating surgeon, Dr. Patrick Walsh, MD, regarding the prior authorization denial for an anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive review of your clinical records, the plan's coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has determined that the initial denial must be UPHELD. The requested surgical procedure is not medically necessary at this time.

CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND RECORD SUMMARY
According to the clinical records submitted:
1. Presenting Diagnosis: You have been diagnosed with right-sided C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy (Clinical Record: Presenting Diagnosis).
2. Imaging Findings: A cervical spine MRI dated September 10, 2024, demonstrated a mild to moderate disc herniation at the C5-C6 level. There is right C6 nerve root contact without measurable compression. Crucially, the radiologist noted no cord contact, no cord compression, no T2 signal change, no myelomalacia, and mild right foraminal narrowing (Clinical Record: Imaging, mri_1).
3. Neurological Examination: A clinical examination performed on September 15, 2024, documented right shoulder and neck pain rated at 6/10, and mild right C6 dermatomal tingling. However, your motor and reflex examinations were entirely normal: bilateral bicep reflexes were symmetric at 2+, grip strength was 5/5 bilaterally, and there was no documented muscle weakness or upper motor neuron (UMN) signs. Your functional assessment indicated that you are working full-time with no activity limitations (Clinical Record: Neurological Exam).
4. Conservative Treatment History:
   - Physical Therapy: You completed 4 weeks of supervised physical therapy (total of 12 sessions), which resulted in mild improvement in your pain. Your physical therapy was halted by Dr. Walsh to seek surgical authorization (Clinical Record: Conservative Treatment, physical_therapy_summary).
   - Pharmacotherapy: You trialed one medication, Naproxen 500mg BID, for a duration of 8 weeks, which provided moderate pain relief (Clinical Record: Conservative Treatment, pharmacotherapy).
   - Interventional Treatment: No interventional treatments (such as cervical epidural steroid injections, medial branch blocks, or trigger point injections) have been performed (Clinical Record: Conservative Treatment, interventional).

PLAN CLINICAL COVERAGE CRITERIA ANALYSIS
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery — Cervical Procedures (Document: CC-SPINE-2024), Section 2.2 outlines the mandatory conservative treatment requirements that must be completed prior to surgical authorization. Your request fails to meet these criteria in the following areas:
- Section 2.2(a) (Physical Therapy): Requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. Your records document only 4 weeks of physical therapy (Clinical Record: Conservative Treatment, physical_therapy_summary).
- Section 2.2(b) (Pharmacotherapy): Requires a trial of at least two distinct classes of medications (such as NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each. Your records document a trial of only one agent (Naproxen, an NSAID) (Clinical Record: Conservative Treatment, pharmacotherapy).
- Section 2.2(c) (Interventional Treatment): Requires at least one interventional treatment, such as a cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point injection series. Your records document that no interventional treatments have been attempted (Clinical Record: Conservative Treatment, interventional).

Furthermore, the Myelopathy Pathway Exception under Section 2.2 is inapplicable. This exception reduces the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks and waives the interventional requirement, but it applies only to members with a confirmed primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (Section 2.1(b)). Your primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, and your MRI and clinical examinations confirm the complete absence of myelopathy (no cord compression, no myelomalacia, and no upper motor neuron signs) (Clinical Record: Presenting Diagnosis, Imaging, Neurological Exam).

Therefore, pursuant to Section 3.2 of CC-SPINE-2024, authorization must be denied because the conservative treatment requirements have not been met.

CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK ANALYSIS
We evaluated your request against California statutory and regulatory requirements to determine if any provisions override the plan's conservative treatment criteria:
1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite): This statute prohibits conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy under specific exceptions. However, none of these exceptions are met:
   - Subdivision (a)(1): Dr. Walsh has not documented that physical therapy is medically contraindicated or unlikely to benefit you.
   - Subdivision (a)(2): You have not reached a documented "functional plateau" (defined in Section 5 of the clinical summary as a failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement over consecutive sessions). Instead, your physical therapy records note "mild improvement in pain" before therapy was prematurely halted (Clinical Record: Conservative Treatment, physical_therapy_summary).
   - Subdivision (a)(3): There is no objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (such as progressive motor weakness or spinal cord compression) that physical therapy cannot address. Your grip strength remains 5/5 with no weakness (Clinical Record: Neurological Exam).
2. Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014: This guidance allows for the waiver of conservative treatment for time-sensitive neurological conditions, specifically cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia. Because your MRI of September 10, 2024, explicitly states "no cord compression" and "no myelomalacia," and your clinical exam shows no neurological deficits, your condition does not qualify as an urgent neurological condition under APL 22-014 (Clinical Record: Imaging, Neurological Exam).

GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, medical necessity is evaluated in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice. We reviewed your case against the Joint Guidelines of the American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS) and the Congress of Neurological Surgeons (CNS) (2023 Update):
- Tier 1 (Strong Indication for Surgery): Reserved for patients with moderate-to-severe myelopathy, progressive neurological deficits, or myelomalacia. Your clinical presentation does not meet any Tier 1 criteria (Clinical Evidence Document: Section 3.1).
- Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication for Surgery): Applies to patients with cervical radiculopathy who have persistent symptoms after an adequate 6-to-12-week trial of conservative treatment, or those with significant functional impairment. Because you have only completed 4 weeks of physical therapy, trialed only one medication, and have no documented functional limitations (working full-time with no activity limitations), your presentation does not meet Tier 2 criteria (Clinical Evidence Document: Section 3.1; Clinical Record: Conservative Treatment, Neurological Exam).
- Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated): Applies to mild radiculopathy that has not yet completed an adequate trial of conservative treatment. Your clinical presentation falls under Tier 3, meaning surgical intervention is not clinically indicated at this time (Clinical Evidence Document: Section 3.1).

RESOLUTION OF PHYSICIAN CONFLICT
Dr. Walsh's appeal letter dated October 18, 2024, strongly disputes the initial denial, arguing that his 25 years of spine surgery experience and direct physical examination of you should supersede the remote review of the plan's reviewer, Dr. Maria Rivera, MD. Dr. Walsh also asserts that you will suffer "permanent disability" if the surgery is not authorized (Clinical Record: Treating Surgeon Statement).

While HealthFirst respects Dr. Walsh's extensive clinical experience, his assertions of clinical urgency and risk of permanent disability are directly contradicted by the objective medical evidence in your clinical record:
1. The objective imaging (MRI dated September 10, 2024) shows only mild-to-moderate disc herniation with right C6 nerve root contact but "no measurable compression" and "no cord compression" (Clinical Record: Imaging, mri_1).
2. Your neurological examination dated September 15, 2024, reveals no objective neurological deficits, normal reflexes, full 5/5 grip strength, and no functional limitations (Clinical Record: Neurological Exam).
3. There is no clinical evidence of progressive neurological decline or spinal cord compromise (myelopathy) that would support an urgent bypass of standard conservative care (Clinical Record: Imaging, Neurological Exam).

Under generally accepted medical standards and California regulatory frameworks, a treating physician's professional authority or emotional declaration cannot override the objective clinical record. Because the objective evidence demonstrates a stable, non-urgent clinical presentation that has not completed a standard, safe course of conservative therapy, the clinical standard supports the plan reviewer's determination to uphold the denial.
