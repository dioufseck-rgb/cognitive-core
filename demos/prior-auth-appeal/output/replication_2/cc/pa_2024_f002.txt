Dear Mr. Clark and Dr. Nguyen,

The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievance Department has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's internal coverage criteria, California statutory and regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, the initial decision to deny coverage has been OVERTURNED. The requested surgical procedure is hereby authorized.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND OBJECTIVE FINDINGS
The clinical record establishes that the patient is a 46-year-old male with a presenting diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy at C5-C6 with significant functional impairment. 
1. Imaging: A cervical spine MRI dated August 15, 2024, demonstrates a large disc herniation at C5-C6 causing severe right C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis. This satisfies the imaging requirement under Section 2.3 of the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024).
2. Neurological Examination: A physical examination dated August 20, 2024, reveals objective neurological deficits correlating precisely with the MRI findings, including right C6 dermatomal numbness, bicep weakness graded at 4/5 on the right, an absent right bicep reflex, and a positive Spurling's sign on the right. This satisfies the diagnostic criteria for cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficit under Section 2.1(a) and (c) of CC-SPINE-2024.

II. RESOLUTION OF THE INITIAL DENIAL GROUND (PHYSICAL THERAPY DOCUMENTATION)
The initial denial dated October 10, 2024, was based on reason code MED-003, citing "conflicting physical therapy documentation" between Riverside Physical Therapy (6 weeks) and Coastal Physical Therapy (4 weeks submitted initially), which created uncertainty regarding the total duration of physical therapy.

This denial is overturned based on the following:
1. Independent Satisfaction of Criteria: Under Section 2.2(a) of CC-SPINE-2024, the plan requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy directed toward the cervical condition. The records from Riverside Physical Therapy (received and verified by the plan) document a complete 6-week course of therapy consisting of 18 sessions from April 1, 2024, to May 10, 2024, with a documented functional plateau. Because the Riverside PT records independently and fully satisfy the plan's 6-week requirement, any documentation discrepancy regarding Coastal PT is clinically and contractually irrelevant to whether the criteria were met.
2. Submission of Complete Records: On appeal, the member submitted the complete records for Coastal Physical Therapy, which document an additional 6 weeks (18 sessions) of therapy from June 1, 2024, to July 12, 2024, with a documented functional plateau (maximum therapeutic benefit reached). This establishes a total of 12 weeks of supervised physical therapy, far exceeding the plan's requirement.
3. Statutory Compliance: Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2), a health plan cannot condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau. Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(c) prohibits the mechanical application of a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or has objective structural neurological compromise. The mechanical denial based on a clerical discrepancy violates these statutory provisions.

III. RESOLUTION OF PHARMACOTHERAPY CRITERIA AND TECHNICAL NON-COMPLIANCE
Under a strict, mechanical reading of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(b), the patient's pharmacotherapy trials are technically non-compliant. Section 2.2(b) requires a trial of at least two distinct categories of medications (NSAIDs/COX-2 inhibitors, Muscle relaxants, Neuropathic agents, or Oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The clinical record indicates that the patient trialed Gabapentin 600mg TID for 5 months and Duloxetine 60mg daily for 3 months. Because both medications are classified as neuropathic agents, they represent only one category, and no second category was trialed.

However, this technical non-compliance is resolved, and the overturn decision is maintained, based on the following hierarchy of authority:

1. Procedural Bar Under California Law (CHSC § 1374.31):
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a)(2), a health plan's denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the denial. The initial denial notice dated October 10, 2024, solely cited physical therapy documentation discrepancies (MED-003) as the basis for denial. The plan did not raise any deficiency regarding pharmacotherapy in the initial denial. Under California regulatory standards, the plan is legally barred from introducing new clinical denial grounds on appeal that were not specified in the initial denial notice. To uphold the denial on appeal based on a newly raised pharmacotherapy issue would violate CHSC § 1374.31 and render the initial denial notice procedurally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b).

2. Supremacy of Clinical Standards Over Restrictive Plan Criteria (CHSC § 1374.32(b) & APL 22-014):
Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the standard of review for medical necessity in California does not require consistency with a plan's internal coverage criteria. If a requested service is in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice and is clinically appropriate, the reviewer must recommend approval regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. This is reinforced by the Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Part II.3), which mandates that plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice, and that where a conflict exists, the clinical standard governs.

3. Alignment with Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:
According to the American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Joint Guidelines (2023 Update) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), the patient's conservative treatment is fully sufficient and represents a Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) for surgical intervention. The patient has completed:
- 12 weeks of supervised physical therapy (Riverside PT and Coastal PT) with documented functional plateaus.
- Over 3 months of dual-agent neuropathic pharmacotherapy (Gabapentin and Duloxetine) with only partial benefit.
- A right C6 epidural steroid injection on August 28, 2024, which provided only 60% temporary relief before symptoms returned to baseline within 3 weeks (satisfying the interventional treatment requirement under Section 2.2(c) of CC-SPINE-2024).
For a patient with objective, matching neurological deficits (4/5 bicep weakness, absent reflex, dermatomal numbness) and severe MRI-confirmed nerve root compression, requiring additional trials of other medication classes (such as NSAIDs or muscle relaxants) is clinically inappropriate, lacks evidence-based support, and poses an unnecessary risk of delaying definitive surgical decompression.

IV. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION
The clinical record demonstrates that the patient has severe, symptomatic C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits that have failed to respond to an extensive and clinically appropriate course of conservative management. The initial denial based on physical therapy documentation is unsubstantiated by the complete record and violates CIC § 10169.5. The technical pharmacotherapy discrepancy under the plan's internal criteria is superseded by generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS Guidelines) pursuant to CHSC § 1374.32(b) and DMHC APL 22-014, and is procedurally barred from serving as a basis for denial under CHSC § 1374.31.

Accordingly, the prior authorization denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level) is OVERTURNED.
