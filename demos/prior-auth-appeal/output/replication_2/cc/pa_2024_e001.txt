HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a two-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 and C6-C7 levels (CPT codes 22551 and 22552). 

Based on a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, we have made a PARTIAL determination:
1. The denial for the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED and is hereby APPROVED.
2. The denial for the C6-C7 level (CPT 22552) is REMANDED to the initial review level to allow for the collection of additional clinical documentation or diagnostic testing.

A detailed clinical and regulatory analysis supporting this determination is provided below.

---

SECTION 1: CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND CASE HISTORY
Mr. Victor Patel is a 60-year-old male presenting with a diagnosis of two-level cervical disc disease at C5-C6 and C6-C7 with asymmetric severity. 
- Imaging: A cervical spine MRI dated September 15, 2024, demonstrates:
  - C5-C6: A large disc herniation, moderate-to-severe right C6 nerve root compression, and foraminal stenosis.
  - C6-C7: A small disc bulge and mild foraminal narrowing, with no identifiable nerve root compression and no spinal cord contact.
- Neurological Examination (September 20, 2024):
  - C6 Level: Right bicep weakness graded at 3+/5, an absent right bicep reflex, and right C6 dermatomal numbness and tingling.
  - C7 Level: Slightly diminished right tricep reflex. No definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms (numbness, tingling, or weakness) were documented. The physician noted that mild C7 symptoms were "possibly present" but not clearly documented.
- Conservative Treatment History:
  - Physical Therapy: Completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy. The physical therapist's week 8 summary notes that C6 symptoms persist, while C7 symptoms were "minimal and possibly resolving."
  - Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 4-month trial of Pregabalin (150mg BID) with partial relief of C6 symptoms and uncertain C7 contribution, and a 3-month trial of Naproxen (500mg BID) with moderate relief.
  - Interventional Treatment: Received a right C6 epidural steroid injection on October 15, 2024, which provided 70% relief of C6 symptoms, though symptoms fully returned after 4 weeks. No C7-specific interventional treatment (such as a selective nerve root block or epidural) was performed.

SECTION 2: ANALYSIS OF C5-C6 LEVEL (CPT 22551) — OVERTURNED (APPROVED)
The prior authorization denial for the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551) is overturned because the clinical record fully satisfies all medical necessity requirements under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria Document CC-SPINE-2024 and generally accepted clinical standards:

1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1): The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy at C5-C6, supported by objective neurological deficits (3+/5 right bicep weakness, absent right bicep reflex, and C6 dermatomal numbness/tingling) and a current MRI (September 15, 2024) demonstrating a large disc herniation and moderate-to-severe right C6 nerve root compression.
2. Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2):
   - Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): The member completed 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy, exceeding the plan's 6-week minimum.
   - Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): The member completed trials of two qualifying agents for at least 4 weeks each (Pregabalin for 4 months and Naproxen for 3 months).
   - Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): The member underwent a right C6 epidural steroid injection on October 15, 2024.
3. Imaging Requirement (Section 2.3): The September 15, 2024 MRI demonstrates structural pathology (large herniation and moderate-severe compression) that directly correlates with the C6 radicular symptoms and deficits.
4. Clinical Standards: Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) and NASS Guidelines (2020), surgical decompression is strongly indicated (Tier 2) for patients with cervical radiculopathy who have persistent, severe symptoms and objective neurological deficits that have failed to respond to a comprehensive course of conservative management.

Therefore, the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551) is medically necessary and is approved.

---

SECTION 3: ANALYSIS OF C6-C7 LEVEL (CPT 22552) — REMANDED
The prior authorization denial for the C6-C7 level (CPT 22552) is remanded. While the treating physician's appeal basis states that both levels are symptomatic and should be addressed simultaneously to prevent adjacent segment degeneration, the current clinical documentation does not support the medical necessity of a fusion at C6-C7 under the plan's criteria or clinical standards:

1. Lack of Objective Diagnostic and Clinical Correlation (Section 2.1 & 2.3): The September 15, 2024 MRI shows only a small disc bulge and mild foraminal narrowing at C6-C7, with no nerve root compression or cord contact. The neurological exam on September 20, 2024, noted "no definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms" and only a "slightly diminished" tricep reflex. Under Section 3.3 of CC-SPINE-2024, surgery is excluded when imaging does not demonstrate pathology consistent with the clinical presentation.
2. Resolving Symptoms: The physical therapy week 8 discharge note indicates that C7 symptoms were "minimal and possibly resolving," suggesting that conservative management was effective for this level and that surgical intervention is premature.
3. Lack of C7-Specific Interventional Trial: While a right C6 epidural was performed, no C7-specific diagnostic or therapeutic injection (such as a selective nerve root block) was attempted to confirm whether the C6-C7 level is a true, independent pain generator.
4. Clinical Standards: Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Tier 3, surgical intervention is not indicated for mild imaging findings without correlating clinical symptoms or deficits.

However, because the treating physician has asserted that both levels are symptomatic, we are REMANDING the C6-C7 level rather than upholding the denial. This allows the provider to:
- Submit updated, detailed clinical documentation clarifying the exact nature, distribution, and severity of any C7 dermatomal symptoms.
- Consider performing a diagnostic C7 selective nerve root block to objectively demonstrate whether the C6-C7 level is a primary pain generator.

SECTION 4: REGULATORY COMPLIANCE AND RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS
Under California law, several procedural and substantive requirements govern this appeal:

1. Procedural Defect in Initial Denial: California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b) mandates that a denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the denial. The initial denial notice dated November 1, 2024, failed to cite the specific sections of Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, stating only that "Two-level fusion [is] not supported." Under § 1374.31(b), this omission constitutes a procedural defect. We have corrected this defect in this appeal determination by providing exhaustive citations to CC-SPINE-2024.
2. Inappropriateness of Blanket Denial: The plan's initial decision to issue a blanket denial for the entire two-level procedure because one level did not meet criteria is inconsistent with California's medical necessity standard under CHSC § 1374.32. Under CHSC § 1374.32, health plans must evaluate and authorize services that are clinically appropriate and medically necessary. Because the C5-C6 level independently met all criteria, a partial approval should have been issued initially.
3. Physical Therapy Requirements: Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5, a plan's physical therapy requirements cannot be applied mechanically. In this case, the member completed a full 8-week course of physical therapy and reached a functional plateau for his C6 symptoms, satisfying both the plan's criteria and statutory requirements.
