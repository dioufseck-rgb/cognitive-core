Dear Ms. Osei and Dr. Torres,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its appellate review of the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 (CPT 22551) at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center. The initial prior authorization request was denied on October 15, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, on the basis that the member had not completed the plan's required conservative treatment protocol (specifically citing 5 weeks of physical therapy completed out of 6 required, and no cervical epidural steroid injection on record).

Upon comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria, California statutory mandates, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has determined that the initial denial must be OVERTURNED. Authorization for the requested ACDF C4-C5 procedure is hereby APPROVED.


I. ACKNOWLEDGEMENT OF PLAN'S INTERNAL CRITERIA GAPS

Under a strict, literal reading of HealthFirst's internal Clinical Coverage Criteria (Document: CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2), the patient's clinical record does not fully satisfy the plan's internal conservative treatment requirements, even when applying the Myelopathy Pathway (Exception 2.2(A)):

1. Physical Therapy: Under Exception 2.2(A), the physical therapy requirement is reduced to a 4-week minimum. The patient completed 5 weeks of physical therapy, which satisfies this specific sub-criterion.

2. Pharmacotherapy: Section 2.2(b) requires a trial of at least two conservative agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each, and Exception 2.2(A) states that this requirement "remains in full." The clinical record shows the patient trialed Baclofen (10mg TID) for 3 months, which satisfies the first agent requirement. However, the second agent, a Methylprednisolone taper, was only completed for "10 days x2" (20 days total). This does not meet the plan's internal 4-week (28-day) minimum requirement for the second pharmacotherapy agent.

3. Interventional Treatment: Exception 2.2(A) states that the interventional treatment requirement (2.2(c)) is waived only "if treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses risk of neurological deterioration." The clinical record documents that the cervical epidural steroid injection was not performed because it was contraindicated due to the patient's permanent pacemaker (implanted in 2019), as documented by the anesthesiology consultation (Dr. Rivera, 2024-09-10). Because a pacemaker contraindication represents a cardiac risk rather than a documented risk of "neurological deterioration," the strict contractual waiver language of Exception 2.2(A) was not technically met.


II. STATUTORY AND REGULATORY OVERRIDES (CALIFORNIA LAW)

While the patient's clinical trial did not strictly satisfy the plan's internal criteria, HealthFirst is legally required to override these internal guidelines under California regulatory standards. The plan's mechanical application of conservative treatment requirements to a patient with progressive structural neurological compromise is legally unenforceable under the following provisions:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Override):
Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where "the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." 
Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(c) mandates that a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the condition involves neurological urgency. 
The patient's MRI dated August 20, 2024, demonstrates severe central cord compression at C4-C5 with confirmed T2 signal change (myelomalacia), representing progressive structural spinal cord injury. Physical therapy cannot address or reverse this structural cord compression. Therefore, conditioning surgical approval on the completion of additional physical therapy is a direct violation of CIC § 10169.5.

2. Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Conservative Treatment Override):
APL 22-014, Section II.1, establishes that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI "constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated." Section II.2 explicitly mandates that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization" when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression. 
Dr. Torres documented that the patient's physical therapy was stopped after 5 weeks because the patient was experiencing active neurological progression (including bilateral lower extremity spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor skills in both hands, and a positive Lhermitte sign). Requiring the patient to complete further conservative trials (such as extending the steroid trial to 4 weeks or undergoing a contraindicated injection) directly violates the DMHC mandate.

3. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 (Clinical Standards Override):
CHSC § 1374.32(b) dictates that the standard of review for medical necessity must be based on generally accepted standards of medical practice, and that the plan's internal medical necessity criteria "shall not be the sole or determinative basis" for a decision if those criteria are more restrictive than generally accepted clinical standards. As detailed below, generally accepted clinical guidelines do not support conservative treatment for progressive myelopathy with myelomalacia.


III. GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS OF MEDICAL PRACTICE

Under CHSC § 1374.32, the medical necessity of the requested ACDF must be evaluated against established clinical guidelines rather than the plan's internal policy:

1. Surgical Indications for Progressive Myelopathy:
According to the AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), surgical decompression is the recommended primary treatment (Tier 1 / Strong Indication) for patients presenting with moderate-to-severe or progressive cervical myelopathy, especially when accompanied by myelomalacia (T2 cord signal change) on MRI.

2. Inefficacy and Risk of Conservative Treatment:
The AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021) state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." 
Because myelomalacia is the strongest predictor of incomplete neurological recovery postoperatively, delaying surgical decompression to satisfy arbitrary conservative treatment durations (such as a 4-week steroid trial) is clinically contraindicated and exposes the patient to a high risk of permanent, irreversible neurological deficits.

3. Medical Contraindication of Interventional Treatment:
Generally accepted standards of medical practice dictate that a fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injection is medically contraindicated in a patient with a permanent pacemaker due to cardiac safety risks, as documented by Dr. Rivera on September 10, 2024. Forcing a patient to undergo a contraindicated interventional procedure to satisfy a plan's internal checklist is a severe deviation from the standard of care.


IV. CONCLUSION AND DISPOSITION

Although the patient did not strictly meet the plan's internal conservative treatment criteria under CC-SPINE-2024 (due to a 20-day steroid trial instead of 28 days, and a cardiac rather than neurological justification for the injection waiver), California regulatory standards and generally accepted clinical guidelines legally override these internal requirements. 

Because the patient has objective MRI evidence of severe C4-C5 cord compression with myelomalacia, progressive neurological deficits, and a documented medical contraindication to interventional treatment, the requested ACDF C4-C5 (CPT 22551) is medically necessary and urgent.

Therefore, the initial denial is OVERTURNED, and the procedure is APPROVED.
