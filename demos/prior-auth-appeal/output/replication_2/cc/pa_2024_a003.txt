HealthFirst Health Plan's Appeal Determination Services has completed its review of the first-level appeal for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 (CPT 22551). The initial decision to deny prior authorization is hereby OVERTURNED. 

While a strict factual review of the clinical record demonstrates that the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria (Document: CC-SPINE-2024) were not fully satisfied, California regulatory mandates and generally accepted clinical standards of care legally supersede these internal plan requirements. Under California law, conditioning the approval of this urgent surgical procedure on the completion of medically contraindicated or clinically inappropriate conservative treatments is legally unenforceable. Accordingly, the requested procedure is approved.


I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION & HISTORY

Ms. Patricia Osei is a 55-year-old female with a diagnosis of progressive cervical myelopathy at C4-C5 with myelomalacia. The clinical record documents the following objective findings:
- Imaging: A cervical spine MRI performed on August 20, 2024 (utilizing a pacemaker-compatible protocol) revealed severe central cord compression at C4-C5 with confirmed T2 signal change (myelomalacia), indicating progressive, active spinal cord injury.
- Neurological Examination: A clinical exam on August 25, 2024, documented significant upper motor neuron signs, including bilateral lower extremity spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor skills in both hands, and a positive Lhermitte sign.
- Comorbidities: The member has a permanent pacemaker (implanted in 2019), type 2 diabetes, and hypertension.
- Conservative Treatment Attempted:
  1. Physical Therapy: Completed 5 weeks (15 sessions). Therapy was discontinued due to pacemaker precautions limiting specific modalities and a clinical determination by the physician that further physical therapy was failing to arrest the member's neurological progression.
  2. Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 3-month trial of Baclofen 10mg TID (resulting in partial spasticity relief) and a 20-day trial of Methylprednisolone (consisting of two 10-day tapers, which provided only temporary relief).
  3. Interventional Treatment: Not performed. A fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injection was determined to be absolutely contraindicated by an anesthesiology consultation (Dr. Rivera, September 10, 2024) due to the patient's permanent pacemaker.


II. EVALUATION OF PLAN'S INTERNAL CRITERIA & ACKNOWLEDGEMENT OF FACTUAL GAPS

Under a strict application of HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), the clinical record contains factual gaps that prevent the member from fully satisfying the plan's internal requirements, even under the Myelopathy Pathway Exception (Section 2.2(A)):

1. Physical Therapy Deficit: Under the standard criteria (Section 2.2(a)), 6 weeks of physical therapy are required. Under the Myelopathy Pathway Exception (Section 2.2(A)), this is reduced to 4 weeks. While the member's 5 weeks of completed physical therapy satisfies the modified duration, the exception itself cannot be fully triggered because other concurrent requirements of the exception were not met.
2. Pharmacotherapy Duration Deficit: Section 2.2(b) requires a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks (28 days) each. This requirement remains in full force under the Myelopathy Pathway Exception. While the member completed a 3-month trial of Baclofen, her trial of Methylprednisolone lasted only 20 days (two 10-day tapers). Because the steroid trial fell short of the mandatory 28-day threshold, the member did not satisfy the plan's internal pharmacotherapy criteria.
3. Interventional Waiver Deficit: Section 2.2(A) states that the interventional treatment requirement (Section 2.2(c)) is waived only if the treating physician documents that the interventional treatment "poses risk of neurological deterioration." In this case, the documented contraindication is a permanent pacemaker, which represents a cardiovascular and device-related risk rather than a risk of direct neurological deterioration. Therefore, the plan's specific contractual waiver was not technically triggered.


III. REGULATORY PREEMPTION & LEGAL OVERRIDE

Although the member did not strictly meet the plan's internal criteria, HealthFirst is legally prohibited from enforcing these criteria under California law. The initial denial is overturned based on the following statutory and regulatory provisions:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Preemption):
   - Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where "the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
   - Ms. Osei's MRI demonstrates severe central cord compression and myelomalacia (T2 signal change), which constitutes definitive, objective structural neurological compromise. Physical therapy cannot decompress the spinal cord or resolve myelomalacia.
   - Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(c) prohibits the mechanical application of a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy without regard to neurological urgency. Denying coverage because the member completed 5 weeks of physical therapy instead of 6 is a direct violation of this statute.

2. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions):
   - APL 22-014 mandates that plans shall not require the completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (specifically citing "cord signal change" or "myelomalacia") and the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression.
   - Ms. Osei's progressive symptoms, positive Lhermitte sign, and MRI-confirmed myelomalacia place her case directly under this mandate. Requiring further conservative trials in the face of active, irreversible spinal cord injury violates APL 22-014.

3. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard of Review):
   - Under California law, the standard for medical necessity is governed by generally accepted standards of medical practice, not a plan's internal, highly restrictive coverage criteria. Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and clinical standards, the clinical standard governs.


IV. CLINICAL EVIDENCE & GENERALLY ACCEPTED STANDARDS OF CARE

Generally accepted clinical guidelines from the American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS), the Congress of Neurological Surgeons (CNS), and the North American Spine Society (NASS) establish that:
1. Surgical Urgency in Myelomalacia: The presence of myelomalacia (T2 signal change) on MRI is a strong predictor of permanent, irreversible neurological deficit if surgical decompression is delayed. For moderate-to-severe progressive cervical myelopathy, surgical decompression (ACDF) is a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) recommendation and the primary standard of care.
2. Futility and Risk of Conservative Treatment: There is no high-quality clinical evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy or spinal injections for patients with progressive myelopathy and myelomalacia. Delaying surgery to complete these trials is clinically inappropriate and places the patient at high risk of permanent neurological deterioration.
3. Absolute Medical Contraindication: While the member's permanent pacemaker did not strictly match the plan's contractual "neurological risk" waiver language, it represents an absolute cardiovascular contraindication to fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injections. Forcing a patient to undergo a medically contraindicated, physically dangerous procedure to satisfy an internal administrative checklist violates the basic clinical standard of care.


V. CONCLUSION

Because the member's clinical presentation involves progressive cervical myelopathy with objective structural compromise (myelomalacia) and a permanent pacemaker, the plan's conservative treatment requirements are legally unenforceable under California Insurance Code § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014, and are clinically contraindicated under national neurosurgical guidelines. 

Therefore, the initial denial is OVERTURNED, and prior authorization for CPT 22551 (ACDF C4-C5, one level) at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center is hereby APPROVED.
