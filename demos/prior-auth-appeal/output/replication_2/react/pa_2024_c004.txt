**I. BACKGROUND**

Raymond Flores, a 54-year-old HealthFirst PPO Gold member, underwent an initial prior authorization review for an ACDF at C5-C6. The request, submitted by Dr. Karen Davis, a spine surgeon, was for prophylactic decompression due to severe cervical stenosis with cord contact. The initial denial cited documented cervical stenosis but no neurological deficit on examination, noting the member had axial neck pain only, with no documented radiculopathy or myelopathy. The denial further stated that surgical intervention is not indicated for axial pain without neurological deficit under HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024).

The appeal basis asserts severe cervical stenosis with cord contact, a risk of myelopathy from minor trauma, the indication of prophylactic surgery for severe stenosis even without current deficits, and the member's occupation as a construction worker with high trauma risk.

**II. CLINICAL SUMMARY**

*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Severe cervical stenosis C5-C6, prophylactic decompression requested.
*   **Occupation:** Construction worker, heavy equipment operation.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-15):** Severe central canal stenosis at C5-C6 with cord contact. Canal diameter 6mm (normal >13mm). *Crucially, no T2 signal change or myelomalacia noted.* Surgeon indicates high risk of acute cord injury with trauma.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-09-20):** Completely normal. No radiculopathy, no myelopathy signs, no upper motor neuron (UMN) signs. Axial neck pain 5/10.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 8 weeks, 24 sessions. Modest improvement in axial pain (7/10 to 5/10). No neurological symptoms were present or treated.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Naproxen 500mg BID for 3 months (moderate pain relief), Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks (sleep improvement only).
    *   **Interventional:** C5-C6 epidural steroid injection (60% pain relief lasting 6 weeks).
*   **Surgeon's Rationale:** Critically narrow canal, high risk of acute spinal cord injury from trauma given occupation, prophylactic decompression indicated to prevent catastrophic injury.

**III. HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY**
The review is conducted applying the hierarchy: **State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.**

**IV. APPLICATION OF CRITERIA AND STANDARDS**

**A. HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)**

1.  **Section 2.1 - Diagnosis Requirement:** This section requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (with radicular symptoms), cervical myelopathy (with myelopathic symptoms), or cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit.
    *   **Finding:** Mr. Flores's neurological examination is explicitly documented as "completely normal," with "no radiculopathy," "no myelopathy signs," and "no UMN signs." His only current symptom is axial neck pain. Therefore, he does not meet the diagnostic requirements of 2.1(a), (b), or (c) as he presents *asymptomatically* from a neurological perspective.
2.  **Section 2.2 - Conservative Treatment Requirement:** Mr. Flores completed 8 weeks of PT, tried two pharmacotherapy agents, and underwent one interventional procedure.
    *   **Finding:** For his axial neck pain, Mr. Flores has largely met the conservative treatment requirements. The PT provided modest pain improvement, and an epidural provided 60% pain relief.
    *   **Exception 2.2(A) - Myelopathy Pathway:** This exception is for "confirmed cervical myelopathy."
        *   **Finding:** As established, Mr. Flores does not have confirmed cervical myelopathy (i.e., myelopathic symptoms/signs). Therefore, this exception does not apply.
3.  **Section 3.1 - Exclusions:** "Surgery is requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy."
    *   **Finding:** Given the normal neurological examination and documentation of "axial neck pain only," this exclusion directly applies to Mr. Flores's current symptomatic presentation. While the surgeon's *rationale* is prophylactic, the *symptoms* triggering the request from a plan's perspective are axial pain without neurological deficit.

**HealthFirst Plan Criteria Conclusion:** The initial denial correctly applied HealthFirst's plan criteria. Mr. Flores does not meet the diagnostic requirements for symptomatic conditions, and his presentation (axial neck pain without neurological deficit) falls under the plan's exclusion for surgical intervention.

**B. California Regulatory Framework**

1.  **CIC § 10169.5 - Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This statute prevents plans from conditioning approval on PT completion where: (1) PT is contraindicated/unlikely to benefit; (2) member reached a functional plateau; or (3) condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address.
    *   **Finding:** Mr. Flores has "severe central canal stenosis" and "cord contact," which constitutes "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address" (3). However, the plan's denial was not primarily for failure to complete conservative treatment (which was substantially met for axial pain), but for the lack of a neurologically symptomatic surgical indication. Therefore, while relevant to PT duration, this statute does not directly override the denial's core reasoning.
2.  **DMHC APL 22-014 - Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions:** This APL provides exceptions for conservative treatment requirements in time-sensitive neurological conditions, specifically citing myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia, or progressive pathology.
    *   **Finding:** Mr. Flores has severe cervical stenosis with cord contact but *no myelomalacia* and *no myelopathic symptoms*. The APL's focus is on *symptomatic* myelopathy or specific objective findings like myelomalacia or progressive pathology, which are absent here. Therefore, this APL does not apply to this case.
3.  **CHSC § 1374.32(b) - Standard of Review for IMR:** This provision mandates that a service meeting "generally accepted standards of medical practice" SHALL be approved regardless of the plan's internal criteria.
    *   **Finding:** This is the highest authority in question here. The core issue becomes whether prophylactic surgery for *asymptomatic* severe stenosis with cord contact in a high-risk occupation is a "generally accepted standard of medical practice" according to the provided clinical documents.

**California Regulatory Framework Conclusion:** While CIC 10169.5 addresses PT requirements, it does not directly overturn the denial based on lack of surgical indication. DMHC APL 22-014 does not apply. The final determination rests on whether the requested procedure meets generally accepted clinical standards as defined in the provided documents.

**C. Clinical Evidence Base (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)**

1.  **Section 1.1 - Cervical Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy - Distinction:** These guidelines differentiate between myelopathy and radiculopathy, requiring distinct symptoms and signs.
    *   **Finding:** Mr. Flores presents with *neither* radiculopathy nor myelopathy symptoms or signs. His neurological exam is normal. This is crucial for applying treatment algorithms.
2.  **Section 1.2 - Myelomalacia - Significance:** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) indicates existing cord injury and is a strong surgical indication, often contraindicating extended conservative treatment.
    *   **Finding:** Mr. Flores's MRI explicitly states "No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia." This absence removes a significant indicator for surgical urgency based on existing cord damage.
3.  **Section 3.1 - Accepted Indications for ACDF (AANS/CNS 2023):**
    *   **TIER 1 (STRONG INDICATION):** Requires moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression, progressive neurological deficit, myelomalacia, or acute severe myelopathy.
        *   **Finding:** Mr. Flores meets none of these criteria. He has cord contact but no myelopathy symptoms, no progressive deficit, and no myelomalacia.
    *   **TIER 2 (REASONABLE INDICATION):** Includes mild myelopathy with stable symptoms, radiculopathy with persistent symptoms, or significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care.
        *   **Finding:** Mr. Flores has no myelopathy or radiculopathy. His axial pain, while present, has shown "modest improvement" and 60% relief from an epidural, indicating some response to conservative care for his *symptoms*.
    *   **TIER 3 (SURGERY NOT INDICATED):** Includes axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy, or imaging findings without correlating symptoms.
        *   **Finding:** Mr. Flores's *symptomatic* presentation ("Axial neck pain only, 5/10") falls directly under this TIER 3 exclusion from the AANS/CNS guidelines. While his imaging shows severe stenosis with cord contact, his *current* symptoms do not correlate with a neurological deficit, aligning with "imaging findings without correlating symptoms" in the context of neurological compromise.
4.  **Prophylactic Surgery for Asymptomatic Stenosis:** The clinical guidelines provided primarily focus on indications for *symptomatic* myelopathy or radiculopathy, or objective evidence of *existing cord injury* (myelomalacia). They do not explicitly establish prophylactic cervical decompression for *asymptomatic* severe stenosis with cord contact in a high-risk occupation as a "strong" or "reasonable" surgical indication. The provided guidelines prioritize treatment for existing neurological deficits or documented cord damage.

**Clinical Evidence Base Conclusion:** The provided clinical standards do not support prophylactic ACDF for an *asymptomatic* patient with severe stenosis and cord contact in the absence of myelomalacia or neurological deficits. In fact, the AANS/CNS guidelines explicitly state that "axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy" is a Tier 3 (surgery not indicated) scenario.

**V. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND DISPOSITION**

The plan's denial is based on the member lacking neurological deficits (radiculopathy or myelopathy) and presenting with axial neck pain only, which is an exclusion under its CC-SPINE-2024 policy.

While the surgeon's rationale for prophylactic surgery due to severe stenosis, cord contact, and high occupational trauma risk is compelling from a preventative standpoint, the provided "generally accepted standards of medical practice" (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine guidelines) do not explicitly establish prophylactic cervical decompression for *asymptomatic* severe stenosis with cord contact (and no myelomalacia) as a Tier 1 (strong) or Tier 2 (reasonable) surgical indication. Instead, the clinical guidelines classify "axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy" as a Tier 3 (surgery not indicated) scenario.

The California regulatory framework, specifically CHSC § 1374.32(b), mandates approval if a service meets "generally accepted standards." In this specific case, based *strictly* on the provided clinical evidence, the request for prophylactic surgery for an *asymptomatic* patient without myelomalacia, despite severe stenosis and occupational risk, does not align with the explicit "generally accepted standards" for surgical intervention outlined in the provided documents. The patient's presentation of "axial neck pain only" with a "completely normal neurological examination" fits the criteria for which surgery is generally not indicated according to the clinical guidelines.

Therefore, the plan's denial, which aligns with the provided clinical standards for symptomatic indications, is supported.

**VI. DISPOSITION**

**UPHELD**

**VII. WARRANT**

The denial for CPT code 22551 for Raymond Flores is UPHELD. The HealthFirst Health Plan denial was consistent with its clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1 and Section 3.1) which require objective neurological deficits (radiculopathy or myelopathy) for surgical intervention and exclude surgery solely for axial neck pain without such deficits.

This plan criteria aligns with the provided generally accepted standards of medical practice from AANS/CNS, NASS, and AO Spine. The AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update, Section 3.1) explicitly state that "Axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy" falls under TIER 3 — SURGERY NOT INDICATED. Mr. Flores's clinical presentation includes "axial neck pain only" and a "completely normal neurological examination," with "no radiculopathy," "no myelopathy signs," and "no UMN signs." While Mr. Flores has severe cervical stenosis with cord contact on MRI, there is no documentation of myelomalacia (T2 signal change), which is a critical indicator of existing cord injury and surgical urgency in the clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, Section 1.2; DMHC APL 22-014, Section II.1).

Although the surgeon argues for prophylactic surgery due to occupational risk and critical stenosis, the provided clinical standards do not establish prophylactic intervention for *asymptomatic* severe stenosis without myelomalacia as a "strong" or "reasonable" indication (AANS/CNS 2023, Section 3.1). The California regulatory framework, specifically DMHC APL 22-014, which addresses urgent neurological conditions, does not apply here due to the absence of myelopathy or myelomalacia. While CIC 10169.5(a)(3) indicates that physical therapy should not be a barrier when structural neurological compromise exists, the denial's primary basis was the lack of an appropriate surgical diagnosis, not conservative treatment completion (which was largely met for pain).

Therefore, based on the provided three-source hierarchy, the plan's denial is consistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice as documented, and is hereby UPHELD.
