### APPEAL DETERMINATION: **UPHELD**

---

### REVIEW PROCESS AND FINDINGS:

This appeal was reviewed by an expert health plan appeals reviewer applying the three-source hierarchy: **State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria**. All provided documentation, including the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, California Regulatory Framework, and Clinical Evidence Base from AANS/CNS, NASS, and AO Spine, was considered.

#### 1. Clinical Presentation Summary:

Mr. Raymond Flores is a 54-year-old construction worker with severe cervical stenosis at C5-C6, evidenced by an MRI showing a 6mm canal diameter and cord contact without compression. Crucially, the MRI also notes "No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia." His neurological examination is "Completely normal," with no signs of radiculopathy or myelopathy. His primary symptom is axial neck pain (5/10), for which he completed 8 weeks of physical therapy (with modest pain improvement), a trial of Naproxen and Cyclobenzaprine, and a C5-C6 epidural steroid injection (60% pain relief for 6 weeks). Dr. Davis requests prophylactic ACDF to prevent acute spinal cord injury due to the critically narrow canal and Mr. Flores's high-risk occupation.

The critical distinction is that Mr. Flores does not currently exhibit any neurological deficits, myelopathic symptoms, or myelomalacia. The request is for *prophylactic* surgery to prevent *future* neurological injury.

#### 2. Application of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):

*   **Section 2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** The plan requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (with radicular symptoms), cervical myelopathy (with myelopathic symptoms/signs), or cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit.
    *   Mr. Flores's clinical record explicitly states "Completely normal neurological examination. No radiculopathy. No myelopathy signs. No UMN signs." His only documented symptom is axial neck pain. Therefore, he does not meet the diagnostic criteria for current radiculopathy or myelopathy as defined by HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, which requires the presence of symptoms and signs.
*   **Section 2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:** Mr. Flores completed 8 weeks of PT (exceeds 6-week minimum), two pharmacotherapy agents for appropriate durations, and one interventional procedure. For his *axial neck pain*, these requirements appear met.
*   **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY:** This exception applies "For members with confirmed cervical myelopathy."
    *   As established above, Mr. Flores does not have confirmed cervical myelopathy (lacks myelopathic symptoms and signs). Therefore, this exception does not apply.
*   **Section 3.1 EXCLUSIONS:** "Surgery is requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy."
    *   Mr. Flores's current presentation aligns precisely with this exclusion. He has severe axial neck pain but no neurological deficits, radiculopathy, or myelopathy. The *purpose* of the surgery is prophylactic, but the *current symptomatic diagnosis* falls under this exclusion.

**Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The initial denial, citing "axial neck pain only" and "no neurological deficit," is **consistent with and upheld by HealthFirst's clinical criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), specifically Section 2.1 and Exclusion 3.1.**

#### 3. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):

The provided clinical guidelines establish surgical indications based on existing neurological compromise or severe, persistent symptomatic conditions.

*   **SECTION 1 — DIAGNOSTIC CLASSIFICATION:** Mr. Flores does not meet the clinical definition of symptomatic cervical myelopathy or radiculopathy.
*   **SECTION 1.2 MYELOMALACIA — SIGNIFICANCE:** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change on MRI) is a strong indicator of existing cord injury and surgical urgency. Mr. Flores's MRI explicitly states "No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia." This absence is a key factor weighing against immediate surgical indication based on the urgency of existing cord damage.
*   **SECTION 3.1 ACCEPTED INDICATIONS FOR ACDF (CPT 22551):**
    *   **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION:** These criteria require moderate-severe myelopathy with objective cord compression, progressive neurological deficit, myelomalacia, or acute severe myelopathy. None of these are present in Mr. Flores's case. He has no current myelopathy, no neurological deficit, and no myelomalacia.
    *   **TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION:** These criteria include mild myelopathy with stable symptoms after conservative trial or radiculopathy with persistent symptoms. Again, Mr. Flores does not meet these as he has no myelopathy or radiculopathy.
    *   **TIER 3 — SURGERY NOT INDICATED:** This tier includes "Axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy" and "Imaging findings without correlating symptoms."
        *   Mr. Flores's presentation (axial neck pain only, severe stenosis on imaging without neurological symptoms) aligns with these Tier 3 criteria.
*   **Prophylactic Surgery:** While Dr. Davis's concern for prophylactic decompression given Mr. Flores's occupation and severe stenosis is clinically understandable, the provided clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) **do not establish prophylactic cervical decompression for asymptomatic severe stenosis with cord contact (in the absence of neurological deficits or myelomalacia) as a generally accepted surgical indication.** The guidelines primarily focus on treating *existing* symptomatic disease or documented cord injury (e.g., myelomalacia).

**Conclusion on Clinical Standard:** The clinical standard **does not support surgical intervention** for prophylactic reasons in a neurologically asymptomatic patient with severe stenosis and cord contact, particularly in the absence of myelomalacia. The denial is consistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice.

#### 4. Application of California Regulatory Requirements:

*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) — Standard of Review for IMR:** This provision states that if a service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, it shall be approved regardless of plan's more restrictive criteria.
    *   As determined above, the requested prophylactic surgery does **not** meet the generally accepted standards of medical practice as outlined in the provided clinical guidelines for this specific presentation. Therefore, this provision does not compel an overturn.
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 — Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions:** This APL provides exceptions to conservative treatment requirements for urgent neurological conditions, specifically highlighting "myelomalacia on MRI" or "progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia)" as triggers.
    *   Mr. Flores's MRI explicitly shows "No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia." He also has no current neurological deficit or signs of progressive myelopathy. Therefore, the key triggers for invoking the urgency provisions and waiving standard conservative treatment requirements under DMHC APL 22-014 are **not met**. The APL's emphasis is on *existing* myelopathy with evidence of cord injury or progression, not the *risk* of future myelopathy in an asymptomatic individual.
*   **CIC § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This statute outlines conditions under which PT cannot be required.
    *   (a)(3) "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." While Mr. Flores has severe *structural* stenosis, he has no *current neurological compromise* (normal exam). PT *was* for his axial pain and provided modest benefit. This clause typically applies when there is an existing neurological deficit that PT cannot functionally improve. Given the absence of current neurological deficit, this provision does not compel a waiver of PT (though PT requirements for his axial pain were met anyway).

**Conclusion on State Law:** No provision within the California Regulatory Framework overrides the denial in this specific case. The criteria for urgent myelopathy and waiver of conservative treatment due to neurological compromise are not met given the absence of current neurological deficits or myelomalacia.

---

### WARRANT:

The initial denial of ACDF C5-C6 for Mr. Raymond Flores is **UPHELD**. The requested procedure is for *prophylactic* decompression to prevent future spinal cord injury, as the member currently presents with severe cervical stenosis and cord contact but no neurological deficits, no myelopathic symptoms, and no evidence of myelomalacia on MRI. His only current complaint is axial neck pain.

1.  **Plan Criteria:** HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 explicitly denies surgical intervention for "axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy" (Exclusion 3.1) and requires specific symptomatic diagnoses (Section 2.1), none of which Mr. Flores currently meets.
2.  **Clinical Standard:** Generally accepted standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine guidelines) do not establish prophylactic cervical decompression for asymptomatic severe stenosis with cord contact (in the absence of neurological deficits or myelomalacia) as a strong or reasonable surgical indication. Their Tier 1 and Tier 2 indications consistently require existing neurological compromise, symptomatic myelopathy, or evidence of cord injury (e.g., myelomalacia). Mr. Flores's presentation falls into Tier 3 ("axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy" or "imaging findings without correlating symptoms").
3.  **State Law:** Relevant California regulations, including DMHC APL 22-014 (regarding urgent neurological conditions and waiver of conservative treatment) and CIC § 10169.5 (regarding physical therapy as a prerequisite), do not compel an overturn in this case. The specific triggers for these provisions, such as the presence of myelomalacia or current neurological compromise, are not met in Mr. Flores's clinical presentation. The absence of myelomalacia is a critical distinction that precludes application of the urgency clauses in APL 22-014.

While the surgeon's concern regarding the high risk of catastrophic injury due to Mr. Flores's occupation and severe anatomical compromise is noted, the current clinical guidelines and regulatory framework focus on intervention for *existing* symptomatic or injured states, not purely prophylactic measures in a neurologically asymptomatic individual.
