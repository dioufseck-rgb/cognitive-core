## HealthFirst PPO Gold — Prior Authorization Appeal Determination

**Case ID:** PA-2024-G002
**Member:** Priya Nair (M-G002)
**Procedure:** ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551)
**Date of Denial:** 2024-10-25
**Reviewer:** HealthFirst Appeals Reviewer

### Background

The member, Priya Nair, age 56, was denied prior authorization for an ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551). The denial reason cited was "Member does not meet conservative treatment requirements under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. Physical therapy: 5 weeks completed. Standard pathway requires 6 weeks minimum. Member has not completed the required minimum conservative treatment."

The appeal argues that the plan applied the incorrect pathway, stating that the Myelopathy pathway (Section 2.2A) applies, which requires a minimum of 4 weeks of physical therapy (PT), and the member completed 5 weeks. The appeal confirms a myelopathy diagnosis with bilateral upper motor neuron (UMN) signs and cord compression on MRI.

### Clinical Record Summary

*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Cervical myelopathy C5-C6, confirmed by neurological exam and MRI. Diagnosis confirmed by neurologist: "Clinical picture consistent with cervical myelopathy. Bilateral UMN signs are pathognomonic. Diagnosis: cervical myelopathy, primary." (ICD-10: G35.89).
*   **Imaging:** MRI dated 2024-09-01 shows C5-C6 moderate central cord compression. Notably, it indicates "No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia."
*   **Neurological Exam:** Dated 2024-09-05, findings include bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia upper extremities, mildly impaired tandem gait, and bilateral hand clumsiness, all confirming UMN signs and myelopathy.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy (PT):** 5 weeks (15 sessions) completed under a "myelopathy protocol." Treating physician halted PT at 5 weeks due to "no improvement in UMN signs" and the myelopathy diagnosis.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Baclofen 10mg TID for 3 months (minimal spasticity relief) and Methylprednisolone dose pack for 3 weeks (temporary improvement only).
    *   **Interventional:** Waived by treating physician (Dr. Chen) who documented: "Interventional procedure not indicated — cord compression with myelopathy requires surgical evaluation, not injection."

### Appeal Determination

**OVERTURN**

### Rationale

The denial should be **OVERTURNED** based on the following analysis, applying the hierarchy of authority: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.**

1.  **Misapplication of HealthFirst Plan Criteria:**
    *   The denial by HealthFirst Utilization Management cited Section 2.2 (standard pathway) of CC-SPINE-2024, which requires 6 weeks of physical therapy.
    *   However, the clinical record unequivocally confirms a primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy C5-C6 with objective UMN signs and cord compression. HealthFirst's own policy, **CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway)**, specifically addresses this presentation.
    *   Under the Myelopathy Pathway, the physical therapy requirement is explicitly **reduced to 4 weeks minimum**. The member completed 5 weeks of PT.
    *   Therefore, the plan **misapplied its own policy** by using the standard pathway instead of the applicable myelopathy pathway, and the member *exceeded* the PT requirement under the correct pathway.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy Review (within Plan Criteria):** The myelopathy pathway maintains the requirement for "at least two... agents for minimum 4 weeks each." The member received Baclofen for 3 months (meets 4-week minimum) and Methylprednisolone for 3 weeks (does not meet 4-week minimum). While this specific aspect of pharmacotherapy was not strictly met under the plan's criteria, it is superseded by state law and clinical standards as outlined below.

2.  **State Law Overrides Plan Criteria for Physical Therapy:**
    *   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)** prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of surgery on completion of PT where specific conditions are met. In this case:
        *   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2): Functional Plateau:** The treating physician documented that PT was halted at 5 weeks due to "no improvement in UMN signs" for myelopathy. The "CLINICAL REFERENCE SUMMARY" defines functional plateau as failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement, and notes that neurological symptoms in myelopathy are not expected to respond to PT. The member therefore reached a functional plateau regarding the myelopathic symptoms.
        *   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3): Objective Structural Neurological Compromise:** The member has objective evidence of C5-C6 moderate central cord compression and confirmed myelopathy with UMN signs, which PT cannot address.
    *   Both conditions (functional plateau and objective structural compromise) are met, **overriding any plan-specific minimum PT duration, whether 6 weeks or 4 weeks.**

3.  **State Law and Clinical Standards Override Pharmacotherapy Requirement:**
    *   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 II.2** states that plans **SHALL NOT** require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when: (b) the treating physician documents conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression, AND (c) surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines.
        *   **Documentation of Failure:** Dr. Chen's note that PT was halted due to "no improvement in UMN signs" and the "minimal" / "temporary" relief from pharmacotherapy clearly indicates conservative treatment failure to address the myelopathy.
        *   **Surgical Indication:** The member's condition (moderate-severe myelopathy with cord compression and UMN signs) is a **TIER 1 (STRONG) surgical indication** according to the **AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine: Guidelines for Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update)**.
    *   Therefore, the DMHC APL 22-014 exceptions for conservative treatment apply, and the plan **cannot require strict adherence to the full pharmacotherapy duration** when failure is documented and surgery is clinically indicated.
    *   **CHSC § 1374.32(b)** emphasizes that the Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard of review is "generally accepted standards of medical practice," which overrides more restrictive plan criteria.

4.  **Adherence to Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:**
    *   The clinical record confirms **Cervical Myelopathy**, which is a distinct and more serious condition than radiculopathy. The "CLINICAL REFERENCE SUMMARY" from AANS/CNS, NASS, and AO Spine consistently emphasizes that conservative treatment has **LIMITED efficacy for established myelopathy** and that **applying radiculopathy-derived PT requirements to myelopathy patients is not supported by evidence.**
    *   The **AO Spine Guidelines (2021)** explicitly state, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." The member's presentation (moderate cord compression, bilateral UMN signs, gait disturbance, hand clumsiness) aligns with moderate-severe myelopathy.
    *   The member meets **TIER 1 (Strong Indication)** for surgery per **AANS/CNS Guidelines (2023)** due to:
        *   (a) Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI.
        *   (b) Progressive neurological deficit despite conservative treatment (lack of improvement in UMN signs).
    *   While myelomalacia (cord signal change) was not present, which would indicate even greater urgency, its absence does not negate the strong surgical indication for established moderate-severe myelopathy with objective neurological deficits.

### Conclusion

The HealthFirst denial was based on a misapplication of its own clinical criteria, failing to recognize and apply the Myelopathy Pathway (Section 2.2A). Even if the plan's criteria were correctly applied, both California state law (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine) override the plan's specific conservative treatment duration requirements for this member's confirmed moderate-severe cervical myelopathy. The evidence overwhelmingly supports the medical necessity of the requested ACDF C5-C6.

Therefore, the prior authorization denial for ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) for Priya Nair is **OVERTURNED**. The requested procedure is medically necessary and should be authorized.
