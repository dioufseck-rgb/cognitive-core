**Procedure:** Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C5-C6 (CPT 22551)
**Date of Denial:** 2024-10-25
**Date of Appeal Review:** 2024-11-01

### Appeal Disposition: OVERTURNED

### Warrant

This appeal concerns the denial of prior authorization for an ACDF C5-C6 for Ms. Priya Nair, a 56-year-old member with a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy. The initial denial was based on the assertion that the member did not meet conservative treatment requirements under HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, specifically stating that 5 weeks of physical therapy (PT) completed was insufficient against a standard pathway requirement of 6 weeks.

Upon comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory framework, and generally accepted clinical standards, the denial is hereby **OVERTURNED** for the following reasons:

1.  **Incorrect Application of Plan Criteria by Initial Reviewer:**
    *   The initial denial incorrectly applied the "Standard pathway" (Section 2.2) of HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 criteria.
    *   The clinical record unequivocally establishes a "Cervical myelopathy C5-C6" as the primary diagnosis, confirmed by neurological examination (bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, gait impairment, hand clumsiness – indicative of upper motor neuron signs) and MRI (C5-C6 moderate central cord compression). The neurologist's consultation explicitly states, "Diagnosis: cervical myelopathy, primary."
    *   HealthFirst's own criteria, under **CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY**, clearly state this exception applies when myelopathy is the primary diagnosis. This exception modifies conservative treatment requirements specifically for myelopathy.
    *   Therefore, the factual claim in the denial notice ("Standard pathway (Section 2.2) requires 6 weeks PT") was misapplied, as the myelopathy pathway was the applicable criterion.

2.  **Compliance with Plan's Myelopathy Pathway (Section 2.2A) for Physical Therapy and Interventional Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy (PT):** Under Section 2.2(A), the PT requirement for confirmed cervical myelopathy is reduced to a "4 weeks minimum." The member completed 5 weeks of supervised physical therapy, which exceeds this 4-week minimum. Furthermore, the clinical record documents "No improvement in UMN signs," indicating a lack of benefit from continued PT for the critical myelopathic symptoms.
    *   **Interventional Treatment:** Section 2.2(A) waives the interventional treatment requirement "if treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses risk of neurological deterioration." Dr. Chen's letter explicitly states, "Interventional procedure not indicated — cord compression with myelopathy requires surgical evaluation, not injection," which adequately documents the inappropriateness and potential risk of delaying definitive surgical care with interventional treatment for this condition.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** While the plan's myelopathy pathway requires a trial of "at least two... for minimum 4 weeks each," the member received Baclofen for 3 months and Methylprednisolone for 3 weeks. Although the Methylprednisolone did not meet the 4-week duration, this minor deviation in duration for one of two agents is superseded by higher authorities as detailed below.

3.  **Override by California State Law:**
    *   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This state law explicitly prohibits conditioning surgical approval on PT completion where:
        *   (a)(1) The physician documents PT is unlikely to benefit: Dr. Chen halted PT at 5 weeks due to myelopathy and lack of UMN sign improvement, implicitly indicating PT was no longer beneficial for the myelopathic symptoms.
        *   (a)(2) The member reached a functional plateau: The "no improvement in UMN signs" after 5 weeks for myelopathy, whose neurological symptoms are not expected to respond to PT, constitutes a functional plateau for the primary condition.
        *   (a)(3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address: The MRI demonstrating C5-C6 moderate central cord compression with confirmed myelopathy falls squarely under this provision. PT cannot resolve spinal cord compression.
        *   Furthermore, CIC 10169.5(c) states that minimum PT weeks "shall not be applied mechanically." The initial denial was a mechanical application.
    *   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This guidance classifies "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression" as an urgent condition with potential for irreversible harm. It directs plans to "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines. While myelomalacia (T2 cord signal change) was not noted in this member's MRI, the underlying principle that plans "shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice" (**APL 22-014 II.3**) remains paramount for myelopathy cases.
    *   **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard of Review):** This critical statute mandates that if a health care service is "medically necessary" based on "generally accepted standards of medical practice," it SHALL be approved, "regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." This overrides any remaining technical non-compliance with the plan's pharmacotherapy duration for one agent.

4.  **Compliance with Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:**
    *   **Diagnosis and Urgency:** Clinical references (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) clearly differentiate cervical myelopathy from radiculopathy, noting that myelopathy involves spinal cord compression with upper motor neuron signs and has limited efficacy with conservative treatment. The presence of objective cord compression and UMN signs in Ms. Nair constitutes a strong indication for surgery.
    *   **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy:** The clinical guidelines emphasize that for moderate-to-severe myelopathy, physical therapy does **not** produce meaningful functional improvement, and there is **no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention.** AO Spine Guidelines (2021) specifically state, "Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
    *   **Surgical Indication:** Ms. Nair's presentation (confirmed cervical myelopathy, objective cord compression on MRI, UMN signs, and failure to improve with conservative efforts) meets **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION** for surgery according to the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023). These guidelines unequivocally state that for this presentation, the clinical standard does NOT require completion of a minimum course of physical therapy.
    *   The attempted pharmacotherapy, including Baclofen for 3 months and a steroid dose pack, along with 5 weeks of PT with no improvement in UMN signs, is entirely consistent with a reasonable and adequate trial of conservative management for myelopathy, given the strong surgical indication and limited efficacy of prolonged non-surgical approaches for this condition.

### Conclusion

The initial denial by HealthFirst was based on an erroneous application of its own internal criteria, failing to recognize and apply the specific myelopathy pathway. While a minor technical discrepancy existed within the plan's pharmacotherapy duration for one agent under the *correct* plan pathway, this is fundamentally overridden by controlling California state laws (CIC § 10169.5, CHSC § 1374.32, and DMHC APL 22-014) and strong, uncontradicted generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine).

The requested ACDF C5-C6 for Ms. Nair is medically necessary, meets generally accepted standards of medical practice for cervical myelopathy, and is consistent with the urgent nature of her condition. Prolonged conservative treatment, particularly delaying surgical decompression, is not supported by clinical evidence for moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy and risks irreversible neurological deterioration.

Therefore, the prior authorization denial is **OVERTURNED**, and authorization for procedure CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) is **APPROVED**.
