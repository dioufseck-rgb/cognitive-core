### I. Case Summary

The member, Mr. Bernard Achebe, a 61-year-old male, was denied prior authorization for an anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). The denial, issued by Dr. Alan Foster on 2024-11-05, cited insufficient completion of conservative treatment per the standard pathway, specifically: 3 weeks of physical therapy (6 required), only one pharmacotherapy agent, and no interventional procedure. The treating neurologist, Dr. Kwame Osei, is appealing the denial, asserting surgical urgency, contraindication to further delay, confirmed myelopathy, and failure of conservative treatment to arrest neurological progression.

Clinical records indicate a diagnosis of "Mild cervical myelopathy C5-C6" with MRI showing "moderate central cord compression." Neurological exam reveals bilateral Hoffman signs and mild hyperreflexia. The mJOA score is 15/18, indicating mild myelopathy. Conservative treatment included 3 weeks of physical therapy (halted by Dr. Osei due to myelopathy concern) and 6 weeks of Baclofen (one agent). No interventional procedures were performed.

### II. Applicable Policies and Guidelines

This appeal determination is based on the following hierarchy: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria**.

1.  **HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
    *   Section 2.2: Standard conservative treatment requires 6 weeks PT, 2 pharmacotherapy agents, and 1 interventional procedure.
    *   Exception 2.2(A) – Myelopathy Pathway: For confirmed myelopathy, PT is reduced to 4 weeks minimum, and interventional treatment is waived if the treating physician documents risk of neurological deterioration. Pharmacotherapy remains 2 agents.

2.  **California Regulatory Framework:**
    *   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5:** Prohibits conditioning surgical approval on PT completion where: (1) physician documents PT contraindicated or unlikely to benefit, OR (2) member reached functional plateau, OR (3) objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address.
    *   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:** Plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when objective imaging shows pathology, physician documents conservative failure, and surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines for conditions risking irreversible harm (e.g., myelopathy).
    *   **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b):** In Independent Medical Review (IMR), medical necessity is determined by generally accepted standards of medical practice, overriding more restrictive plan criteria.

3.  **Clinical Evidence Base:**
    *   **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023):** Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression is a TIER 1 strong surgical indication. Surgery is appropriate in mild myelopathy, but urgency for acute progression/myelomalacia is a separate classification. No PT minimum duration established for myelopathy.
    *   **AO Spine International (2021):** No high-quality evidence supports mandatory PT prior to surgery for moderate to severe cervical myelopathy; delay may result in neurological deterioration. Application of radiculopathy-derived PT requirements to myelopathy is not supported by evidence.
    *   **NASS Guidelines (2020):** Conservative treatment may be considered for mild myelopathy with close monitoring, but surgery is primary for moderate-severe.

### III. Analysis

**1. Primary Clinical Presentation:**
The member has a confirmed diagnosis of **cervical myelopathy** (mild, C5-C6), supported by MRI showing moderate central cord compression and clinical findings of bilateral Hoffman signs and mild hyperreflexia. This is a critical distinction, as myelopathy (spinal cord compression) has a different treatment algorithm than radiculopathy (nerve root compression). The plan's standard conservative pathway is largely derived from radiculopathy evidence, which is inappropriate for myelopathy.

**2. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
*   **Initial Denial:** The initial denial applied the standard conservative treatment requirements (6 weeks PT, 2 pharmacotherapy agents, 1 interventional procedure). This was incorrect, as the member's primary diagnosis is myelopathy, triggering Exception 2.2(A).
*   **Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A) Review:**
    *   **Physical Therapy (PT):** The exception reduces the requirement to 4 weeks. The member completed 3 weeks. Thus, strictly by the plan's exception, this requirement was not met.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** The exception does not modify the pharmacotherapy requirement, which remains a trial of at least two agents. The member used only one (Baclofen). Thus, this requirement was not met.
    *   **Interventional Treatment:** The exception waives the interventional requirement if the treating physician documents risk of neurological deterioration. Dr. Osei's letter states, "further delay risks irreversible neurological deterioration" and "I cannot overstate the urgency." This clearly satisfies the plan's condition for waiving the interventional procedure.

*   **Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** Even with the myelopathy exception, the request would be deniable based on insufficient PT (3/4 weeks) and pharmacotherapy (1/2 agents).

**3. Application of California Regulatory Framework:**

*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy):** This state law directly addresses the PT denial.
    *   **(a)(1) Physician documentation:** Dr. Osei halted PT, citing "myelopathy concern," and declared in his appeal letter that "further delay is medically contraindicated" and "risks irreversible neurological deterioration." This constitutes sufficient documentation that further PT is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member, particularly for the neurological progression of myelopathy.
    *   **(a)(3) Structural neurological compromise:** The member has confirmed "moderate central cord compression" on MRI and myelopathy with bilateral UMN signs. This is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address.
    *   **Conclusion:** Under CIC § 10169.5, HealthFirst cannot deny the procedure based on the incomplete physical therapy requirement.

*   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This APL mandates that plans shall not require a full course of conservative treatment where delay risks irreversible harm and surgery is indicated.
    *   **Risk of irreversible harm:** Myelopathy with objective cord compression is a condition identified by the DMHC as having potential for irreversible harm if untreated (APL 22-014, II.1).
    *   **Physician documents conservative failure:** Dr. Osei's declaration, "conservative treatment has failed to arrest neurological progression," fulfills this condition.
    *   **Surgical intervention indicated:** Per AANS/CNS (2023), surgery is "appropriate" for mild myelopathy (mJOA 15) and a "strong indication" for moderate myelopathy with objective cord compression. While the member's myelopathy is classified as mild, and myelomalacia is not present, the objective cord compression and UMN signs establish a medically necessary surgical indication.
    *   **Conclusion:** DMHC APL 22-014 dictates that HealthFirst SHALL NOT require completion of a *full course* of conservative treatment (including the second pharmacotherapy agent) given the documentation from the treating physician and the underlying condition.

**4. Application of Clinical Standard:**

*   **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021) strongly differentiate myelopathy from radiculopathy, emphasizing that conservative treatment evidence for radiculopathy does not apply to myelopathy.
*   **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** AO Spine (2021) explicitly states: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients... is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." NASS (2020) indicates conservative treatment has "LIMITED efficacy for established myelopathy." The plan's PT requirement (even 4 weeks in its exception) for myelopathy is inconsistent with generally accepted clinical standards.
*   **Pharmacotherapy/Interventional for Myelopathy:** While less explicit on pharmacotherapy duration, the overall clinical consensus (AANS/CNS, AO Spine, NASS) underscores the limited efficacy of conservative treatment for *established myelopathy* and the risk of delaying definitive surgical treatment.
*   **Surgical Indication:** The member has moderate central cord compression and objective UMN signs (Hoffman, hyperreflexia) consistent with myelopathy. ACDF (CPT 22551) is an accepted treatment for this condition. While the mJOA score of 15/18 is considered "mild myelopathy," the AANS/CNS guidelines indicate surgery is "appropriate" in mild myelopathy.

**5. Conflict Resolution (Hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):**

*   **Physical Therapy:** The plan's requirement for 4 weeks of PT (per its myelopathy exception) is directly overridden by **CIC § 10169.5 (a)(1) and (a)(3)**, as the treating physician documented concern regarding PT for myelopathy and objective structural neurological compromise is present. Clinical standards also refute mandatory PT duration for myelopathy.
*   **Interventional Treatment:** The plan's own **Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A)** waives this requirement, as Dr. Osei explicitly documented risk of neurological deterioration/contraindication to further delay.
*   **Pharmacotherapy (Second Agent):** The plan's requirement for a second agent (even under its exception) is superseded by **DMHC APL 22-014 (II.2)**. The treating physician documented conservative treatment failure to arrest progression, and surgery is indicated for myelopathy under generally accepted guidelines. Requiring a "full course" of multiple pharmacotherapy agents (when one has already been trialed) would constitute an unnecessary delay for a condition with potential for irreversible harm, contrary to the APL's intent and inconsistent with the limited efficacy of conservative treatment for myelopathy noted in clinical standards.
*   **Overall:** The plan's denial relies on conservative treatment requirements that are either directly overridden by state law, explicitly waived by its own policy (when correctly applied), or contradicted by generally accepted clinical standards for myelopathy. CHSC § 1374.32(b) reinforces that if a service meets generally accepted standards, it SHALL be approved regardless of more restrictive plan criteria.

### IV. Determination

The prior authorization denial for procedure CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) for member Bernard Achebe (PA-2024-G003) is **OVERTURNED**.

### V. Warrant

The denial, based on uncompleted conservative treatment, is not supported by the member's clinical presentation, state law, or generally accepted clinical standards.

1.  **Diagnosis:** The member has confirmed cervical myelopathy, not radiculopathy. The plan's conservative treatment requirements, particularly for physical therapy, are primarily derived from radiculopathy evidence and are not appropriate or supported by evidence for myelopathy. (Clinical Standard: AO Spine 2021, NASS 2020, AANS/CNS 2023).

2.  **Physical Therapy Requirement:** The requirement for additional physical therapy is directly overridden by **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5**.
    *   Per **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1)**, Dr. Osei's documentation (halting PT due to myelopathy concern, declaring "further delay risks irreversible neurological deterioration") demonstrates that further PT is unlikely to benefit or is contraindicated.
    *   Per **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)**, the member has "moderate central cord compression" and bilateral upper motor neuron signs (Hoffman's, hyperreflexia), which constitute objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address.
    *   Furthermore, **AO Spine (2021) and NASS (2020)** guidelines indicate no high-quality evidence supporting mandatory PT prior to surgery for moderate to severe cervical myelopathy, and warn against delays.

3.  **Interventional Procedure Requirement:** The plan's denial for lack of an interventional procedure is invalid under its own **Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), Section 2.2(A) Myelopathy Exception**. Dr. Osei's declaration of urgency and risk of "irreversible neurological deterioration" if treatment is delayed meets the plan's condition for waiving this requirement.

4.  **Pharmacotherapy Requirement:** The denial for insufficient pharmacotherapy (one agent instead of two) is superseded by **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (II.2)**. The APL states that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when the treating physician documents conservative treatment failure (as Dr. Osei implicitly does by stating surgical urgency and risks of delay) and surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. For myelopathy with objective cord compression and UMN signs, surgical intervention is indicated (AANS/CNS 2023, TIER 1/2). Requiring a trial of a second pharmacotherapy agent for myelopathy when one has already been used and symptoms persist, particularly with a physician declaration of urgency, constitutes enforcing a "full course" that may delay necessary treatment. The clinical standards emphasize the limited efficacy of conservative treatment for established myelopathy.

Therefore, applying the three-source hierarchy, the state laws and clinical standards override the plan's criteria regarding conservative treatment for cervical myelopathy in this case. The requested ACDF is medically necessary and should be approved.

**Action:** Overturn the denial and authorize CPT 22551.
