## PRIOR AUTHORIZATION APPEAL DETERMINATION

**Case ID:** PA-2024-G003
**Member:** Bernard Achebe (M-G003), 61 years old
**Plan:** HealthFirst PPO Gold — Group Plan 77441, California
**Procedure:** ACDF C5-C6, one level (CPT 22551)
**Date of Review:** 2024-11-12
**Reviewer:** [Your Name/Title], Expert Health Plan Appeals Reviewer

### I. Case Summary

The member, Bernard Achebe, is a 61-year-old male with a diagnosis of mild cervical myelopathy at C5-C6, confirmed by MRI showing moderate central cord compression and clinical findings of bilateral Hoffman signs and hyperreflexia. His modified Japanese Orthopaedic Association (mJOA) score is 15/18, indicating mild myelopathy, and his activities of daily living (ADLs) are fully intact. Symptoms have been stable for 6 months.

The treating neurologist, Dr. Kwame Osei, requested immediate surgical intervention (ACDF C5-C6), citing severe and progressive compression, contraindication to further delay, and risk of irreversible neurological deterioration.

The initial prior authorization request was denied by HealthFirst Utilization Management (Dr. Alan Foster) on 2024-11-05. The denial reason cited incomplete conservative treatment: 3 of 6 weeks of physical therapy (PT) completed, only one pharmacotherapy agent used, and no interventional procedure on record.

### II. Appeal Basis

The member's appeal is based on:
1.  Treating neurologist's declaration of surgical urgency and contraindication to further delay.
2.  Myelopathy confirmed on clinical exam.
3.  Conservative treatment has failed to arrest neurological progression.

### III. Reviewer's Analysis

This appeal determination applies the three-source hierarchy: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.**

#### 1. Application of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)

*   **2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (2.1(b)) with objective MRI findings (moderate central cord compression) and clinical signs (bilateral Hoffman, hyperreflexia). This criterion is **met**. Myelopathy is the primary diagnosis.
*   **2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:**
    *   **Standard Pathway:** The member completed 3 weeks of PT (plan requires 6), used 1 pharmacotherapy agent (plan requires 2 for 4+ weeks each), and had no interventional procedures (plan requires 1). Thus, the standard pathway requirements are **not met**.
    *   **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY:** This exception applies because myelopathy is the primary diagnosis.
        *   **Physical Therapy:** Reduced to 4 weeks minimum. The member completed 3 weeks. This requirement is **not met** by the plan's exception. Dr. Osei halted PT after 3 weeks due to "myelopathy concern."
        *   **Interventional Treatment:** Waived if treating physician documents risk of neurological deterioration. Dr. Osei's letter states: "Further delay is medically contraindicated. I cannot overstate the urgency — any further delay risks irreversible neurological deterioration." This statement reasonably documents a risk of neurological deterioration from delay, which would include delaying for an interventional procedure. This requirement is considered **waived** per the plan's exception.
        *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Requirement (2.2(b)) remains in full. The member used one agent (Baclofen 5mg BID for 6 weeks). The plan requires two agents. This requirement is **not met**.

*   **Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** Based solely on HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, the denial appears consistent due to unfulfilled requirements for physical therapy (3 of 4 weeks) and pharmacotherapy (1 of 2 agents), even with the myelopathy exception applied.

#### 2. Application of California Regulatory Framework (State Law)

California State Law holds the highest authority in this hierarchy.

*   **CIC § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This statute explicitly limits a plan's ability to require PT completion.
    *   **(a)(1) Physician documents PT contraindicated or unlikely to benefit:** Dr. Osei halted PT after 3 weeks "citing myelopathy concern" and declared "further delay is medically contraindicated." This meets the condition that the physician documented PT as unlikely to benefit or contraindicated.
    *   **(a)(3) Objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address:** The clinical record shows "C5-C6 moderate central cord compression" on MRI and "Bilateral Hoffman signs present. Bilateral hyperreflexia mild" on neurological exam. This constitutes objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (spinal cord compression with upper motor neuron signs) that physical therapy cannot resolve.
    *   **Conclusion on PT Requirement:** Pursuant to **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3)**, HealthFirst **cannot** condition approval of surgery on completion of further physical therapy. The plan's requirement for 4 or 6 weeks of PT is superseded by state law.

*   **DMHC APL 22-014 — Prior Authorization Requirements for Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions:**
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** The APL notes that myelopathy with cord compression and *myelomalacia* is urgent. However, the MRI for Mr. Achebe *explicitly states "No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia."* While Mr. Achebe has myelopathy, the absence of myelomalacia, combined with an mJOA score of 15/18 (mild myelopathy) and documented "stable" symptoms for 6 months, means the most impactful urgency triggers of APL 22-014, II.1 are not fully present in the objective record.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** For waiver of conservative treatment due to risk of irreversible harm, the APL requires "(a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia, or progressive herniation on serial imaging)." The clinical record lacks serial imaging to show progression and explicitly notes "No myelomalacia." While Dr. Osei declares progression, it is not objectively documented by imaging as defined in this clause.
    *   **Conclusion on DMHC APL 22-014:** While the APL provides strong guidance for myelopathy generally, the specific objective criteria (e.g., myelomalacia, documented progression on serial imaging) for its most forceful conservative treatment waivers are not met in this specific case based on the provided record.

*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) — Standard of Review for IMR:** This provision states that if a service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice," it shall be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. This applies when considering the pharmacotherapy requirement against clinical standards.

#### 3. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)

Clinical standards inform "generally accepted standards of medical practice."

*   **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy (Clinical Reference Section 1.1):** The clinical standards emphasize that myelopathy and radiculopathy are distinct, with different treatment algorithms. Applying conservative treatment requirements derived from radiculopathy evidence to myelopathy is a "category error." The plan's standard 6-week PT requirement, as noted in the domain knowledge, is derived from radiculopathy.
*   **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy (Clinical Reference Section 2.1):** "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy." For *mild* myelopathy (like Mr. Achebe's mJOA 15/18), NASS (2020) states "Conservative treatment may be considered... with close monitoring for progression." However, "surgical decompression is recommended as the primary treatment" for moderate to severe. While Mr. Achebe's myelopathy is mild, the AO Spine Guidelines (2021) strongly caution against delaying surgery for unproven PT trials.
*   **Conservative Treatment Duration (Clinical Reference Section 2.2):** "No randomized controlled trials support a defined minimum duration of conservative treatment before surgery" for myelopathy. The 6-week PT requirement has "NO evidentiary basis for myelopathy."
*   **Surgical Indications (Clinical Reference Section 3.1):**
    *   Mr. Achebe's condition of "mild cervical myelopathy C5-C6" with "moderate central cord compression" and UMN signs (Hoffman, hyperreflexia) but "stable" symptoms and "no myelomalacia" does not meet **TIER 1 (Strong Indication)** criteria (which typically requires moderate-severe myelopathy, progression, or myelomalacia).
    *   However, it fits **TIER 2 (Reasonable Indication)**: "(a) Mild myelopathy with stable symptoms after adequate conservative trial." The question is the "adequacy" of the trial.

#### 4. Conflict Resolution and Disposition

Applying the hierarchy (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):

1.  **Physical Therapy (PT):** The plan denied because 3 of 6 weeks (or 3 of 4 under myelopathy exception) were completed. However, **California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(3)** directly overrides this. Mr. Achebe has "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise" (moderate central cord compression, Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia) that PT cannot address. Additionally, Dr. Osei's halting of PT due to myelopathy concern could also invoke **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1)**. Therefore, the plan's PT requirement is **overridden by state law**.

2.  **Interventional Procedure:** The plan's denial cited "no interventional procedure on record." However, **HealthFirst's own Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Exception)**, explicitly states the interventional treatment requirement is WAIVED if the treating physician documents that it "poses risk of neurological deterioration." Dr. Osei's declaration that "further delay risks irreversible neurological deterioration" is a reasonable and sufficient documentation of risk to meet this waiver within the plan's own criteria. Therefore, the interventional procedure requirement is **waived per plan criteria**.

3.  **Pharmacotherapy:** The plan requires a trial of at least two pharmacotherapy agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each, and the myelopathy exception does not waive this. Mr. Achebe used only one agent (Baclofen) for 6 weeks.
    *   DMHC APL 22-014's strongest waiver provisions are not fully met due to the absence of objective myelomalacia or documented progression.
    *   However, the clinical standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) do not establish a minimum *number* of pharmacotherapy agents for myelopathy. The general principle articulated in the "Clinical Reference Summary" (Section 1.1) is that applying radiculopathy-derived conservative treatment requirements (which often include multiple agents) to myelopathy is a "category error."
    *   Given that myelopathy is a structural condition causing spinal cord compression and upper motor neuron signs, and one pharmacotherapy agent (Baclofen) was trialed for 6 weeks with "mild spasticity improvement," requiring a *second* pharmacotherapy agent for an *additional* 4+ weeks as a prerequisite for surgery is arguably more restrictive than "generally accepted standards of medical practice" for myelopathy, where the focus is on addressing the structural compression rather than indefinitely trialing palliative medications, especially when the treating physician declares urgency. **CHSC § 1374.32(b)** directs that services meeting generally accepted standards shall be approved even if plan criteria are more restrictive.

**Conclusion:**
The plan's denial was based on conservative treatment requirements that are either directly overridden by California state law (for physical therapy) or waived by its own criteria (for interventional procedures). The remaining pharmacotherapy requirement for a second agent, while part of plan criteria, is not explicitly supported by clinical guidelines for myelopathy and, in the context of a structural cord compression with UMN signs, is considered more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. While the objective evidence for *acute* urgency (e.g., myelomalacia, rapid progression) is not fully present, the diagnosis of myelopathy with UMN signs is established, and delaying surgery for requirements not strongly supported by evidence for this condition risks further neurological compromise.

### IV. Disposition

**OVERTURN**

### V. Warrant

The denial is OVERTURNED because HealthFirst PPO Gold's prior authorization denial relies on conservative treatment requirements that are inconsistent with California state law and generally accepted clinical standards for cervical myelopathy.

1.  **Physical Therapy (PT) Requirement Overridden by State Law:** The plan's requirement for further physical therapy is directly superseded by **California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(3)**. The member has objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (moderate central cord compression at C5-C6 with bilateral Hoffman signs and hyperreflexia) that physical therapy cannot address. Furthermore, the treating physician's decision to halt PT after 3 weeks due to "myelopathy concern" also satisfies **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1)**, indicating PT is unlikely to benefit. Therefore, the plan cannot lawfully deny coverage on the basis of incomplete physical therapy.

2.  **Interventional Procedure Requirement Waived by Plan Criteria:** The plan's own **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Exception)**, waives the interventional treatment requirement if the treating physician documents a risk of neurological deterioration. Dr. Osei explicitly documented that "further delay risks irreversible neurological deterioration," which reasonably satisfies this condition.

3.  **Pharmacotherapy Requirement Inconsistent with Clinical Standards:** While the plan requires a trial of two pharmacotherapy agents, clinical guidelines for cervical myelopathy (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) do not establish a mandatory minimum *number* of such agents. The general clinical understanding, as emphasized in the provided domain knowledge and clinical references, is that applying conservative treatment paradigms derived from radiculopathy to myelopathy constitutes a "category error," as myelopathy is a structural cord compression. Requiring a second, additional pharmacotherapy agent to further delay surgery for a structural issue causing myelopathy (even mild, but with objective UMN signs) after one agent has been trialed for 6 weeks with some benefit, is considered more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice and not supported by high-quality evidence for myelopathy. Per **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**, services meeting generally accepted standards must be approved regardless of more restrictive plan criteria.

Based on the highest authority (State Law) overriding the PT requirement, the plan's own criteria waiving the interventional requirement, and the lack of strong evidence in clinical standards supporting the specific multi-agent pharmacotherapy requirement for myelopathy, the denial is without sufficient medical necessity basis. The requested ACDF C5-C6 for mild cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and upper motor neuron signs is consistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice under these circumstances.
