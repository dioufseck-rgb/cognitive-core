## APPEAL DETERMINATION: PA-2024-D003

### I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The denial for prior authorization of an ACDF at C6-C7 for Ms. Jessica Turner is **OVERTURNED**.

The plan's denial is procedurally defective under California state law for failing to provide a written denial notice that specifies the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reason for the denial, and for failing to include notice of Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights. Furthermore, the plan's application of its conservative treatment requirements, specifically the physical therapy duration, is in direct conflict with California state law, which waives such requirements when a treating physician documents a functional plateau.

### II. CASE BACKGROUND

Ms. Jessica Turner, a 39-year-old member, requested prior authorization for a single-level ACDF at C6-C7 due to cervical radiculopathy. The plan denied the request following a peer-to-peer review, citing that "clinical criteria for surgery not met" and that detailed criteria were provided verbally during the call. The denial notice provided to the member did not include written criteria or IMR notice.

The member's clinical record supports a diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy C6-C7 with objective imaging (MRI showing C6-C7 disc herniation, right C7 nerve root compression, moderate foraminal stenosis) and neurological findings (right C7 dermatomal numbness/tingling, diminished tricep reflex, grip 4+/5). Conservative treatment included 5 weeks of physical therapy (with the treating physician declaring a plateau), 3 months of Meloxicam (partial relief), and 2 weeks of Prednisone (temporary improvement). No interventional procedures were completed.

### III. REVIEW ANALYSIS

This review applies the three-source hierarchy: **State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria**.

**1. Procedural Adequacy of Denial (State Law - CHSC § 1374.31(a) and (b))**

*   **Finding:** The plan's denial is procedurally defective. The denial was communicated verbally during a peer-to-peer call, and the member never received a written denial notice specifying the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial. Furthermore, the denial notice failed to include information regarding the member's right to an Independent Medical Review (IMR).
*   **Warrant:** California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a) explicitly mandates that when a plan issues a denial based on medical necessity, it **SHALL** provide written notice, include the specific criteria applied, the specific clinical reason for the denial, and notice of IMR rights. CHSC § 1374.31(b) further clarifies that a denial notice failing to specify the clinical criteria or reason is "DEFECTIVE." A verbal communication during a peer-to-peer discussion does not satisfy these statutory requirements. This procedural defect, by itself, is sufficient grounds to overturn the denial.

**2. Application of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)**

Assuming, for the sake of completeness, that the procedural defect is set aside, we assess the denial against the plan's criteria:

*   **Diagnosis Requirement (2.1):** Met. The member has a confirmed diagnosis of Cervical Radiculopathy C6-C7 with objective neurological findings and imaging consistent with nerve root compression.
*   **Imaging Requirement (2.3):** Met. MRI from 2024-09-01 (within 6 months) demonstrates structural pathology consistent with symptoms.
*   **Conservative Treatment Requirement (2.2):** This is where the plan's denial likely originated.
    *   **(a) Physical Therapy:** Plan requires a minimum of 6 weeks. Member completed 5 weeks. **Not met by plan criteria.**
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** Plan requires at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. Member completed 3 months of Meloxicam (met) but only 2 weeks of Prednisone (not met for duration). **Partially met by plan criteria.**
    *   **(c) Interventional Treatment:** Plan requires one interventional procedure. None completed. **Not met by plan criteria.**
*   **Myelopathy Exception (2.2(A)):** Not applicable. The primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, and there is no documentation of cervical myelopathy (e.g., bilateral symptoms, upper motor neuron signs, myelomalacia) in the clinical record.

**Conclusion under Plan Criteria (alone):** If strictly applying the plan's criteria, the conservative treatment requirements (PT duration, full pharmacotherapy, interventional treatment) were not entirely met.

**3. Application of California Regulatory Requirements (State Law)**

*   **Physical Therapy Requirement (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)):**
    *   **Finding:** The plan's denial based on the member not completing 6 weeks of PT is superseded by state law. The treating physician documented that the member reached a functional plateau after 5 weeks of physical therapy.
    *   **Warrant:** California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2) explicitly states that a health insurer **shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau.** The plan's 6-week minimum is overridden by this statutory provision. Therefore, the PT requirement has been met as per state law.
*   **DMHC APL 22-014 & CHSC § 1374.32(b) (Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard):** These provisions generally state that plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice, and that services meeting generally accepted standards shall be approved regardless of more restrictive plan criteria. While DMHC APL 22-014 primarily focuses on myelopathy, its general principle regarding restrictive criteria is relevant.

**4. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)**

*   **Diagnosis:** The member's presentation is consistent with Cervical Radiculopathy C6-C7 (nerve root compression with unilateral symptoms and corresponding neurological deficits).
*   **Conservative Treatment for Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines for radiculopathy (e.g., AANS/CNS Tier 2) suggest surgery as a reasonable option for "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx."
    *   The member has completed 5 weeks of PT (with a physician-documented plateau, which is a key clinical indicator of conservative treatment failure for that modality) and 3 months of an NSAID (Meloxicam, with partial relief). While Prednisone was only for 2 weeks and no interventional procedure was performed, the combination of conservative measures attempted, especially with persistent objective neurological deficits from nerve root compression, aligns with an "adequate conservative trial" as understood by generally accepted medical practice when statutory waivers for PT duration are considered.
    *   The absence of specific mandates in the clinical guidelines for *every single component* of conservative treatment (e.g., two distinct pharmacotherapy agents for specific durations, *and* an interventional procedure) suggests that the plan's stringent requirements, particularly after a physician-declared PT plateau and significant duration of pharmacotherapy, may be more restrictive than the general clinical standard. The overall clinical picture, especially with the state law override for PT, suggests that the member has undergone a reasonable and failed conservative trial for radiculopathy.

### IV. CONFLICT RESOLUTION (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria)

1.  **Procedural Defect:** The most significant and immediate conflict is the plan's failure to issue a procedurally adequate denial notice. **State law (CHSC § 1374.31) is paramount.** This defect alone warrants an overturn.
2.  **Physical Therapy:** The plan's 6-week PT minimum is directly overridden by **State law (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2))** because the treating physician documented a functional plateau after 5 weeks.
3.  **Remaining Conservative Treatment (Pharmacotherapy & Interventional):** While the member did not strictly meet all plan criteria for pharmacotherapy duration and interventional procedures, the application of **State law (CHSC § 1374.32(b) and DMHC APL 22-014)** dictates that the service should be approved if it meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, even if the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. Given the objective neurological findings, structural compromise on imaging, and documented failure of conservative measures (including a statutorily recognized PT plateau and significant pharmacotherapy), proceeding with surgery is consistent with generally accepted clinical standards for radiculopathy where symptoms persist after a reasonable conservative trial. The specific, multi-layered requirements of the plan for *all* conservative modalities may be considered more restrictive than generally accepted practice in this context.

### V. DISPOSITION

**OVERTURN**

### VI. WARRANT

The prior authorization denial for Jessica Turner's ACDF at C6-C7 is OVERTURNED for the following reasons:

1.  **Procedural Defect:** The denial is procedurally defective and invalid under California state law. The plan failed to provide a written denial notice that specified the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reason for the denial, and also failed to include notice of the member's right to Independent Medical Review (IMR), in direct violation of **California Health & Safety Code § 1374.31(a)(2) and (b)**. A verbal peer-to-peer discussion cannot substitute for a legally required written denial.
2.  **Physical Therapy Requirement Overridden by State Law:** The plan's denial, in part, was based on the member not completing a full 6-week course of physical therapy. However, the treating physician documented that the member reached a functional plateau after 5 weeks of PT. **California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2)** explicitly prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of surgery on the completion of physical therapy when the treating physician documents such a plateau. This state law overrides the plan's minimum duration requirement.
3.  **Alignment with Clinical Standards Despite Minor Deviations from Plan Criteria:** While the member did not strictly meet every conservative treatment component of the plan's criteria (specifically, the duration of Prednisone and the lack of an interventional procedure), the overall clinical picture, combined with the state law override for the physical therapy requirement, supports the medical necessity of the requested procedure. The member presents with objective neurological findings and imaging evidence of structural nerve root compression, and has undergone a reasonable trial of conservative management which the physician has deemed to have plateaued. **CHSC § 1374.32(b)** and **DMHC APL 22-014 (II.3)** affirm that services meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice shall be approved, even if a plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. For persistent radiculopathy with objective findings, surgical intervention is considered a reasonable option after 6-12 weeks of failed conservative treatment, which Ms. Turner has effectively met or exceeded considering the physician-declared plateau and chronic symptoms.
