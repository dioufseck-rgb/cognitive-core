### DECISION RATIONALE

This appeal review determines that the denial for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C6-C7, one level) for Jessica Turner must be **OVERTURNED**. This decision is based on a critical procedural defect in the denial process, which violates California state law, as well as an analysis of the clinical record against state law and generally accepted clinical standards, which override certain aspects of the plan's internal criteria.

**1. Procedural Defect in Denial Notice (State Law - CHSC § 1374.31)**

The initial denial issued by HealthFirst Health Plan is procedurally defective. The denial states that the "Detailed criteria review provided verbally during peer-to-peer call" and "written_denial_basis" was "Verbal only — peer-to-peer call." Furthermore, the "imr_notice_included" field explicitly states "false."

California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a) mandates that when a plan issues a denial based on medical necessity, it **SHALL** provide written notice that includes:
*   The specific criteria applied.
*   The specific clinical reason for the denial.
*   Notice of Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights.

CHSC § 1374.31(b) further clarifies that "A denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is DEFECTIVE and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."

In this case, HealthFirst Health Plan failed to provide a written denial notice, verbally communicated the criteria, and did not include notice of IMR rights. This constitutes a clear and unequivocal violation of state law. A denial that is procedurally defective under state law is invalid, and therefore, the initial request for prior authorization should be treated as if no denial was issued. This procedural violation alone warrants an overturn of the denial.

**2. Clinical Review and Application of Hierarchy (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria)**

Even if the procedural defect were not present, a substantive review of the denial against the three-source hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria) indicates that the denial should be overturned.

**a. Primary Clinical Presentation: Cervical Radiculopathy**
The clinical record documents a "presenting_diagnosis" of Cervical radiculopathy C6-C7. Imaging (MRI 2024-09-01) shows "C6-C7 disc herniation, right C7 nerve root compression, moderate foraminal stenosis." The neurological exam (2024-09-05) reveals "Right C7 dermatomal numbness and tingling, tricep reflex diminished, grip 4+/5." These findings are classic for cervical radiculopathy and do not include signs of cervical myelopathy (e.g., upper motor neuron signs, bilateral symptoms, myelomalacia). Therefore, the plan's myelopathy exception (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A) Note) is not applicable.

**b. Physical Therapy Requirement (Plan Criteria vs. State Law)**
*   **Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a)):** Requires a "minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy." The member completed 5 weeks of PT.
*   **State Law (California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3)):** This state law explicitly states that a health insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
*   **Application:** The clinical record demonstrates objective evidence of structural neurological compromise: C6-C7 disc herniation with right C7 nerve root compression and moderate foraminal stenosis, directly corresponding to the objective neurological deficits (diminished tricep reflex, dermatomal numbness/tingling). Physical therapy cannot reverse this structural compression. Therefore, under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), HealthFirst cannot deny authorization solely on the basis that the member completed 5 weeks of PT instead of the required 6 weeks, especially as the treating physician declared a plateau. State law overrides the plan's specific duration requirement for PT in this instance.

**c. Pharmacotherapy and Interventional Treatment Requirements (Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard)**
*   **Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(b) & (c)):** Requires a trial of at least two pharmacotherapy agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each, and one interventional treatment. The member completed 3 months of Meloxicam (NSAID) but only 2 weeks of Prednisone (corticosteroid), meaning only one pharmacotherapy agent met the 4-week duration. No interventional treatment was performed.
*   **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023, Section 3.1 TIER 2(b)):** Indicates that surgery is a "REASONABLE INDICATION" for "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx."
*   **Application:** While the plan's specific criteria for pharmacotherapy and interventional procedures were not strictly met, the member did undergo a significant course of conservative management including 5 weeks of PT (which, as discussed, satisfies the requirement under state law) and 3 months of an NSAID (Meloxicam), with persistent objective neurological deficits. The overall duration of conservative treatment (PT + 3 months NSAID) exceeds the 6-12 week timeframe generally indicated by clinical standards for considering surgical intervention for radiculopathy.
    California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) states that the Independent Medical Review standard (which guides appeals decisions) does **NOT** require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice," it **SHALL** be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. Given the persistent objective neurological symptoms and a substantial trial of conservative management, the plan's specific, more granular requirements for the *types* of conservative modalities may be considered more restrictive than generally accepted clinical standards which broadly consider "6-12 weeks conservative tx" as a threshold for surgical consideration in radiculopathy cases.

**3. Conclusion**

The denial for CPT 22551 for Jessica Turner is **OVERTURNED**. This determination is primarily based on the clear violation of California Health & Safety Code § 1374.31 regarding the requirement for a written, detailed, and compliant denial notice. Such a defect renders the denial invalid.

Furthermore, a substantive review supports overturn. California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(3) overrides the plan's strict 6-week physical therapy requirement due to the documented objective structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. While the plan's other specific conservative treatment criteria (pharmacotherapy and interventional procedures) were not fully met, the overall duration and attempt at conservative management, coupled with persistent objective neurological deficits, align with generally accepted clinical standards for considering surgical intervention for radiculopathy, consistent with CHSC § 1374.32(b).
