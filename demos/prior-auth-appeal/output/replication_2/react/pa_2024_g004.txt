**I. BACKGROUND OF DENIAL**

The request for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 was initially denied by HealthFirst on October 15, 2024. The denial, reviewed by Dr. Maria Rivera, cited that imaging showed mild-moderate disc herniation with no cord compression and no neurological deficit on examination, and that the conservative treatment pathway had not been completed, therefore not meeting medical necessity criteria for surgical intervention at this time.

The appeal, submitted by Dr. Walsh, disputes Dr. Rivera's interpretation, emphasizes Dr. Walsh's 25 years of spine surgery experience and direct patient examination, and warns of permanent disability without immediate authorization.

---

**II. CLINICAL RECORD REVIEW SUMMARY**

*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Cervical radiculopathy C5-C6, right-sided.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-10):** Shows C5-C6 mild to moderate disc herniation, right C6 nerve root contact *without measurable compression*. Crucially, the MRI indicates *no cord contact, no cord compression, no T2 signal change, and no myelomalacia*. There is mild right foraminal narrowing.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-09-15):** Documents right shoulder and neck pain (6/10) and *mild* right C6 dermatomal tingling. However, the exam explicitly notes *no weakness, no upper motor neuron (UMN) signs*, and *symmetric 2+ bicep reflexes* with *5/5 grip strength bilateral*. Functional assessment indicates the patient is *working full-time with no activity limitations*.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy (PT):** 4 weeks, 12 sessions. Noted "Mild improvement in pain. No neurological deficit to track." Dr. Walsh elected to halt PT.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Naproxen 500mg BID for 8 weeks, with "moderate pain relief".
    *   **Interventional:** None.
*   **Treating Surgeon Statement:** Dr. Walsh asserts the patient requires surgery now, disputes Dr. Rivera's review, and states his clinical judgment as treating surgeon must take precedence to prevent permanent disability.

---

**III. APPEAL ANALYSIS USING HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY**
*(State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria)*

**1. Primary Clinical Presentation:**
The clinical record overwhelmingly supports a diagnosis of **cervical radiculopathy**, not cervical myelopathy.
*   The MRI explicitly states "no cord compression" and "no T2 signal change" (myelomalacia).
*   The neurological exam notes "no weakness" and "no UMN signs" (such as Hoffman's sign or hyperreflexia), which are hallmarks of myelopathy.
*   The symptoms are described as unilateral dermatomal tingling and pain, consistent with radiculopathy.
*(Source: Clinical Record, Domain Knowledge - Classification, Clinical Standard Section 1.1)*

This distinction is critical as myelopathy and radiculopathy have different treatment algorithms and requirements for conservative therapy. The plan's reviewer correctly identified the absence of cord compression and neurological deficits associated with myelopathy in the initial denial.

**2. Application of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
The requested procedure (22551) is subject to HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria for cervical spinal surgery.
*   **2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** The diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy is present.
*   **2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:** The member has *not* met the standard conservative treatment requirements for radiculopathy:
    *   **Physical Therapy:** Only 4 weeks of PT were completed, falling short of the required minimum of 6 weeks. *(Source: HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a), Clinical Record)*
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Only one pharmacotherapy agent (Naproxen) was trialed for 8 weeks. The criteria require a trial of *at least two* agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. *(Source: HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b), Clinical Record)*
    *   **Interventional Treatment:** No interventional treatments (e.g., epidural steroid injection) were performed, which is a required component of the conservative pathway. *(Source: HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(c), Clinical Record)*
*   **Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A):** This exception to conservative treatment requirements does *not* apply because the member does not have a confirmed primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy. The objective findings of "no cord compression" and "no myelomalacia" further confirm that the myelopathy exception is not applicable. *(Source: HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(A) NOTE, Clinical Record)*

Based on plan criteria, the request fails to meet the conservative treatment requirements.

**3. Application of California Regulatory Requirements:**
*   **CHSC § 1374.31(b) (Denial Notice Adequacy):** The denial notice specified the reason (MED-003, conservative treatment not completed, no cord compression/neurological deficit) and implicitly referred to medical necessity criteria. This notice is procedurally adequate.
*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute prohibits conditioning surgery on PT completion where specific conditions are met:
    *   **(a)(1) PT contraindicated or unlikely to benefit:** Dr. Walsh *halted* PT, but the record does not document that it was explicitly contraindicated or unlikely to benefit for radiculopathy. Mild pain improvement was noted.
    *   **(a)(2) Functional Plateau:** While 4 weeks of PT showed mild improvement in pain and "no neurological deficit to track," there is no therapist documentation of a "functional plateau" as defined by clinical standards (<10% improvement over 3 sessions or maximum benefit reached). The PT was halted prematurely by the surgeon rather than reaching a documented plateau.
    *   **(a)(3) Objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address:** This provision typically applies to severe, progressive neurological compromise like myelopathy with myelomalacia. The clinical record for this member shows *no cord compression, no myelomalacia, no weakness, and no UMN signs*, meaning this provision does not apply to her current presentation of mild radiculopathy.
    *(Source: CIC § 10169.5, Clinical Record)*

*   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This All-Plan Letter specifically addresses prior authorization for "Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions" like cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia, which may warrant waiving conservative treatment. As established, this member does *not* have myelopathy or myelomalacia. Therefore, this APL does not apply to this case. *(Source: DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.1, II.2, Clinical Record)*

**4. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
*   **Radiculopathy Treatment:** Generally accepted clinical guidelines differentiate radiculopathy from myelopathy. For radiculopathy, conservative treatment is often effective, and surgical consideration typically occurs after 6-12 weeks of such treatment. *(Source: Clinical Standard Section 1.1, 3.1 Tier 2)*
*   **Conservative Treatment Duration:** The clinical evidence base for radiculopathy supports a conservative trial of 6-12 weeks. The plan's 6-week PT requirement for radiculopathy aligns with this. The patient completed only 4 weeks of PT, only one of two required pharmacotherapy agents, and no interventional treatment. This falls short of generally accepted conservative treatment pathways for radiculopathy before considering surgery. *(Source: Clinical Standard Section 2.2, 3.1 Tier 2)*
*   **Urgency:** The clinical standard highlights surgical urgency for *moderate-severe myelopathy, especially with myelomalacia*. This patient does not have these indicators (no myelomalacia, no cord compression, no weakness, no UMN signs, working full-time). The risk of delay highlighted in guidelines does not apply to this mild radiculopathy presentation. *(Source: Clinical Standard Section 1.2, 2.3, 3.2)*
*   **Treating Surgeon's Judgment:** While the treating surgeon's direct examination and experience are valued, their clinical judgment must be supported by objective findings and generally accepted standards of care. Dr. Walsh's assertions of "significant cervical pathology" and risk of "permanent disability" are not supported by the objective MRI findings (no cord compression, no myelomalacia) or neurological exam (no weakness, no UMN signs) which clearly indicate radiculopathy, not myelopathy. His statement, in this context, does not provide new clinical evidence that would override the established clinical criteria for surgery.

**5. Conflict Resolution:**
In this case, there is no conflict. The higher authorities (California state law and generally accepted clinical standards) do not override the plan's criteria for this specific clinical presentation. Instead, they reinforce the necessity for a more complete trial of conservative management for cervical radiculopathy without features of myelopathy or severe, progressive neurological compromise.

---

**IV. CONCLUSION**

The member's clinical presentation, specifically cervical radiculopathy without objective signs of cord compression, myelomalacia, or significant neurological deficits (such as weakness or UMN signs), does not warrant bypassing the standard conservative treatment pathway. The treating surgeon's strong appeal, while noting concerns for permanent disability, does not introduce new objective clinical evidence to contradict the findings of mild radiculopathy or to support an urgent surgical indication.

The conservative treatment completed (4 weeks PT, one pharmacotherapy agent, no interventional procedures) falls short of both HealthFirst's plan criteria (6 weeks PT, two pharmacotherapy agents, one interventional procedure) and generally accepted clinical standards which recommend 6-12 weeks of conservative therapy for radiculopathy prior to surgical consideration. California state laws regarding waiver of conservative treatment due to neurological urgency (CIC 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) are not applicable given the absence of myelopathy or severe, progressive neurological compromise.

Therefore, the initial denial for lack of completed conservative treatment is consistent with the member's clinical presentation, HealthFirst's plan criteria, relevant state regulations, and generally accepted clinical standards.

---

**V. DISPOSITION**

The prior authorization denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) is **UPHELD**.

**Warrant:** The denial is upheld because the member's diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy does not meet the criteria for urgent surgical intervention that would waive conservative treatment requirements. The clinical record confirms the absence of myelopathy, cord compression, myelomalacia, weakness, or upper motor neuron signs. The member has not completed the required duration and components of conservative treatment as outlined in the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2. Neither California state law (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) nor generally accepted clinical standards for radiculopathy (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine Guidelines) support waiving conservative treatment requirements for this specific clinical presentation. The treating surgeon's statement, while asserting clinical judgment, does not provide objective clinical evidence to justify immediate surgery in the context of mild radiculopathy without severe neurological compromise.

**Recommended Action:** The member should complete the full conservative treatment pathway as outlined in HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2, for cervical radiculopathy, and may resubmit a prior authorization request for surgical intervention upon completion.
