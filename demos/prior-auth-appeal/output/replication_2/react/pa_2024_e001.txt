### I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The initial denial for a two-level ACDF at C5-C6 and C6-C7 is **OVERTURNED IN PART**.

Based on a comprehensive review of the submitted clinical documentation, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory guidance, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, the Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at **C5-C6 is deemed medically necessary**. However, surgical intervention at **C6-C7 is not supported by the clinical evidence or current medical standards**.

Therefore, the denial of the **two-level fusion is overturned to authorize a one-level ACDF at C5-C6**. The request for the second level (C6-C7) is upheld as not medically necessary.

---

### II. CLINICAL BACKGROUND & DENIAL REASONING

Mr. Patel is a 60-year-old male diagnosed with two-level cervical disc disease at C5-C6 and C6-C7, with asymmetric severity.

**Clinical Findings for C5-C6:**
*   **Imaging (MRI 09/15/2024):** Large disc herniation, moderate-severe right C6 nerve root compression, foraminal stenosis.
*   **Neurological Exam (09/20/2024):** Right bicep weakness 3+/5, absent right bicep reflex, right C6 dermatomal numbness and tingling.
*   **Conservative Treatment:** Completed 8 weeks of physical therapy (symptoms persist), 4 months of Pregabalin (partial C6 relief), 3 months of Naproxen (moderate relief), and a right C6 epidural steroid injection (70% relief, returned in 4 weeks).

**Clinical Findings for C6-C7:**
*   **Imaging (MRI 09/15/2024):** Small disc bulge, mild foraminal narrowing, no nerve root compression identifiable, no cord contact.
*   **Neurological Exam (09/20/2024):** Right tricep reflex slightly diminished. No definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms. Physician notes "mild C7 symptoms possibly present but not clearly documented."
*   **Conservative Treatment:** Physical therapy notes indicated C7 symptoms were "minimal and possibly resolving per PT note at week 8." No C7-specific interventional treatment was performed.

The initial denial correctly identified that the C5-C6 level met surgical criteria but denied the two-level fusion due to the C6-C7 level showing "mild changes only" without sufficient support for surgical intervention. The appeal asserts that both levels are symptomatic and meet full criteria, and that adjacent levels should be addressed simultaneously.

---

### III. REVIEW ANALYSIS & APPLICATION OF HIERARCHY

The appeal was reviewed using the established hierarchy of authority: **State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.**

**1. Assessment against HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

*   **C5-C6 Level:**
    *   **2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** Member has confirmed cervical radiculopathy (2.1a) with objective neurological deficit (2.1c) (C6 nerve root compression, weakness, absent reflex, dermatomal symptoms). This criterion is **MET**.
    *   **2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:**
        *   Physical therapy: 8 weeks completed, meeting the 6-week minimum (2.2a).
        *   Pharmacotherapy: Trial of Pregabalin (4 months) and Naproxen (3 months) meets the 2 agents, 4-week minimum (2.2b).
        *   Interventional treatment: Right C6 epidural steroid injection performed (2.2c).
        *   Conservative treatment for C5-C6 is deemed **MET and FAILED** to resolve symptoms.
    *   **2.3 Imaging Requirement:** MRI demonstrates structural pathology (large disc herniation, moderate-severe C6 nerve root compression) consistent with symptoms. This criterion is **MET**.
    *   **Conclusion for C5-C6:** This level meets all of HealthFirst's criteria for surgical authorization.

*   **C6-C7 Level:**
    *   **2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:**
        *   Cervical radiculopathy (2.1a): MRI shows "no nerve root compression identifiable," and neurological exam states "no definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms." This criterion is **NOT MET**.
        *   Cervical myelopathy (2.1b): No signs of myelopathy (e.g., cord compression, upper motor neuron signs) are documented. This criterion is **NOT MET**.
        *   Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit (2.1c): The "small disc bulge," "mild foraminal narrowing," and "slightly diminished tricep reflex" without definitive dermatomal symptoms or nerve root compression do not constitute an objective neurological deficit requiring surgery under these criteria. This criterion is **NOT MET**.
    *   **2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:** While 8 weeks of PT was completed, and pharmacotherapy requirements were met, no C7-specific interventional treatment (2.2c) was performed. Even if this were performed, the lack of a definitive diagnosis makes conservative treatment adequacy less relevant. The Myelopathy Exception (2.2A) does not apply as myelopathy is not diagnosed.
    *   **2.3 Imaging Requirement:** Imaging shows "mild changes only" with "no nerve root compression identifiable," which is not consistent with the need for surgical intervention given the lack of definitive clinical correlation. This criterion is **NOT MET**.
    *   **Conclusion for C6-C7:** This level does not meet HealthFirst's criteria for surgical authorization.

**2. Assessment against California Regulatory Framework:**

*   **CHSC § 1374.31(b) (Denial notice adequacy):** The initial denial notice specified a clinical reason ("C6-C7 shows mild changes only, two-level fusion not approved when one level meets criteria and one does not"), which is procedurally adequate.
*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Prerequisite):**
    *   For C5-C6: Mr. Patel completed 8 weeks of PT, and symptoms persisted, indicating a failure of conservative therapy. The PT requirement was met and did not adequately resolve symptoms.
    *   For C6-C7: The "minimal and possibly resolving" C7 symptoms per PT notes suggest that conservative treatment was either effective or that the symptoms were not significant enough to warrant further therapy or surgery. This section does not override the lack of a clear surgical indication for C6-C7.
*   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This guidance primarily addresses myelopathy with cord compression/myelomalacia. Since Mr. Patel's diagnosis is primarily radiculopathy for C5-C6 and very mild/non-definitive for C6-C7, and no myelopathy or myelomalacia is documented, this APL's provisions for waiving conservative treatment requirements for urgent myelopathy cases do **not apply** here.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR standard is NOT plan criteria):** This is the paramount standard: if a service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, it shall be approved regardless of the plan's internal criteria. This necessitates a review against clinical standards.

**3. Assessment against Clinical Standard Knowledge (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

*   **C5-C6 Level:**
    *   The clinical presentation (persistent, moderate-severe C6 radiculopathy with objective weakness, absent reflex, and corresponding disc herniation/nerve root compression on MRI, failed conservative treatment) falls under **TIER 1 or TIER 2(b) of the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023)**. Specifically, it meets "Progressive neurological deficit despite conservative treatment" (Tier 1b) or "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" (Tier 2b). The 8 weeks of PT and 4 months of pharmacotherapy, along with a C6 epidural, demonstrate an adequate and failed conservative trial for symptomatic radiculopathy.
    *   **Conclusion for C5-C6:** Surgical ACDF at C5-C6 is **medically necessary** under generally accepted clinical standards.

*   **C6-C7 Level:**
    *   The clinical record for C6-C7 shows "small disc bulge, mild foraminal narrowing, no nerve root compression identifiable, no cord contact" on MRI. The neurological exam shows a "slightly diminished tricep reflex" but "no definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms," with the physician noting "mild C7 symptoms possibly present but not clearly documented."
    *   These findings do **not meet TIER 1 or TIER 2 criteria** for surgical indication under AANS/CNS guidelines. There is no clear nerve root compression, no definitive radicular symptoms, and no myelopathy.
    *   The absence of identifiable nerve root compression and definitive symptoms places this level closer to **TIER 3(b)** "Imaging findings without correlating symptoms" or a condition that would not typically warrant surgical intervention. The argument for addressing adjacent levels simultaneously for "prevention of accelerated degeneration" is not a primary surgical indication for fusion when the adjacent level lacks definitive pathology and symptoms requiring surgical decompression.
    *   **Conclusion for C6-C7:** Surgical ACDF at C6-C7 is **not medically necessary** under generally accepted clinical standards.

**4. Conflict Resolution:**
For C5-C6, both HealthFirst's criteria and generally accepted clinical standards support surgical authorization.
For C6-C7, both HealthFirst's criteria and generally accepted clinical standards do *not* support surgical authorization.
There are no conflicts between the sources regarding the medical necessity of each individual level. The conflict arises from the denial of the *entire* two-level procedure when one level is clearly indicated.

---

### IV. DISPOSITION

**DISPOSITION: OVERTURN (IN PART)**

The initial denial of the two-level ACDF C5-C6 and C6-C7 (CPT 22551 + 22552) is **OVERTURNED**. HealthFirst Health Plan is directed to **APPROVE** authorization for a **one-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551)**. The request for the second level (C6-C7, CPT 22552) is **UPHELD as not medically necessary**.

---

### V. WARRANT

The denial of the two-level fusion is overturned in part because the C5-C6 level clearly demonstrates medical necessity, meeting both HealthFirst's plan criteria and generally accepted clinical standards for surgical intervention, which overrides any more restrictive plan language per CHSC § 1374.32(b).

1.  **C5-C6 Medical Necessity:** Mr. Patel presents with objective C6 radiculopathy (3+/5 right bicep weakness, absent right bicep reflex, C6 dermatomal symptoms) directly correlated with a large disc herniation and moderate-severe right C6 nerve root compression on MRI. He has completed a comprehensive course of conservative treatment (8 weeks of PT, 4 months of Pregabalin, 3 months of Naproxen, and a right C6 epidural) which has failed to provide sustained relief, with persistent symptoms. This presentation meets HealthFirst's criteria for cervical surgery (CC-SPINE-2024, Sections 2.1a, 2.1c, 2.2, 2.3) and is a strong indication for surgery per AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023 Update) Tier 1b ("Progressive neurological deficit despite conservative treatment") and Tier 2b ("Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx").

2.  **C6-C7 Not Medically Necessary:** The C6-C7 level, as per the clinical record, shows only "small disc bulge, mild foraminal narrowing" on MRI, with "no nerve root compression identifiable, no cord contact." The neurological exam noted only a "slightly diminished tricep reflex" and "no definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms." The treating physician's note of "mild C7 symptoms possibly present but not clearly documented" is insufficient to establish a definitive radiculopathy or objective neurological deficit requiring surgery. These findings do not meet HealthFirst's diagnostic or imaging criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Sections 2.1, 2.3) and are not supported by generally accepted clinical standards for surgical intervention for radiculopathy (AANS/CNS Guidelines 2023, Tier 3b: "Imaging findings without correlating symptoms"). Furthermore, the arguments regarding simultaneous treatment for adjacent level degeneration are not a primary surgical indication in the absence of definitive pathology and correlating symptoms.

Therefore, while a two-level fusion is not medically necessary, a one-level ACDF at C5-C6 is medically indicated and should be approved.
