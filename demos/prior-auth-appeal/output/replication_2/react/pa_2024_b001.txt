**DISPOSITION: PARTIALLY OVERTURNED**

The appeal for a two-level ACDF at C5-C6 and C6-C7 is **PARTIALLY OVERTURNED.**

*   **ACDF C6-C7 is DENIED** as not medically necessary.

**WARRANT AND RATIONALE:**

This appeal determination is based on a comprehensive review of the submitted clinical documentation, HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, California state law (CIC, CHSC, DMHC APL), and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine guidelines). The review follows the established hierarchy of authority: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.

**1. Clinical Presentation Assessment:**
The member, Linda Park, presents with a clear clinical picture of **right C6 cervical radiculopathy.**
*   **Symptoms:** Right C6 dermatomal numbness and tingling, diminished bicep reflex, and 4/5 right grip strength.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-01):** C5-C6 shows a large disc herniation with moderate-severe nerve root compression and moderate foraminal stenosis, clearly correlating with C6 radicular symptoms.
*   **Absence of Myelopathy:** Crucially, the clinical record documents *no C7 findings, no bilateral symptoms, and no upper motor neuron signs* (such as Hoffman's sign or hyperreflexia). Imaging demonstrates *no spinal cord compression and no myelomalacia (cord signal change)*. Therefore, the primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, not cervical myelopathy. This distinction is critical as it renders myelopathy-specific exceptions in plan criteria and regulatory guidance inapplicable.

**2. Review of ACDF C5-C6 (Approval Rationale):**

Based on the evidence, the proposed ACDF at C5-C6 meets the criteria for medical necessity under HealthFirst's plan criteria, California state law, and generally accepted clinical standards.

*   **A. HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
    *   **2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** The member meets criterion 2.1(a) "Cervical radiculopathy – nerve root compression with radicular symptoms" and 2.1(c) "Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit." The MRI clearly demonstrates structural pathology consistent with C6 radiculopathy (moderate-severe nerve root compression). **(MET)**
    *   **2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:** The member has completed a documented course of conservative treatment:
        *   **Physical Therapy:** 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised PT with persistent C6 radicular symptoms. This exceeds the minimum 6 weeks requirement. **(MET)**
        *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Gabapentin (4 months) and Cyclobenzaprine (6 weeks). Both trials exceed the 4-week minimum for at least two agents. **(MET)**
        *   **Interventional Treatment:** A right C6 epidural steroid injection provided only 50% temporary relief, which fully returned by week 4. **(MET)**
        *   **Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A):** This exception is not applicable as the primary diagnosis is radiculopathy, not myelopathy. However, standard conservative requirements *have been met*.
    *   **2.3 Imaging Requirement:** The MRI findings at C5-C6 (large disc herniation, moderate-severe nerve root compression) are consistent with the member's documented C6 radicular symptoms. **(MET)**
    *   **2.4 Physician Documentation:** The surgeon has provided clinical rationale, attested to conservative treatment completion, and described functional limitations. **(MET)**

*   **B. California State Law:**
    *   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** Even if the duration of PT was in question, this statute prohibits denial where (a)(2) the member has reached a functional plateau (C6 symptoms persist after 8 weeks of PT), or (a)(3) the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address (large disc herniation with nerve root compression and objective deficit). **(Supports Approval)**
    *   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This APL is specific to myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia, which are not present in this case. Therefore, the APL's provisions for waiving conservative treatment requirements do not apply.
    *   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This standard mandates approval if the service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, even if plan criteria are more restrictive. As detailed below, clinical standards support the C5-C6 level.

*   **C. Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
    *   The AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) identify ACDF as a "TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION" for "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx." The member has completed 8 weeks of PT, 4 months of gabapentin, 6 weeks of cyclobenzaprine, and one epidural, with persistent C6 radicular symptoms. This aligns with generally accepted clinical standards for surgical intervention for symptomatic cervical radiculopathy refractory to adequate conservative management. **(Supports Approval)**

**3. Review of ACDF C6-C7 (Denial Rationale):**

The denial of ACDF at C6-C7 is upheld because the procedure does not meet HealthFirst's plan criteria or generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice for medical necessity.

*   **A. HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
    *   **2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** The clinical record states "No C7 findings documented" on neurological exam. The MRI shows "mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing, no cord contact, no nerve root compression identifiable" at C6-C7. There is no confirmed diagnosis of radiculopathy or myelopathy attributable to C6-C7. **(NOT MET)**
    *   **2.3 Imaging Requirement:** Imaging at C6-C7 demonstrates only "mild degenerative changes" that are *not consistent with the member's symptoms* because no C7 symptoms are present. **(NOT MET)**
    *   **3.3 Exclusions:** The exclusion "Imaging does not demonstrate pathology at the proposed surgical level consistent with clinical presentation" applies to the C6-C7 level. **(APPLIES)**
    *   **Surgeon's Rationale:** Dr. Wilson's rationale for addressing C6-C7 is "prophylactically stabilize... to prevent adjacent segment disease." HealthFirst's criteria do not include prophylactic fusion for adjacent segment disease prevention as a medically necessary indication for ACDF.

*   **B. California State Law (CHSC § 1374.32(b)):**
    *   While the IMR standard requires approval if a service meets generally accepted standards, prophylactic fusion for adjacent segment disease in the absence of symptomatic pathology is *not* a generally accepted standard for medical necessity, as detailed below.

*   **C. Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
    *   The AANS/CNS guidelines classify "Imaging findings without correlating symptoms" as a "TIER 3 — SURGERY NOT INDICATED." The C6-C7 level has imaging findings (mild degenerative changes) but no correlating C7 symptoms or objective neurological deficit.
    *   Generally accepted clinical guidelines for spinal surgery focus on addressing existing symptomatic pathology or severe myelopathy. Prophylactic fusion to prevent adjacent segment disease, while a theoretical concern, is not considered a medically necessary indication for fusion in the absence of current compression or neurological deficit at the level in question. The evidence does not support mandatory prophylactic fusion. **(Does Not Support Approval)**

**4. Conclusion:**

The initial denial was for a *two-level* ACDF. While the C5-C6 level is clearly medically necessary and meets all plan criteria, state law, and clinical standards, the C6-C7 level does not demonstrate medical necessity based on current symptoms, neurological findings, or imaging that correlates with any C7 pathology. The prophylactic rationale for the C6-C7 level is not supported by current medical necessity guidelines.

Therefore, the appeal is **PARTIALLY OVERTURNED** to approve ACDF C5-C6 and **UPHELD** for ACDF C6-C7.
