**DISPOSITION: OVERTURNED**

---

**WARRANT AND RATIONALE:**

Upon review of the submitted prior authorization appeal, clinical record, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, California regulatory framework (CIC, CHSC, DMHC APL), and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, the denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level) for member Theresa Kamau is hereby **OVERTURNED**.

The initial denial by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, dated November 20, 2024, cited "MED-003: Request denied. Member has a prior authorization denial history for cervical spine surgery (three-level fusion denied May 2024). Review of current request in context of prior denial pattern suggests the member's cervical pathology has not materially changed since the prior denial. Current request does not demonstrate sufficient change in clinical status to warrant reconsideration." This denial contains multiple procedural and clinical flaws as detailed below:

**1. Procedural Defect in Denial Notice (State Law Override):**
*   **CHSC § 1374.31(b) (California Regulatory Framework):** States that a denial notice is defective if it fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial.
*   **Analysis:** The plan's denial explicitly notes internally that "Prior PA history — NOT a valid criterion under CC-SPINE-2024" and `denial_is_procedurally_flawed: true`. The denial basis cited by HealthFirst Utilization Management ("prior authorization denial history," "prior denial pattern," "insufficient change in clinical status to warrant reconsideration") is not a legitimate criterion for medical necessity within HealthFirst's own published clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024). A plan cannot deny a current request based solely on the history of a prior, different request. This constitutes a procedurally defective denial under CHSC § 1374.31(b), which alone is grounds for overturning.

**2. Member Meets HealthFirst's Own Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
Despite the procedural flaw, a thorough review confirms the member meets all outlined criteria:
*   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy C5-C6 with objective neurological deficit. The current MRI (October 2024) demonstrates a large disc herniation with moderate-severe right C6 nerve root compression, consistent with symptoms of right C6 dermatomal numbness and bicep weakness (3+/5). This fulfills criterion 2.1(a) and 2.1(c).
*   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:** The member has completed a documented and adequate course of conservative treatment:
    *   **(a) Physical Therapy:** A NEW 8-week course of supervised physical therapy was completed (July-September 2024), exceeding the 6-week minimum required by CC-SPINE-2024. Crucially, the therapist explicitly documented a "functional plateau: 'no further improvement expected, neurological deficit persistent and worsening.'"
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** The member completed trials of Gabapentin (6 months) and two courses of Methylprednisolone, meeting the requirement for two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
    *   **(c) Interventional Treatment:** A right C6 epidural steroid injection was performed (November 2024).
    *   **Conclusion:** All components of the conservative treatment requirement under CC-SPINE-2024, applicable to radiculopathy, have been met.
*   **2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:** Current MRI (October 2024) is within 6 months and demonstrates pathology (C5-C6 large disc herniation, severe C6 nerve root compression, new foraminal stenosis) consistent with symptoms and the proposed surgical level.
*   **2.4 PHYSICIAN DOCUMENTATION:** The appeal basis and clinical record imply the treating physician has provided the necessary attestations and rationale for surgery.

**3. State Law Overrides Regarding Physical Therapy (CIC § 10169.5):**
*   **CIC § 10169.5 (a)(2):** A health insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
*   **Analysis:** The member completed 8 weeks of PT, and a functional plateau was explicitly documented by the therapist. This documentation, indicating no further improvement expected and persistent/worsening neurological deficit, definitively satisfies CIC § 10169.5 (a)(2). This state law overrides any mechanical application of a PT duration requirement if a plateau is reached, further reinforcing that the conservative treatment requirement has been adequately addressed.
*   **DMHC APL 22-014 (II.2):** While not a primary myelopathy case, the MRI shows "interval increase in C5-C6 herniation size" and the neurological exam notes "interval worsening" of weakness. This constitutes objective evidence of progressive pathology. Combined with documented failure of conservative treatment, this guidance supports that full, extended conservative treatment requirements should not be a barrier to medically necessary surgery.

**4. Alignment with Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (CHSC § 1374.32(b), AANS/CNS 2023):**
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b):** The Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard requires approval if a service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice," regardless of the plan's internal criteria if they are more restrictive.
*   **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) and NASS Guidelines (2020):** These guidelines identify Tier 1 Strong Indications for surgery including "Progressive neurological deficit despite conservative treatment" and "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care."
*   **Analysis:** Ms. Kamau presents with objective evidence of progressive C5-C6 radiculopathy (interval increase in herniation size, interval worsening of C6 weakness from 3+/5). Her symptoms (8/10 pain at rest, unable to work full-time) demonstrate significant functional impairment despite a robust trial of conservative therapy. The documented functional plateau after 8 weeks of PT and temporary relief from other modalities confirms conservative treatment failure. This clinical scenario meets the "strong indication" for surgical intervention per generally accepted clinical guidelines.

**5. Material Change in Clinical Status:**
*   The plan's denial asserts that the member's "cervical pathology has not materially changed since the prior denial" (May 2024). This is directly contradicted by the clinical record:
    *   New MRI (October 2024) shows "interval increase in C5-C6 herniation size" and "new foraminal stenosis at C5-C6."
    *   Neurological exam (October 2024) notes "Bicep weakness 3+/5 right — interval worsening from prior exam."
    *   A new, distinct 8-week PT course was completed, along with a new injection, since the prior denial.
*   Furthermore, the prior denial was for a *three-level* fusion, which is a significantly different procedure from the current *single-level* ACDF C5-C6 request. Denying a current, distinct, and clinically supported request based on a prior request for a different procedure and an unsubstantiated claim of "no material change" is inappropriate and inaccurate.

**CONCLUSION:**

The denial for CPT 22551 for Theresa Kamau is procedurally defective under California law (CHSC § 1374.31(b)) as it relies on an invalid criterion not found in the plan's own policy. Furthermore, the member fully meets HealthFirst's published medical necessity criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), has satisfied conservative treatment requirements (including a documented functional plateau as per CIC § 10169.5), and presents with a progressive neurological deficit that constitutes a strong indication for surgery under generally accepted clinical standards. The plan's assertion of "no material change" is factually incorrect based on the provided clinical evidence.

Therefore, the prior authorization for ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is medically necessary and is **OVERTURNED**. HealthFirst Health Plan is directed to approve the requested procedure.
