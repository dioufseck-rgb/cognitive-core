## HealthFirst Health Plan
**Appeals Review Determination**

**Member:** David Santos (M-101122), Age 63

**Reviewer:** Appeals Review Committee, HealthFirst Health Plan

**I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

This appeal reviews the denial of prior authorization for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 for Mr. David Santos. The initial denial (by Dr. Patricia Huang) cited failure to meet the myelopathy exception criteria, arguing that while myelopathy was present, it was not the *primary diagnosis* given the predominant right-sided radicular symptoms. The appeal contends that T2 signal change (myelomalacia) overrides standard requirements and that both myelopathy and radiculopathy are present.

Applying the three-source hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), this appeal finds that the plan's denial criteria, specifically the "primary diagnosis" requirement for the myelopathy exception and the insistence on full standard conservative treatment, are overridden by California state law and generally accepted clinical standards.

**Disposition: OVERTURNED**

---

**II. CASE BACKGROUND**

**Member Clinical Summary:**
Mr. Santos, a 63-year-old male, presents with symptoms diagnosed as mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-01):** Reveals C6-C7 moderate cord compression with significant T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord, and right C7 foraminal stenosis.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-09-05):** Findings include predominant right C7 radicular symptoms (tricep weakness 4/5, finger numbness, 8/10 pain). Critically, myelopathic signs are also present: bilateral hyperreflexia (right > left), positive right Hoffman sign, equivocal left Hoffman sign, and a mildly wide-based gait.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 6 weeks (18 sessions) of "Myelopathy protocol." Documented "No improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms."
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Pregabalin 150mg BID for 3 months (partial right-sided pain relief); Naproxen 500mg BID for 2 months (inadequate).
    *   **Interventional:** Right C7 epidural on 2024-09-28, yielding 50% right-sided relief for 3 weeks, with full return of symptoms. Notably, "No change in bilateral hyperreflexia" was observed.

**Plan Denial (2024-10-05):**
The initial denial (reason code MED-003) stated: "Myelopathy exception applied but criteria not met. MRI shows cord compression and T2 signal change. However, member's symptoms are primarily right-sided (radicular) with only mild bilateral components. Primary diagnosis remains radiculopathy. Myelopathy exception requires myelopathy as primary diagnosis." The denial effectively forced Mr. Santos into the standard conservative treatment pathway due to myelopathy not being deemed his "primary" diagnosis.

**Member Appeal Basis:**
The appeal highlighted three key points:
1.  T2 signal change (myelomalacia) is present, triggering a regulatory override.
2.  The presentation is a mixed myelopathy/radiculopathy, with both diagnoses objectively present.
3.  The plan cannot deny the myelopathy exception when myelomalacia is objectively documented.

---

**III. REGULATORY & CLINICAL REVIEW**

**1. Procedural Adequacy of Denial:**
The denial notice specified a reason code and a specific clinical rationale (myelopathy not primary diagnosis). This fulfills the procedural requirements for a denial notice under CHSC § 1374.31(b). The issue lies with the substance of the denial criterion itself.

**2. Application of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A)) allows for a reduced physical therapy requirement (4 weeks) and waives interventional treatment for myelopathy if documented as the *primary diagnosis*. The plan's initial reviewer applied this criterion as written, concluding that because radicular symptoms were predominant, myelopathy was not the *primary diagnosis*, thereby denying the exception and requiring the full standard conservative treatment. *While the plan applied its own criteria as written, the validity of that specific criterion must be assessed against higher authorities.*

**3. Application of California Regulatory Requirements:**

*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Section II.2) — Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions:**
    This APL, a binding state regulation, explicitly addresses cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia, classifying it as a condition with "potential for irreversible harm if untreated." It states that plans **SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment** when:
    *   (a) Objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (Mr. Santos has myelomalacia on MRI).
    *   (b) Treating physician documents conservative treatment failure (Mr. Santos's PT showed no improvement in bilateral neurological signs, and the epidural did not improve hyperreflexia, indicating failure for the myelopathic component).
    *   (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines (as discussed below).
    The APL specifically identifies **myelomalacia on MRI** as a finding that *may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements* even where a full course has not been completed.
    *Conclusion: The plan's denial directly contradicts DMHC APL 22-014 by requiring a full course of conservative treatment despite the presence of myelomalacia and documented neurological compromise.*

*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:**
    This state law prohibits plans from conditioning surgical approval on completion of physical therapy where "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Mr. Santos's moderate cord compression with T2 signal change (myelomalacia) unequivocally constitutes "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise" that physical therapy cannot reverse. Furthermore, his 6 weeks of PT documented "No improvement in bilateral neurological signs," indicating a functional plateau for his myelopathy.
    *Conclusion: The plan's implicit requirement for further PT, stemming from its denial of the myelopathy exception, is overridden by CIC § 10169.5(a)(3).*

*   **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) — IMR Standard of Review:**
    This statute mandates that if a service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice," it **SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.** This provides a critical override for plan-specific criteria that are not aligned with clinical consensus.

**4. Application of Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

*   **Diagnosis of Myelopathy:** Mr. Santos's clinical presentation (bilateral hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman, gait disturbance) and MRI findings (cord compression with T2 signal change/myelomalacia) are textbook for cervical myelopathy. The presence of myelomalacia specifically indicates existing spinal cord injury. While right-sided radicular symptoms are prominent, the objective myelopathic signs are undeniably present and significant.
*   **Surgical Indication:** Per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI are **TIER 1 strong surgical indications.**
*   **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy:**
    *   AO Spine Guidelines (2021) state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
    *   The NASS Guidelines (2020) emphasize that for moderate to severe myelopathy, surgical decompression is the recommended primary treatment.
    *   The clinical evidence base consistently highlights that applying radiculopathy-derived conservative treatment requirements to myelopathy patients is a "category error" and can be harmful.
*   **Myelomalacia Significance:** Clinical standards uniformly consider myelomalacia (T2 signal change) a strong indicator of cord injury, increased risk of permanent deficits if surgery is delayed, and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials.

*Conclusion: The requested ACDF is fully in line with generally accepted standards of medical practice for Mr. Santos's diagnosis of cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia. The clinical standard explicitly contraindicates delaying surgery for further conservative treatment in this scenario.*

**5. Cross-Source Conflict Resolution (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):**
The plan's internal criterion, requiring myelopathy to be the *primary diagnosis* for its myelopathy exception, is in direct conflict with both California state law and established clinical standards.
*   California's DMHC APL 22-014 and CIC § 10169.5 explicitly create exceptions or waivers for conservative treatment requirements in the presence of myelopathy with myelomalacia, irrespective of whether it's deemed the "primary" presenting symptom relative to pain.
*   Clinical guidelines underscore the urgency of surgical intervention for myelopathy with myelomalacia and the lack of evidence supporting prolonged conservative trials.
*   Per CHSC § 1374.32(b), services meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice must be approved, overriding more restrictive plan criteria.

Therefore, the plan's denial is based on an overly restrictive internal criterion that is superseded by higher authorities.

---

**IV. DETERMINATION**

Based on a comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst Health Plan's clinical criteria, California state law and regulations, and generally accepted clinical standards, the denial of prior authorization for ACDF C6-C7 for Mr. David Santos is hereby **OVERTURNED**.

**WARRANT:**

The denial was based on the plan's internal criterion that the myelopathy exception applies "only when myelopathy is the PRIMARY diagnosis." However, this criterion directly conflicts with higher authorities for Mr. Santos's specific clinical presentation:

1.  **Objective Myelopathy with Myelomalacia:** Mr. Santos presents with clear and objective evidence of cervical myelopathy (moderate cord compression, T2 signal change/myelomalacia on MRI, bilateral hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign, and gait disturbance). While he also has predominant radicular pain, the myelopathic findings indicate a more serious condition with different treatment urgency.
2.  **State Regulatory Override (DMHC APL 22-014):** California's DMHC APL 22-014 explicitly mandates that for conditions like cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia, plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment if objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia) and the physician documents conservative treatment failure. Mr. Santos's PT and interventional therapies failed to improve his myelopathic signs, thus meeting the criteria for waiver of further conservative treatment. The presence of myelomalacia specifically identifies this as a condition requiring urgent consideration and potential waiver of standard conservative treatment.
3.  **State Law Override (CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)):** This law prohibits conditioning surgery on PT completion where there is objective structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. Mr. Santos's myelomalacia and cord compression represent such compromise, and his PT had reached a functional plateau regarding his myelopathic symptoms.
4.  **Clinical Standard Mandate:** Generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021) consider myelopathy with myelomalacia a TIER 1 strong surgical indication. These guidelines explicitly state there is **no high-quality evidence supporting mandatory physical therapy** prior to surgery for moderate to severe myelopathy, and delaying surgery in such cases is not supported and may lead to irreversible neurological deterioration. The plan's "primary diagnosis" requirement and insistence on standard conservative therapy are therefore contrary to accepted medical practice.
5.  **CHSC § 1374.32(b) — Clinical Standard Governs:** As the requested surgery is medically necessary and indicated under generally accepted standards of medical practice, California law requires approval regardless of any more restrictive plan criteria.

For these reasons, the HealthFirst Health Plan's internal criteria cannot be applied in this case to deny medically necessary care. The procedure for ACDF C6-C7 for Mr. David Santos is medically necessary and approved.
