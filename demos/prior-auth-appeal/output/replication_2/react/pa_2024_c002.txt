**RE: APPEAL DETERMINATION FOR DAVID SANTOS (ACDF C6-C7)**

This letter serves as the determination for the first-level appeal of the prior authorization denial for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 for Mr. David Santos.

**1. Clinical Summary:**

Mr. David Santos, a 63-year-old male, presents with a diagnosis of mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy, with the primary diagnosis disputed by the initial reviewer. Recent imaging (MRI dated 2024-09-01) reveals moderate cord compression at C6-C7 with significant T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord, and right C7 foraminal stenosis more prominent than the left.

Neurological examination (2024-09-05) findings indicate a predominant right C7 distribution with 4/5 right tricep weakness and right finger numbness, alongside critical bilateral findings including bilateral hyperreflexia (right > left), a positive right Hoffman sign, an equivocal left Hoffman sign, and a mildly wide-based gait. Pain is predominantly right-sided (8/10).

Conservative treatment included 6 weeks of physical therapy (18 sessions following a myelopathy protocol) which yielded *no improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms*. Pharmacotherapy included Pregabalin 150mg BID for 3 months (partial right-sided pain relief) and Naproxen 500mg BID for 2 months (inadequate relief). An interventional right C7 epidural injection provided 50% right-sided relief, which fully returned within 3 weeks, with *no change in bilateral hyperreflexia*.

**2. Plan's Initial Denial Rationale:**

The initial denial, dated 2024-10-05, stated reason code MED-003, indicating that the myelopathy exception (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2A) was considered but criteria not met. The denial noted that while MRI shows cord compression and T2 signal change, the member's symptoms are primarily right-sided (radicular) with only mild bilateral components. The initial reviewer concluded that the "Primary diagnosis remains radiculopathy" and that the "Myelopathy exception requires myelopathy as primary diagnosis." This determination led to the application of standard conservative treatment requirements (6 weeks PT, 2 pharmacotherapy agents, 1 interventional procedure) which were deemed not fully met for a radiculopathy primary diagnosis.

**3. Appeal Basis:**

The appeal asserts that:
*   T2 signal change (myelomalacia) is present, which should trigger a regulatory override.
*   The presentation is a mixed myelopathy/radiculopathy, with both diagnoses present.
*   The plan cannot deny the myelopathy exception when myelomalacia is objectively documented.

**4. Review Findings and Analysis:**

This appeal was reviewed applying the established hierarchy of authority: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria**.

**4.1. Clinical Presentation: Myelopathy Confirmation**
The clinical record unequivocally supports the presence of cervical myelopathy. While the member experiences prominent radicular symptoms, the objective findings of **moderate cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia)** on MRI, combined with **bilateral hyperreflexia (right > left), positive right Hoffman sign, equivocal left Hoffman sign, and mildly wide-based gait**, are definitive indicators of spinal cord pathology (myelopathy). Myelomalacia specifically signifies existing spinal cord injury (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2).

**4.2. Plan Criteria Application:**
HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(b), defines cervical myelopathy as "spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms (gait disturbance, bilateral upper extremity weakness, hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign, bowel/bladder dysfunction)." Mr. Santos meets this diagnostic criterion.

The denial hinged on the "NOTE" in Section 2.2(A) of the plan's criteria, which states the myelopathy exception applies "only when myelopathy is the PRIMARY diagnosis." The initial reviewer determined that despite objective myelopathy findings, the *primary* diagnosis remained radiculopathy due to symptom predominance.

**4.3. Analysis under Clinical Standard:**
Generally accepted clinical standards directly contradict the plan's restrictive application of its myelopathy exception, particularly in the presence of myelomalacia:
*   **Myelomalacia as Strong Surgical Indication:** The AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update) identify myelomalacia on MRI (cord signal change) as a **TIER 1 strong surgical indication** (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.1). It indicates existing cord damage and is a strong predictor of permanent deficit if surgery is delayed (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2, 2.3).
*   **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy:** The AO Spine Guidelines (2021) explicitly state, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1). The NASS Guidelines (2020) concur, recommending surgical decompression as primary treatment for moderate to severe myelopathy (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1).
*   **Functional Plateau:** Mr. Santos completed 6 weeks of physical therapy specifically for his myelopathy, with "no improvement in bilateral neurological signs." Per clinical standard, a patient who achieves partial pain relief but no improvement in neurological symptoms has reached a functional plateau, as neurological symptoms in myelopathy are not expected to respond to PT regardless of duration (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 5).
*   The plan's underlying criteria requiring 6 weeks of PT for a patient with myelopathy and myelomalacia, by effectively denying the myelopathy exception, are **not consistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice** (CHSC § 1374.32(a)).

**4.4. Analysis under California State Law:**
California state laws and regulations directly override the plan's denial reasoning:
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgency):** This state guidance mandates that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent..." It further specifies that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment... when: (a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia...), AND (b) Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression, AND (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines..." The APL specifically identifies **myelomalacia on MRI as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements** (DMHC APL 22-014, Section II.1, II.2).
    *   Mr. Santos's case clearly meets these conditions: Myelomalacia is present, PT failed to improve neurological signs, and surgery is indicated under clinical guidelines. The plan's denial, by insisting on full conservative treatment requirements due to a "primary diagnosis" clause, directly violates DMHC APL 22-014.
*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Prerequisite):** This law states that a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where: "(3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
    *   Mr. Santos's myelomalacia and cord compression represent **objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address**. The PT summary confirms "No improvement in bilateral neurological signs." Therefore, the PT requirement is waived by state law.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This section is critical, stating that the IMR standard (which this appeal emulates) "does NOT require that the service be consistent with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the service meets the standard in subdivision (a) [generally accepted standards of medical practice], the reviewer SHALL recommend approval regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive."
    *   This explicitly allows for approval even if HealthFirst's internal criteria are more restrictive than clinical standards and state law, as is the case here with the "primary diagnosis" clause.

**4.5. Conflict Resolution:**
The plan's denial is based on a narrow interpretation of its own internal criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2A "NOTE") regarding "primary diagnosis." However, both California state law (DMHC APL 22-014, CIC § 10169.5) and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) explicitly state that for patients with myelopathy and objective findings such as myelomalacia and cord compression, the requirement for an extended or specific duration of conservative treatment, particularly physical therapy, is waived, not supported by evidence, and potentially harmful.

Applying the hierarchy of authority (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria), the state laws and clinical standards supersede the plan's restrictive "primary diagnosis" clause. To uphold the denial would place plan criteria above state regulatory mandates and medical evidence, which is impermissible.

**5. Disposition:**

**OVERTURN**

**6. Warrant:**

The denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) for Mr. David Santos is **OVERTURNED**. The member's clinical presentation includes confirmed cervical myelopathy, evidenced by moderate cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia) on MRI, along with objective upper motor neuron signs (hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign, gait disturbance) on neurological exam.

California state law, specifically DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014, mandates that plans treat myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia as urgent, requiring a waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements. Additionally, CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) waives physical therapy requirements where objective structural neurological compromise (such as myelomalacia) is present and PT cannot address it. Mr. Santos's PT showed no improvement in bilateral neurological signs, confirming this waiver.

Furthermore, generally accepted clinical standards from AANS/CNS, NASS, and AO Spine unequivocally categorize myelomalacia as a strong surgical indication (TIER 1) and advise against delaying surgery for extended conservative treatment in moderate to severe myelopathy due to the risk of irreversible neurological deterioration. These clinical guidelines establish that physical therapy is not effective for improving neurological signs in myelopathy.

The plan's denial, predicated on a restrictive "myelopathy as primary diagnosis" clause in its internal criteria, is directly contradicted and superseded by these higher authorities of California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice. Therefore, the requested procedure is medically necessary and meets the criteria for approval.

**7. Decision:**

The request for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) for Mr. David Santos is **APPROVED**.
