**DISPOSITION: OVERTURNED**

**WARRANT AND RATIONALE:**

This appeal determination is based on a comprehensive review of the member's clinical record, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), relevant California state laws and regulations, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice. The appeal is decided using the established hierarchy of authority: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.

**I. Procedural Adequacy of Initial Denial**

The initial denial dated 2024-11-08 is found to be procedurally defective.
*   The denial notice explicitly referenced an "attached criteria document" (Reason Code MED-003, "See attached criteria document"), which was not provided to the member or the treating physician.
*   The denial notice failed to include information regarding the member's right to Independent Medical Review (IMR), as required.

**California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b)** states that "A denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is DEFECTIVE and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines." The failure to provide the referenced criteria document effectively means the specific clinical criteria were not adequately communicated, rendering the denial defective. This procedural defect alone is sufficient grounds to Overturn the denial.

**II. Clinical Review and Application of Criteria**

Even if the procedural defects were disregarded, a substantive review of the clinical record against the relevant criteria supports overturning the denial.

**A. Clinical Presentation and Diagnosis:**
The member's clinical presentation, including C6 dermatomal numbness, diminished left bicep reflex, and left bicep weakness (4/5), with MRI showing C5-C6 large disc herniation and moderate left C6 nerve root compression *without cord contact or myelomalacia*, is consistent with **cervical radiculopathy**. This is not cervical myelopathy, which requires distinct clinical signs (e.g., bilateral symptoms, gait disturbance, hyperreflexia, Hoffman sign) and imaging findings (e.g., spinal cord compression, myelomalacia). Therefore, the myelopathy-specific exceptions or requirements (e.g., HealthFirst's Section 2.2(A) Exception, DMHC APL 22-014) are not directly applicable for this diagnosis classification but the general principle of not applying more restrictive criteria remains.

**B. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
The requested procedure, CPT 22551, is within the scope of the policy (Section 1).
1.  **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with objective nerve root compression and radicular symptoms, supported by MRI findings. This criterion is met.
2.  **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **(a) Physical Therapy:** The plan requires a "minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy with documented functional outcomes." The member completed 7 weeks (21 sessions) of PT, and the PT notes document a functional plateau at week 6, with persistent radicular symptoms. This criterion is met for duration and documentation of outcome.
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** The plan requires a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The member completed a 4-month trial of Gabapentin (partial relief) and a 3-month trial of Naproxen (inadequate relief). This criterion is met.
    *   **(c) Interventional Treatment:** The plan requires one interventional treatment. The member underwent a left C6 epidural, which provided 40% relief but returned to baseline in 3 weeks. This criterion is met.
3.  **2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:** A recent MRI (within 6 months) demonstrates structural pathology (C5-C6 large disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression) consistent with symptoms and the proposed surgical level. This criterion is met.
4.  **2.4 PHYSICIAN DOCUMENTATION:** While not explicitly detailed in the appeal, there is no indication in the denial reason that documentation from the treating physician was insufficient.

Based on the available clinical record, the member **does meet** HealthFirst's own clinical coverage criteria for cervical radiculopathy. The denial reason ("Member does not meet criteria for requested procedure under plan policy") is therefore unsubstantiated by the evidence.

**C. Application of California State Law:**
1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This state law directly addresses requirements for physical therapy prior to surgery.
    *   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)** states that a plan "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
    *   The member completed 7 weeks of physical therapy, and the PT notes confirm a "plateau at week 6" with "Radicular symptoms persist." This explicitly satisfies CIC § 10169.5(a)(2). Therefore, the plan cannot deny authorization based on the physical therapy requirement, as the member has reached a functional plateau after an adequate trial.
2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014:** While primarily focused on myelopathy, Section II.3 states: "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for conditions identified in this guidance. Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs." This principle reinforces the importance of clinical standards in appeal reviews.

**D. Application of Clinical Standard:**
**AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine: Guidelines for Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update)**, in its section on surgical indications (Tier 2), identifies surgery as a "reasonable option" for:
*   "(b) Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx."

The member has persistent radicular symptoms and objective neurological deficits after 7 weeks of physical therapy (with documented plateau), a multi-month course of two pharmacotherapy agents, and one interventional epidural procedure that provided only temporary, partial relief. This duration and demonstrated failure of conservative treatment aligns with the clinical standard for surgical indication for cervical radiculopathy. The clinical evidence base (AO Spine 2021, NASS 2020) supports surgical intervention for radiculopathy that has failed appropriate non-operative management, typically defined as 6-12 weeks of such treatment.

**III. Conflict Resolution and Conclusion**

The hierarchy of authority dictates that State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.
1.  The denial is procedurally defective under **California State Law (CHSC § 1374.31(b))**.
2.  Substantively, the member's conservative treatment (specifically PT) meets the requirements of **California State Law (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2))** by virtue of having completed a course and reached a functional plateau.
3.  The member's condition and history of conservative treatment satisfy **generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023)** for surgical intervention in cases of persistent cervical radiculopathy.
4.  Finally, the clinical record demonstrates the member **does meet HealthFirst's own Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)** for conservative treatment duration and outcomes for radiculopathy. The initial denial's claim that the member "does not meet criteria" is not supported by the evidence in the record when evaluated against the plan's own document.

For these multiple and compounding reasons, the denial of prior authorization for CPT code 22551 for Michael Green is **OVERTURNED**. The requested procedure is deemed medically necessary.

HealthFirst is directed to approve prior authorization for ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) for Michael Green.
