## APPEAL DETERMINATION: Prior Authorization PA-2024-C001

**Case ID:** PA-2024-C001
**Member:** Frances Kim (M-990011)
**Plan:** HealthFirst PPO Gold — Group Plan 77441 (California)
**Procedure:** ACDF C5-C6, one level (CPT 22551)
**Requested By:** Dr. Robert Adams, MD (Neurosurgery)
**Original Denial Date:** 2024-10-30
**Reviewer:** Expert Health Plan Appeals Reviewer

---

**DISPOSITION: OVERTURN DENIAL**

---

**WARRANT**

Upon review of the submitted prior authorization appeal, member's clinical record, HealthFirst PPO Gold Plan Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), relevant California regulatory frameworks, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial denial for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) is **OVERTURNED**.

The denial was primarily based on two erroneous premises:
1.  An incorrect interpretation of the diagnostic criteria for cervical myelopathy, specifically requiring imaging confirmation of cord compression and signal change despite compelling clinical signs.
2.  An incorrect application of conservative treatment requirements, failing to acknowledge that the member had met or exceeded applicable thresholds, and that further physical therapy is medically unnecessary and potentially harmful given the clinical presentation.

This determination is based on the following findings, applying the hierarchy of authority: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.

**1. Establishment of Cervical Myelopathy Diagnosis:**
The denial stated, "Myelopathy exception does not apply. Imaging shows mild cord contact only — no cord compression, no cord signal change. Upper motor neuron signs on exam are not conclusive for myelopathy in the absence of imaging confirmation." This reasoning is inconsistent with generally accepted clinical standards.

*   **Clinical Record:** The member's neurological examination on 2024-09-05 clearly documents multiple positive upper motor neuron (UMN) signs: bilateral Hoffman's sign, bilateral upper extremity hyperreflexia, sustained clonus at bilateral ankles (3 beats), positive finger escape sign, and a mildly wide-based gait.
*   **Clinical Standard Overrides Plan Criteria:** The AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine Guidelines (2023 Update) explicitly state that "Clinical myelopathy diagnosis requires UMN signs on examination. Imaging confirmation supports but is not required for diagnosis. Imaging-negative myelopathy is a recognized entity where clinical signs precede MRI changes. Multiple positive UMN signs in the absence of other explanation = clinical myelopathy." (Clinical Record, "clinical_standard_note" and Source Document 3, Section 1.1).
    *   HealthFirst's Plan Criteria 2.1(b) defines cervical myelopathy as "spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms." While this criterion *includes* imaging findings, the clinical standard clarifies that imaging is supportive, not mandatory, for diagnosis when UMN signs are present.
    *   Under California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), the standard for medical necessity during review is "generally accepted standards of medical practice," not solely the plan's internal coverage criteria if they are more restrictive.
*   **Conclusion:** Based on the robust clinical presentation of UMN signs and the prevailing clinical standards, Frances Kim has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, despite the absence of measurable cord compression or T2 signal change on MRI. The denial's requirement for imaging confirmation for myelopathy, when strong UMN signs are present, is contrary to medical evidence.

**2. Fulfillment of Conservative Treatment Requirements:**

*   **Plan's Myelopathy Exception:** HealthFirst's own Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY) reduces the physical therapy requirement to "4 weeks minimum" for members with confirmed cervical myelopathy.
    *   Given the establishment of myelopathy per clinical standards, the plan's myelopathy exception should have applied. The member completed 5 weeks of physical therapy ("5 weeks PT under myelopathy protocol"), which exceeds the plan's *own* reduced 4-week requirement for myelopathy.
*   **California State Law Overrides Plan Criteria:** Even if the plan's criteria were not met, California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a) prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where:
    *   **(1) Physician documentation:** The treating physician halted PT citing "myelopathy concern," indicating PT was unlikely to benefit the member given the nature of the condition.
    *   **(2) Functional Plateau:** The member completed 5 weeks of PT, and "Neurological signs [were] unchanged." Clinical standards define a functional plateau as documented failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement, and specifically note that "Neurological symptoms in myelopathy (weakness, hyperreflexia) are not expected to respond to PT regardless of duration." (Source Document 3, Section 5).
    *   **(3) Objective Neurological Compromise:** The presence of multiple UMN signs (Hoffman, hyperreflexia, clonus, finger escape, gait disturbance) constitutes "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Physical therapy cannot reverse spinal cord damage indicated by UMN signs.
*   **Clinical Standard Overrides Plan Criteria:** Leading clinical guidelines emphasize that prolonged conservative treatment, particularly physical therapy, is *not* supported by evidence for moderate to severe cervical myelopathy and may be detrimental.
    *   The AO Spine Guidelines (2021) state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." (Source Document 3, Section 2.1).
    *   The 6-week PT requirement, as originally applied by the plan, is derived from radiculopathy evidence and has "NO evidentiary basis for myelopathy" (Source Document 3, Section 2.2).
*   **Conclusion:** The member has met and exceeded the plan's own reduced PT requirement for myelopathy. Furthermore, state law (CIC § 10169.5) and generally accepted clinical standards independently waive any further PT requirement due to the objective neurological compromise, functional plateau, and the treating physician's assessment.

**3. Application of Higher Authority:**
California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) mandates that health care services meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice shall be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. In this case, HealthFirst's interpretation of myelopathy diagnosis and conservative treatment requirements is more restrictive and less aligned with evidence-based clinical practice than the prevailing standards.

The DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (II.3) further clarifies that "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for conditions identified in this guidance. Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs."

**Summary of Overriding Factors:**
*   **Clinical Myelopathy Confirmed:** Despite imaging showing only "mild cord contact" without measurable compression or signal change, the member has definitive clinical myelopathy based on multiple UMN signs, per AANS/CNS guidelines. The denial's premise regarding the lack of imaging confirmation for myelopathy is flawed.
*   **PT Requirement Met/Waived:** The member completed 5 weeks of PT. Once myelopathy is recognized, this exceeds the plan's own 4-week myelopathy-specific PT requirement. Moreover, state law (CIC 10169.5) waives the PT requirement entirely due to objective neurological compromise and functional plateau, an interpretation strongly supported by current clinical guidelines which advise against delaying surgery for myelopathy for PT.

Given the established diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, the fulfillment of conservative treatment requirements under both the plan's myelopathy exception and state law, and the clear guidance from clinical standards, the requested ACDF C5-C6 is medically necessary.

---

**FINAL DETERMINATION:**

The prior authorization denial for Frances Kim (PA-2024-C001) for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) is **OVERTURNED**. Authorization for the requested procedure is approved.
