## Appeal Determination: Prior Authorization Request for ACDF C5-C6

**Case ID:** PA-2024-C001
**Member:** Frances Kim (M-990011), 58 years old
**Plan:** HealthFirst PPO Gold — Group Plan 77441, California
**Procedure Requested:** CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level)
**Requesting Provider:** Dr. Robert Adams, MD (Neurosurgery)
**Initial Denial Date:** 2024-10-30
**Initial Denial Reason:** MED-003 – "Myelopathy exception does not apply. Imaging shows mild cord contact only — no cord compression, no cord signal change. Upper motor neuron signs on exam are not conclusive for myelopathy in the absence of imaging confirmation. Standard 6-week PT requirement applies; member completed 5 weeks." (Dr. Alan Foster, MD)
**Appeal Basis:** Member has three positive upper motor neuron (UMN) signs establishing clinical myelopathy regardless of imaging; mild cord contact on MRI plus UMN signs equals myelopathy per clinical definition; 5 weeks PT completed under myelopathy pathway (4 weeks required).

---

### Executive Summary

The initial denial for an ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) for Ms. Frances Kim is **OVERTURNED**.

The denial was based on a misapplication of the plan's criteria regarding the diagnosis of cervical myelopathy and the conservative treatment requirements. Specifically, the initial reviewer incorrectly asserted that upper motor neuron signs are not conclusive for myelopathy in the absence of imaging confirmation of cord compression or signal change. This contradicts generally accepted clinical standards and California state law, which mandates that clinical standards supersede more restrictive plan criteria.

Ms. Kim's clinical presentation, with multiple objective upper motor neuron signs, meets the diagnostic criteria for cervical myelopathy under generally accepted standards. Consequently, the plan's myelopathy exception (requiring 4 weeks of PT) should have been applied. Furthermore, even under the myelopathy exception, the member's completion of 5 weeks of PT, with unchanged neurological signs and the physician's decision to halt therapy due to myelopathy concerns, meets the criteria for waiving further PT under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5.

---

### Detailed Review and Rationale

**1. Primary Clinical Presentation: Cervical Myelopathy**
The clinical record clearly indicates a diagnosis of "Suspected cervical myelopathy — imaging-negative UMN syndrome." The neurological exam reveals multiple, unambiguous upper motor neuron signs, including:
*   Positive Hoffman bilateral
*   Hyperreflexia bilateral upper extremities
*   Sustained clonus at bilateral ankles (3 beats)
*   Positive Finger escape sign
*   Mildly wide-based gait

These findings are classic indicators of cervical myelopathy, not radiculopathy. The patient denies lower extremity symptoms, but the presence of bilateral UMN signs (clonus at ankles, Hoffman's, hyperreflexia) unequivocally points to spinal cord involvement.

**2. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)**

*   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** The plan's criterion 2.1(b) for cervical myelopathy requires "spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms." The MRI noted "mild cord contact" but "no measurable cord compression" and "no T2 signal change." If interpreted strictly by the plan as requiring *measurable* compression, this would be a point of conflict. However, criterion 2.1(c) covers "Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit." The C5-C6 disc herniation and the multiple objective UMN signs satisfy this criterion. More importantly, the *interpretation* of myelopathy is critical and is addressed below under clinical standards and state law.
*   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Member completed Baclofen (2 months) and Methylprednisolone taper (10 days). Baclofen meets the 4-week duration for a muscle relaxant, and oral corticosteroids are also listed. This requirement is met.
    *   **Interventional:** If the myelopathy exception applies, this is waived.
    *   **Physical Therapy:** Member completed 5 weeks (15 sessions). The plan's standard requirement is 6 weeks. The myelopathy exception (2.2(A)) reduces this to 4 weeks minimum.

    The crux of the denial was that the myelopathy exception "does not apply." Therefore, the plan's reviewer defaulted to the 6-week PT requirement, which the member did not fully meet by one week.

**3. Assessment of Myelopathy Diagnosis: Plan vs. Clinical Standard**

*   **Plan's Denial Rationale:** The initial reviewer stated, "Upper motor neuron signs on exam are not conclusive for myelopathy in the absence of imaging confirmation."
*   **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine):** The clinical record's embedded AANS/CNS note explicitly states: "Clinical myelopathy diagnosis requires UMN signs on examination. Imaging confirmation supports but is not required for diagnosis. Imaging-negative myelopathy is a recognized entity where clinical signs precede MRI changes. Multiple positive UMN signs in the absence of other explanation = clinical myelopathy." This is further corroborated by the comprehensive Clinical Reference Summary, which distinguishes myelopathy by UMN signs and recognizes that "Conservative treatment has LIMITED efficacy for established myelopathy."
*   **Conflict Resolution:** There is a direct conflict between the plan's implicit requirement for imaging confirmation of compression (as interpreted by the initial reviewer) and the generally accepted clinical standard that recognizes clinical myelopathy based on UMN signs alone.

**4. Application of California Regulatory Requirements (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria)**

*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This vital provision states that the IMR standard does *not* require consistency with plan criteria. If a service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, it **SHALL** be approved regardless of more restrictive plan criteria. This directly addresses the conflict regarding the diagnosis of myelopathy. The member's presentation meets the clinical standard for myelopathy, overriding the plan's more restrictive imaging-based definition.
*   **DMHC APL 22-014, Section II.3 (Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard):** This APL explicitly mandates: "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice... Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs." This further strengthens the argument that the clinical definition of myelopathy (based on UMN signs) must prevail over the plan reviewer's more restrictive interpretation requiring imaging compression.
*   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) & (2) (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This state law prohibits plans from conditioning surgery on PT completion where:
    *   **(a)(1) Physician documents PT contraindicated or unlikely to benefit:** The clinical record states the "Physician halted PT citing myelopathy concern." This directly implies PT was deemed unlikely to benefit the neurological condition and may even pose risk, thus satisfying this criterion.
    *   **(a)(2) Member completed PT and reached a functional plateau:** The member completed 5 weeks of PT, and the "Neurological signs [were] unchanged." The Clinical Standard Note explicitly states, "Neurological symptoms in myelopathy (weakness, hyperreflexia) are not expected to respond to PT regardless of duration." The lack of improvement in neurological signs, despite 5 weeks of PT, confirms a functional plateau for the myelopathic condition.

**5. Clinical Standard Evidence for Conservative Treatment in Myelopathy:**

*   **AO Spine Guidelines (2021) and NASS Guidelines (2020):** These guidelines unequivocally state that "there is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy." They caution against delaying surgery, noting it "is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." The 6-week (or even 4-week) PT requirement, while common in plan policies, lacks evidentiary basis for myelopathy and is largely derived from radiculopathy studies. Applying radiculopathy protocols to myelopathy patients is considered a "category error."

**Conclusion of Conflict Resolution:**

1.  **Diagnosis of Myelopathy:** Based on the robust objective UMN signs in Ms. Kim's neurological exam, clinical myelopathy is established per generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS). The plan's initial denial, which required imaging *confirmation* in the form of measurable cord compression or signal change, is more restrictive than these standards. Under CHSC § 1374.32(b) and DMHC APL 22-014 II.3, the clinical standard governs. Therefore, the myelopathy exception (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A)) *should* have been applied.
2.  **Conservative Treatment (PT):** Even with the myelopathy exception's reduced 4-week PT requirement, Ms. Kim completed 5 weeks. More critically, her neurological signs remained unchanged, and her physician halted PT due to myelopathy concerns. This satisfies the conditions under CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) and (2) for waiving further PT. Furthermore, clinical guidelines explicitly state that mandatory PT for myelopathy is not evidence-based and delaying surgery can be detrimental.

The initial reviewer's denial was based on an inaccurate understanding of myelopathy diagnosis and an inappropriate application of conservative treatment requirements for this condition.

---

### Disposition

**OVERTURNED**

### Warrant

The denial is **OVERTURNED** because the initial reviewer's assertion that myelopathy was not confirmed due to a lack of imaging evidence directly contradicts generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS Guidelines 2023) which recognize "clinical myelopathy" and "imaging-negative myelopathy" based on objective upper motor neuron signs. California law (CHSC § 1374.32(b) and DMHC APL 22-014, Section II.3) mandates that clinical standards take precedence over more restrictive plan criteria in such conflicts.

With the diagnosis of cervical myelopathy confirmed per clinical standards, the plan's conservative treatment requirements, specifically the physical therapy duration, are overridden. Both leading clinical guidelines (AO Spine 2021, NASS 2020) state that there is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgery for moderate-to-severe myelopathy, and that delay may be harmful. Furthermore, California law (CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) and (2)) explicitly prohibits conditioning surgical approval on PT completion when the treating physician documents that PT is unlikely to benefit or the member has reached a functional plateau, both of which apply in this case given the unchanged neurological signs and the physician halting PT due to myelopathy concern. The 5 weeks of PT completed, in the context of the clinical diagnosis and statutory waivers, are more than sufficient.

Therefore, the requested ACDF procedure is medically necessary and should be approved.
