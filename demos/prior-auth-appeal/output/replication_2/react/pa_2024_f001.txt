MRI on 2024-09-01 shows a large disc herniation at C6-C7, bilateral C7 nerve root compression, bilateral foraminal stenosis, and mild cord contact without compression. A neurological exam on 2024-09-05 revealed bilateral C7 dermatomal symptoms, bilateral tricep weakness (4/5), bilateral tricep reflex diminished, and bilateral finger tingling.

Conservative treatment included:
*   **Physical Therapy:** A total of 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy was completed across two providers: Valley Physical Therapy (4 weeks, 2024-06-01 to 2024-06-28) and Mountain Physical Therapy (4 weeks, 2024-07-15 to 2024-08-09). Valley PT documented "moderate improvement then plateau." Mountain PT documented "no improvement in neurological symptoms. Plateau documented by PT at discharge." The records from Mountain PT, confirming the additional 4 weeks, were provided with this appeal.
*   **Pharmacotherapy:** Pregabalin 150mg BID for 3 months (partial relief) and Naproxen 500mg BID for 2 months (inadequate).
*   **Interventional:** Bilateral C7 epidural steroid injection on 2024-09-20, resulting in temporary partial relief that returned to baseline.

### Reviewer Analysis:

My review is based on the three-source hierarchy: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.

1.  **Application of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
    *   **Diagnosis (Section 2.1):** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy C6-C7, bilateral, with objective neurological deficits (bilateral weakness, diminished reflex) and MRI findings (large disc herniation, bilateral C7 nerve root compression, bilateral foraminal stenosis, mild cord contact). This meets the diagnostic criteria.
    *   **Conservative Treatment (Section 2.2):**
        *   **(a) Physical Therapy:** The plan requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. With the submission of the Mountain PT records, the member has now documented 8 weeks of physical therapy (4 weeks from Valley PT + 4 weeks from Mountain PT). This exceeds the plan's 6-week requirement.
        *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** The member completed a trial of Pregabalin (3 months) and Naproxen (2 months), meeting the requirement for two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
        *   **(c) Interventional Treatment:** The member received a bilateral C7 epidural steroid injection, meeting the requirement for one interventional procedure.
    *   **Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2(A)):** While the MRI shows "mild cord contact" and the symptoms are bilateral, definitive myelopathic signs (e.g., hyperreflexia, Hoffman's sign, gait disturbance, or myelomalacia on MRI) are not explicitly documented in the clinical record as primary. Therefore, the plan would likely apply its standard radiculopathy pathway, not the myelopathy exception, which requires myelopathy to be the "PRIMARY diagnosis."
    *   **Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** Based on the submitted appeal information, the member has now met all conservative treatment requirements of the HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 policy for cervical radiculopathy. The original denial reason related to insufficient PT documentation is no longer valid.

2.  **Application of California Regulatory Requirements (State Law):**
    *   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This state law specifies conditions under which a health insurer cannot condition approval of surgery on completion of physical therapy.
        *   **Subsection (a)(2):** Applies when "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." Both Valley PT and Mountain PT documentation confirms a "plateau" was reached, with Mountain PT specifically noting "no improvement in neurological symptoms." This criterion is met.
        *   **Subsection (a)(3):** Applies when "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The MRI findings (large disc herniation, bilateral nerve root compression, mild cord contact) and neurological deficits (bilateral weakness, diminished reflex) represent objective structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot resolve. This criterion is met.
    *   **Conclusion on State Law:** Even if the plan's 6-week PT requirement were still an issue, state law (CIC § 10169.5) explicitly overrides such requirements when a functional plateau has been reached or objective structural neurological compromise exists, both of which are present in this case.

3.  **Application of Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
    *   The member has completed an extensive course of conservative therapy including 8 weeks of physical therapy, trials of two pharmacotherapy agents, and an epidural injection, with documentation of functional plateau and persistent bilateral neurological deficits.
    *   Per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), surgical intervention is a "Reasonable Indication" (Tier 2) for "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care." The member has completed 8 weeks of PT, 3 months of pharmacotherapy, and an interventional procedure, with persistent bilateral C7 weakness and diminished reflexes, and a documented plateau. This aligns with generally accepted clinical standards for surgical intervention.
    *   The "mild cord contact" and bilateral neurological symptoms are concerning, suggesting a condition approaching myelopathy, even if not fully meeting the strict diagnostic criteria for myelopathy. For such presentations, the clinical evidence base does not support further prolonged conservative treatment (AO Spine 2021).

### Conclusion and Disposition:

The initial denial was based on a lack of complete documentation for physical therapy duration. With the submission of the Mountain Physical Therapy records, the member has now documented 8 weeks of physical therapy, which exceeds HealthFirst's 6-week requirement.

Furthermore, California state law (CIC § 10169.5) mandates that approval for surgery cannot be conditioned on further physical therapy where a functional plateau has been reached and objective structural neurological compromise exists that PT cannot address. Both conditions are met in Ms. James's case.

Finally, the clinical evidence supports surgical intervention for persistent and severe bilateral radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits that have failed an adequate trial of conservative treatment, as demonstrated by Ms. James's clinical presentation.

Therefore, applying the three-source hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), the denial for procedure code 22551 (ACDF C6-C7, one level) should be OVERTURNED.

### Determination: **OVERTURNED**

**Warrant:**
The denial is overturned because the member has met the plan's 6-week physical therapy requirement by completing 8 weeks of therapy, as confirmed by the newly submitted records. Additionally, even if further PT were to be considered, California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2) and (a)(3) explicitly states that a health insurer cannot require further physical therapy when a member has reached a functional plateau and/or has objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. Both conditions are evident in Ms. James's clinical record. The clinical record further demonstrates persistent bilateral neurological deficits and a failure of an adequate conservative treatment trial, which, according to generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS, NASS), constitutes a medical necessity for surgical intervention.
