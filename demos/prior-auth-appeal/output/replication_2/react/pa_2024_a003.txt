**Summary of Appeal Findings:**

This review addresses the appeal for prior authorization of an ACDF at C4-C5 for member Patricia Osei. The initial denial was based on failure to meet HealthFirst PPO Gold Plan criteria Section 2.2, specifically regarding the duration of physical therapy (5 of 6 weeks completed) and the lack of a cervical epidural steroid injection (CESI).

Upon review of the clinical record, the member's appeal basis, HealthFirst's own clinical coverage criteria, California state law, and generally accepted clinical standards, it has been determined that the initial denial was inconsistent with HealthFirst's own policy, state regulations, and clinical evidence. The member's primary diagnosis of **progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia** triggers specific exceptions in both plan criteria and state law, which were not correctly applied in the initial review.

**Disposition:** **OVERTURNED**

**Warrant for Disposition:**

The initial denial is **OVERTURNED** for the following reasons, applied in accordance with the established hierarchy: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.**

**1. Failure to Apply Plan Criteria Correctly (Plan Criteria Conflict):**

The denial incorrectly applied the standard conservative treatment requirements without considering the explicit myelopathy exception within HealthFirst's own Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024).

*   **Diagnosis:** The member's presenting diagnosis is "Progressive cervical myelopathy C4-C5 with myelomalacia" with supporting neurological exam findings (bilateral lower extremity spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor, Lhermitte sign) and MRI findings (severe central cord compression, T2 signal change/myelomalacia). This clearly meets **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(b) (Cervical myelopathy)** and identifies myelopathy as the primary diagnosis.
*   **Physical Therapy (PT):** The denial cited 5 weeks of PT completed, with 6 weeks required. However, **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Exception 2.2(A) \u2014 MYELOPATHY PATHWAY** reduces the physical therapy requirement to a "4 weeks minimum" for confirmed cervical myelopathy. The member completed 5 weeks of PT, thus satisfying the reduced requirement under the plan's own myelopathy exception.
*   **Interventional Treatment (CESI):** The denial cited no cervical epidural steroid injection (CESI) on record. However, **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Exception 2.2(A) \u2014 MYELOPATHY PATHWAY** explicitly states that the "Interventional treatment requirement (2.2(c)) is WAIVED if treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses risk of neurological deterioration." The clinical record clearly documents: "CONTRAINDICATED \u2014 Patient has permanent pacemaker. Fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injection is contraindicated per anesthesiology consultation (Dr. Rivera, 2024-09-10). Written contraindication documented in chart." This documentation satisfies the waiver condition.
*   **Pharmacotherapy:** The member completed trials of Baclofen (3 months) and Methylprednisolone taper (10 days x2), which meets the plan's requirement for at least two agents for 4+ weeks each (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(b)).

**Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The initial denial failed to correctly apply **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Exception 2.2(A)**, which, when applied, demonstrates the member has met all conservative treatment requirements for cervical myelopathy.

**2. Violation of California State Law (State Law Override):**

Even if HealthFirst's plan criteria were interpreted to deny coverage (which they should not be, as per point 1), California state law overrides such a denial.

*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):**
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** This guidance states that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent..." The member's MRI confirms severe central cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia), aligning directly with this provision.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** APL 22-014 mandates that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia) and the physician documents conservative treatment failure, and surgery is indicated by guidelines. The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is *specifically identified* as supporting waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements. The member has myelomalacia and progressive neurological deficits, and the physician documented PT was not addressing neurological progression.
    *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** APL 22-014 further clarifies that "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice." The denial's insistence on a longer PT course and an interventional procedure (when contraindicated) for myelopathy with myelomalacia is more restrictive than clinical standards, as detailed below.
*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):**
    *   This statute prohibits conditioning surgical approval on PT completion where:
        *   **(a)(1) Physician documents PT unlikely to benefit:** The clinical record notes PT was "stopped due to... physician determination that further PT was not addressing neurological progression."
        *   **(a)(2) Member reached functional plateau:** The neurological symptoms (spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor) characteristic of myelopathy are not typically improved by PT, indicating a functional plateau for the critical neurological deficits.
        *   **(a)(3) Objective evidence of structural neurological compromise:** The member has "Progressive cervical myelopathy C4-C5 with myelomalacia" and "severe central cord compression" with "Progressive cord injury." Myelomalacia specifically indicates existing cord injury that PT cannot address.
    *   **(c) Mechanical application of PT requirement:** The denial's mechanical application of a 6-week PT minimum, without regard for myelomalacia or functional plateau, violates this section.

**Conclusion on State Law:** The initial denial is in direct violation of California state law, specifically DMHC APL 22-014 and CIC § 10169.5, which mandate a waiver of conservative treatment requirements for myelopathy with myelomalacia.

**3. Inconsistency with Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (Clinical Standard Override):**

The denial's insistence on further conservative treatment is inconsistent with current medical evidence for cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia.

*   **Diagnosis:** The member's condition of progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and objective neurological signs meets **TIER 1 \u2014 STRONG INDICATION** for surgery (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023, Section 3.1 & 3.2). Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) is a "strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2).
*   **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration" (AO Spine Guidelines 2021, quoted in Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1). The 6-week PT requirement is derived from radiculopathy evidence, not myelopathy, and its application here is a "category error" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.1, 2.2).
*   **Risk of Delay:** "Each month of delay in surgical treatment for moderate-severe myelopathy is associated with decreased likelihood of neurological recovery. Myelomalacia on preoperative MRI is the strongest predictor of incomplete neurological recovery postoperatively" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.3). This underscores the urgency for surgical intervention.
*   **Interventional Treatments:** For myelopathy, the primary pathology is spinal cord compression, which interventional procedures like epidural steroid injections are not designed to resolve and may pose risks without benefit (AANS/CNS Guidelines 2023). The member also has a clear contraindication (pacemaker).

**Conclusion on Clinical Standard:** Generally accepted clinical standards strongly recommend surgical intervention for progressive myelopathy with myelomalacia and do not support mandatory, extended conservative treatment in such cases. The denial conflicts directly with these standards.

**Conclusion:**

Based on the hierarchy of authority (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria), the initial denial of authorization for ACDF C4-C5 for Patricia Osei is **OVERTURNED**.

The member has met the requirements of HealthFirst's own myelopathy exception (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A)) for both physical therapy and the interventional procedure. Furthermore, California state law (DMHC APL 22-014, CIC § 10169.5) and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021) unequivocally support the waiver of conservative treatment requirements for progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and indicate surgical urgency.

The requested procedure is medically necessary and should be authorized.
