**WARRANT FOR OVERTURN:**

The denial for CPT 22551 for Patricia Osei is **OVERTURNED** based on the following analysis, applying the three-source hierarchy (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):

**1. Failure to Apply Plan's Own Criteria Correctly (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
The initial denial incorrectly applied HealthFirst's standard conservative treatment requirements without considering the specific myelopathy exception.
*   **Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1):** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (2.1(b)) with objective imaging, which is met.
*   **Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2A):** The member's primary diagnosis is cervical myelopathy. This exception *reduces the physical therapy requirement to a minimum of 4 weeks* and *waives the interventional treatment requirement if the treating physician documents that it poses a risk of neurological deterioration*.
    *   **Physical Therapy:** The member completed 5 weeks of PT. Under the myelopathy exception, only 4 weeks are required. Thus, the PT requirement is *met*.
    *   **Interventional Treatment:** The member has a permanent pacemaker, and a fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injection was documented as contraindicated by an anesthesiologist. This directly satisfies the condition for *waiving* the interventional treatment requirement under Section 2.2A.
*   **Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The plan's initial denial failed to apply its own "EXCEPTION 2.2(A) \u2014 MYELOPATHY PATHWAY," which, if correctly applied, demonstrates that the member *does meet* HealthFirst's criteria for surgical authorization.

**2. Violation of California State Law:**
Even if the plan's criteria were applied correctly, the denial would be in direct conflict with specific California regulations designed to protect patients with myelopathy and similar conditions.
*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of surgery on completion of physical therapy where:
    *   (a)(1) The treating physician documents PT is unlikely to benefit given the nature and severity of the condition. The physician documented that further PT "was not addressing neurological progression."
    *   (a)(3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. The member's MRI shows "severe central cord compression" and "T2 signal change (myelomalacia) confirmed. Progressive cord injury." Myelomalacia signifies existing cord damage that PT cannot reverse (Source: Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2, 2.1).
    *   (c) A plan's minimum PT requirement shall not be applied mechanically without reference to neurological urgency or functional plateau. The plan's mechanical application of the 6-week rule for myelopathy is prohibited.
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This APL specifically addresses conditions like the member's.
    *   II.1 states that cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm, requiring urgent treatment.
    *   II.2 mandates that plans SHALL NOT require full conservative treatment when objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia, progressive cord injury), the physician documents conservative treatment failure, and surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. All these conditions are met in this case. Myelomalacia is explicitly identified as a finding that supports waiver of standard conservative requirements.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (Standard of Review for IMR):** Although this is an internal appeal, the principle dictates that a service meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice SHALL be approved regardless of the plan's internal criteria if they are more restrictive. This reinforces the primacy of clinical standards over plan criteria when conflict exists.

**3. Inconsistency with Generally Accepted Clinical Standards of Medical Practice:**
Leading clinical guidelines strongly support surgical intervention for the member's condition and do not mandate prolonged conservative treatment in this scenario.
*   **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** Clinical standards explicitly differentiate myelopathy from radiculopathy, emphasizing that they require different treatment algorithms. Conservative treatment evidence is primarily for radiculopathy, not myelopathy (Source: Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.1).
*   **Myelomalacia Significance:** The presence of myelomalacia on MRI indicates existing spinal cord injury, higher risk of permanent deficit, and is considered a *strong indication for surgical intervention* and a *contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials* (Source: Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2).
*   **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** For moderate-severe myelopathy, physical therapy does *not* produce meaningful functional improvement. "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients... may result in neurological deterioration" (Source: AO Spine Guidelines, Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1). The plan's 6-week PT requirement has no evidentiary basis for myelopathy.
*   **Risk of Delay:** Delaying surgery in patients with moderate-severe myelopathy and myelomalacia is associated with decreased likelihood of neurological recovery and worse outcomes (Source: Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.3).
*   **Surgical Indication:** The member's presentation \u2013 progressive cervical myelopathy with severe cord compression, myelomalacia, and objective neurological deficits \u2013 meets TIER 1 STRONG INDICATION criteria for ACDF as per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) (Source: Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.1, 3.2).

**CONCLUSION:**
The initial denial failed to apply HealthFirst's own established myelopathy exception. Furthermore, state laws (CIC §10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014) explicitly prohibit requiring further conservative treatment for this member's condition due to objective neurological compromise and documented contraindications/ineffectiveness of further therapy. Consistent with these state laws, generally accepted clinical guidelines from authoritative bodies such as AANS/CNS, NASS, and AO Spine strongly recommend surgical intervention without requiring a prolonged conservative trial for progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia.

Therefore, the requested ACDF C4-C5 (CPT 22551) is determined to be medically necessary and the prior authorization denial is **OVERTURNED**. Authorization for CPT 22551 is hereby **APPROVED**.
