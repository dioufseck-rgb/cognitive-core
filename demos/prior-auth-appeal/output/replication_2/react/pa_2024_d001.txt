**Member:** Sandra White, M-334466, Age 44

**Reviewer:** Dr. Alan Foster, MD (Initial Review Level)

### I. Introduction

This appeal review addresses the prior authorization denial for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C6-C7 for member Sandra White. The original denial, issued on 2024-10-05, cited "MED-003: Does not meet criteria. Conservative treatment incomplete. Physical therapy and medication requirements not satisfied." The denial notice provided a generic reference to plan criteria without specific section numbers or criteria. The appeal basis highlights procedural deficiencies in the denial notice and claims the member cannot meaningfully appeal.

The review is conducted by an expert health plan appeals reviewer applying the hierarchical framework of State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.

### II. Clinical Information Summary

*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Cervical radiculopathy C6-C7.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-08-15):** C6-C7 disc herniation, moderate right C7 nerve root compression. **No cord contact. No myelomalacia.**
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-08-20):** Right C7 dermatomal numbness, tricep weakness 4/5 right, tricep reflex diminished.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 4 weeks, 12 sessions completed. Member claims functional plateau, but PT discharge note does not use that specific language.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Ibuprofen 800mg TID for 6 weeks, with reported partial relief (one agent).
    *   **Interventional Treatment:** None documented.

### III. Plan's Stated Denial Reason

The denial reason provided by HealthFirst was: "Does not meet criteria. Conservative treatment incomplete. Physical therapy and medication requirements not satisfied." The notice referenced "Generic reference to plan criteria" without specifying the exact criteria or section numbers and did not include an Independent Medical Review (IMR) notice.

### IV. Appeal Basis

The member's appeal is based on the following:
1.  The denial notice fails to specify which criteria were not met.
2.  The denial does not include an IMR notice, as required by California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31.
3.  The member cannot meaningfully appeal without knowing the specific criteria that failed.

### V. Reviewer's Analysis

This review will proceed according to the established hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.

**A. Procedural Adequacy of Denial (State Law Review)**

The initial point of review is the procedural adequacy of the denial notice, as mandated by California state law.
*   **CHSC § 1374.31(b)** explicitly states: "A denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is DEFECTIVE and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."
*   The clinical record and appeal basis confirm that the original denial notice issued by Dr. Alan Foster on 2024-10-05 suffered from multiple procedural defects:
    *   It did not specify the exact clinical criteria applied.
    *   It did not include notice of the member's right to IMR.
    *   It provided only a generic reason ("conservative treatment incomplete") without specific reference to which requirements (e.g., specific plan criteria sections) were not met.

**Conclusion on Procedural Adequacy:** The denial notice is procedurally defective in direct violation of CHSC § 1374.31(b). This state law mandates that such a defective denial shall be treated "as if no denial was issued." A procedurally defective denial cannot be upheld.

**B. Substantive Medical Necessity Review (Applying Hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria)**

While the procedural defect alone warrants overturning the denial, a substantive review is conducted to understand the clinical context.

1.  **Clinical Presentation Classification:**
    *   Based on the MRI findings ("moderate right C7 nerve root compression. No cord contact. No myelomalacia.") and neurological exam (right C7 dermatomal numbness, tricep weakness 4/5, diminished tricep reflex), the primary diagnosis is **cervical radiculopathy**.
    *   The absence of myelomalacia, cord contact, and classical upper motor neuron signs (e.g., hyperreflexia, Hoffman sign, gait disturbance) confirms this is not cervical myelopathy as defined by clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021, and the plan's own criteria Section 2.1(b)).
    *   This distinction is critical as treatment algorithms and conservative treatment requirements differ significantly for radiculopathy versus myelopathy (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.1).

2.  **HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
    *   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** The member meets criteria 2.1(a) (cervical radiculopathy) and 2.1(c) (cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit).
    *   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
        *   **(a) Physical therapy:** Requires a "minimum 6 weeks." The member completed 4 weeks. **Requirement not met.**
        *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** Requires "at least two... for minimum 4 weeks each." The member used Ibuprofen for 6 weeks (one agent). **Requirement not met.**
        *   **(c) Interventional treatment:** Requires "one of the following interventional treatments." None were documented. **Requirement not met.**
    *   **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY:** This exception modifies conservative treatment requirements for "confirmed cervical myelopathy." As established, the member has radiculopathy, not myelopathy. Therefore, this exception **does not apply.**

    *   **Conclusion (Plan Criteria):** Under the plan's own criteria, the member has not completed the required conservative treatment for radiculopathy.

3.  **California State Law Overrides for Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This law waives PT requirements under specific conditions:
        *   (a)(1) "Physician documents PT contraindicated or unlikely to benefit": No such documentation from Dr. Lee is provided.
        *   (a)(2) "Member completed PT and reached a functional plateau": While the member claims this, the PT discharge note does not explicitly use this language. Clinical standard defines functional plateau as "<10% improvement over 3 sessions OR therapist documents maximum benefit reached" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 5). This is not definitively met based on the provided notes.
        *   (a)(3) "Objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address": This typically applies to more severe, progressive conditions like myelopathy with myelomalacia. For radiculopathy without myelomalacia, physical therapy is generally considered a beneficial and appropriate conservative treatment (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 4 Table). The current condition of "moderate right C7 nerve root compression" for radiculopathy does not, by itself, universally contraindicate or make PT completely ineffective in the way severe myelopathy would.
        *   **Conclusion (CIC § 10169.5):** The conditions for waiving PT under CIC § 10169.5 are not clearly met for this case of radiculopathy.

    *   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This All-Plan Letter specifically addresses myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia, providing exceptions for conservative treatment. Since the member has radiculopathy and specifically **no myelomalacia**, this APL **does not apply** to this case.

4.  **Clinical Standard Review (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
    *   For cervical radiculopathy, clinical standards confirm that conservative treatment is typically effective (70-80% resolution) and that surgery is generally indicated after 6-12 weeks of persistent symptoms despite conservative treatment (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.1, TIER 2(b), and Section 4 Table).
    *   The 6-week PT requirement in the plan criteria aligns with the generally accepted duration for conservative treatment trials for radiculopathy (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 4 Table).
    *   The member has completed only 4 weeks of PT and only one pharmacotherapy agent, with no interventional treatment. This falls short of generally accepted conservative treatment guidelines for radiculopathy.

**C. Resolution of Conflicts:**

The most significant finding is the procedural defect in the denial notice, which directly violates California state law (CHSC § 1374.31(b)). This violation means the denial, as issued, is invalid. State law takes precedence over all other criteria.

While a substantive review indicates that the member's request for surgery for radiculopathy does not, at this time, meet either the plan's criteria or generally accepted clinical standards due to incomplete conservative treatment, the procedural invalidity of the denial itself is paramount. A plan cannot issue a legally defective denial and expect it to be upheld.

Therefore, the denial must be overturned on procedural grounds. This does not equate to an approval of the requested surgical procedure but rather invalidates the current denial, requiring the plan to issue a procedurally compliant notice if it wishes to deny the service again.

### VI. Disposition

**OVERTURN**

### VII. Warrant

The denial is **OVERTURNED** based on a critical procedural defect in violation of California State Law.

1.  **Violation of CHSC § 1374.31(b):** The denial notice failed to specify the exact clinical criteria applied, did not provide the specific clinical reason for the denial beyond a generic statement, and notably did not include the member's right to Independent Medical Review (IMR). CHSC § 1374.31(b) mandates that a denial notice with these omissions is "DEFECTIVE and shall be treated as if no denial was issued." As such, the denial dated 2024-10-05 is procedurally invalid and cannot be upheld.
2.  **Hierarchy of Authority:** State law takes precedence over plan criteria and clinical standards when in conflict. The procedural requirements for a denial notice are statutory, and their violation directly impacts the validity of the denial itself.
3.  **Substantive Medical Necessity (for context only):** It is noted that, substantively, the requested surgical procedure does not currently meet HealthFirst's plan criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2) or generally accepted clinical standards for cervical radiculopathy (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) due to incomplete conservative treatment (4 weeks of PT instead of 6+, one pharmacotherapy agent instead of two, and no interventional treatment). Furthermore, state law provisions that waive conservative treatment requirements (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) do not apply to this specific clinical presentation of radiculopathy without myelomalacia. However, these substantive issues cannot override the fundamental procedural defect of the denial notice.

**Therefore, the HealthFirst denial for PA-2024-D001 is overturned due to its procedural inadequacy. HealthFirst is required to issue a new, procedurally compliant denial notice if it intends to deny the service based on medical necessity, or to approve the service.**
