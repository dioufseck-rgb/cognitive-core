**WARRANT**

This appeal determination is based on a comprehensive review of the provided case information, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria, relevant California regulatory framework, and generally accepted clinical standards, applied in strict adherence to the hierarchy of authority: **State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.**

**1. Procedural Adequacy of the Denial (State Law Review):**
The initial denial notice dated 2024-10-05 is procedurally defective and non-compliant with California state law.
*   **CHSC 1374.31(b) mandates that a denial notice SHALL:**
    *   Specify the exact clinical criteria applied.
    *   Include the specific clinical reason for the denial.
    *   Provide notice of the member's right to Independent Medical Review (IMR).
*   **Review Findings:** The denial notice in Case ID PA-2024-D001 failed on all three counts. It used a generic reference to "plan criteria" without section numbers or specific criteria, stated "Conservative treatment incomplete. Physical therapy and medication requirements not satisfied" without specific clinical details, and did not include notice of IMR rights.
*   **Consequence under CHSC 1374.31(b):** "A denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is DEFECTIVE and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."
*   **Further Consequence under CHSC 1367.01 (Part IV.3):** California law stipulates that if a plan fails to issue a decision within required timeframes (and a defective denial treated "as if no denial was issued" constitutes such a failure), the request is **DEEMED APPROVED** for the period of the delay.

The plan's failure to issue a procedurally compliant denial notice, as explicitly required by California Health & Safety Code Section 1374.31(b), constitutes a fundamental violation of the member's statutory rights and renders the denial invalid. Under the governing hierarchy, state law dictates the outcome here.

**2. Clinical Presentation and Applicability of Criteria/Standards:**
*   **Diagnosis:** The member's presenting diagnosis is "Cervical radiculopathy C6-C7." Imaging shows "C6-C7 disc herniation, moderate right C7 nerve root compression. No cord contact. No myelomalacia." The neurological exam findings (right C7 dermatomal numbness, tricep weakness 4/5 right, tricep reflex diminished) are consistent with radiculopathy.
*   **Myelopathy Exception (Plan Criteria 2.2(A) & State Law DMHC APL 22-014, CIC 10169.5):** The clinical record clearly indicates radiculopathy, not myelopathy. There is no evidence of spinal cord contact, myelomalacia, or upper motor neuron signs consistent with myelopathy. Therefore, the myelopathy-specific exceptions in HealthFirst's plan criteria (Section 2.2(A)), DMHC APL 22-014, and portions of CIC 10169.5 regarding waiver of conservative treatment for urgent neurological conditions or myelomalacia are **not applicable** to this case.

**3. Substantive Review (Plan Criteria & Clinical Standards for Radiculopathy - *Secondary to Procedural Defect*):**
*   **HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2):** For cervical radiculopathy, the plan's standard conservative treatment requirements are:
    *   (a) Minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. (Member completed 4 weeks)
    *   (b) Trial of at least two pharmacotherapy agents for minimum 4 weeks each. (Member completed 6 weeks of one agent, Ibuprofen)
    *   (c) One interventional treatment. (Member had none)
*   **Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020):** For radiculopathy, surgical intervention is generally considered a reasonable option after "6-12 weeks conservative tx" (AANS/CNS Tier 2).
*   **Substantive Conclusion:** Had the denial been procedurally adequate, a substantive review would likely find that the member's conservative treatment (4 weeks PT, one medication, no interventional procedures) is incomplete when measured against both the plan's criteria for radiculopathy and generally accepted clinical guidelines for radiculopathy.

**4. Conflict Resolution:**
While a substantive review of the clinical evidence against HealthFirst's plan criteria and generally accepted clinical standards for radiculopathy suggests that conservative treatment requirements were not fully met, the procedural defect in the denial notice takes precedence. State law is the highest authority. The failure to provide a compliant denial notice, as outlined in CHSC 1374.31(b), renders the denial invalid and requires that it be **OVERTURNED**. This action is mandated by state law, which governs the legality and validity of the denial itself, irrespective of the underlying medical necessity findings.

---

**CONCLUSION**

The denial of prior authorization for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) for member Sandra White is **OVERTURNED**.

This determination is made based on the plan's failure to comply with California Health & Safety Code Section 1374.31(b) regarding the issuance of a procedurally adequate denial notice. The denial notice was defective because it failed to specify the exact clinical criteria applied, the specific clinical reason for the denial, and did not include notice of the member's right to Independent Medical Review (IMR).

Per CHSC 1374.31(b), such a defective denial is to be treated "as if no denial was issued." Consequently, under CHSC 1367.01 (Part IV.3), the request for prior authorization is deemed approved due to the plan's failure to issue a valid and timely decision.

HealthFirst must now approve the requested procedure for Sandra White.
