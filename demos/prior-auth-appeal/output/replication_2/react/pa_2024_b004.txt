**To the Member, Kevin Watkins, and Treating Physician, Dr. Sarah Martinez:**

This letter serves as the determination of your appeal regarding the prior authorization denial for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C5-C6. Your request was initially denied on 2024-11-10, citing that the "surgical request is premature" as "Member's symptoms have been present for only 6 weeks" and "Plan criteria require at least 6 weeks of symptoms before surgical consideration. Recent onset radiculopathy has a high rate of spontaneous resolution."

We have completed a comprehensive review of your appeal, clinical records, HealthFirst Plan Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California state regulations, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice. Our review applies the hierarchy of authority: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.

**Clinical Summary:**
Mr. Kevin Watkins, a 41-year-old professional violinist, presented with acute cervical radiculopathy at C5-C6 with significant functional impairment in his right hand. MRI dated 2024-10-01 demonstrated a large C5-C6 disc herniation with moderate right C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis. Importantly, there was no evidence of spinal cord contact or myelomalacia. Neurological examination showed right C6 dermatomal numbness, diminished right bicep reflex, 4/5 right grip strength, and documented inability to perform violin fingering tasks due to fine motor deficits.

Over the past 6 weeks, Mr. Watkins underwent intensive conservative treatment, including:
*   **Physical Therapy:** 6 weeks (18 sessions) with documented functional plateau, no improvement in fine motor deficits or radicular symptoms, and occupational therapy confirming inability to perform professional duties.
*   **Pharmacotherapy:** 6 weeks of Methylprednisolone (dose pack x3) providing temporary partial relief, and 5 weeks of Gabapentin providing partial pain relief but no motor improvement.
*   **Interventional Treatment:** One right C6 epidural steroid injection (2024-10-28) resulting in only 25% temporary relief, no motor improvement, and symptoms returning to baseline within 2 weeks.

**Appeal Basis:**
The appeal highlighted that Mr. Watkins is a professional musician whose right hand weakness is career-ending, all conservative treatment completed within the 6-week period has failed, a functional plateau has been reached, and his physician recommends surgery given the severe functional impairment.

---

### **Review and Determination:**

**1. Primary Clinical Presentation:**
Based on the clinical record, including the MRI findings (moderate right C6 nerve root compression, no cord contact, no myelomalacia) and neurological exam (unilateral dermatomal numbness, diminished reflex, weakness in C6 distribution), Mr. Watkins' primary diagnosis is **Cervical Radiculopathy**, not Myelopathy. This distinction is critical for applying appropriate conservative treatment duration guidelines.

**2. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
*   **Section 2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** Met. Confirmed cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficit and supporting MRI.
*   **Section 2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:**
    *   **2.2(a) Physical Therapy:** The plan requires a "minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy." Mr. Watkins completed 6 weeks of intensive PT.
    *   **2.2(b) Pharmacotherapy:** The plan requires a trial of "at least two... for minimum 4 weeks each." Mr. Watkins completed trials of Methylprednisolone (6 weeks) and Gabapentin (5 weeks).
    *   **2.2(c) Interventional Treatment:** The plan requires "one... interventional treatment." Mr. Watkins received a right C6 epidural steroid injection.
    *   **Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** Mr. Watkins has *met all explicit conservative treatment requirements* outlined in HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2.
*   **Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A):** Not applicable, as this is a case of radiculopathy.
*   **Plan's Denial Rationale Analysis:** The denial states "Member's symptoms have been present for only 6 weeks. Plan criteria require at least 6 weeks of symptoms before surgical consideration." This specific criterion (6 weeks of *symptoms* as a prerequisite for surgical consideration, distinct from 6 weeks of PT) is not explicitly detailed in the provided HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 document. However, even if such an implicit criterion exists, it is subject to review under state law and clinical standards.

**3. Application of California Regulatory Requirements:**
*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This state law is paramount in this case.
    *   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2):** This provision prohibits an insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
        *   **Application:** Mr. Watkins has completed 6 weeks of intensive PT, which is documented to have reached a "functional plateau" with "no improvement in fine motor deficits or radicular symptoms," and occupational therapy has confirmed "inability to perform professional duties." This precisely fulfills the conditions of CIC § 10169.5(a)(2).
    *   **CIC § 10169.5(c):** This provision further states that "A plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau..."
        *   **Application:** The plan's denial, based solely on the 6-week duration of symptoms, without adequately considering the documented functional plateau and the failure of intensive conservative treatment to improve critical neurological deficits and functional impairment, constitutes a mechanical application of a duration requirement explicitly prohibited by this statute.
*   **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) — IMR Standard of Review:** This code specifies that for IMR, the service SHALL be approved if it meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, "regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." This principle applies to the internal appeal process as well, requiring consideration of clinical standards beyond plan criteria.
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:** While the specific myelopathy and myelomalacia provisions are not directly applicable, the underlying principle that "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice" is relevant.

**4. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
*   **Cervical Radiculopathy Treatment:** The clinical guidelines acknowledge that conservative treatment is often effective for radiculopathy. However, they also identify specific circumstances warranting surgical intervention.
*   **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023):** For ACDF, surgery is considered a "TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION" for:
    *   "(b) Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx"
    *   "(c) Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care"
    *   **Application:** Mr. Watkins is at the 6-week mark of persistent symptoms after a comprehensive, intensive, and failed conservative treatment regimen. More importantly, his "significant functional impairment" (inability to perform his occupation as a professional violinist due to right hand fine motor deficits) that is "not responding to conservative care" strongly supports surgical intervention under generally accepted clinical standards. While spontaneous resolution can occur in recent onset radiculopathy, this typically applies to milder cases or those without failed intensive, multi-modal conservative therapy and career-ending functional deficits.
*   **Functional Plateau (Clinical Standard):** Clinical standards define a functional plateau as a documented failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement, or therapist documentation of maximum benefit reached. The clinical record here clearly meets this definition, particularly regarding the lack of improvement in neurological symptoms (motor deficits).

**5. Conflict Resolution (Hierarchy: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria):**
The HealthFirst PPO Gold plan's denial, based on the duration of symptoms being only 6 weeks, directly conflicts with California State Law. **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)** mandates that approval cannot be conditioned on further physical therapy if a functional plateau has been reached after a course of therapy, which has explicitly occurred in this case. Furthermore, **CIC § 10169.5(c)** prohibits the mechanical application of duration requirements when a functional plateau has been achieved.

Beyond state law, the clinical standard supports surgical intervention given the severity of functional impairment impacting Mr. Watkins' livelihood, and the documented failure of comprehensive conservative therapy to address his neurological deficits, even at the 6-week mark, which falls within the range for surgical consideration for persistent radiculopathy. The principle from CHSC § 1374.32(b) reinforces that services meeting generally accepted standards shall be approved regardless of more restrictive plan criteria.

---

**Disposition:** **OVERTURNED**

**Warrant:**
The denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) is **OVERTURNED**.

The HealthFirst PPO Gold plan's denial was based on the premise that the "surgical request is premature" due to "Member's symptoms have been present for only 6 weeks," implying that the conservative treatment period was insufficient. However, the clinical record clearly demonstrates that the member has completed a 6-week course of intensive physical therapy, along with appropriate pharmacotherapy and interventional treatment, fully satisfying the explicit conservative treatment requirements of HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2.

Crucially, the member's treating physical therapist and occupational therapist have documented that a "functional plateau" has been reached at 6 weeks, with no improvement in fine motor deficits or radicular symptoms, and an inability to perform professional duties (violinist). This directly triggers **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2)**, which states that a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." The plan's mechanical application of a symptom duration requirement, despite a documented functional plateau, is prohibited under **CIC § 10169.5(c)**.

Furthermore, generally accepted clinical standards, as outlined in the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), recognize "significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care" as a reasonable indication (Tier 2) for surgical intervention in radiculopathy, even within the 6-12 week timeframe for persistent symptoms. Mr. Watkins' career-ending inability to perform as a professional violinist due to his fine motor deficits, despite comprehensive conservative treatment, represents such a significant functional impairment.

Therefore, in adherence to the established hierarchy of authority where State Law and Clinical Standards govern over plan criteria, the denial for ACDF C5-C6 is overturned. The requested procedure is deemed medically necessary.
