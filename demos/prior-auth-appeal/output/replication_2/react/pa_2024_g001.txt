### I. INTRODUCTION

This letter serves as the appeal determination for the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 for Mr. Marco Reyes. The initial request was denied on 2024-11-01 by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, of HealthFirst Utilization Management. This appeal has been reviewed in accordance with HealthFirst's internal appeal processes, California state law, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.

### II. CASE SUMMARY

**Original Request:** Prior authorization for CPT 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) for a diagnosis of persistent right-sided C6-C7 cervical radiculopathy.

**Original Denial Reason (MED-003):** "Member does not meet conservative treatment requirements under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. Physical therapy: member has completed only 3 weeks of physical therapy. Standard pathway requires 6 weeks minimum. Functional plateau not documented. All other criteria remain unmet."

**Appeal Basis:** The member's treating provider asserts that:
1.  The denial miscounts physical therapy (PT) duration; the member completed 3 weeks at Valley PT and 6 weeks at Capital PT, totaling 9 weeks.
2.  Records from both PT providers were submitted with the original authorization request.
3.  PT discharge from Capital PT explicitly documents a functional plateau.
4.  All other conservative treatment criteria (pharmacotherapy and interventional injection) were completed and documented.

### III. CLINICAL FINDINGS

Upon review of the submitted clinical record, the following relevant findings are noted:

*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Cervical radiculopathy C6-C7, right-sided, persistent.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-08-20):** Reveals a C6-C7 large disc herniation with moderate right C7 nerve root compression and right foraminal stenosis. Crucially, there is **no cord contact and no myelomalacia** documented.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-08-25):** Documents right C7 dermatomal numbness, diminished right tricep reflex, and 4/5 right grip strength. **No bilateral findings and no upper motor neuron (UMN) signs** are noted. These findings are consistent with radiculopathy, not myelopathy.
*   **Conservative Treatment History:**
    *   **Physical Therapy (PT):**
        *   Valley Physical Therapy: 2024-06-01 to 2024-06-21 (3 weeks, 9 sessions). Outcome: Partial improvement, symptoms persist.
        *   Capital Physical Therapy: 2024-07-08 to 2024-08-16 (6 weeks, 18 sessions). Outcome: Functional plateau reached. Discharge summary explicitly states: "Maximum therapeutic benefit achieved. No further improvement expected from continued PT."
        *   **Total PT duration: 9 weeks (exceeds plan's 6-week minimum). Functional plateau explicitly documented.** Both PT records were submitted with the original prior authorization request.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Gabapentin 600mg TID for 4 months (partial pain relief, radicular symptoms persist) and Naproxen 500mg BID for 3 months (inadequate response).
    *   **Interventional Treatment:** Right C7 epidural steroid injection on 2024-09-15, providing 35% temporary relief that returned to baseline within 3 weeks.

### IV. REVIEW STANDARDS AND ANALYSIS

This appeal is reviewed applying the three-source hierarchy: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.**

**A. Primary Clinical Presentation:**
Based on the MRI findings (no cord contact, no myelomalacia) and neurological exam (no bilateral findings, no UMN signs), the primary diagnosis is **cervical radiculopathy**, not cervical myelopathy. This distinction is critical as it dictates the applicability of various criteria and exceptions. The "Myelopathy Exception" in HealthFirst's policy and specific state/clinical guidance regarding myelopathy urgency (e.g., DMHC APL 22-014 on myelomalacia) are not directly applicable to this member's primary diagnosis.

**B. HealthFirst Plan Clinical Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
The original denial cited non-compliance with Section 2.2, specifically the physical therapy requirement.

1.  **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficit and supporting imaging. **MET.**
2.  **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **(a) Physical Therapy:** The denial stated the member completed "only 3 weeks" of PT and that a "functional plateau not documented." This is factually incorrect. The submitted records clearly show **9 weeks of supervised physical therapy** (3 weeks at Valley PT + 6 weeks at Capital PT), which **exceeds the plan's 6-week minimum requirement.** Furthermore, the Capital PT discharge summary explicitly documents that "Maximum therapeutic benefit achieved. No further improvement expected from continued PT," confirming a **documented functional plateau.** Therefore, the plan's criterion for physical therapy **IS MET.**
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** The member completed trials of two pharmacotherapy agents (Gabapentin for 4 months, Naproxen for 3 months). **MET.**
    *   **(c) Interventional Treatment:** The member underwent one cervical epidural steroid injection. **MET.**
    *   **Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A):** This exception is not applicable as the member's primary diagnosis is radiculopathy.
3.  **2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:** Current MRI confirms pathology at C6-C7 consistent with symptoms. **MET.**
4.  **2.4 PHYSICIAN DOCUMENTATION:** Not a basis for the denial, and it is implied that the treating physician provided necessary attestations. **PRESUMED MET.**

**Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The HealthFirst Utilization Management denial was based on a **factual error** regarding the duration of physical therapy completed and the documentation of a functional plateau. When the facts are correctly applied, Mr. Reyes has fully met HealthFirst's conservative treatment requirements as outlined in CC-SPINE-2024.

**C. California Regulatory Framework:**

1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):**
    This state law prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where:
    *   **(a)(2):** "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
        *   Mr. Reyes completed 9 weeks of physical therapy, and the Capital PT discharge summary explicitly documents a functional plateau while symptoms persist. This criterion **is met.**
    *   **(c):** "A plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau..."
        *   The original denial's mechanical application of a 6-week minimum, based on an erroneous 3-week count and ignoring documented plateau, directly conflicts with this state regulation.

2.  **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) (Standard of Review for IMR):**
    While this is an internal appeal and not an Independent Medical Review (IMR), the principle that services meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice SHALL be approved regardless of whether plan's internal criteria are more restrictive is a guiding principle for all medical necessity determinations in California.

**Conclusion on State Law:** California state law explicitly supports that Mr. Reyes cannot be required to undergo further physical therapy given his completion of a course of therapy and documented functional plateau. The plan's denial, based on a misapplication of its PT requirement, is contrary to CIC § 10169.5.

**D. Clinical Evidence Base (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

1.  **Distinction:** The clinical references clearly distinguish between cervical radiculopathy and myelopathy, highlighting that conservative treatment evidence for myelopathy is limited, and delays may be harmful, especially with myelomalacia. In this case, Mr. Reyes has **radiculopathy**, not myelopathy, so the general conservative treatment approach for radiculopathy applies.
2.  **Surgical Indications for Radiculopathy:** Per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update), ACDF (CPT 22551) for radiculopathy falls under **TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION** when there is "persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and "significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care."
    *   Mr. Reyes has persistent right C7 radicular symptoms (numbness, diminished reflex, 4/5 grip strength).
    *   He has undergone an adequate conservative trial, including 9 weeks of PT, 4 months of pharmacotherapy, and a cervical epidural injection.
    *   A functional plateau has been reached with persistent neurological deficits and symptoms.
    *   These findings **meet the generally accepted clinical indications for surgical intervention** for cervical radiculopathy.

**Conclusion on Clinical Standards:** The extensive conservative treatment undertaken by Mr. Reyes, including 9 weeks of PT with documented functional plateau, multiple pharmacotherapies, and an epidural injection, aligns with and exceeds the conservative treatment duration generally accepted by clinical guidelines for radiculopathy before surgical intervention is considered.

### V. CONFLICT RESOLUTION (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria)

The original denial's basis (failure to meet PT requirement) is directly contradicted by the factual evidence in the appeal. Mr. Reyes completed 9 weeks of PT, exceeding the 6-week minimum in CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a), and a functional plateau was explicitly documented, which is consistent with the plan's requirement for "documented functional outcomes." Even if there were a conflict with the plan's criteria (which there is not, once facts are corrected), California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2) clearly states that a plan cannot deny based on PT completion where a functional plateau has been reached. Furthermore, generally accepted clinical standards support surgical intervention for persistent radiculopathy after a comprehensive conservative trial, which Mr. Reyes has completed.

### VI. DISPOSITION

**OVERTURN**

### VII. WARRANT

The denial of prior authorization for ACDF C6-C7 for Mr. Marco Reyes (PA-2024-G001) is hereby **OVERTURNED**.

The initial denial was based on an inaccurate factual assessment of the member's completed physical therapy and documentation of a functional plateau. The appeal and comprehensive review of the clinical record confirm the following:
1.  **Factual Error Corrected:** Mr. Reyes completed a total of **9 weeks of supervised physical therapy**, exceeding the HealthFirst plan criterion of a "minimum 6 weeks" as per CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a).
2.  **Functional Plateau Documented:** The Capital PT discharge summary explicitly documents that "Maximum therapeutic benefit achieved. No further improvement expected from continued PT," fulfilling the plan's implicit requirement for functional outcomes and explicitly meeting the definition of a functional plateau under both the Clinical Reference Summary (Section 5) and California Insurance Code § 10169.5(b).
3.  **State Law Compliance:** Under California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2), a health insurer cannot condition approval of surgery on further physical therapy when the member has completed a course of therapy and reached a documented functional plateau. HealthFirst's denial, therefore, violates state law.
4.  **Clinical Standard Compliance:** The member's persistent cervical radiculopathy, objective neurological deficits, and documented failure to respond to an extensive conservative treatment regimen (9 weeks PT, 4 months pharmacotherapy, one epidural injection) meet the generally accepted clinical indications for ACDF as a Tier 2 reasonable indication, per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update).
5.  **All Other Plan Criteria Met:** The member has also satisfied the plan's diagnosis (2.1), pharmacotherapy (2.2b), interventional treatment (2.2c), and imaging (2.3) requirements.

Therefore, authorization for CPT 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) is approved as medically necessary.
