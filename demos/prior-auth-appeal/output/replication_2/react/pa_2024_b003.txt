Dear Ms. Morrison and Dr. Nguyen,

This letter serves as the final determination for the appeal of the prior authorization denial for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C3-C4 for Ms. Angela Morrison.

The initial denial, issued on 2024-10-01, stated the following reason (Reason Code: MED-003): "Member meets conservative treatment requirements. However, imaging does not show sufficient pathology at C3-C4 to support surgical intervention. MRI shows mild degenerative changes. Procedure not medically necessary."

Upon comprehensive review of the submitted clinical record, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, California regulatory framework (CIC, CHSC, DMHC APL), and generally accepted clinical standards, the denial of prior authorization is **OVERTURNED**.

### Rationale for Overturn:

The decision to overturn is based on the following findings, which demonstrate that the requested procedure is medically necessary under HealthFirst's own criteria, California state law, and generally accepted clinical guidelines:

1.  **Objective Imaging Demonstrates Sufficient Pathology:**
    *   The original denial relied solely on the MRI findings, stating "MRI shows mild degenerative changes" and "insufficient pathology." However, the clinical record clearly indicates that the initial MRI (2024-08-15) was significantly limited by "SIGNIFICANT ARTIFACT from C4-C5 hardware limiting evaluation."
    *   To overcome this limitation, a **CT myelogram** was appropriately performed on 2024-09-10. This CT myelogram definitively revealed "C3-C4: moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression confirmed. Significant foraminal stenosis. MRI underestimated pathology due to hardware artifact from prior fusion."
    *   HealthFirst's own Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1) explicitly states that "MRI or CT myelogram within 6 months of request demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms" is required. The CT myelogram directly addresses the imaging requirement by demonstrating objective, severe structural pathology at the symptomatic level, directly refuting the denial's claim of insufficient pathology. The denial's failure to account for the CT myelogram constitutes a flawed clinical reason for denial.

2.  **Conservative Treatment Requirements Met and Failed:**
    *   The initial denial correctly noted that "Member meets conservative treatment requirements." This is confirmed by the clinical record:
        *   **Physical Therapy (CC-SPINE-2024, 2.2a):** Ms. Morrison completed 10 weeks (30 sessions) of physical therapy, exceeding the 6-week minimum requirement. Documentation clearly states "No improvement in left C4 radicular symptoms," indicating a functional plateau.
        *   **Pharmacotherapy (CC-SPINE-2024, 2.2b):** Ms. Morrison trialed Pregabalin for 6 months (partial relief) and Duloxetine for 3 months (minimal benefit), meeting the requirement for two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
        *   **Interventional Treatment (CC-SPINE-2024, 2.2c):** A left C4 epidural steroid injection was performed, providing only "Good initial relief (60%), returned to baseline by week 3," satisfying the requirement for one interventional procedure.
    *   Furthermore, **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5** states that a plan cannot condition approval on completion of physical therapy where the member has completed therapy and reached a functional plateau (as documented here) or where there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address (as confirmed by the CT myelogram).

3.  **Medical Necessity Under Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:**
    *   Ms. Morrison's clinical presentation involves cervical radiculopathy at C3-C4, with objective neurological deficits (left C4 dermatomal numbness, deltoid weakness 3+/5, diminished bicep reflex) and significant functional limitations ("No improvement in left C4 radicular symptoms").
    *   The CT myelogram confirms "moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression" and "significant foraminal stenosis," which correlates directly with her symptoms.
    *   According to **AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine (2023 Update) and NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020)**, surgical intervention for radiculopathy is a "TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION" when patients present with "persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and "significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care." Ms. Morrison clearly meets these criteria.
    *   The argument that the procedure is "not medically necessary" is directly contradicted by these widely accepted clinical guidelines, particularly when considering the full and accurate imaging findings.

4.  **California Regulatory Precedent:**
    *   **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b)** mandates that during an appeal review, if a service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice," it **SHALL** be approved "regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." In this case, the procedure meets generally accepted standards for radiculopathy with failed conservative treatment and objective nerve root compression.
    *   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (II.3)** further clarifies that "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice... Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs." The plan's interpretation that the imaging does not support intervention, by ignoring the CT myelogram, effectively creates a criterion more restrictive than generally accepted standards.

### Conclusion:

The original denial's premise that imaging does not show sufficient pathology at C3-C4 is unsubstantiated when all available clinical evidence, particularly the CT myelogram, is considered. The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits and has exhausted an appropriate and documented course of conservative treatment without improvement. This clinical presentation, coupled with the definitive imaging findings, meets the criteria for medical necessity under HealthFirst's policy, California state law, and generally accepted clinical standards.

Therefore, the prior authorization denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C3-C4) for Ms. Angela Morrison is **OVERTURNED**. HealthFirst is directed to approve the requested procedure.
