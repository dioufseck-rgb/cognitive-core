**I. BACKGROUND**

Diana Torres, a 61-year-old member, presented with a 4-month history of progressive bilateral hand clumsiness and gait instability. Neurological examination revealed significant upper motor neuron signs (bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia in all four extremities, positive Babinski bilaterally, and gait ataxia). MRI imaging dated 2024-08-15 confirmed severe central cord compression at C5-C6 with T2 hyperintensity within the spinal cord, indicating myelomalacia. The treating neurosurgeon, Dr. James Kim, requested prior authorization for ACDF C5-C6.

The request was denied by HealthFirst on 2024-11-01, citing a failure to meet conservative treatment requirements under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, specifically regarding the duration of physical therapy (2 weeks completed vs. 6 weeks required) and the number of pharmacotherapy agents trialed (one agent for 2 weeks vs. two agents for 4+ weeks each).

The appeal submitted on behalf of Ms. Torres asserts that the denial is inappropriate given the presence of severe myelomalacia and a treating physician's declaration that physical therapy is contraindicated, invoking California Insurance Code (CIC) 10169.5(a)(3).

---

**II. CLINICAL REVIEW AND APPLICATION OF CRITERIA**

This review applies the hierarchy of authority: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.**

**A. Diagnosis Classification:**
The clinical record unequivocally supports a diagnosis of **Progressive Cervical Myelopathy C5-C6 with Myelomalacia**. Key findings include:
*   Objective imaging evidence of severe central cord compression at C5-C6.
*   T2 hyperintensity (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord.
*   Clinical presentation of bilateral upper extremity clumsiness and gait instability.
*   Presence of multiple upper motor neuron signs (Hoffman, hyperreflexia, Babinski, ataxia).
This presentation indicates moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy, which is distinct from radiculopathy and carries different treatment considerations (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.1). Myelomalacia further signifies existing cord injury and potential for irreversible harm (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2).

**B. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
1.  **Section 2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** Met. Member has confirmed cervical myelopathy (2.1(b)) with supporting MRI documentation.
2.  **Section 2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:**
    *   The plan's denial correctly identified that the member did not complete 6 weeks of PT or trial two pharmacotherapy agents for 4+ weeks each, as required by the standard pathway of Section 2.2.
    *   **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY:** This exception reduces the PT requirement to 4 weeks and may waive the interventional requirement. The pharmacotherapy requirement remains in full.
        *   Even with this exception, the member completed only 2 weeks of PT (vs. 4 weeks required by exception) and one pharmacotherapy agent (vs. two required).
        *   The plan's denial explicitly cited the *standard* pathway (6 weeks PT), indicating it either failed to apply its own myelopathy exception or found it not met.
3.  **Section 2.3 Imaging Requirement:** Met. Current MRI shows severe central cord compression and myelomalacia consistent with symptoms.
4.  **Section 2.4 Physician Documentation:** Met. Dr. Kim's letter provides rationale, functional limitations, and urgency.

*Initial Conclusion Based Solely on Plan Criteria:* The member does not meet the conservative treatment requirements, even under the plan's myelopathy exception (2.2(A)), regarding PT duration (2 weeks vs. 4 weeks) and pharmacotherapy (1 agent vs. 2 agents). Therefore, a denial would be *upheld* if only plan criteria were considered in isolation.

**C. Application of California State Law:**
1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This statute directly addresses requirements for physical therapy prior to surgery.
    *   **CIC 10169.5(a)(1):** "A health insurer... shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where: The member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition."
        *   **MET.** Dr. Kim's letter explicitly states, "PT is contraindicated in this patient. Myelomalacia is present on initial MRI. Continuing PT risks irreversible cord injury."
    *   **CIC 10169.5(a)(3):** "A health insurer... shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where: The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
        *   **MET.** The MRI findings of "Severe central cord compression C5-C6" and "T2 hyperintensity within cord (myelomalacia)" constitute objective evidence of structural neurological compromise. Physical therapy cannot address this structural issue or reverse existing cord damage (myelomalacia).
    *   **CIC 10169.5(c):** "A plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or whether the condition involves neurological urgency under subdivision (a)(3)."
        *   The plan's denial applied its PT requirement mechanically (6 weeks or 4 weeks for myelopathy) without acknowledging the physician's contraindication or the presence of myelomalacia, which signifies neurological urgency.

2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 — Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions:** This guidance directly applies to the member's condition.
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent unless compelling clinical evidence supports otherwise."
        *   **MET.** The member's presentation precisely matches this description. The case should be treated as urgent.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** For conditions where delay risks irreversible harm (like this case), plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when:
        *   **(a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia...).** **MET.** Myelomalacia is present.
        *   **(b) Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression...** **MET.** PT was attempted for 2 weeks and caused symptom exacerbation, leading to discontinuation. Methylprednisolone offered only temporary, mild benefit. Dr. Kim documented urgency.
        *   **(c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines.** **MET.** (See Clinical Standard below).
    *   APL 22-014 explicitly states: "The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements even where a full course has not been completed, when the treating physician documents urgency."
        *   **This directly supports waiving the plan's conservative treatment requirements for both PT and pharmacotherapy in this case.**
    *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for conditions identified in this guidance. Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs."
        *   This reinforces that state law and clinical standards supersede the plan's more restrictive criteria.

**D. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
1.  **Significance of Myelomalacia:** Myelomalacia is a strong indicator for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials, denoting surgical urgency and a higher risk of permanent deficit if surgery is delayed (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2).
2.  **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:**
    *   For moderate-severe myelopathy, physical therapy does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement (Cochrane Review, 2021; NASS Guidelines, 2020).
    *   AO Spine Guidelines (2021) state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
    *   The 6-week (or even 4-week myelopathy exception) PT requirement is primarily derived from radiculopathy evidence and is a "category error" when applied to myelopathy (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.2).
3.  **Surgical Indication:** Ms. Torres's presentation (moderate-severe myelopathy, objective cord compression, myelomalacia, progressive neurological deficits including UMN signs and gait instability) meets **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION** for surgical intervention per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023, Section 3.1). These guidelines do NOT require a minimum course of PT as a prerequisite for surgery in this setting (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.2).

**III. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND DISPOSITION**

There is a direct conflict between HealthFirst's plan criteria for conservative treatment duration and both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice for cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia.

Applying the hierarchy: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.**

1.  **State Law (CIC 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) overrides Plan Criteria:**
    *   CIC 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3) explicitly prohibit conditioning surgery approval on PT completion where the treating physician documents contraindication or where objective structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) is present. Both conditions are met in this case. Therefore, HealthFirst cannot require further PT for Ms. Torres.
    *   DMHC APL 22-014 mandates that plans waive standard conservative treatment requirements for myelopathy with myelomalacia when urgency is documented by the physician, and prohibits plans from applying internal criteria more restrictive than clinical standards. This applies to both the PT and pharmacotherapy requirements in this case.

2.  **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS, AO Spine, NASS) overrides Plan Criteria:**
    *   The prevailing clinical evidence and guidelines do not support requiring a fixed duration of physical therapy or delaying surgery for extended conservative treatment in moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy, particularly in the presence of myelomalacia. Doing so is considered inconsistent with accepted standards of medical practice and may risk irreversible neurological deterioration.

Given that California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice directly contradict the basis for HealthFirst's denial, the plan's internal criteria, as applied, are superseded.

---

**IV. DISPOSITION**

**OVERTURN**

**V. WARRANT**

The prior authorization denial for ACDF C5-C6 for Diana Torres is hereby OVERTURNED. The member's condition, Progressive Cervical Myelopathy C5-C6 with Myelomalacia, constitutes an urgent surgical indication under generally accepted clinical standards, and the denial based on unfulfilled conservative treatment requirements is contrary to California state law and evidence-based medical guidelines.

Specifically:
1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3)** prohibits HealthFirst from conditioning approval of the surgical procedure on the completion of further physical therapy. The treating physician, Dr. Kim, documented that PT is contraindicated and risks irreversible cord injury due to the myelomalacia, and objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (severe cord compression with myelomalacia) is present on MRI. (California Insurance Code § 10169.5).
2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014** requires health plans to treat cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia as an urgent condition. The APL further mandates that plans shall not require completion of a full course of conservative treatment (including PT and pharmacotherapy) where objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia), the physician documents conservative treatment failure/urgency, and surgery is indicated by clinical guidelines. All these conditions are met. (DMHC APL 22-014, Sections II.1, II.2).
3.  **Generally Accepted Clinical Standards** (AANS/CNS Joint Section, NASS, AO Spine International) establish that moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia is a TIER 1 strong surgical indication. These guidelines explicitly state there is no high-quality evidence supporting mandatory physical therapy prior to surgery for this condition, and delaying surgery may lead to neurological deterioration. The plan's conservative treatment requirements are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for this specific diagnosis. (Clinical Reference Summary, Sections 1.2, 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 3.1, 3.2).

The plan's denial applied its criteria mechanically without regard for the specific and urgent clinical circumstances, the treating physician's expert medical opinion, or the superseding California state laws and clinical standards. The requested ACDF C5-C6 is medically necessary and should be authorized.
