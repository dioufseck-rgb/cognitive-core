**I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

This appeal concerns the denial of prior authorization for an anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 for Ms. Helen Brooks. The initial denial cited that the "Myelopathy exception does not apply" due to "minimal" MRI T2 signal change and the absence of an interventional procedure. Upon comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria, relevant California state laws and regulations, and generally accepted clinical standards, the denial is found to be based on a misapplication of the plan's own criteria and is also inconsistent with higher authoritative sources.

**Disposition: OVERTURN**

**II. CASE OVERVIEW**

Ms. Helen Brooks, a 49-year-old member with HealthFirst PPO Gold, was diagnosed with early cervical myelopathy with C4-C5 cord compression. Surgical intervention (ACDF C4-C5) was requested by her neurosurgeon, Dr. James Park. The initial prior authorization was denied by HealthFirst on 2024-11-20, citing denial reason MED-003, stating that the myelopathy exception did not apply due to minimal T2 signal change on MRI and the lack of an interventional procedure.

The appeal basis highlights a second radiologist's opinion confirming early myelomalacia and the treating physician's independent documentation of myelopathy on clinical grounds.

**III. CLINICAL RECORD SUMMARY**

*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Early cervical myelopathy with cord compression at C4-C5. Myelomalacia status is disputed between the plan's radiologist and the treating hospital's radiologist.
*   **Imaging:**
    *   Original MRI (2024-10-01) by Radiologist A noted C4-C5 disc herniation with cord contact and "Possible early T2 signal change — equivocal, may represent artifact."
    *   Second MRI read (2024-10-15) by Radiologist B (treating hospital) confirmed "C4-C5 cord compression with early myelomalacia. T2 hyperintensity is reproducible on multiple sequences and represents early cord signal change, not artifact. Consistent with early myelopathy."
    *   Plan radiologist review (2024-11-05) stated T2 signal change was "below threshold for clinical significance. Not consistent with myelomalacia. Represents artifact or normal variant."
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-10-05):** Significant objective findings consistent with myelopathy, including bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia in upper extremities, and subtle balance impairment on tandem gait. No lower extremity findings were noted.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** Completed 8 weeks (24 sessions). Documented "No improvement in UMN signs."
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Gabapentin 400mg TID for 3 months (partial benefit) and Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks (minimal benefit).
    *   **Interventional:** Not performed. The treating physician explicitly documented this was "due to myelopathy concern — epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated in cord compression."

**IV. APPLICATION OF HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY**

The review proceeds according to the established hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.

**A. Plan Criteria Review (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)**

1.  **Diagnosis (2.1):** The clinical record clearly confirms cervical myelopathy (2.1(b)) with objective neurological deficits (bilateral Hoffman, hyperreflexia, gait impairment) and MRI evidence of cord compression. This criterion is **MET**.
2.  **Conservative Treatment (2.2):** This was the primary basis for the denial.
    *   **Physical Therapy (2.2(a)):** 8 weeks completed. This exceeds the standard 6-week requirement and the myelopathy exception's 4-week minimum. This criterion is **MET**.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2(b)):** Two agents (Gabapentin for 3 months, Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks) were tried. This criterion is **MET**.
    *   **Interventional Treatment (2.2(c)):** This was not performed, which was a core reason for the denial.
    *   **Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A):** This exception explicitly states that for confirmed cervical myelopathy, the interventional treatment requirement (2.2(c)) is **WAIVED** if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration. The treating physician documented: "Treating physician did not refer for epidural due to myelopathy concern — epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated in cord compression." This directly fulfills the condition for waiver. The plan's statement in the denial that the "Myelopathy exception does not apply" is a **misapplication of its own criteria**. The myelopathy is confirmed by clinical exam (UMN signs), and the physician documented the risk of interventional treatment.

**Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The initial denial was incorrect even under HealthFirst's own criteria, as the myelopathy exception, which waives the interventional treatment, was met.

**B. California Regulatory Requirements (State Law)**

1.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):**
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** The clinical findings of bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, and gait impairment, coupled with MRI evidence of cord compression and the second radiologist's confirmation of early myelomalacia, classify this case as cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia. The DMHC guidance requires such cases to be treated as urgent due to potential for irreversible harm.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** This section states that plans **SHALL NOT** require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when: (a) objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia confirmed by Radiologist B), AND (b) the treating physician documents conservative treatment failure (8 weeks PT with no UMN sign improvement, partial/minimal pharma benefit, and interventional contraindicated due to risk), AND (c) surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines (as discussed below, it is). The APL specifically identifies myelomalacia as a finding supporting waiver of standard conservative requirements.
2.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):**
    *   This law stipulates that a plan shall not condition approval of surgery on PT completion where: (a)(3) "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The presence of cervical myelopathy with cord compression and objective upper motor neuron signs (Hoffman, hyperreflexia, gait impairment) constitutes such structural neurological compromise. While PT was completed for 8 weeks, this provision would legally override any remaining PT requirement if the plan tried to assert it.
3.  **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) (Standard of Review for IMR):**
    *   This code mandates that an IMR determination uses "generally accepted standards of medical practice" and specifically states that the service "SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." This provision ensures that even if HealthFirst's criteria were more stringent or conflicted, the prevailing clinical standards would govern.

**Conclusion on State Law:** California state regulations explicitly support waiving conservative treatment requirements for myelopathy cases with objective neurological compromise and myelomalacia, overriding more restrictive plan criteria. The plan's denial is inconsistent with these legal mandates.

**C. Clinical Standard Knowledge (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)**

1.  **Diagnostic Classification (Section 1):** The clinical presentation (bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, gait disturbance) unequivocally points to **cervical myelopathy**, not radiculopathy. The clinical standards emphasize that these are fundamentally different diseases requiring different treatment algorithms. Applying radiculopathy-derived conservative treatment protocols to myelopathy patients is a "category error."
2.  **Myelomalacia Significance (1.2):** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) indicates existing spinal cord injury, a higher risk of permanent deficit with delay, and is a strong indication for surgical intervention. While disputed by the plan's radiologist, the treating hospital radiologist confirmed early myelomalacia, and the robust clinical findings support the severity of the myelopathy.
3.  **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy (Section 2):**
    *   **Physical Therapy (2.1):** For moderate-severe myelopathy, physical therapy does **NOT** produce meaningful functional improvement. AO Spine Guidelines (2021) state there is "no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery... is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." Ms. Brooks completed 8 weeks of PT with "no improvement in UMN signs," consistent with this evidence.
    *   **Conservative Treatment Duration (2.2):** No randomized controlled trials support a defined minimum duration of conservative treatment before surgery for myelopathy. The 6-week PT requirement often derives from radiculopathy evidence.
    *   **Risk of Delay (2.3):** Delaying surgery for moderate-severe myelopathy, especially with myelomalacia, is associated with decreased neurological recovery.
4.  **Surgical Indications (Section 3):**
    *   Ms. Brooks meets **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION** for ACDF as per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) due to:
        *   (a) Moderate cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI.
        *   (b) Progressive neurological deficit (no improvement in UMN signs despite conservative treatment).
        *   (c) Myelomalacia on MRI (confirmed by treating hospital radiologist).
        *   (d) Objective neurological deficits (Hoffman sign, hyperreflexia, gait disturbance).

**Conclusion on Clinical Standard:** Current clinical guidelines strongly recommend surgical intervention for Ms. Brooks' condition without mandating extended conservative treatment trials, particularly interventional procedures contraindicated due to risk. The plan's criteria for conservative treatment are inconsistent with these generally accepted standards for myelopathy.

**V. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND RATIONALE**

The denial is based on an incorrect application of HealthFirst's own criteria. The plan's myelopathy exception (2.2(A)) clearly waives the interventional treatment requirement when the physician documents risk, which was done here. Therefore, the denial is invalid even under the plan's own policy.

Furthermore, applying the hierarchy of authority, both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice supersede the plan's more restrictive criteria for conservative treatment in cases of cervical myelopathy.

*   **DMHC APL 22-014** and **CIC § 10169.5** explicitly allow for the waiver of conservative treatment requirements where myelopathy, cord compression, and myelomalacia are present, and the treating physician identifies risks or futility with conservative measures.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b)** mandates that services meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice must be approved, regardless of whether a plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.
*   The **AANS/CNS, NASS, and AO Spine guidelines** unequivocally classify Ms. Brooks' presentation (myelopathy with UMN signs, cord compression, and myelomalacia) as a strong indication for surgery, with no evidence supporting or recommending prolonged conservative trials (especially interventional procedures) that could delay definitive treatment and risk irreversible harm.

The plan's radiologist's dispute regarding the clinical significance of T2 signal change is superseded by the treating hospital's radiologist's confirmed diagnosis of myelomalacia and, more importantly, by the clear and objective clinical findings of myelopathy documented by the treating physician (bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, gait impairment).

**VI. FINAL DISPOSITION**

Based on the foregoing analysis, the denial of prior authorization for ACDF C4-C5 for Ms. Helen Brooks is hereby:

**OVERTURNED.**

The requested procedure (ACDF C4-C5, CPT 22551) is medically necessary and should be authorized.

**VII. WARRANT**

The initial denial by HealthFirst was based on a misapplication of its own clinical coverage criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024). The plan incorrectly asserted that the Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2(A)) did not apply. The clinical record clearly shows that Ms. Brooks has confirmed cervical myelopathy with objective upper motor neuron signs, and her treating physician explicitly documented that interventional procedures were relatively contraindicated due to cord compression, thereby fulfilling the condition for waiver of the interventional treatment requirement under the plan's own exception.

Beyond the misapplication of internal policy, the denial's conservative treatment requirements are superseded by California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice. California's DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (II.2) and CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) mandate the waiver of conservative treatment requirements, including physical therapy and interventional procedures, in cases of cervical myelopathy with objective neurological compromise (such as cord compression with myelomalacia and UMN signs) where the treating physician documents risk or unlikelihood of benefit. Furthermore, CHSC § 1374.32(b) stipulates that services meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice shall be approved regardless of more restrictive plan criteria.

According to AANS/CNS (2023), NASS (2020), and AO Spine (2021) guidelines, Ms. Brooks' presentation—cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression, upper motor neuron signs (Hoffman, hyperreflexia, gait impairment), and confirmed early myelomalacia by the treating hospital's radiologist—constitutes a TIER 1 strong surgical indication. These guidelines explicitly state that delaying surgery for extended conservative treatment (especially with myelomalacia) is not supported by evidence and carries a significant risk of irreversible neurological deterioration. The plan's insistence on a prerequisite interventional procedure is contrary to these widely accepted clinical standards for myelopathy.

Therefore, the requested ACDF C4-C5 is medically necessary and should be approved.
