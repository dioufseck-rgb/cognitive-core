**REVIEW DISPOSITION: OVERTURN DENIAL**

**WARRANT:**
The prior authorization denial for Helen Brooks' ACDF C4-C5 is hereby **OVERTURNED**. The denial was based on a misapplication of HealthFirst's own plan criteria, is directly contradicted by California state law, and is inconsistent with generally accepted clinical standards for cervical myelopathy.

**1. Primary Clinical Presentation: Cervical Myelopathy**
The clinical record unequivocally demonstrates that Ms. Brooks presents with cervical myelopathy. Her neurological exam findings of bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia in upper extremities, and subtle balance impairment on tandem gait are classic Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) signs, which are definitive for myelopathy (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.1). MRI consistently shows C4-C5 disc herniation with cord contact. While the plan's radiologist disputed the significance of T2 signal change, a second radiologist (treating hospital) confirmed "early myelomalacia" and noted the T2 hyperintensity was "reproducible on multiple sequences and represents early cord signal change, not artifact. Consistent with early myelopathy." The treating physician also independently documented myelopathy on clinical grounds. Thus, Ms. Brooks meets HealthFirst's own diagnosis requirement for cervical myelopathy (Section 2.1(b) of CC-SPINE-2024).

**2. Misapplication of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)**
The plan's denial stated that the myelopathy exception "does not apply" because the T2 signal change was deemed minimal or artifactual by the plan's reviewer, leading to the application of the standard conservative pathway which requires an interventional procedure. This is incorrect for the following reasons:
    *   **Myelopathy Exception Applicability:** Even if the myelomalacia was disputed by one reviewer, the *clinical* diagnosis of myelopathy (based on UMN signs) is clear and meets criterion 2.1(b). HealthFirst's "EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY" explicitly states that the interventional treatment requirement (2.2(c)) is WAIVED if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration. The treating physician, Dr. Park, clearly documented this risk: "Treating physician did not refer for epidural due to myelopathy concern — epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated in cord compression." Therefore, the interventional procedure requirement should have been waived under HealthFirst's own policy for myelopathy. The denial directly contravenes the plan's own written exception.

**3. Override by California State Law**
California state law explicitly overrides the plan's denial criteria:
    *   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This APL states that for cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when objective imaging shows progressive pathology and surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. Ms. Brooks has clear cord compression and a documented second opinion of early myelomalacia. Her treating physician documented that 8 weeks of PT provided "No improvement in UMN signs" and that pharmacotherapy only provided partial/minimal benefit, indicating conservative treatment failure for her myelopathy. The APL specifically identifies myelomalacia as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements. This APL mandates that the plan cannot apply internal criteria more restrictive than generally accepted standards.
    *   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This code prohibits conditioning surgical approval on PT completion where: (1) the physician documents PT is unlikely to benefit (8 weeks PT with "no improvement in UMN signs" for myelopathic symptoms), (2) the member reached a functional plateau (consistent with the clinical standard definition, Section 5 of Clinical Reference Summary), or (3) the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address (cord compression and myelomalacia). All three conditions apply here, rendering any mechanical PT requirement for myelopathy non-applicable.

**4. Inconsistency with Generally Accepted Clinical Standards**
The denial is inconsistent with authoritative clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):
    *   **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** Clinical standards clearly distinguish myelopathy from radiculopathy, noting that conservative treatment requirements for radiculopathy (e.g., specific PT durations, interventional procedures) are **not** applicable to myelopathy. Applying radiculopathy-derived requirements to myelopathy is a "category error" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.1).
    *   **Surgical Indication:** Ms. Brooks' presentation of cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression, UMN signs, and early myelomalacia (confirmed by second read) constitutes a **TIER 1 strong surgical indication** (AANS/CNS Guidelines 2023, Section 3.1 & 3.2).
    *   **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy:** For moderate-severe myelopathy, physical therapy "does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement," and "there is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1 & 2.2). Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial "may result in neurological deterioration" (AO Spine Guidelines 2021, Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1). The presence of myelomalacia is considered a "contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2). The patient's 8 weeks of PT with no improvement in UMN signs confirms the ineffectiveness of PT for her myelopathy.
    *   **CHSC § 1374.32(b):** This provision mandates that if a service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, it SHALL be approved, regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. The requested ACDF for Ms. Brooks' myelopathy with myelomalacia meets these standards.

**Conclusion:**
Based on the application of the three-source hierarchy (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria), the HealthFirst plan's denial is unwarranted. The plan misapplied its own myelopathy exception, and its criteria are superseded by California state laws (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) and generally accepted clinical standards. Ms. Brooks' clinical presentation and imaging findings strongly indicate cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia, a condition for which surgical intervention is strongly indicated, and extended conservative treatment requirements are not supported by evidence and may be harmful.

**Recommendation:** The prior authorization for CPT 22551 (ACDF C4-C5) for Helen Brooks is approved as medically necessary.
