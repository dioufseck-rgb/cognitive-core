### I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The prior authorization denial for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 for Mr. Marcus Webb is **OVERTURNED**. The initial denial was based on a mechanical application of the plan's conservative treatment requirements, specifically the duration of physical therapy, and a rigid interpretation of the primary diagnosis based on a billing code rather than the comprehensive clinical picture. This determination finds that the requested procedure is medically necessary based on overwhelming clinical evidence, direct mandates from California state law and regulations, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.

---

### II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION & DIAGNOSIS

Mr. Webb, a 47-year-old male, presented with symptoms highly consistent with cervical myelopathy. His clinical record documents bilateral hand weakness (3/5 grip), coordination problems, positive Hoffman sign bilaterally, bilateral upper extremity hyperreflexia, and impaired tandem gait. An MRI dated 2024-07-10 revealed a C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate cord compression and T2 signal change within the cord consistent with early myelomalacia.

While the treating physician's initial note and billing code (M47.812) listed "cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy," the clinical record explicitly notes this as a "BILLING DISCREPANCY," stating that "treating physician narrative notes consistently describe cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia on MRI. Physician states billing code is administrative artifact." Based on the combination of objective neurological findings (upper motor neuron signs, bilateral deficits, gait disturbance) and imaging confirmation of cord compression and myelomalacia, the primary clinical diagnosis is unequivocally **Cervical Spondylotic Myelopathy with Myelomalacia at C6-C7**.

---

### III. ANALYSIS OF DENIAL REASON AND APPEAL BASIS

**Original Denial Reason:** "Member completed 4 weeks of physical therapy. Standard pathway requires 6 weeks minimum. Myelopathy exception requires myelopathy as primary diagnosis — treating physician's most recent note lists 'cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy' as primary diagnosis. Member does not qualify for the myelopathy exception."

**Appeal Basis:**
1.  Myelomalacia documented on MRI — regulatory override applies regardless of diagnosis labeling.
2.  Treating physician used 'cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy' as billing code but clinical notes clearly document myelopathy.
3.  APL 22-014 mandates waiver of conservative treatment when myelomalacia present.

**Reviewer Analysis:**

1.  **Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024) - Section 2.2(A) Myelopathy Exception:**
    *   The plan's policy states that the physical therapy (PT) requirement is reduced to 4 weeks minimum for "members with confirmed cervical myelopathy." Mr. Webb completed 4 weeks of PT.
    *   The plan's denial hinges on the "NOTE" in Section 2.2(A), which states the myelopathy exception applies "only when myelopathy is the PRIMARY diagnosis" and explicitly excludes cases where it is "documented as a finding but radiculopathy is the primary complaint."
    *   However, the clinical evidence definitively establishes myelopathy as the primary diagnosis, supported by specific neurological findings and the presence of myelomalacia. The reliance on a billing code (M47.812) over the treating physician's narrative and objective clinical findings constitutes a misapplication of the plan's own policy. The plan failed to correctly identify the primary diagnosis based on clinical reality, thus incorrectly disallowing the myelopathy exception.

2.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 - Physical Therapy as Prerequisite (State Law):**
    *   This state law prohibits plans from conditioning surgery approval on PT completion where:
        *   **(a)(1) Physician documents PT is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit:** The treating physician halted PT after myelomalacia was confirmed and stated in a letter that further PT was contraindicated. This condition is met.
        *   **(a)(2) Member reached a functional plateau:** Despite 4 weeks of PT and pharmacotherapy, Mr. Webb experienced "partial pain relief, no improvement in weakness." Given that neurological symptoms of myelopathy (weakness, hyperreflexia) are not expected to respond to PT, the member has reached a functional plateau for the critical myelopathic symptoms, as defined by clinical standards (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 5). This condition is met.
        *   **(a)(3) Condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address:** Mr. Webb's MRI demonstrates moderate cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia), which represents existing spinal cord injury. Physical therapy cannot reverse such structural damage. This condition is met.
    *   **Conclusion:** CIC § 10169.5 overrides the plan's 6-week PT requirement, as all three waiver conditions are met.

3.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 - Prior Authorization for Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions (State Regulation):**
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** This APL states that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent." Mr. Webb's condition meets this description.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** The APL mandates that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when (a) objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia present), AND (b) the treating physician documents conservative treatment failure (attempted PT and meds, physician halted PT), AND (c) surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines (myelomalacia and moderate myelopathy are strong surgical indications). All these conditions are met.
    *   The APL specifically identifies "myelomalacia on MRI as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements."
    *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** The APL reiterates that plans "shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice," and "Where a conflict exists... the clinical standard governs."
    *   **Conclusion:** DMHC APL 22-014 directly mandates a waiver of full conservative treatment requirements in this case due to the presence of myelomalacia and myelopathy, overriding the plan's policy.

4.  **Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) - (Clinical Standard):**
    *   **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines clearly distinguish between these two conditions, emphasizing that myelopathy involves spinal cord compression with bilateral upper motor neuron signs and gait disturbance, requiring different treatment algorithms than radiculopathy (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.1).
    *   **Myelomalacia Significance:** The presence of myelomalacia is a "strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials," indicating existing cord damage and a "higher risk of permanent deficit if surgery is delayed" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2).
    *   **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration" (AO Spine Guidelines, Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1). The 6-week PT requirement is noted as a "category error" when applied to myelopathy.
    *   **Surgical Indication:** Mr. Webb's presentation (moderate myelopathy, objective cord compression, myelomalacia, objective neurological deficits) meets the TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION criteria for ACDF (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines, Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.1, 3.2).
    *   **Conclusion:** Clinical standards strongly support immediate surgical intervention and explicitly do not recommend delaying surgery for a prolonged course of physical therapy in cases of moderate-severe myelopathy with myelomalacia.

---

### IV. CONFLICT RESOLUTION (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria)

The plan's denial is based on a narrow interpretation of its internal criteria, which is directly contradicted by higher authorities:

*   **Plan's Misapplication of its Own Criteria:** The plan failed to recognize myelopathy as the primary diagnosis based on extensive clinical evidence, instead relying on a documented billing discrepancy. Even under its own criteria, the myelopathy exception's 4-week PT requirement was met.
*   **Conflict with California State Law (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014):** Both state statute and regulatory guidance explicitly override the plan's ability to mandate further physical therapy given the physician's documentation of contraindication, the member reaching a functional plateau in neurological symptoms, and the presence of objective structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia). These sources also mandate that plans not apply criteria more restrictive than generally accepted clinical standards for myelopathy with myelomalacia.
*   **Conflict with Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:** Leading spine societies' guidelines (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) overwhelmingly support surgical intervention for Mr. Webb's condition and explicitly state that delaying surgery for extended conservative treatment (like a mandatory 6-week PT) is not evidence-based and may be detrimental.

### V. DISPOSITION

**OVERTURN**

---

### VI. WARRANT

The denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) for Mr. Marcus Webb is **OVERTURNED**. The requested procedure is medically necessary and should be approved.

The plan's denial for failure to complete 6 weeks of physical therapy and for not qualifying for the myelopathy exception due to a perceived "primary diagnosis" of radiculopathy is not supported by the clinical record or applicable regulatory and clinical standards.

1.  **Clinical Diagnosis:** The clinical record, including neurological exam findings (bilateral weakness, hyperreflexia, Hoffman sign, gait disturbance) and MRI evidence (cord compression, myelomalacia), unequivocally confirms Cervical Spondylotic Myelopathy as the primary diagnosis. The notation of "radiculopathy" on a billing code is an administrative artifact and does not reflect the patient's true clinical presentation, rendering the plan's interpretation of its "primary diagnosis" clause in its myelopathy exception (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A)) incorrect.
2.  **Plan Criteria Met:** With myelopathy correctly identified as the primary diagnosis, Mr. Webb meets the plan's myelopathy exception, which reduces the physical therapy requirement to a minimum of 4 weeks. He completed 4 weeks of PT.
3.  **California Insurance Code § 10169.5 Override:** This state law explicitly prohibits a health plan from requiring physical therapy as a prerequisite for surgery when any of three conditions are met. All three conditions apply in this case:
    *   The treating physician documented that further PT was contraindicated (CIC § 10169.5(a)(1)).
    *   Mr. Webb reached a functional plateau for his critical neurological symptoms (weakness) without adequate resolution after 4 weeks of PT and other conservative measures (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)).
    *   Objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia on MRI with cord compression) exists that physical therapy cannot address (CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)).
4.  **DMHC APL 22-014 Mandate:** This California regulatory guidance mandates that for cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia, plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment, explicitly identifying myelomalacia as a finding that supports waiver of standard conservative requirements (DMHC APL 22-014, Section II.2). It further states that clinical standards, not more restrictive plan criteria, shall govern such cases (DMHC APL 22-014, Section II.3).
5.  **Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:** Per AANS/CNS, NASS, and AO Spine guidelines, moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia is a TIER 1 strong indication for surgical decompression. These guidelines explicitly state there is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgery for such conditions, and that delaying surgery may result in neurological deterioration (Clinical Reference Summary, Sections 2.1, 2.3, 3.1, 3.2). Applying radiculopathy-derived PT requirements to myelopathy is a "category error."

Based on the compelling clinical evidence, the mandates of California state law (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014), and generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine), the plan's denial is unsupported and must be reversed.
