### **DISPOSITION: OVERTURN**

The initial denial for anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 for member Marcus Webb is hereby **OVERTURNED**. Authorization for the requested procedure is **APPROVED**.

---

### **WARRANT & RATIONALE FOR DECISION**

**1. Summary of Initial Denial:**
The initial denial (MED-003) stated that the member completed 4 weeks of physical therapy, falling short of the standard 6-week minimum. The denial also claimed the myelopathy exception did not apply because the treating physician's most recent note listed 'cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy' as the primary diagnosis, thus failing the exception's requirement for myelopathy to be the primary diagnosis.

**2. Summary of Appeal Basis:**
The appeal asserts that myelomalacia documented on MRI mandates a regulatory override regardless of diagnosis labeling, that the treating physician's clinical notes clearly document myelopathy despite using a billing code for radiculopathy, and that DMHC APL 22-014 mandates a waiver of conservative treatment when myelomalacia is present.

**3. Clinical Presentation and Diagnosis Clarification:**
A thorough review of the clinical record reveals a clear presentation of cervical myelopathy with objective neurological compromise.
*   **Neurological Exam:** Documented bilateral grip weakness (3/5), positive Hoffman sign bilateral, bilateral hyperreflexia, and impaired tandem gait. These are classic upper motor neuron signs and symptoms consistent with cervical myelopathy.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-07-10):** Demonstrates C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate cord compression and, crucially, **T2 signal change within the cord consistent with early myelomalacia**. Myelomalacia is objective evidence of spinal cord injury.
*   **Billing Discrepancy:** The clinical record explicitly clarifies a billing discrepancy, stating that while ICD-10 code M47.812 (spondylosis with radiculopathy) was used on PT referrals, the treating physician's narrative notes consistently describe cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia on MRI, with the physician confirming the billing code is an "administrative artifact."

Based on the objective clinical findings (UMN signs, bilateral symptoms, gait disturbance) and MRI evidence of cord compression with myelomalacia, Mr. Webb's primary diagnosis is **Cervical Spondylotic Myelopathy C6-C7**. The initial denial's reliance on a billing code rather than the comprehensive clinical picture to determine the primary diagnosis was incorrect.

**4. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

*   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** Met. The member has a confirmed diagnosis of Cervical Myelopathy (2.1(b)) supported by MRI and neurological findings.
*   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2(b)):** Met. Gabapentin (3 months) and Naproxen (2 months) were trialed for durations exceeding the 4-week minimum.
    *   **Interventional Treatment (2.2(c)):** Met. A cervical epidural was performed.
    *   **Physical Therapy (2.2(a)):** The initial denial correctly noted 4 weeks of PT. However, the plan's denial failed to correctly apply its own **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY**.
        *   The myelopathy exception applies because Mr. Webb has **confirmed cervical myelopathy as his primary diagnosis**, as established by the clinical evidence.
        *   Under this exception, the physical therapy requirement is reduced to **4 weeks minimum**. Mr. Webb completed 4 weeks of PT.
        *   Furthermore, the exception waives interventional treatment if the treating physician documents risk. While an epidural was performed, the treating physician halted PT after myelomalacia was confirmed and stated further PT was contraindicated. This action aligns with the spirit of the exception recognizing the contraindication or risk associated with continued conservative therapy in the presence of myelopathy.

The initial denial incorrectly applied the standard pathway's 6-week PT requirement and erroneously excluded the myelopathy exception based on a billing code rather than the patient's actual clinical presentation.

**5. Application of California Regulatory Requirements (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):**

California state law provides clear mandates that override HealthFirst's internal criteria in this case.

*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute explicitly prohibits conditioning surgical approval on completion of PT where:
    *   **(a)(1) "The member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit..."** The treating physician halted PT after myelomalacia was confirmed and documented that further PT was contraindicated. This condition is met.
    *   **(a)(3) "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."** The presence of myelomalacia (T2 signal change within the spinal cord) on MRI definitively constitutes objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. This condition is met.
    *   Therefore, the plan's 6-week PT requirement cannot be enforced for Mr. Webb.

*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This regulatory guidance directly addresses Mr. Webb's situation.
    *   **II.1:** "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent..." Mr. Webb's case perfectly aligns with this description.
    *   **II.2:** "plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment... when (a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia...), AND (b) Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed... AND (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines."
        *   **(a) Met:** Myelomalacia is present on MRI.
        *   **(b) Met:** 4 weeks of PT were completed, pharmacotherapy provided only partial pain relief with no improvement in weakness, and an epidural offered minimal improvement. The treating physician halted PT due to myelomalacia and indicated further PT was contraindicated.
        *   **(c) Met:** As detailed below, surgery is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines.
    *   This APL specifically states that "The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements..."
    *   **II.3:** This section mandates that plans "shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice."

*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (Standard of Review for IMR):** Although this is an internal appeal, this section highlights that medical necessity should be determined "in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice," and that a plan's internal criteria "SHALL NOT be the sole or determinative basis" if more restrictive. This reinforces the primacy of clinical standards over plan-specific criteria when there is a conflict.

**6. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

*   **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines emphasize the fundamental difference between myelopathy and radiculopathy, stating they require different treatment algorithms. Applying radiculopathy-derived conservative treatment requirements to myelopathy is a "category error" (AO Spine 2021).
*   **Myelomalacia Significance:** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) indicates existing cord injury, higher risk of permanent deficit if compression continues, and is a strong surgical indication, often considered a **contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials** (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021).
*   **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy:**
    *   "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration" (AO Spine Guidelines 2021).
    *   For moderate-severe myelopathy, physical therapy does not produce meaningful functional improvement (Cochrane Review 2021).
*   **Surgical Indication:** Mr. Webb's presentation, including moderate cord compression, myelomalacia, and objective neurological deficits (bilateral weakness, UMN signs), meets **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION** for surgical intervention per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023).

**7. Conclusion & Conflict Resolution:**

The initial denial was based on a mechanical application of the plan's standard conservative treatment requirements, incorrectly identifying the primary diagnosis based on a billing code, and failing to apply the plan's own myelopathy exception.

Crucially, HealthFirst's requirement for a 6-week minimum physical therapy course for a patient with confirmed cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia is directly contradicted by:
1.  **California State Law (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014):** These statutes and regulations explicitly permit or mandate the waiver of conservative treatment requirements when objective neurological compromise (like myelomalacia) is present or when the treating physician documents contraindication.
2.  **Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine, NASS):** These guidelines do not support mandatory prolonged conservative treatment, specifically physical therapy, for moderate-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia, and in fact, warn against treatment delay.

Applying the hierarchy of authority (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards override the plan's more restrictive criteria and its misapplication of the myelopathy exception. The requested ACDF is medically necessary for Mr. Webb's condition.

The denial for CPT 22551 for Marcus Webb is **OVERTURNED**. Authorization for the requested ACDF C6-C7 is approved.
