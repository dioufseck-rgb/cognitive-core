**REVIEWER'S FINDINGS:**

As an expert health plan appeals reviewer, I have meticulously reviewed the submitted prior authorization appeal, the initial denial, the member's clinical record, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), relevant California regulatory framework (CIC, CHSC, DMHC APL), and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine).

My review applies the three-source hierarchy: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.

1.  **Clinical Presentation Assessment:**
    *   **Diagnosis:** The presenting diagnosis is clearly documented as Cervical Radiculopathy C5-C6 with functional impairment.
    *   **Imaging:** MRI dated 2024-08-15 shows "C5-C6 large disc herniation, severe right C6 nerve root compression, foraminal stenosis." These findings are consistent with radiculopathy. Crucially, the MRI report **does not indicate spinal cord compression or myelomalacia.**
    *   **Neurological Exam:** The exam dated 2024-08-20 reveals "Right C6 dermatomal numbness, bicep weakness 4/5 right, bicep reflex absent right, positive Spurling right." These are classic signs and symptoms of C6 radiculopathy.
    *   **Conclusion:** Based on the robust clinical and imaging evidence, Steven Clark's primary condition is cervical radiculopathy. Therefore, the HealthFirst Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2A) and specific regulatory guidance related to myelopathy/myelomalacia (e.g., DMHC APL 22-014, CIC 10169.5(a)(3)) for *waiving* conservative treatment requirements are not directly applicable. The case should be evaluated under the standard radiculopathy pathway.

2.  **Review of Original Denial and Appeal Basis:**
    *   The initial denial was based solely on a documentation discrepancy regarding the duration of physical therapy, citing "uncertain" verifiable PT weeks.
    *   The appeal has successfully resolved this administrative issue by providing complete records from Coastal Physical Therapy, verifying 6 weeks of treatment. This, combined with the 6 weeks from Riverside Physical Therapy, confirms a total of 12 weeks of documented physical therapy.

3.  **Review Against HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
    *   **2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** **MET.** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (2.1(a)), supported by imaging and neurological exam findings.
    *   **2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:**
        *   **(a) Physical Therapy:** **MET.** The plan requires a "minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy" (2.2(a)). The member has now documented a total of 12 weeks of PT (Riverside PT: 6 weeks, Coastal PT: 6 weeks). Both providers documented a "functional plateau" and "no improvement in neurological deficit" / "maximum therapeutic benefit reached" with persistent symptoms. This significantly exceeds the plan's minimum requirement.
        *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** **MET.** The member completed trials of Gabapentin (5 months) and Duloxetine (3 months), meeting the requirement for at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each (2.2(b)).
        *   **(c) Interventional Treatment:** **MET.** The member underwent a right C6 epidural steroid injection, meeting the requirement for one interventional treatment (2.2(c)).
    *   **2.3 Imaging Requirement:** **MET.** The MRI from 2024-08-15 is current (within 6 months) and demonstrates structural pathology (disc herniation, nerve root compression, foraminal stenosis) consistent with the member's C5-C6 radiculopathy and the proposed surgical level.
    *   **2.4 Physician Documentation:** The denial was not based on inadequate physician documentation, and it is presumed that standard attestations and rationales have been provided or can be readily furnished.
    *   **Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** With the submission of complete documentation, the member now **fully meets all applicable HealthFirst medical necessity criteria** for cervical spine surgery under the radiculopathy pathway.

4.  **Review Against California Regulatory Requirements:**
    *   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where "(2) The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
        *   **Finding:** The member completed 12 weeks of PT, and both physical therapy providers documented a functional plateau with persistent symptoms and lack of neurological improvement. This condition under state law is explicitly met, rendering any further PT requirement or denial based on PT duration/completion invalid. This law *supports overturning* the denial.
    *   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** While this APL primarily addresses myelopathy, its overarching principle that plans shall not apply internal criteria more restrictive than generally accepted standards (II.3) is noted and reinforces the importance of clinical standards.
    *   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This provision dictates that services meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice SHALL be approved, regardless of whether a plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.

5.  **Review Against Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
    *   **Functional Plateau:** The clinical standard defines a functional plateau as "less than 10% improvement in validated functional outcome measure... over 3 consecutive therapy sessions, OR therapist documentation that patient has reached maximum therapeutic benefit" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 5). The member's PT records explicitly document reaching a functional plateau and maximum therapeutic benefit, which fully aligns with this clinical definition.
    *   **Surgical Indications for Radiculopathy:** AANS/CNS (2023) guidelines state that surgery is a "reasonable indication" (TIER 2) for "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.1).
        *   **Finding:** The member has completed 12 weeks of comprehensive conservative treatment (physical therapy, two pharmacotherapy agents, and an epidural injection) with documented failure to achieve adequate resolution of symptoms or neurological deficits, having reached a functional plateau. This course of treatment, followed by persistent symptoms and functional impairment, fully aligns with accepted clinical standards for considering surgical intervention for cervical radiculopathy.

6.  **Cross-Source Conflict Resolution:**
    There is no conflict among the applicable sources.
    *   The original administrative reason for denial (documentation discrepancy) has been fully resolved.
    *   The member has now demonstrably met and exceeded HealthFirst's own conservative treatment criteria.
    *   California state law (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)) explicitly mandates that physical therapy completion cannot be required where a functional plateau has been reached.
    *   Generally accepted clinical standards support the appropriateness of surgical intervention after such an extensive and failed course of conservative treatment for radiculopathy.

**DISPOSITION:**
**OVERTURN**

**WARRANT:**
The denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) for Steven Clark is OVERTURNED. The initial denial was based on a documentation discrepancy regarding physical therapy duration. The appeal successfully resolved this by providing complete records, demonstrating the member completed 12 weeks of physical therapy, exceeding HealthFirst's 6-week minimum requirement (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a)).

Furthermore, both physical therapy providers documented that the member reached a functional plateau with persistent symptoms and no improvement in neurological deficits. This directly triggers California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2), which prohibits a plan from requiring further physical therapy when a functional plateau has been reached.

Clinically, for cervical radiculopathy, surgical intervention is considered a reasonable option after 6-12 weeks of failed conservative treatment, especially when a functional plateau is reached and neurological deficits persist (AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines, NASS 2020 Guidelines, AO Spine 2021 Guidelines; Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.1 & 5). The member has completed 12 weeks of PT, 5 months of Gabapentin, 3 months of Duloxetine, and a cervical epidural injection, all of which failed to provide sustained relief, thus meeting generally accepted clinical standards for surgical consideration.

Therefore, the requested procedure is medically necessary and meets all applicable criteria under state law, clinical standards, and the plan's own policy (once documentation issues are resolved).
