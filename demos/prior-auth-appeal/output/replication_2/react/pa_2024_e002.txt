**I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY:**

This appeal concerns a prior authorization denial for a three-level ACDF (C3-C4, C4-C5, C5-C6) for Catherine Mills, a 53-year-old member with multi-level cervical spondylosis with myeloradiculopathy. The initial denial approved only the C4-C5 level, citing "insufficient severity" for C3-C4 and C5-C6.

Upon comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's internal clinical criteria, California state laws (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014, CHSC § 1374.32), and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021), the denial is **PARTIALLY OVERTURNED**.

The appeal is **OVERTURNED** for levels C4-C5 and C5-C6. The C4-C5 level presents with severe myelopathy and myelomalacia, meeting urgent surgical indications with statutory waivers for conservative treatment. The C5-C6 level demonstrates radiculopathy with objective findings and documented failure of conservative therapy, meeting generally accepted surgical indications. The plan's "insufficient severity" determination for these levels is inconsistent with higher authoritative sources.

The appeal is **UPHELD** for level C3-C4. The clinical documentation for this level, specifically the correlation between imaging findings ("mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing") and objective neurological deficits or specific radicular symptoms, is not sufficiently compelling in the provided record to warrant surgical intervention under generally accepted clinical standards.

---

**II. CASE REVIEW FINDINGS:**

**A. Clinical Presentation:**
The member presents with **cervical myelopathy** (C4-C5) and **cervical radiculopathy** (C5-C6, C4-C5).
*   **Myelopathy (C4-C5):** MRI shows severe cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia) at C4-C5. Neurological exam reveals classical myelopathic signs including bilateral hyperreflexia, bilateral Hoffman sign, wide-based gait, and bilateral C5 deltoid weakness (4/5). Myelopathic symptoms did not improve after 9 weeks of physical therapy (PT) or an epidural injection.
*   **Radiculopathy (C5-C6):** MRI shows moderate disc herniation and moderate left C6 nerve root compression with foraminal stenosis. Neurological exam demonstrates left C6 bicep weakness (4-/5) and diminished bicep reflex. C5-C6 radicular symptoms persisted despite 9 weeks of PT and an epidural injection.
*   **C3-C4:** MRI shows mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing. Neurological exam notes "neck pain with C3-C4 palpation but no specific radicular pattern" and no objective weakness or reflex changes are specifically attributed to this level.

**B. Prior Authorization Denial Rationale:**
The initial denial, issued by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, stated: "Three-level ACDF not approved. Review of imaging and clinical record supports surgical intervention at one level (C4-C5) only. C3-C4 and C5-C6 do not demonstrate pathology of sufficient severity to warrant surgical intervention at this time."

**C. Conservative Treatment Review:**
The member completed 9 weeks of supervised physical therapy (exceeding HealthFirst's 6-week standard and 4-week myelopathy exception). Two pharmacotherapy agents (Baclofen x3 months, Pregabalin x4 months) were trialed, and one interventional procedure (epidural injection) was performed. Myelopathic symptoms were "stable but not improved," and C5-C6 radicular symptoms "persist."

---

**III. APPLICATION OF HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY:**

**1. PLAN CRITERIA (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**

*   **2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** Met. Member has confirmed cervical myelopathy (2.1.b) and radiculopathy (2.1.a).
*   **2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:**
    *   The member completed 9 weeks of PT, exceeding the standard 6-week requirement and the 4-week requirement under the **Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A)**. Two pharmacotherapy agents for 3-4 months and one interventional procedure were completed.
    *   The plan's initial denial did not explicitly state that conservative treatment requirements were *not met* in terms of *duration*. However, the implicit assumption for denying C3-C4 and C5-C6 appears to be insufficient resolution of symptoms.
    *   **Crucially, the initial denial failed to acknowledge or apply the Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A)**, which reduces PT requirements to 4 weeks and can waive interventional treatments if risk is documented for myelopathy as a primary diagnosis. Myelopathy is clearly the primary diagnosis here, given the myelomalacia and extensive UMN signs.
*   **2.3 Imaging Requirement:** Met for C4-C5 (severe cord compression, myelomalacia) and C5-C6 (moderate disc herniation, nerve root compression). For C3-C4, imaging shows mild pathology, but clinical correlation for surgical indication is weak.
*   **Denial (Section 3.3):** The plan's denial reasoning ("pathology of sufficient severity") is based on a threshold applied to imaging and clinical correlation, rather than a failure of conservative treatment *duration*.

**2. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK (State Law):**

*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):**
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** The presence of cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia (at C4-C5) mandates treatment as an urgent condition due to potential for irreversible harm. The plan's review failed to address this urgency.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** For conditions risking irreversible harm (like myelopathy with myelomalacia), plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when (a) objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia at C4-C5), (b) conservative treatment has failed (myelopathic symptoms stable/not improved after 9 weeks PT, pharmacotherapy, epidural), and (c) surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. All three conditions are met. This directly overrides any plan criteria that might implicitly require further conservative treatment or a higher bar for "failure" for the myelopathic component.
    *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** Plans "shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice." The plan's "insufficient severity" for C5-C6 appears to be more restrictive than clinical standards for persistent radiculopathy.

*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):**
    *   **(a)(2) Functional Plateau:** The member completed 9 weeks of PT, with myelopathic symptoms remaining "stable but not improved" and C5-C6 radicular symptoms persisting. This constitutes reaching a functional plateau (Clinical Standard Section 5).
    *   **(a)(3) Structural Neurological Compromise:** The severe cord compression with myelomalacia at C4-C5 represents "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." This provision explicitly prohibits conditioning surgical approval on further PT in such cases.

*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (Standard of Review for IMR):** This mandates that if a service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice," it SHALL be approved regardless of the plan's internal criteria being more restrictive. This applies to both the myelopathy and the radiculopathy levels.

**3. CLINICAL STANDARD (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

*   **Myelomalacia Significance (Section 1.2):** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) indicates existing spinal cord injury, a higher risk of permanent deficit, and reduced recovery, signifying "surgical urgency" and "a strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials."
*   **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy (Section 2):** For moderate-severe myelopathy, physical therapy "does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement" (Cochrane Review 2021) and "Delaying surgery...is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration" (AO Spine 2021). No high-quality evidence supports mandatory PT prior to surgery for moderate-severe myelopathy.
*   **Surgical Indications for ACDF (Section 3.1):**
    *   **TIER 1 (Strong Indication):** The member's presentation for C4-C5 (moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression, myelomalacia, and progressive/stable neurological deficit despite conservative treatment) meets multiple Tier 1 criteria.
    *   **TIER 2 (Reasonable Indication):** The member's presentation for C5-C6 (radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative treatment and significant functional impairment) meets Tier 2 criteria.

---

**IV. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND RATIONALE FOR DISPOSITION:**

The primary conflict arises from the plan's denial for "insufficient severity" at C3-C4 and C5-C6 and its implicit disregard for the profound myelopathic findings at C4-C5 and their implications for conservative treatment. Applying the hierarchy of **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria**:

1.  **C4-C5 Level (Myelopathy): OVERTURNED**
    *   The member has severe cervical myelopathy at C4-C5 with myelomalacia and severe cord compression, presenting with objective upper motor neuron signs and bilateral weakness (Clinical Record).
    *   This condition is classified as urgent with potential for irreversible harm (DMHC APL 22-014, II.1). Myelomalacia is a strong indication for surgery and a contraindication to extended conservative trials (Clinical Standard, Section 1.2).
    *   State law (CIC 10169.5(a)(3) and DMHC APL 22-014, II.2) explicitly waives or modifies conservative treatment requirements due to structural neurological compromise and myelomalacia. The 9 weeks of PT with "stable but not improved" myelopathic symptoms constitutes a failed conservative trial and functional plateau (CIC 10169.5(a)(2)).
    *   This meets AANS/CNS (2023) Tier 1 strong surgical indications.
    *   The plan's denial, which only approved this level, correctly identified its severity but failed to extend the appropriate urgency and conservative treatment waiver implications to the entire case.

2.  **C5-C6 Level (Radiculopathy): OVERTURNED**
    *   The member exhibits moderate disc herniation and C6 nerve root compression at C5-C6, with objective left C6 weakness, diminished reflex, and persistent radicular symptoms despite 9 weeks of conservative therapy (Clinical Record).
    *   This clinical presentation, including objective findings and documented failure of an adequate conservative trial (functional plateau), meets AANS/CNS (2023) Tier 2 "reasonable indication" for surgical intervention (Clinical Standard, Section 3.1).
    *   The plan's determination that this level lacks "sufficient severity" is more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. Under CHSC 1374.32(b) and DMHC APL 22-014, HealthFirst cannot apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted clinical standards.

3.  **C3-C4 Level: UPHELD**
    *   The clinical record indicates "mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing" at C3-C4 (Clinical Record).
    *   While the member experiences neck pain with palpation at this level, the record explicitly states "no specific radicular pattern" and does not attribute any objective neurological deficits (weakness, reflex changes, or contribution to myelopathy) directly to C3-C4 (Clinical Record).
    *   Under generally accepted clinical standards, imaging findings without correlating symptoms or objective neurological deficits do not typically warrant surgical intervention (AANS/CNS 2023, Tier 3 criteria).
    *   The treating surgeon's general assertion of "reasoning for three-level fusion" in the appeal basis is not sufficiently supported by explicit clinical correlation for the C3-C4 level within the provided medical record. Therefore, for this specific level, the plan's denial based on "insufficient severity" is consistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice.

---

**V. CONCLUSION:**

Based on the review of the submitted clinical record and application of the established hierarchy of authority (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria), the initial denial for Catherine Mills' ACDF is **PARTIALLY OVERTURNED**.

*   Authorization for **ACDF at C4-C5 and C5-C6** is approved due to the severe myelopathy with myelomalacia at C4-C5 and documented radiculopathy with failed conservative treatment at C5-C6, both meeting California regulatory requirements and generally accepted clinical standards for surgical intervention.
*   Authorization for **ACDF at C3-C4** remains denied, as the clinical documentation provided does not sufficiently support surgical intervention at this specific level under generally accepted standards of medical practice.

This determination is effective immediately.
