This letter serves as the determination for your appeal regarding the prior authorization denial for a three-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) procedure. We have conducted a comprehensive review of your clinical record, the initial denial, your appeal basis, HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria, applicable California state regulations, and generally accepted clinical standards.

**Summary of Request and Initial Denial:**
Dr. James Torres requested prior authorization for a three-level ACDF at C3-C4, C4-C5, and C5-C6. HealthFirst's initial denial, issued by Dr. Alan Foster, MD on 2024-11-25, approved surgical intervention at the C4-C5 level only. The denial stated that "C3-C4 and C5-C6 do not demonstrate pathology of sufficient severity to warrant surgical intervention at this time."

**Appeal Basis:**
Your appeal asserts that:
1.  All three levels are symptomatic.
2.  The treating surgeon has documented reasoning for three-level fusion.
3.  Conservative treatment has been completed for all levels.

*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-10-01):**
    *   **C3-C4:** Mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing.

    *   **C5-C6:** Moderate disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression, foraminal stenosis.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-10-05):**
    *   **Myelopathy Findings:** Bilateral hyperreflexia, bilateral Hoffman sign, wide-based gait.

**Applicable Criteria and Standards:**

This review applies the three-source hierarchy: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.

1.  **HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
    *   **2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** Member meets criteria for both Cervical Myelopathy (2.1(b)) due to cord compression, myelopathic signs, and myelomalacia, and Cervical Radiculopathy (2.1(a)) at C5-C6.
    *   **2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:**
        *   Standard Pathway: Requires 6 weeks PT, 2 pharmacotherapy agents for 4+ weeks each, and 1 interventional procedure. The member completed 9 weeks of PT, 3-4 months of two pharmacotherapy agents, and one epidural. Thus, the standard pathway requirements have been met.
        *   Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A): For primary myelopathy, PT is reduced to 4 weeks minimum, and interventional is waived if risk documented. The member's primary diagnosis is myelopathy, and 9 weeks of PT were completed, exceeding the 4-week minimum. Pharmacotherapy requirements were also met.
    *   **2.3 Imaging Requirement:** Requires imaging demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms and proposed surgical level.

2.  **California Regulatory Framework:**
    *   **CHSC § 1374.32(b):** The Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard dictates that service meeting "generally accepted standards of medical practice" SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.
    *   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):**
        *   II.1: Cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI SHALL be treated as urgent, risking irreversible harm if untreated.
        *   II.2: Plans SHALL NOT require full conservative treatment where objective imaging shows progressive pathology (e.g., myelomalacia), treating physician documents failure, and surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. Myelomalacia specifically supports waiver of standard conservative requirements.
        *   II.3: Plans SHALL NOT apply internal criteria more restrictive than generally accepted standards for conditions identified in this guidance.
    *   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** Prohibits conditioning surgery on PT completion where (a)(3) there is "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address" (e.g., myelomalacia with cord compression), or (a)(2) the member reached a "functional plateau." Both apply here.

3.  **Clinical Evidence Base (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
    *   **Myelopathy:** Defined by spinal cord compression with upper motor neuron signs and often gait disturbance. Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) indicates existing cord injury, higher risk of permanent deficit, and is a strong surgical indication, often a contraindication to extended conservative trials.
    *   **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy:** Limited efficacy for moderate-severe myelopathy. No high-quality evidence supports mandatory PT prior to surgery for moderate-severe myelopathy, and delay may result in neurological deterioration.
    *   **Surgical Indications (AANS/CNS Tier 1 - Strong Indication):** Includes moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression, progressive neurological deficit despite conservative treatment, and myelomalacia on MRI.
    *   **Surgical Indications (AANS/CNS Tier 2 - Reasonable Indication):** Includes radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment.

**Analysis:**

1.  **C4-C5 Level:** The initial denial approved the C4-C5 level. This aligns with all reviewed criteria: severe cord compression with myelomalacia at this level, clear myelopathic symptoms, and significant C5 radiculopathy make this a **Tier 1 strong surgical indication** per AANS/CNS guidelines. This level is also explicitly recognized as urgent by DMHC APL 22-014.

2.  **C5-C6 Level:** The denial states C5-C6 does not demonstrate "pathology of sufficient severity."
    *   **Clinical Record:** MRI shows "moderate disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression, foraminal stenosis." Neurological exam demonstrates "left bicep weakness 4-/5, bicep reflex diminished left." Conservative treatment notes indicate "C5-C6 radicular symptoms persist" after 9 weeks of PT, 4 months of Pregabalin, and an epidural.
    *   **Clinical Standard:** These findings clearly meet the AANS/CNS Tier 2 criteria for surgery: "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx." The member completed 9 weeks of PT and 4 months of pharmacotherapy with persistent symptoms, exceeding the suggested trial duration.
    *   **State Law Override:** The plan's determination that this level lacks "sufficient severity" is more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. Under CHSC § 1374.32(b) and DMHC APL 22-014 (II.3), the clinical standard must govern. Therefore, the denial for C5-C6 is inappropriate.

3.  **C3-C4 Level:** The denial states C3-C4 does not demonstrate "pathology of sufficient severity."
    *   **Clinical Record:** MRI shows "mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing." The neurological exam notes "C4 level: unclear — neck pain with C3-C4 palpation but no specific radicular pattern," and the PT summary states "C3-C4 contribution unclear."
    *   **Clinical Standard:** Based on the provided objective evidence, the pathology at C3-C4 is mild, and its contribution to the member's specific neurological deficits (myelopathy or radiculopathy) is not clearly established. Surgical intervention is typically not indicated for mild imaging findings without clear corresponding neurological symptoms that have failed conservative management. The plan's determination for "insufficient severity" at this level aligns with generally accepted clinical standards.

4.  **Conservative Treatment (General Context):** Although the denial was not based on incomplete conservative treatment, it is important to note that the member's conservative treatment course (9 weeks PT, 2 pharmacotherapies for 3-4 months, 1 epidural) meets or exceeds HealthFirst's standard and myelopathy-specific criteria. Furthermore, due to the presence of myelomalacia and objective neurological compromise, California state law (CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) and DMHC APL 22-014 (II.2)) would allow for waiver of further conservative treatment requirements even if they had not been fully met. Myelopathic symptoms did not improve with conservative care.

**Disposition:**
The prior authorization denial for the three-level ACDF is **PARTIALLY OVERTURNED and PARTIALLY UPHELD.**

*   **OVERTURNED for ACDF at levels C4-C5 and C5-C6.**
*   **UPHELD for ACDF at level C3-C4.**

**Warrant/Rationale:**

The decision to overturn the denial for the C5-C6 level is based on the following:

1.  **C4-C5:** The plan's approval of C4-C5 is consistent with all clinical and regulatory guidelines. The presence of severe cord compression and myelomalacia, coupled with myelopathic signs and C5 radiculopathy, constitutes a Tier 1 strong surgical indication (AANS/CNS 2023) and an urgent condition under California law (DMHC APL 22-014, II.1).
2.  **C5-C6:** The denial's assertion that C5-C6 lacks "sufficient severity" for surgical intervention is inconsistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice. The clinical record clearly documents moderate disc herniation, nerve root compression, and corresponding C6 radicular weakness and diminished reflex. These symptoms have persisted after an adequate and completed course of conservative treatment, meeting AANS/CNS (2023) Tier 2 indications for surgery. Per CHSC § 1374.32(b) and DMHC APL 22-014 (II.3), HealthFirst's internal criteria or review determination cannot be more restrictive than generally accepted clinical standards. Therefore, surgical intervention for C5-C6 is medically necessary.
3.  **C3-C4:** The denial for C3-C4, citing insufficient severity, is upheld. The clinical record presents only mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing, with an "unclear" correlation to specific neurological deficits at the C4 level. These findings do not meet the criteria for surgical intervention under generally accepted clinical standards.
