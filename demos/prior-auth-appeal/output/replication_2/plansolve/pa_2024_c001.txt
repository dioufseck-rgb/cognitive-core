### FORMAL DETERMINATION
The initial denial of prior authorization for **Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C5-C6 (CPT 22551)** is **UPHELD**. 

The clinical record establishes that the member has mild, imaging-negative clinical cervical myelopathy. Under both plan criteria and generally accepted clinical standards, mild myelopathy without objective spinal cord compression or myelomalacia requires the completion of a standard 6-week course of conservative physical therapy. Because the member completed only 5 weeks of physical therapy and does not meet the statutory or clinical criteria for a waiver of conservative treatment, the requested surgical intervention is not medically necessary at this time.

---

### CASE SUMMARY
On October 30, 2024, HealthFirst issued an initial denial (Reviewer: Dr. Alan Foster, MD) for an ACDF at C5-C6. The denial was based on reason code **MED-003**: the member completed only 5 weeks of physical therapy (PT) out of the plan’s standard 6-week requirement. The plan determined that the modified 4-week "Myelopathy Pathway" exception did not apply because the member's MRI on September 1, 2024, showed only "mild cord contact" and lacked objective "cord compression" or "cord signal changes" (myelomalacia). 

The treating neurosurgeon, Dr. Robert Adams, appealed on the basis that:
1. The member exhibits multiple objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, establishing clinical myelopathy regardless of imaging.
2. Clinical myelopathy is defined by the combination of mild cord contact on MRI and positive UMN signs.
3. The member's 5-week PT trial satisfies the modified 4-week PT requirement for myelopathy under the plan's Exception 2.2(A).

---

### EVALUATION STEPS

#### Step 1: Procedural Adequacy and Defect Review (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
*   **Analysis:** Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), a denial notice must explicitly cite the clinical coverage criteria applied and provide specific clinical reasons for the determination. 
*   **Finding:** The initial denial notice dated October 30, 2024, explicitly cited Plan Criteria document *HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2*, and provided a detailed clinical rationale (lack of imaging-confirmed cord compression/signal change to justify the myelopathy exception, and failure to complete the standard 6-week PT trial). Mandatory Independent Medical Review (IMR) notifications were included.
*   **Result:** The denial notice is **Procedurally Adequate**. Proceed to Step 2.

#### Step 2: Clinical Classification and Symptom Profile (Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy)
*   **Analysis:** We must distinguish between cervical radiculopathy and cervical myelopathy based on the clinical examination.
*   **Finding:** Dr. Adams' neurological examination on September 5, 2024, documents the following objective signs:
    *   Bilateral positive Hoffmann sign (UMN sign)
    *   Bilateral upper extremity hyperreflexia (UMN sign)
    *   Sustained clonus at bilateral ankles (3 beats) (UMN sign)
    *   Positive finger escape sign (UMN sign)
    *   Mildly wide-based gait (myelopathic gait)
*   **Result:** **Cervical Myelopathy Confirmed (Clinical).** Multiple objective, bilateral UMN signs and gait disturbance are present. This establishes clinical myelopathy as the primary diagnosis. Proceed to Step 3.

#### Step 3: Objective Imaging Concordance (MRI Analysis)
*   **Analysis:** We must evaluate the September 1, 2024 MRI of the cervical spine to verify the presence of structural spinal cord pathology at the C5-C6 level.
*   **Finding:** The radiologist's MRI report states: *"C5-C6 disc herniation. Mild cord contact. No measurable cord compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia. NOTE: Cord contact is present but not quantifiable as compression on standard sequences."*
*   **Result:** **Negative Finding (No Structural Cord Compromise).** The MRI demonstrates "mild cord contact" without actual cord deformation, measurable compression, or T2-weighted hyperintense cord signal changes (myelomalacia). Proceed to Step 4.

#### Step 4: Application of Plan Criteria and Myelopathy Exception (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2A)
*   **Analysis:** Plan criteria Section 2.1(b) defines Cervical Myelopathy as *"spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms,"* and requires an MRI demonstrating *"structural pathology consistent with symptoms."* Exception 2.2(A) reduces the required physical therapy duration from 6 weeks to 4 weeks when myelopathy is the *primary* diagnosis. 
*   **Finding:** 
    *   The member has clinical myelopathic symptoms, but the MRI fails to demonstrate "spinal cord compression" (defined by structural deformation). It shows "mild cord contact" only. 
    *   Because the plan's criteria strictly tie "confirmed cervical myelopathy" to structural cord compression on imaging, the plan correctly determined that the member did not qualify for the 4-week modified PT pathway under Section 2.2(A). 
    *   Under the standard conservative pathway, the member must complete **6 weeks** of PT (Section 2.2(a)) and trial **two** pharmacotherapy agents for a minimum of **4 weeks each** (Section 2.2(b)). The member completed only 5 weeks of PT (15 sessions). Furthermore, the member’s pharmacotherapy trial was incomplete: while Baclofen was trialed for 2 months, the methylprednisolone taper was completed for only 10 days, falling short of the plan's 4-week requirement.
*   **Result:** **Plan Criteria Not Satisfied.**

#### Step 5: Application of State Law Overrides (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)
*   **Analysis:** California law limits a health plan's ability to mechanically enforce minimum physical therapy durations under specific circumstances. We must evaluate if a statutory override applies:
    1.  **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) (Structural Neurological Compromise):** Applies when there is objective evidence of structural compromise that PT cannot address. Because the MRI shows "no measurable cord compression" and "no myelomalacia," there is no structural compromise that is mechanically exempt from conservative management under this provision.
    2.  **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) (Functional Plateau):** Requires therapist documentation that the member completed therapy and reached a "functional plateau" (<10% improvement over 3 sessions or maximum benefit reached). In this case, PT was halted prematurely by the treating physician due to myelopathy concerns, not because a functional plateau was documented by the therapist.
    3.  **DMHC APL 22-014 (Progressive Pathology):** Restricts plans from requiring conservative therapy when imaging shows progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia) and the physician documents urgency. The member’s MRI explicitly notes "no myelomalacia," and the clinical notes indicate stable, mild symptoms (patient denies lower extremity symptoms, gait is only "mildly" wide-based). No acute neurological urgency is established.
*   **Result:** **State Law Override Not Triggered.**

#### Step 6: Application of Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (CHSC § 1374.32(b))
*   **Analysis:** Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the standard of review for medical necessity is "generally accepted standards of medical practice," which supersedes restrictive plan criteria. We evaluate the request under AANS/CNS (2023) and AO Spine (2021) guidelines.
*   **Finding:**
    *   **AANS/CNS (2023) & AO Spine (2021):** Surgical decompression is recommended as a **Tier 1 (Strong Indication)** first-line treatment for *moderate-to-severe* myelopathy with *objective cord compression* or *myelomalacia* on MRI. 
    *   For **mild, stable myelopathy** without objective cord compression or myelomalacia (such as imaging-negative clinical myelopathy), clinical guidelines recommend **conservative management as the appropriate first-line treatment** (NASS 2020 Grade B evidence; AO Spine 2021).
    *   Clinical guidelines do not support bypassing a standard conservative trial for mild, non-compressive clinical myelopathy. A 6-week course of conservative therapy remains the standard of care for this presentation.
*   **Result:** **Clinical Standards Support Conservative Treatment.** Because the clinical presentation is mild and non-compressive, an complete 6-week conservative trial is clinically indicated before surgery.

---

### DISPOSITION SUMMARY
Following the authorized evaluation plan and the established Hierarchy of Authority (State Law > Clinical Standards > Plan Criteria):

1.  **Step 1:** The denial was procedurally adequate.
2.  **Step 2:** The member has clinical myelopathy based on UMN signs.
3.  **Step 3:** The MRI is negative for objective cord compression or myelomalacia (showing only mild contact).
4.  **Step 4/5/6:** Because the member has mild, imaging-negative clinical myelopathy, clinical standards (AANS/CNS/AO Spine) and plan criteria require a full course of conservative management. The standard 6-week physical therapy requirement has not been completed (5 weeks completed), and no clinical or statutory exceptions (CIC § 10169.5 / DMHC APL 22-014) are triggered.

Therefore, the requested Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is not medically necessary at this time. The member must complete the remaining 1 week of physical therapy and a compliant 4-week trial of a second pharmacotherapy agent, or document clinical deterioration, to establish medical necessity for surgical intervention.

**Final Decision:** **UPHOLD** the initial denial of coverage.
