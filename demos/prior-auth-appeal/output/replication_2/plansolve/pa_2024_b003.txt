### **SECTION 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

This appeal concerns the medical necessity of a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C3-C4 (CPT 22551) for a 67-year-old female with adjacent segment disease and severe left-sided C4 radiculopathy following a successful C4-C5 ACDF performed in 2019. 

The health plan’s initial reviewer, Dr. Alan Foster, MD, issued a denial on October 1, 2024, citing code MED-003. The denial asserted that although the member satisfied conservative treatment requirements, the cervical MRI dated August 15, 2024, demonstrated only "mild degenerative changes" and did not show sufficient pathology at C3-C4 to justify surgical intervention. 

A comprehensive review of the complete clinical file reveals that the initial review was clinically flawed and procedurally defective. The plan reviewer relied on an MRI report that was explicitly noted by the radiologist to be non-diagnostic at the target level due to severe hardware artifact from the prior 2019 fusion. Crucially, the plan reviewer completely ignored a confirmatory high-resolution **CT Myelogram performed on September 10, 2024**, which bypassed the artifact and confirmed **moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression and significant foraminal stenosis** at C3-C4. 

The member’s clinical presentation exhibits classic left C4 radiculopathy with objective neurological compromise (deltoid weakness of 3+/5 and a diminished biceps reflex). She has fully exhausted the plan's conservative therapy guidelines, including 10 weeks of physical therapy (resulting in a functional plateau), multi-agent pharmacotherapy, and a target-specific epidural steroid injection. Therefore, pursuant to the governing **Hierarchy of Authority**, which places California State Law and national clinical standards above restrictive or misapplied plan guidelines, the initial denial is overturned.

### **SECTION 2: PROCEDURAL DEFECT ANALYSIS (CHSC § 1374.31(b))**

Under California law, health plans are bound by strict procedural requirements when issuing prior authorization denials. 

1. **Statutory Standard (CHSC § 1374.31(b)):** Any medical necessity denial notice issued by a health care service plan must specify the clinical criteria applied, the specific clinical reasons for the denial, and detailed notice of the member’s right to an Independent Medical Review (IMR). A denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria or the clinical evidence relied upon is legally **defective** and must be treated as if no denial was issued for the purpose of appeal timelines.
2. **Analysis of the Initial Denial Notice:**
   * The initial denial notice issued by Dr. Alan Foster on October 1, 2024, cited "MED-003" and stated that "imaging does not show sufficient pathology."
   * The notice **failed to reference the plan's governing clinical criteria document (CC-SPINE-2024)**.
   * The notice **failed to address, analyze, or acknowledge the CT Myelogram report dated September 10, 2024**, which was in the clinical record at the time of review.
   * By relying on an artifact-occluded MRI while ignoring the diagnostic CT myelogram, the plan failed to provide a valid, clinically sound reason for denial.
3. **Procedural Finding:** The denial notice is procedurally defective under **CHSC § 1374.31(b)**. This defect alone constitutes sufficient grounds to overturn the denial, as the plan did not issue a legally compliant clinical denial. However, to ensure a complete and robust administrative record, this determination also evaluates the clinical merits of the case.

### **SECTION 3: CLINICAL & IMAGING CONCORDANCE REVIEW**

The core clinical dispute is whether there is objective imaging evidence of pathology at the C3-C4 level that correlates with the member's clinical symptoms.

#### **Clinical Presentation & Neurological Examination**
The neurological examination conducted on August 20, 2024, reveals active, progressive neurological deficits corresponding precisely to the left C4 nerve root:
* **Motor Weakness:** Left deltoid motor strength is graded at **3+/5** (active movement against gravity but not resistance), a significant deficit that is new since her 2023 baseline.
* **Sensory Deficit:** Numbness in the left C4 dermatomal distribution (shoulder and lateral upper arm).
* **Reflex Changes:** Diminished left biceps reflex.

#### **Imaging Discordance & Resolution of Hardware Artifact**
* **Cervical MRI (August 15, 2024):** The radiologist noted: *"C3-C4 region: SIGNIFICANT ARTIFACT from C4-C5 hardware limiting evaluation. Mild degenerative signal changes visible. Cannot exclude moderate compression due to artifact."* 
* **Clinical Error by Plan Reviewer:** Dr. Foster mechanically interpreted the MRI's mention of "mild degenerative changes" as the definitive pathology, ignoring the radiologist's clear diagnostic caveat that hardware artifact limited the evaluation and that moderate compression could not be ruled out.
* **CT Myelogram (September 10, 2024):** Because the MRI was compromised by metal artifact, Dr. Nguyen appropriately ordered a CT myelogram. The findings were definitive: *"CT myelogram performed due to MRI artifact limitation. C3-C4: moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression confirmed. Significant foraminal stenosis. MRI underestimated pathology due to hardware artifact from prior fusion."*

#### **Concordance Finding**
The clinical and imaging findings demonstrate **100% concordance**. The CT myelogram objectively confirms severe left C4 nerve root compression at the C3-C4 level, which perfectly explains the member's left C4 dermatomal numbness, diminished left biceps reflex, and severe left deltoid weakness (3+/5). The clinical record establishes adjacent segment disease at the level immediately above her prior 2019 fusion.

### **SECTION 5: STATE REGULATORY & STATUTORY OVERRIDES**

Under the **Hierarchy of Authority**, California State Law and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) regulations govern and override any restrictive plan criteria.

1. **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Overrides):**
   * Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)**, a health plan cannot condition surgical approval on the completion of further physical therapy if the member has already completed a course of therapy and reached a "functional plateau" without symptom resolution. The member's physical therapy notes from her 10-week, 30-session trial explicitly document "no improvement in left C4 radicular symptoms," establishing a functional plateau.
   * Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)**, a health plan cannot condition surgical approval on physical therapy where the condition involves *"objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."* Moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression with associated **3+/5 motor weakness** is a structural, mechanical compromise of the motor axon. Physical therapy cannot decompress a bone-on-nerve foraminal stenosis. Conditioning surgical approval on further PT is a violation of California law.
2. **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL 22-014):**
   * APL 22-014 prohibits plans from applying internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for time-sensitive neurological conditions. The APL identifies progressive neurological pathology and objective structural compromise as bases for waiving standard conservative requirements.
3. **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standards):**
   * The statutory standard for determining medical necessity in California is based on **generally accepted standards of medical practice**, not a health plan's internal guidelines. If a service meets generally accepted medical standards, the plan must approve it regardless of restrictive internal criteria.

### **SECTION 6: CLINICAL GUIDELINES & EVIDENCE-BASED ANALYSIS**

Generally accepted national clinical standards strongly support immediate surgical intervention for this member’s clinical presentation.

1. **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update):**
   * The joint guidelines issued by the American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS) and the Congress of Neurological Surgeons (CNS) state that surgical decompression is a **Tier 1 (Strongly Indicated)** intervention for patients with cervical radiculopathy who exhibit **objective motor weakness (e.g., 3+/5 deltoid weakness)** and matching nerve root compression on high-quality imaging, especially when they have failed a multi-modal course of conservative care. 
   * The guidelines state that delaying surgical decompression in the presence of active motor deficits increases the risk of permanent, irreversible axonal injury and chronic motor weakness.
2. **North American Spine Society (NASS) Guidelines (2020):**
   * NASS evidence-based clinical guidelines state that in patients with a history of prior cervical fusion, MRI artifact from the existing titanium or cobalt-chromium instrumentation frequently makes the MRI non-diagnostic at the adjacent segments. 
   * In these cases, a **CT Myelogram is the gold-standard diagnostic tool** to evaluate foraminal stenosis and nerve root compression. Relying on an artifact-distorted MRI to deny surgery while ignoring a clean CT myelogram is a fundamental clinical error that violates the standard of care.
3. **AO Spine Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):**
   * AO Spine guidelines state that cervical radiculopathy resulting in progressive or stable significant motor deficits (defined as motor strength $\le$ 4/5) with matching foraminal stenosis is an indication for prompt surgical decompression. There is no evidence supporting extended conservative management for patients with objective motor weakness.

### **SECTION 7: FINAL DETERMINATION & ORDER**

Based on the governing **Hierarchy of Authority**, the clinical records, and California statutory standards:
1. **Procedural Defect:** The initial denial notice dated October 1, 2024, is legally defective under **CHSC § 1374.31(b)** because it failed to specify the clinical criteria applied (CC-SPINE-2024) and ignored the definitive CT myelogram in the record.
2. **Plan Criteria Compliance:** The member fully satisfied every requirement of the plan's **CC-SPINE-2024** clinical policy. The initial reviewer committed a severe clinical error by ignoring the CT myelogram, which demonstrated severe structural pathology (left C4 nerve root compression) matching the member's clinical deficits.
3. **Statutory Override:** Under **CIC § 10169.5**, the plan’s denial of surgical care based on a lack of "sufficient pathology" despite a documented functional plateau and objective structural compromise (3+/5 deltoid weakness) violates California insurance law.
4. **Clinical Standard:** Under **AANS/CNS (2023)** and **NASS (2020)** guidelines, a single-level ACDF at C3-C4 (CPT 22551) is highly indicated, medically necessary, and the standard of care to prevent permanent neurological injury.
