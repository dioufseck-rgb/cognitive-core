### **STEP-BY-STEP APPEAL EVALUATION**

#### **Step 1: Procedural Defect Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))**
*   **Finding:** **Positive Finding (Procedural Adequacy)**  
*   **Discussion:** The plan's initial denial notice dated November 2, 2024, is procedurally adequate under CHSC § 1374.31(b). It clearly specified the clinical criteria applied (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024), detailed the specific clinical reason for the denial (inability to verify the 6-week physical therapy requirement due to missing records from Mountain Physical Therapy), and provided proper notice of Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights to the member and provider. Because the clinical record does not support a primary diagnosis of myelopathy (see Step 2), the plan was not procedurally defective for failing to cite myelopathy-specific exceptions or waivers at the initial review stage.

#### **Step 2: Diagnostic & Clinical Classification Verification**
*   **Finding:** **Negative Finding (Primary Diagnosis is Cervical Radiculopathy)**  
*   **Discussion:** Clinical and imaging records were analyzed to determine whether the patient's condition classifies as myelopathy or radiculopathy:
    *   *Imaging:* The cervical spine MRI dated September 1, 2024, demonstrates a "large disc herniation at C6-C7, bilateral C7 nerve root compression, and bilateral foraminal stenosis." Crucially, it notes only "**mild cord contact without compression**" and contains no evidence of spinal cord compression, myelomalacia, or T2 cord signal changes.
    *   *Clinical Exam:* The neurological exam dated September 5, 2024, documents "bilateral C7 dermatomal symptoms: bilateral tricep weakness 4/5, bilateral tricep reflex diminished, and bilateral finger tingling." There is an absence of upper motor neuron (UMN) signs such as hyperreflexia, a positive Hoffman sign, clonus, or gait disturbance.
    *   *Conclusion:* Because there is no spinal cord compression or UMN symptom pathology, the patient does not meet the diagnostic threshold for cervical myelopathy. The clinical record establishes the primary diagnosis as **bilateral cervical radiculopathy (C6-C7)**.

#### **Step 3: Plan Criteria Exception Applicability (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A))**
*   **Finding:** **Negative Finding (Exception Does Not Apply; Standard Pathway Governs)**  
*   **Discussion:** Under HealthFirst policy CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway), the conservative treatment requirements are modified only when myelopathy is the *primary* diagnosis. Because the member's verified primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, the Myelopathy Pathway exception does not apply. The member is subject to the plan's standard conservative treatment criteria under Section 2.2.

#### **Step 4: Statutory Overrides & Regulatory Compliance (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)**
*   **Finding:** **Positive Finding (Statutory Override of Mechanical Plan Criteria)**  
*   **Discussion:** 
    *   Under **DMHC APL 22-014**, conservative treatment waivers for progressive structural neurological compromise do not apply directly here, as the MRI does not document spinal cord compression or myelomalacia.
    *   However, under **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2)**, a health plan is expressly prohibited from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of further physical therapy if the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician/therapist documents that the member reached a **functional plateau** without adequate resolution of symptoms.
    *   The complete clinical record submitted with this appeal now includes the Mountain Physical Therapy records. These records prove the member completed **4 additional weeks** of PT (July 15, 2024, to August 9, 2024; 12 sessions) in addition to the **4 weeks** completed at Valley Physical Therapy (June 1, 2024, to June 28, 2024; 12 sessions). This constitutes a **total of 8 weeks (24 sessions) of conservative physical therapy**.
    *   The Mountain PT discharge summary explicitly documents: *"No improvement in neurological symptoms. Plateau documented by PT at discharge."* 
    *   Under CIC § 10169.5(c), the plan's standard 6-week physical therapy requirement cannot be applied mechanically to deny coverage when a functional plateau has been clinically documented. Thus, the member's physical therapy trial is legally and clinically complete.

#### **Step 5: Medical Necessity under Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023 & AO Spine 2021)**
*   **Finding:** **Positive Finding (Surgical Decompression is Medically Necessary)**  
*   **Discussion:** Under generally accepted standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023 and NASS Guidelines 2020), single-level ACDF (CPT 22551) is highly indicated and medically necessary for cervical radiculopathy when there is objective neurological deficit (such as the documented 4/5 tricep weakness and diminished reflexes) that has failed to improve after a comprehensive trial of conservative management.
    *   The clinical record confirms the member failed an 8-week course of physical therapy (reaching a documented functional plateau).
    *   The member failed two trials of pharmacotherapy: Pregabalin 150mg BID for 3 months (partial relief) and Naproxen 500mg BID for 2 months (inadequate relief), satisfying Section 2.2(b) of the plan's criteria.
    *   The member failed interventional treatment: a bilateral C7 epidural steroid injection on September 20, 2024, which yielded only temporary relief (50% left-sided, 30% right-sided) before returning to baseline, satisfying Section 2.2(c) of the plan's criteria.
    *   Delaying decompression surgery in a patient with a large disc herniation and persistent, objective motor weakness (4/5 tricep strength) violates generally accepted clinical guidelines (AO Spine 2021), as prolonged delay risks irreversible neurological deterioration.

---

### **RECONCILIATION & RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS**
Applying the mandatory **Hierarchy of Authority**:
1. **Plan Criteria vs. Submitted Evidence:** The initial denial was based solely on the plan's inability to verify the standard 6-week PT requirement due to missing records. With the formal submission of the Mountain PT records on appeal, the plan's standard clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2) are **fully satisfied** (8 weeks of PT completed, 2 pharmacotherapy agents trialed, 1 epidural steroid injection completed, and current MRI confirming matching structural pathology).
2. **Statutory Application:** Under **CIC § 10169.5**, the documented functional plateau at the completion of the physical therapy course legally overrides any further plan-mandated conservative therapy.
3. **Clinical Standard Alignment:** Under **AANS/CNS 2023** and **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**, the patient exhibits a classic Tier 2 indication for surgical decompression due to persistent radiculopathy with objective motor deficits failing conservative treatment. The clinical benefits of immediate surgical intervention outweigh any further delay.

---

### **DECISION**
The physical therapy records from Mountain Physical Therapy submitted with this appeal successfully bridge the initial documentation gap. Because the member has completed a total of 8 weeks of physical therapy, 2 pharmacotherapy trials, and a cervical epidural injection, all medical necessity criteria under **HealthFirst Policy CC-SPINE-2024** and **California Insurance Code § 10169.5** are met.

The requested **ACDF C6-C7 (CPT 22551) at UCLA Medical Center by Dr. Paul Adams, MD, is hereby APPROVED.**
