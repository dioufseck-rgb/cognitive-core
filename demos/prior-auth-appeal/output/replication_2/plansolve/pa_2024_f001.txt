### I. DISPOSITION

### II. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

On November 2, 2024, HealthFirst initially denied prior authorization for a single-level anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7. The sole basis for the denial (Reason Code: MED-003) was a failure to satisfy the plan's 6-week conservative physical therapy (PT) requirement. Specifically, the plan had only received 4 weeks of PT records from Valley Physical Therapy and could not verify the member's claim of an additional 4 weeks completed at Mountain Physical Therapy. 

Upon first-level appeal, the member's representative submitted the complete clinical records from Mountain Physical Therapy, verifying an additional 4 weeks (12 sessions) of supervised cervical physical therapy.

A comprehensive clinical review of the entire medical record—including the newly submitted PT records, the cervical spine MRI, the neurological examination, and the documentation of failed pharmacotherapy and interventional treatments—demonstrates that the member has fully satisfied the standard medical necessity criteria under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria **CC-SPINE-2024**. Furthermore, a mechanical denial based on conservative therapy timelines violates California statutory protections (**CIC § 10169.5** and **DMHC APL 22-014**) and diverges from generally accepted clinical standards (**AANS/CNS 2023**). Therefore, the initial denial is overturned, and the requested surgical procedure is authorized.

---

### III. CLINICAL PROFILE & DIAGNOSTIC CLASSIFICATION

#### 1. Diagnostic Distinction: Radiculopathy vs. Myelopathy
Under clinical review guidelines (including *AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines 2023*), a primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy requires objective spinal cord compression on imaging, T2 cord signal changes (myelomalacia), and upper motor neuron (UMN) signs (e.g., Hoffman sign, hyperreflexia, clonus, gait disturbance). 

*   **Imaging Review:** The member's cervical spine MRI dated September 1, 2024, demonstrates a large C6-C7 disc herniation, bilateral C7 nerve root compression, and bilateral foraminal stenosis. Crucially, the MRI notes *"mild cord contact without compression"* and no evidence of myelomalacia or spinal cord signal changes.
*   **Neurological Exam Review:** Dr. Adams’ clinical evaluation on September 5, 2024, documents bilateral C7 dermatomal symptoms, including bilateral tricep weakness (graded 4/5), diminished bilateral tricep reflexes, and bilateral finger tingling. 
*   **Classification:** Because there are no upper motor neuron signs (diminished reflexes indicate lower motor neuron/nerve root pathology), no cord compression, and no myelomalacia, the member's condition is clinically classified as **Bilateral Cervical Radiculopathy C6-C7**, rather than cervical myelopathy. The Myelopathy Exception under plan policy CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A) is not applicable; therefore, the case must be evaluated under the standard conservative treatment pathway.

### V. STATUTORY & REGULATORY ALIGNMENT (California Law)

As a California plan, this appeal must comply with state regulatory authorities, which govern and override restrictive internal plan criteria.

1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Prerequisites):**
    Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)**, a health plan is legally prohibited from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of further physical therapy if the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating clinician documents that the member reached a **functional plateau** without adequate resolution of symptoms.
    *   *Application:* The Mountain Physical Therapy discharge records explicitly state: *"No improvement in neurological symptoms. Plateau documented by PT at discharge."* 
    *   *Result:* Because a functional plateau was achieved and documented after a reasonable course of therapy, mechanically enforcing additional physical therapy or denying the request due to administrative gaps in initial PT record collection violates **CIC § 10169.5(c)**.
2.  **Independent Medical Review (IMR) Standard (CHSC § 1374.32):**
    Under **CHSC § 1374.32**, the statutory standard for medical necessity is determined by generally accepted clinical practice standards rather than a health plan's internal policies. The clinical evidence demonstrates that this member has objective, progressive neurological deficits (bilateral 4/5 tricep weakness, diminished reflexes) that have failed a highly robust, multi-modal conservative trial (8 weeks PT, 3 months neuropathic medication, 2 months NSAIDs, and an epidural injection). Delaying decompression surgery in the setting of progressive motor weakness risks permanent nerve root damage.

---

### VI. CLINICAL STANDARDS ALIGNMENT (AANS/CNS & NASS)

According to the **AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023)** and the **North American Spine Society (NASS) Guidelines (2020)**:
*   A surgical recommendation for cervical radiculopathy is considered a **Tier 2 clinical indication** (highly appropriate) when a patient presents with persistent radicular pain and objective motor weakness (4/5 tricep weakness) that has failed to respond to 6 to 12 weeks of conservative management.
*   The member's trial of 8 weeks of PT, 3 months of pregabalin, and a targeted epidural steroid injection represents an exemplary clinical trial of conservative therapy. Continuing conservative care in the face of persistent, documented objective motor weakness (4/5 tricep strength) is clinically contraindicated as it increases the risk of irreversible axonotmesis (nerve fiber damage) and permanent motor deficit.

Because the complete clinical record now confirms that the member met all standard conservative treatment criteria outlined in **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024**, and because further conservative delay is clinically contraindicated and legally restricted under **California Insurance Code § 10169.5**, the initial adverse determination is **OVERTURNED**.
