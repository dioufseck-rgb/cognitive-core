### **I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & DISPOSITION**

Pursuant to California health insurance regulations and generally accepted standards of neurological practice, the initial denial of coverage for CPT 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) is hereby **OVERTURNED IN FULL**. 

The health plan’s initial denial, which cited a failure to complete a minimum of 6 weeks of physical therapy (PT), was based on an administrative and mechanical application of internal plan guidelines. This review determines that the member possesses **objective, structural spinal cord compromise (myelomalacia and moderate cord compression)** at C6-C7, accompanied by progressive upper motor neuron deficits. 

Under **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3)** and **Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014**, standard conservative treatment requirements (such as physical therapy) are legally waived when objective structural neurological compromise is present. Furthermore, under **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b)**, the health plan's restrictive internal criteria cannot override generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023 guidelines), which designate moderate-to-severe myelopathy with myelomalacia as a Tier 1 strong surgical indication requiring prompt decompression to avoid irreversible neurological decline. 

Therefore, CPT 22551 is medically necessary and is authorized immediately.

### **II. GOVERNING HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY**

In resolving conflicts between clinical, administrative, and legal guidelines, the following hierarchy of authority has been strictly applied:
$$\text{California State Law \& Regulations} > \text{Generally Accepted Clinical Standards} > \text{Health Plan Internal Criteria}$$

Where sources conflict, the higher authority governs. Plan-specific administrative designations, such as the sequencing or "primary" labeling of ICD-10 diagnostic codes, cannot override statutory mandates or established clinical standards.

### **III. STEP-BY-STEP EVALUATION**

#### **Step 1: Procedural Adequacy and Defect Review (CHSC § 1374.31(b))**
* **Evaluation:** The initial denial notice dated November 5, 2024, issued by Dr. Patricia Huang, was evaluated for compliance. The notice cited the standard 6-week PT requirement, reference code MED-003, and specified the clinical reason regarding the "primary" diagnosis label of "cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy."
* **Finding:** **Positive Finding.** The denial notice met the minimum procedural requirements of CHSC § 1374.31(b) by specifying the applied clinical criteria and the individualized clinical reason for the determination. *Action: Proceed to Step 2.*

#### **Step 2: Diagnostic Classification and Objective Pathology Verification**
* **Evaluation:** The medical record was analyzed to determine if the clinical presentation represents cervical radiculopathy (nerve root compression) or cervical myelopathy (spinal cord compression/injury).
* **Neurological Examination (2024-07-01):** Confirms objective **upper motor neuron (UMN) signs**:
  * Bilateral hyperreflexia of the upper extremities.
  * Bilateral positive Hoffman signs.
  * Impaired tandem gait (coordination problems).
  * Objective bilateral grip weakness (3/5).
* **Imaging (MRI, 2024-07-10):** Confirms structural pathology at C6-C7:
  * Moderate spinal cord compression due to disc herniation.
  * **T2-weighted hyperintensity (signal change) within the cord**, consistent with **early myelomalacia** (existing, active spinal cord injury).
* **Finding:** **Positive Finding.** The combination of objective clinical UMN signs and diagnostic imaging demonstrating cord compression with T2 signal changes establishes a clear, undeniable diagnosis of **Cervical Spondylotic Myelopathy with Myelomalacia** at C6-C7, rather than simple cervical radiculopathy. *Action: Proceed to Step 3.*

#### **Step 3: Statutory Physical Therapy Exemptions (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)**
* **Evaluation:** The clinical record was assessed against California statutory exemptions to determine if the health plan’s 6-week physical therapy requirement is legally waived.
* **Exemption 3.1 (Structural Compromise - CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) / APL 22-014):**
  * The member’s cervical MRI dated 2024-07-10 provides objective, radiologist-confirmed evidence of **myelomalacia** and spinal cord compression.
  * Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)**, a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the condition involves "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
  * **DMHC APL 22-014** explicitly states that the presence of myelomalacia on MRI is a progressive spinal pathology that supports the immediate waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements.
  * Furthermore, the treating surgeon, Dr. Sarah Chen, documented in writing that further physical therapy was **contraindicated** and halted at 4 weeks (12 sessions) because of the risk of neurological deterioration, satisfying **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1)**.
* **Finding:** **Positive Finding.** Under California law (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014), the health plan is prohibited from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of the standard 6-week PT course. The 6-week requirement is legally waived.  
* **Action: Stop Review. The denial must be OVERTURNED on statutory grounds.**

### **IV. ADDITIONAL CLINICAL AND ADMINISTRATIVE FINDINGS**

To ensure a comprehensive review, the clinical standards and internal plan criteria were evaluated to address the arguments raised by both the plan and the appealing provider.

#### **1. Clinical Standard Alignment (CHSC § 1374.32(b))**
* Under **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**, the standard for medical necessity is aligned with generally accepted standards of medical practice, not more restrictive plan-specific policies.
* **AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023)** and **AO Spine Guidelines (2021)** state that moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia constitutes a **Tier 1 (Strong Indication) surgical presentation**. 
* There is no high-quality clinical evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy for this population. Because myelomalacia indicates an existing, active cord injury, delaying surgery to complete an arbitrary physical therapy timeline exposes the patient to an unacceptable risk of permanent, irreversible neurological deficits (e.g., loss of hand dexterity, permanent gait spasticity). 
* Therefore, the plan’s 6-week PT requirement is clinically contraindicated for this presentation, and clinical standards override the plan criteria.

#### **2. Health Plan Criteria Misapplication (CC-SPINE-2024)**
* The initial reviewer, Dr. Huang, denied the "Myelopathy Exception" (Section 2.2A), which reduces the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks (which the member fully completed with 12 sessions). The plan's sole basis for this denial was that the primary billing diagnosis on the most recent note was listed as "cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy" (ICD-10 M47.812).
* This administrative denial represents a severe, mechanical misapplication of the plan's own clinical policy:
  * The member’s medical records consistently and unequivocally describe cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia in the clinical narrative.
  * The treating physician documented that the "radiculopathy" label on the referral was an administrative billing artifact.
  * The member's clinical presentation met all criteria of Section 2.1(b) (Cervical Myelopathy).
  * Prioritizing a billing code sequence over objective clinical examination findings and diagnostic MRI scans to deny a critical myelopathy exception violates prudent clinical utilization review standards. Clinically, the patient qualified for the Section 2.2A exception.
