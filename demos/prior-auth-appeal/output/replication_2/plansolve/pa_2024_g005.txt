## I. DECISION SUMMARY

### **FINAL DETERMINATION: OVERTURN**

The initial decision by HealthFirst Utilization Management on November 8, 2024, to deny coverage for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is **OVERTURNED**. 

The medical clinical records demonstrate that the member meets all standard plan-level medical necessity criteria under the HealthFirst Spinal Surgery Policy (CC-SPINE-2024). The health plan's initial denial was based on a critical factual error regarding the duration of completed physical therapy and the improper application of extra-contractual clinical criteria (perioperative HbA1c levels) that do not exist within the plan's published clinical coverage policies. Under California law, the requested procedure is contractually and clinically indicated and must be approved.

---

## II. CLINICAL HISTORY & DISPUTED ISSUES

### 1. Clinical Presentation and Symptoms
Mr. Gerald Obi is a 58-year-old male with a history of progressive, severe left-sided cervical radiculopathy. His symptoms include left C7 dermatomal pain and numbness, diminished left triceps reflex, and objective 4/5 weakness in left triceps extension and 4+/5 grip strength. The patient’s functional impairment is severe, resulting in reduced working hours.

### 2. Diagnostic Imaging
A cervical MRI performed on September 20, 2024, reveals a large C6-C7 disc herniation causing moderate left-sided C7 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis. There is no evidence of spinal cord contact, cord compression, or myelomalacia (T2 signal change).

### 3. Plan's Denial Grounds
On November 8, 2024, Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, on behalf of HealthFirst, denied the request citing two grounds:
*   **Ground 1 (PT Shortfall):** The plan asserted the member completed only 5 weeks of physical therapy, failing to meet the plan’s 6-week minimum requirement.
*   **Ground 2 (Diabetes Control):** The plan asserted that the member's Type 2 Diabetes is suboptimally controlled (HbA1c of 8.2% on October 15, 2024) and that surgical authorization is withheld until HbA1c is optimized to mitigate perioperative wound healing risks.

### 4. Appeal Basis
Dr. Amina Diallo appealed the decision on the following grounds:
1.  The health plan miscounted the physical therapy duration; the member actually completed 7 weeks of physical therapy.
2.  Diabetic control is a surgical planning consideration managed preoperatively by the surgical team, not a contractually defined prior authorization criterion under plan policy CC-SPINE-2024.
3.  All cervical spine criteria under plan policy CC-SPINE-2024 have been met.

---

## III. STEP-BY-STEP EVALUATION & LEGAL ANALYSIS

Applying the mandated **Hierarchy of Authority** ($California State Law & Regulations > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Plan Coverage Criteria$), the clinical and administrative evidence is evaluated as follows:

### Step 1: Procedural Adequacy and Disclosure Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
The denial notice dated November 8, 2024, cited the plan's conservative treatment policy and listed the clinical reasons for denial (PT duration and HbA1c level). While the notice is procedurally adequate in terms of disclosure, the substantive grounds utilized are factually and contractually deficient as detailed below.

### Step 2: Diagnostic and Clinical Classification Verification
Based on clinical documentation:
*   **Clinical Findings:** Left-sided, unilateral C7 dermatomal sensory deficit, left triceps reflex reduction, and localized C7 motor weakness. 
*   **Imaging Findings:** MRI shows C6-C7 disc herniation and nerve root compression. It explicitly notes: "*No cord contact. No myelomalacia.*"
*   **Classification:** The primary pathology is **Cervical Radiculopathy (C6-C7)**. The member does not demonstrate upper motor neuron signs (e.g., hyperreflexia, Hoffman sign, clonus, gait instability) or cord compression. Therefore, the myelopathy exceptions in Plan policy Section 2.2(A) or DMHC APL 22-014 do not apply. This case is evaluated under the standard radiculopathy pathway.

### Step 3: Evaluation of Plan-Level Criteria and Exceptions (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)
To obtain authorization for CPT 22551 for radiculopathy, the member must satisfy the medical necessity criteria under Section 2.2 of plan policy CC-SPINE-2024. A comparison of the plan requirements against the member's clinical record reveals:

1.  **Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1):** Met. The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy consistent with C6-C7 disc herniation and corresponding clinical findings (C7 weakness and reflex change).
2.  **Imaging Requirement (Section 2.3):** Met. The MRI from September 20, 2024 (within 6 months) demonstrates structural pathology consistent with symptoms.
3.  **Conservative Treatment Requirements (Section 2.2):**
    *   **Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a) - 6 weeks required):** **MET.**
        *   *Health Plan Claim:* The initial denial stated only 5 weeks were completed.
        *   *Objective Clinical Record:* Structured PT records document continuous treatment from **July 1, 2024, through August 16, 2024**, totaling **7 weeks and 21 sessions**. The plan’s reviewer committed a factual miscount of the record.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b) - 2 agents for 4+ weeks each required):** **MET.**
        *   The record documents *Pregabalin 150mg BID* for 5 months (neuropathic agent) and *Duloxetine 60mg* daily for 3 months (neuropathic agent).
    *   **Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c) - minimum 1 required):** **MET.**
        *   The record documents a *left C7 epidural steroid injection* on October 10, 2024, which provided only temporary (45%) relief for 4 weeks before symptoms returned to baseline.

**Finding:** The member met 100% of the plan's internal medical necessity criteria for standard cervical radiculopathy surgical authorization. The health plan’s physical therapy objection (Ground 1) was based on a false factual premise.

### Step 4: Evaluation of Statutory Overrides and Regulatory Exceptions
Even if the member had completed only 5 weeks of physical therapy (as the plan incorrectly claimed), the physical therapy prerequisite is legally waived under California law:
*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2):** A health plan cannot condition approval of surgery on the completion of a physical therapy course if the member has already reached a "functional plateau."
*   **Clinical Document Evidence:** At the time of physical therapy discharge on August 16, 2024, the physical therapist explicitly documented a **functional plateau**: *"no further functional improvement expected."*
*   **Statutory Effect:** Because the treating therapist documented a functional plateau, enforcing a mechanical 6-week minimum to deny authorization violates **CIC § 10169.5(c)**. Ground 1 is legally invalid on both factual and statutory grounds.

### Step 5: Clinical Standards Comparison and Extraneous Criteria Evaluation (Diabetes Control)
The plan's second ground for denial (Ground 2) is that the member's Type 2 Diabetes is suboptimally controlled (HbA1c 8.2%), which presents an elevated risk of wound healing complications.
*   **Contractual Analysis:** A comprehensive review of the plan's published medical policy, **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024**, reveals **no exclusion, criterion, or clinical barrier regarding HbA1c levels, diabetic control, or surgical optimization.**
*   **Regulatory Standard (California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32(b)):** Under California Independent Medical Review standards, a plan is prohibited from using extra-contractual clinical guidelines, personal medical preferences, or non-plan criteria as hard barriers to deny authorization when standard clinical medical necessity is demonstrated.
*   **Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine):** Generally accepted clinical standards establish that while diabetes control is an important aspect of preoperative optimization, it is a clinical planning consideration under the sole purview of the treating surgical team and the primary care physician or endocrinologist. It is not an administrative barrier used to deny prior authorization for spinal cord or nerve root decompression. Dr. Diallo’s notes confirm that standard perioperative protocols will be followed to optimize glucose control preoperatively.
*   **Statutory Effect:** Because diabetes control is not a contractually integrated criterion in CC-SPINE-2024, and the member meets all published criteria, the plan's application of Ground 2 represents an unlawful extra-contractual denial under **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**.

## IV. CONCLUSION & FINAL DISPOSITION

Applying the **Disposition Decision Tree**:
1.  The primary diagnosis is Cervical Radiculopathy (Step 2).
2.  The member successfully completed 7 weeks of physical therapy, 2 neuropathic pharmacotherapy trials, and 1 cervical epidural steroid injection, thereby fully meeting the plan's standard coverage criteria under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 (Step 3).
3.  Even if a physical therapy shortfall existed, it was legally waived under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) due to a documented functional plateau (Step 4).
4.  The health plan's denial based on HbA1c levels (Ground 2) is contractually invalid because it relies on non-plan, extra-contractual clinical criteria in violation of CHSC § 1374.32(b) (Step 5).

Therefore, the health plan's initial denial is medically and legally unsupported. The appropriate disposition is a complete **OVERTURN**.

### **ORDER OF THE BOARD**
HealthFirst is hereby **ORDERED** to immediately authorize the requested single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) for member Gerald Obi, as requested by Dr. Amina Diallo, MD, to be performed at Stanford Medical Center. This authorization is effective immediately for the standard 90-day coverage window.
