## FINAL APPEAL DETERMINATION
Pursuant to California regulatory requirements and generally accepted standards of medical practice, the health plan’s initial decision to deny prior authorization for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level — prophylactic) is **UPHELD**. 

Surgical intervention is not medically necessary under either plan criteria or generally accepted clinical standards for axial neck pain in the absence of objective clinical myelopathy, radiculopathy, or structural spinal cord compression.

---

## EVALUATION & FINDINGS

### 1. PROCEDURAL COMPLIANCE REVIEW (Step 1)
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), a denial notice is legally defective if it fails to specify the exact clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reasoning, or if it misrepresents the clinical record. 

*   **Analysis:** The initial denial notice issued on October 25, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, cited the specific plan policy applied (**HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024**). The notice provided a medically accurate clinical rationale matching the submitted records—specifically, that the member presents with axial neck pain only, with no documented radiculopathy or myelopathy. The denial notice also included the mandatory Independent Medical Review (IMR) disclosures.
*   **Finding:** The denial notice is **procedurally adequate and valid** under CHSC § 1374.31(b). The review must therefore proceed to clinical evaluation.

### 2. DIAGNOSTIC CLASSIFICATION & VERIFICATION (Step 2)
The clinical record was reviewed to verify the member’s true diagnosis by cross-referencing specialist exams and objective imaging.

*   **Imaging Review (Cervical MRI, 2024-09-15):** The MRI of the cervical spine demonstrates severe central canal stenosis at C5-C6 with a canal diameter of 6mm (normal >13mm). Notably, the radiologist documented "cord contact **without compression**." Furthermore, there is **no T2 signal change** and **no evidence of myelomalacia** (spinal cord injury).
*   **Neurological Examination (2024-09-20):** The physical examination conducted by the specialist is completely normal. There are no signs of cervical radiculopathy (no unilateral dermatomal pain, numbness, or weakness) and no signs of cervical myelopathy (no gait disturbance, no bilateral upper extremity weakness, and no upper motor neuron signs, including a negative Hoffman sign and normal reflexes). The member's clinical presentation is limited to localized axial neck pain (rated 5/10).
*   **Finding:** The member does not meet the diagnostic criteria for cervical myelopathy or cervical radiculopathy. The verified diagnosis is **axial neck pain with asymptomatic severe cervical stenosis** (cord contact without structural compression or cord injury).

### 3. APPLICABILITY OF STATE LAW OVERRIDES (Step 3)
California statutory provisions restrict health plans from mechanically enforcing physical therapy (PT) or conservative care prerequisites under specific circumstances.

*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5:** Insurers cannot condition surgical approval on PT where there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address (such as cord compression with myelomalacia).
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:** Standard conservative treatment pathways are waived for time-sensitive, progressive neurological conditions where objective imaging shows progressive pathology (such as progressive cord signal changes/myelomalacia) and the physician documents that conservative trials are futile.
*   **Analysis:** While the member's MRI shows a narrow canal (6mm) with cord contact, there is **no objective cord compression, no myelomalacia, and no progressive neurological deficits**. Because there is no structural neurological compromise (the spinal cord is structurally intact and undamaged), the exceptions under CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014 do not apply.
*   **Finding:** State law overrides to conservative treatment prerequisites are **not applicable** in this case.

### 4. CLINICAL STANDARDS EVALUATION (Step 4)
Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the standard of review for medical necessity is governed by generally accepted standards of medical practice (e.g., AANS/CNS and AO Spine guidelines), which supersede restrictive plan criteria.

*   **AANS/CNS (2023) & AO Spine (2021) Guidelines:** These major spine societies establish that surgical decompression (such as CPT 22551) is recommended as a primary treatment for patients presenting with moderate-to-severe *symptomatic* cervical myelopathy, objective cord compression, or myelomalacia (Tier 1 Strong Indications).
*   **Tier 3 Classification (Surgery Not Indicated):** For patients presenting with **axial neck pain only** without clinical radiculopathy or myelopathy, and where imaging shows stenosis/cord contact without myelomalacia or active compression, surgical intervention is classified as **Tier 3 (Not Indicated)**. 
*   **Prophylactic Surgery Rationale:** Dr. Davis requested prophylactic surgery on the basis that the member works as a heavy equipment construction worker and is at high risk for acute spinal cord injury from minor cervical trauma. However, clinical evidence bases (including the Cochrane Review, 2021) do not support prophylactic cervical fusion for asymptomatic stenosis without objective neurological deficits, even in high-risk occupations. The potential surgical risks of an elective ACDF (including adjacent segment disease, pseudarthrosis, and dysphagia) outweigh the statistical, unproven benefit of prophylactic decompression in an asymptomatic individual.
*   **Finding:** The requested prophylactic surgery **violates generally accepted clinical standards** and is considered clinically unsupported.

### 5. PLAN CLINICAL CRITERIA COMPLIANCE (Step 5)
The requested procedure was evaluated against the requirements of HealthFirst Plan Clinical Criteria CC-SPINE-2024.

*   **Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement):** To qualify for cervical spine surgery, the member must have a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (with matching dermatomal symptoms) or cervical myelopathy (with matching clinical deficits and cord compression on imaging).
*   **Section 3.1 (Exclusions):** This section explicitly states: *"Authorization will be DENIED when... Surgery is requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy."*
*   **Analysis:** The member has successfully completed conservative treatment for his axial neck pain, including 8 weeks (24 sessions) of physical therapy, 3 months of NSAIDs (Naproxen), 6 weeks of muscle relaxants (Cyclobenzaprine), and a C5-C6 epidural steroid injection (providing 60% temporary relief). However, because the patient’s primary and only symptom is axial neck pain, and clinical exams demonstrate a completely normal neurological status, the exclusion under Section 3.1 is triggered. The modified "Myelopathy Exception" (Section 2.2(A)) cannot be utilized because the diagnostic threshold for myelopathy is not met.
*   **Finding:** The request fails to meet the medical necessity criteria of **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1**, and is explicitly excluded under **Section 3.1**.

## CONCLUSION OF THE DISPOSITION TREE
1.  **Step 1:** The initial denial was **Adequate** (proceed to Step 2).
2.  **Step 2:** The clinical record verifies **Axial Only** pain with asymptomatic stenosis, without radiculopathy or myelopathy.
3.  **Path:** The decision tree dictates that for **Axial Only** presentations where no procedural defect exists, the correct clinical and legal outcome is to **UPHOLD** the denial.

---

## FORMAL DETERMINATION

The request for prior authorization of **CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level — prophylactic)** is **denied**, and the original clinical denial is **UPHELD**. 

### Rationale:
Although the member has a significantly narrowed cervical canal (6mm) at C5-C6, both objective imaging (MRI) and clinical examinations confirm the absence of spinal cord compression, myelomalacia, radiculopathy, or clinical myelopathy. Generally accepted medical guidelines (AANS/CNS, AO Spine) and plan coverage documents (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024) do not support major reconstructive spinal surgery (fusion) for localized axial neck pain or for prophylactic purposes based solely on occupational trauma risk.
