### **DETAILED EVALUATION REPORT**

#### **Step 1: Procedural Defect & Adequacy Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))**
*   **Protocol:** Review the initial denial notice dated November 5, 2024, issued by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, to verify if the notice explicitly cited the clinical coverage criteria applied and provided the specific clinical reasons for the denial.
*   **Findings:** The denial notice was issued within the statutory timeframes required under CHSC § 1367.01. The notice explicitly cited the standard conservative treatment pathways. It provided specific clinical metrics: completion of only 3 of the 6 required weeks of physical therapy, completion of only 1 pharmacotherapy agent (Baclofen), and the absence of any documented interventional procedure.
*   **Conclusion:** The denial notice is procedurally adequate under **CHSC § 1374.31(b)**. It clearly articulated the clinical standard applied (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024) and outlined the precise deficiencies. Therefore, no procedural defect is present. The evaluation proceeds to Step 2.

#### **Step 2: Clinical Diagnosis & Pathology Classification**
*   **Protocol:** Analyze the clinical documentation and imaging studies to classify the pathology as either cervical radiculopathy (root compression) or cervical myelopathy with structural neurological compromise (spinal cord compression, upper motor neuron signs, and myelomalacia/T2 cord signal change).
*   **Findings:** 
    *   **Clinical Presentation:** The member, a 61-year-old male, presents with mild cervical myelopathy at C5-C6. Neurological examination on October 5, 2024, notes bilateral Hoffman signs and mild bilateral hyperreflexia, which are objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs. Tandem gait is intact, bilateral hand grip strength is preserved at 4+/5, and there are no frank motor or sensory dermatomal deficits. Activities of daily living (ADLs) are fully intact and independent. The functional status is categorized as mild, with a modified Japanese Orthopaedic Association (mJOA) score of 15/18.
    *   **Imaging (MRI dated October 1, 2024):** The MRI demonstrates moderate central cord compression at C5-C6 and a canal diameter of 8mm. However, the radiologist explicitly notes: **"No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia. Cord contact present without compression."** 
*   **Conclusion:** Although the patient has clinical signs of mild myelopathy (UMN signs, mJOA score of 15), there is **no objective evidence of myelomalacia or T2 cord signal change** on the MRI. This indicates that while there is structural narrowing and cord contact, active spinal cord injury/parenchymal damage (myelomalacia) has not occurred. The pathology is classified as **mild, clinically stable cervical myelopathy without myelomalacia**. Per the evaluation protocol, because myelomalacia is absent, we proceed to Step 4.

#### **Step 3: State Statutory & Regulatory Override Evaluation (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)**
*   *For completeness of review, statutory overrides were evaluated:*
*   **Protocol:** Determine if the member's presentation triggers a mandatory statutory waiver of conservative treatment. Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)** and **DMHC APL 22-014**, plans cannot require physical therapy or conservative trials if objective imaging shows progressive pathology or "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address" (explicitly defined as myelomalacia/T2 cord signal change).
*   **Findings:** The member's MRI explicitly rules out myelomalacia ("No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia"). Furthermore, the clinical records document that the patient's symptoms have been stable for 6 months. There is no serial imaging demonstrating progressive pathology, nor is there clinical documentation of progressive neurological decline on exam.
*   **Conclusion:** Because there is no objective evidence of myelomalacia, structural neurological compromise of an irreversible nature, or progressive clinical deterioration, the statutory exemptions under **CIC § 10169.5** and **DMHC APL 22-014** do not apply. The plan is not legally prohibited from requiring conservative treatment.

#### **Step 4: Plan Criteria Application & Myelopathy Exception Review (Section 2.2A)**
*   **Protocol:** Evaluate whether the member met the plan's modified "Myelopathy Pathway Exception" under HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(A).
*   **Criteria under Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Exception):**
    1.  **Physical Therapy:** Reduced from 6 weeks to a minimum of **4 weeks**.
    2.  **Interventional Treatment:** Waived if the treating physician documents that interventional procedures pose a neurological risk.
    3.  **Pharmacotherapy:** Remains in full (trial of at least **two** agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each).
*   **Findings vs. Member's Record:**
    1.  *Physical Therapy:* The member completed only **3 weeks** (9 sessions) of physical therapy. It was halted prematurely by Dr. Osei on November 1, 2024, citing general myelopathy concerns, before reaching the modified 4-week plan threshold.
    2.  *Interventional Treatment:* No interventional procedures were performed. Dr. Osei's letter dated November 1, 2024, documented a generalized risk of delay but did not provide a specific clinical rationale stating that an interventional procedure (such as a selective nerve block or epidural) posed an imminent risk of neurological deterioration.
    3.  *Pharmacotherapy:* The member completed a trial of only **one** agent (Baclofen 5mg BID for 6 weeks, which resulted in mild spasticity improvement). No second agent (e.g., NSAIDs, gabapentinoids, oral corticosteroids) was attempted or documented as medically contraindicated.
*   **Conclusion:** The member did not meet the modified Myelopathy Pathway criteria under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(A). The physical therapy duration fell short of the 4-week exception minimum, and the pharmacotherapy requirement of two agents was not met (only one trialed). Therefore, the initial denial was a correct application of the plan's internal coverage criteria. The evaluation proceeds to Step 5.

#### **Step 5: Generally Accepted Clinical Standards Review (CHSC § 1374.32(b))**
*   **Protocol:** Evaluate the clinical presentation against external clinical standards (**AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines**, **AO Spine 2021 Guidelines**, and **NASS 2020 Guidelines**) to determine if the patient presents with a "Tier 1 — Strong Surgical Indication" that supersedes plan criteria under **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**.
*   **Clinical Standards Analysis:**
    *   **AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines:** Surgical decompression is a Tier 1 (Strong) recommendation for patients presenting with *moderate-to-severe* cervical myelopathy (typically defined as mJOA < 15) or patients with *progressive* neurological deficits or *myelomalacia* on MRI. For *mild, stable* cervical myelopathy (mJOA 15–17), conservative management with close clinical monitoring is a highly supported and widely accepted Tier 2 pathway.
    *   **AO Spine 2021 Clinical Practice Guidelines:** Recommend surgical intervention as the primary treatment for moderate-to-severe myelopathy. However, for patients with mild degenerative cervical myelopathy (mJOA score 15–17) who are stable and without objective myelomalacia, AO Spine recommends initial conservative management or structured clinical observation, as these patients often remain stable for extended periods without progressive deficit.
    *   **Clinical Findings Alignment:** 
        *   The patient's mJOA score is **15/18**, placing him squarely in the "mild myelopathy" category.
        *   Symptom duration is **6 months and documented as stable**.
        *   Tandem gait is fully intact, hand grip is functional (4+/5), and ADLs are fully preserved and independent.
        *   There is **no myelomalacia** on MRI.
        *   While Dr. Kwame Osei’s letter of November 1, 2024, asserts "surgical urgency," this subjective declaration is directly contradicted by the objective clinical findings in his own notes (stable symptoms, intact gait, mild hyperreflexia, normal ADLs) and the objective radiologist report (no T2 signal change, no myelomalacia).
*   **Conclusion:** The clinical presentation represents stable, mild cervical myelopathy without spinal cord parenchymal injury (no myelomalacia). Under generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021, NASS 2020), this presentation does not meet the criteria for immediate surgical decompression (Tier 1). Structured conservative therapy is clinically appropriate and represents the standard of care. Because the clinical presentation does not meet Tier 1 standards, generally accepted guidelines do not override the plan’s conservative treatment requirements.

There is no conflict of authority in this case. California statutory overrides and generally accepted clinical standards both reserve mandatory conservative therapy waivers for cases involving objective structural injury (myelomalacia), progressive neurological deterioration, or moderate-to-severe functional impairment. Because Mr. Achebe's condition is clinically mild and stable, and lacks imaging signs of cord injury, the standard conservative treatment requirements outlined in **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(A)** are medically appropriate, clinically sound, and legally enforceable.

The member must complete the remaining requirements of the modified Myelopathy Pathway—specifically completing at least one additional week of physical therapy (to reach the 4-week threshold) and trialing a second pharmacotherapy agent (such as an NSAID, gabapentinoid, or muscle relaxant) for 4 weeks—or demonstrate objective evidence of clinical/radiographical progression before surgical prior authorization can be approved.

The initial denial of coverage for CPT 22551 is **UPHELD**.
