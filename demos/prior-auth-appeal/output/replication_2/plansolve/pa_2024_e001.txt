### **PART II: STEP-BY-STEP CLINICAL & REGULATORY EVALUATION**

#### **Step 1: Procedural Adequacy and Defect Check**
The initial denial notice issued on November 1, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, was evaluated against **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b)**. 
*   **Findings:** The denial notice explicitly identified the clinical coverage policy applied (*HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024*), supplied a specific clinical rationale for the non-approval of the C6-C7 level ("shows mild changes only; two-level fusion not approved when one level meets criteria and one does not"), and provided mandatory Independent Medical Review (IMR) notification rights to the member.
*   **Conclusion:** The initial denial letter is procedurally adequate. There are no procedural defects under CHSC § 1374.31(b). This review must proceed to a clinical evaluation of the disputed levels.

#### **Step 2: Diagnosis and Clinical Disease Classification**
The member’s clinical presentation, neurological examination, and imaging records were analyzed to classify the disease state.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-15):** Shows a large disc herniation at C5-C6 causing moderate-to-severe right C6 nerve root compression. Crucially, there is *no* spinal cord compression, *no* cord contact, and *no* evidence of T2 cord signal change (myelomalacia) at either level.
*   **Neurological Examination (2024-09-20):** Reveals clear unilateral, right-sided C6 dermatomal symptoms: bicep weakness (3+/5), absent right bicep reflex, and C6 dermatomal numbness/tingling. There are no upper motor neuron (UMN) signs (e.g., hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman's sign, clonus, gait instability, or bilateral upper extremity weakness).
*   **Classification:** The member's primary pathology is classified as **Cervical Radiculopathy** of the right C6 nerve root. There is **no clinical or imaging evidence of Cervical Myelopathy**. Because myelopathy is not present, standard radiculopathy pathways apply.

#### **Step 3: Evaluation of Myelopathy Exceptions and State Law Waivers**
*   **State Law Evaluation:** Under **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3)** and **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014**, standard conservative therapy requirements (like physical therapy) are waived or modified *only* when there is objective imaging evidence of progressive structural neurological compromise (such as spinal cord compression with myelomalacia) or when physical therapy is documented as contraindicated.
*   **Application to Case:** MRI findings confirm no spinal cord compression, no cord contact, and no myelomalacia at either the C5-C6 or C6-C7 levels. No clinical documentation suggests physical therapy was medically contraindicated. Therefore, the statutory conservative treatment waivers of CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014 do not apply to this case. The patient's clinical necessity must be evaluated under standard clinical criteria.

#### **Step 5: Multi-Level Surgical Necessity and Clinical Standard Alignment**
The treating surgeon's appeal asserts that "adjacent levels should be addressed simultaneously to prevent accelerated degeneration" and that both levels are symptomatic. This assertion is evaluated against **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**, which mandates that health services be evaluated against generally accepted standards of medical practice (**AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023** and **AO Spine 2021**), overriding restrictive plan guidelines when clinical standards dictate.

*   **Clinical Standards for C5-C6:** Under **AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines**, a single-level ACDF (CPT 22551) at C5-C6 is highly supported as a **Tier 1 (Strong Indication)** surgical intervention due to persistent, severe radiculopathy, 3+/5 motor deficit, and correlating moderate-to-severe C6 nerve root compression that failed a comprehensive 8-week course of conservative therapy.
*   **Clinical Standards for C6-C7:** Under **AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines (Section 3.1, Tier 3)**, surgical decompression and fusion is **not indicated** for levels exhibiting mild, clinically insignificant degenerative changes without objective cord or nerve root compression and without correlating, objective dermatomal symptoms.
*   **Refutation of "Prophylactic Adjacent-Level" Fusion:** 
    *   The treating surgeon's argument that the C6-C7 level must be fused to "prevent accelerated degeneration" directly conflicts with established peer-reviewed spinal literature and clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021).
    *   Fusing an asymptomatic adjacent segment with only mild degenerative changes does *not* prevent adjacent-segment pathology. On the contrary, expanding a fusion from one level (C5-C6) to two levels (C5-C7) unnecessarily restricts cervical range of motion, increases surgical morbidity, and increases mechanical stress on the newly adjacent C7-T1 segment, actually accelerating its degeneration.
    *   Clinical standards mandate preserving mobile spinal segments unless there is objective, symptomatic structural compromise at that specific level. Consequently, the plan’s exclusion of the C6-C7 level is entirely aligned with generally accepted standards of medical practice.

### **PART III: FINAL DISPOSITION**

Pursuant to the step-by-step clinical and regulatory review:
1.  The authorization request for a **single-level ACDF at C5-C6 (CPT 22551)** is **APPROVED** (Overturned). The member has documented C6 radiculopathy with correlating moderate-to-severe nerve root compression and has failed a complete, documented trial of conservative care, satisfying both Plan Criteria and AANS/CNS Tier 1 surgical standards.
2.  The authorization request for the **additional level at C6-C7 (CPT 22552)** is **DENIED** (Upheld). Current imaging and clinical exams establish that C6-C7 exhibits only mild, clinically insignificant degenerative changes without objective nerve root compression or correlating clinical symptoms. Performing an adjacent-level fusion for prophylactic purposes is clinically unsupported, increases patient risk, and violates AANS/CNS and AO Spine guidelines.
