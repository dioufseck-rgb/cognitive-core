**REVIEW SUMMARY**

This appeal concerns a denial for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) procedure at C5-C6 for Frances Kim, a 58-year-old member diagnosed with suspected cervical myelopathy. The initial denial (MED-003) stated the myelopathy exception did not apply due to imaging showing "mild cord contact only — no cord compression, no cord signal change," and that upper motor neuron (UMN) signs were not conclusive without imaging confirmation. Consequently, the standard 6-week Physical Therapy (PT) requirement was applied, which the member did not meet (5 weeks completed).

The appeal contends that clinical myelopathy is established by the presence of three positive UMN signs regardless of imaging, that mild cord contact plus UMN signs constitutes myelopathy, and that 5 weeks of PT under a myelopathy pathway (requiring 4 weeks) is sufficient.

A comprehensive review was conducted applying the three-source hierarchy: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.

---

**DISPOSITION: OVERTURN**

The denial for CPT code 22551 for Frances Kim is **OVERTURNED**.

---

**WARRANT**

The denial of prior authorization for ACDF (CPT 22551) is overturned based on the following findings, which demonstrate that the member's request aligns with state law and generally accepted clinical standards, overriding more restrictive plan criteria:

1.  **Confirmed Diagnosis of Cervical Myelopathy (Clinical Standard vs. Plan Criteria):**
    *   **Plan's Denial:** The plan denied the myelopathy exception, arguing that imaging did not show definitive cord compression or signal change, and therefore UMN signs were not conclusive for myelopathy. This implies a strict imaging requirement for diagnosis.
    *   **Clinical Standard:** Generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023, Section 1.1; NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines 2020, Section 1.1; Clinical Record Note) explicitly state that a clinical diagnosis of cervical myelopathy is established by the presence of multiple positive upper motor neuron signs on examination. Imaging confirmation supports, but is *not required* for diagnosis; "imaging-negative myelopathy" is a recognized entity. Frances Kim presents with robust clinical evidence of myelopathy, including positive Hoffman bilateral, bilateral upper extremity hyperreflexia, sustained clonus, a positive finger escape sign, and a mildly wide-based gait.
    *   **Conflict Resolution:** The clinical standard for myelopathy diagnosis, which prioritizes clinical presentation over strict imaging findings, supersedes the plan's more restrictive requirement for unequivocal imaging confirmation (CHSC § 1374.32(b)). Therefore, the member **does have a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy**, and the plan's myelopathy exception should have been considered.

2.  **Physical Therapy Requirement (State Law & Clinical Standard Override Plan Criteria):**
    *   **Plan's Myelopathy Exception:** For confirmed myelopathy, the plan's criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A)) reduce the PT requirement to a 4-week minimum. The member completed 5 weeks, thus meeting this reduced requirement.
    *   **State Law Override (CIC § 10169.5):** California Insurance Code § 10169.5 prohibits health plans from conditioning surgical approval on PT completion where:
        *   (a)(1) The treating physician documents PT is unlikely to benefit: The physician halted PT "citing myelopathy concern," indicating a determination that further PT was not beneficial for the myelopathic condition.
        *   (a)(2) A functional plateau has been reached: The clinical record notes "Neurological signs unchanged" after 5 weeks of PT. Clinical standards define a functional plateau as a lack of meaningful improvement, and for myelopathy, neurological symptoms are generally not expected to respond to PT (Clinical Standard Section 5).
        *   (a)(3) The condition involves objective structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address: Clinically confirmed cervical myelopathy with multiple UMN signs and objective cord contact on MRI constitutes such compromise.
    *   **Clinical Standard Override:** Leading guidelines (AO Spine Guidelines 2021, Section 2.1; NASS Guidelines 2020, Section 2.1; Cochrane Review 2021, Section 2.1) state there is *no high-quality evidence* supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery for such a trial is not supported by evidence and may lead to neurological deterioration. Applying radiculopathy-derived PT requirements to myelopathy is a "category error."
    *   **Conflict Resolution:** Both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards unequivocally override the plan's PT duration requirements, including the 4-week minimum under its myelopathy exception. The 5 weeks of PT completed, combined with the documented lack of neurological improvement and physician's rationale for cessation, are more than sufficient.

3.  **Conservative Treatment Requirements (Pharmacotherapy & Interventional — DMHC APL 22-014 Override):**
    *   **Plan's Myelopathy Exception:** While the member completed 5 weeks of PT, the pharmacotherapy requirement for two agents for 4+ weeks each was not fully met (Methylprednisolone for 10 days), and interventional waiver requires explicit physician documentation of risk (which was not present).
    *   **State Law Override (DMHC APL 22-014, Section II.2):** This APL states that for conditions risking irreversible harm, plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when: (b) The treating physician documents conservative treatment failure, AND (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. The clinical record documents that Baclofen provided only "minimal spasticity relief" and Methylprednisolone resulted in "No improvement in UMN signs," demonstrating conservative treatment failure for the primary myelopathic pathology.
    *   **Clinical Standard:** Surgery for myelopathy with clinical signs and objective cord contact is a TIER 1 strong surgical indication (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023, Section 3.1). Conservative treatment has "LIMITED efficacy for established myelopathy" and should not delay surgery (Clinical Standard Section 2.1, 2.3).
    *   **Conflict Resolution:** Given the clinically established myelopathy, the documented failure of conservative therapies to improve neurological signs, and the strong surgical indication per clinical guidelines, DMHC APL 22-014 and the overarching principle that clinical standards supersede restrictive plan criteria (CHSC § 1374.32(b)) require approval of surgery, even if all aspects of the plan's conservative treatment criteria (beyond PT) were not met.

In conclusion, Frances Kim's clinical presentation meets the diagnostic criteria for cervical myelopathy under generally accepted clinical standards. The requirements for conservative treatment, particularly physical therapy duration, are overridden by both California state law and clinical evidence-based guidelines. The continued denial of surgical intervention for this condition poses a risk of neurological deterioration, which is contrary to accepted standards of medical care.
