**EXECUTIVE SUMMARY:**
The initial prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 for member Frances Kim (M-990011) was denied based on insufficient imaging confirmation of myelopathy and failure to complete a 6-week physical therapy (PT) requirement. Upon review of the clinical record, plan criteria, California regulatory framework, and generally accepted clinical standards, the denial is hereby **OVERTURNED**. The member's clinical presentation clearly establishes cervical myelopathy, and the physical therapy requirement is satisfied under the plan's own myelopathy exception or, alternatively, waived by state law and unsupported by clinical evidence for myelopathy.

---

**REVIEW OF DENIAL REASON:**
The initial denial, dated 2024-10-30, cited reason code MED-003, stating: "Myelopathy exception does not apply. Imaging shows mild cord contact only – no cord compression, no cord signal change. Upper motor neuron signs on exam are not conclusive for myelopathy in the absence of imaging confirmation. Standard 6-week PT requirement applies; member completed 5 weeks."

---

**CLINICAL RECORD SUMMARY:**
*   **Member:** Frances Kim, 58 years old, HealthFirst PPO Gold, California.
*   **Procedure Requested:** ACDF C5-C6, one level (CPT 22551).
*   **Diagnosis:** Suspected cervical myelopathy – imaging-negative UMN syndrome.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-01):** C5-C6 disc herniation with mild cord contact. No measurable cord compression. No T2 signal change/myelomalacia. Note: Cord contact is present but not quantifiable as compression on standard sequences.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-09-05):** Positive Hoffman bilateral, hyperreflexia bilateral upper extremities, sustained clonus at bilateral ankles (3 beats), positive finger escape sign, mildly wide-based gait. Patient denies lower extremity symptoms. These are all definitive Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) signs.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 5 weeks (15 sessions) completed under myelopathy protocol. Neurological signs unchanged. Physician halted PT citing myelopathy concern.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Baclofen 5mg TID for 2 months (minimal spasticity relief); Methylprednisolone taper for 10 days (no improvement in UMN signs).
    *   **Interventional:** None.

---

**APPEAL REVIEW AND ANALYSIS (Applying Hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):**

**1. Myelopathy Diagnosis (Conflict Resolution):**
    *   **Plan Denial:** Asserts that UMN signs are "not conclusive for myelopathy in the absence of imaging confirmation" and notes "no cord compression, no cord signal change" on MRI.
    *   **Clinical Standard (Higher Authority):** The AANS/CNS guidelines (per "Clinical Standard Note" in case record and Source Document 3, Section 1.1) state: "Clinical myelopathy diagnosis requires UMN signs on examination. Imaging confirmation supports but is not required for diagnosis. Imaging-negative myelopathy is a recognized entity where clinical signs precede MRI changes. Multiple positive UMN signs in the absence of other explanation = clinical myelopathy." The member presents with unequivocal UMN signs (bilateral Hoffman, hyperreflexia, clonus, finger escape, wide-based gait).
    *   **Conclusion:** The plan's requirement for definitive imaging confirmation to diagnose myelopathy, despite clear clinical UMN signs, is more restrictive than generally accepted medical practice. Based on clinical standards, Frances Kim *does* have cervical myelopathy.

**2. Physical Therapy (PT) Requirement (Conflict Resolution):**
    *   **Plan Denial:** States the "myelopathy exception does not apply" and therefore "Standard 6-week PT requirement applies; member completed 5 weeks."
    *   **Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A) EXCEPTION — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY):** For confirmed cervical myelopathy, the physical therapy requirement is reduced to a "4 weeks minimum."
    *   **Analysis:** Since myelopathy is confirmed based on clinical standards (as resolved above), the plan's own myelopathy exception applies. The member completed 5 weeks of PT, which *exceeds* the 4-week minimum required under the myelopathy pathway. Thus, the PT requirement is *met* under the plan's own criteria.
    *   **State Law (Higher Authority — California Insurance Code § 10169.5):** Even if the plan's 6-week PT requirement were to apply, CIC § 10169.5 mandates that a plan shall not condition approval on PT completion where:
        *   **(a)(1) Physician documents PT unlikely to benefit:** The treating physician halted PT citing "myelopathy concern," indicating a belief that further PT was unlikely to benefit the underlying neurological condition.
        *   **(a)(2) Member reached a functional plateau:** After 5 weeks of PT, the clinical record notes "Neurological signs unchanged." Clinical standards define functional plateau as failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement, and explicitly state that "Neurological symptoms in myelopathy (weakness, hyperreflexia) are not expected to respond to PT regardless of duration" (Source Document 3, Section 5). Therefore, a functional plateau for the myelopathic symptoms has been reached.
        *   **(a)(3) Condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address:** Cervical disc herniation with cord contact and established clinical myelopathy constitutes such compromise. PT cannot resolve spinal cord compression or reverse UMN damage.
    *   **Clinical Standard (Higher Authority — Source Document 3, Section 2.1 & 2.2):** AO Spine Guidelines (2021) state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." The guidelines also clarify that the 6-week PT requirement often derives from radiculopathy evidence and is a "category error" when applied to myelopathy.
    *   **DMHC APL 22-014, Section II.3 (Higher Authority):** This APL states: "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice... Where a conflict exists... the clinical standard governs." The plan's rigid PT requirement, especially for myelopathy, is more restrictive than clinical standards.
    *   **Conclusion:** The PT requirement has been met under the plan's own myelopathy exception. Furthermore, state law (CIC § 10169.5) and generally accepted clinical standards unequivocally override any mandatory minimum PT duration for this myelopathic condition.

**3. Medical Necessity and Overall Review:**
    *   The member has clinically confirmed cervical myelopathy, a condition for which surgical decompression (ACDF) is indicated under generally accepted standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS Guidelines 2023, Source Document 3, Section 3.1, Tier 1/2 indications). The plan's criteria for diagnosis and conservative treatment are more restrictive than these standards.
    *   **State Law (CHSC § 1374.32(b) — IMR Standard):** This provision explicitly states that an Independent Medical Review (IMR) *shall recommend approval* if the service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, "regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." This principle applies to internal appeals where state law or clinical standards override plan criteria.

---

**DISPOSITION:**
The denial issued for Frances Kim's ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is **OVERTURNED**.

**WARRANT:**
The denial is overturned for the following reasons:
1.  **Myelopathy Diagnosis Confirmed:** The member's neurological examination exhibits multiple, unambiguous Upper Motor Neuron signs, which, according to AANS/CNS guidelines, unequivocally establish a diagnosis of cervical myelopathy. Imaging confirmation, while supportive, is not a prerequisite for diagnosis in the presence of clear clinical findings, and "imaging-negative myelopathy" is a recognized entity. The plan's assertion that UMN signs are inconclusive without imaging confirmation is inconsistent with generally accepted clinical standards (Clinical Standard Note in Case Record; Source Document 3, Section 1.1).
2.  **Physical Therapy Requirement Met/Waived:**
    *   Given the confirmed diagnosis of myelopathy, the plan's own **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A) Myelopathy Pathway Exception** reduces the minimum PT requirement to 4 weeks. The member completed 5 weeks of PT, thereby satisfying this criterion.
    *   Furthermore, even if the standard 6-week PT requirement were to apply, **California Insurance Code § 10169.5** overrides it due to: a) the treating physician documenting that PT was unlikely to benefit and halting treatment due to myelopathy concern, b) the member having reached a functional plateau with unchanged neurological signs, and c) the presence of objective structural neurological compromise (clinical myelopathy from disc herniation and cord contact) that PT cannot address (Source Document 2, Part III; Source Document 3, Section 5).
    *   Finally, generally accepted clinical standards (AO Spine 2021) explicitly state there is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgery for moderate to severe cervical myelopathy, and such delays may result in neurological deterioration (Source Document 3, Section 2.1 & 2.2). This is further supported by **DMHC APL 22-014, Section II.3**, which prohibits plans from applying internal criteria more restrictive than generally accepted standards.
3.  **Medical Necessity Under Clinical Standards:** The requested ACDF procedure for established cervical myelopathy, where conservative treatment has failed to improve neurological signs, is consistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS 2023, Source Document 3, Section 3.1). **CHSC § 1374.32(b)** mandates approval of services meeting generally accepted standards, irrespective of more restrictive plan criteria (Source Document 2, Part I).

Therefore, the requested surgical procedure is medically necessary and should be authorized.
