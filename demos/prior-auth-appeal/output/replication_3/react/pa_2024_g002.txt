**I. SUMMARY OF PRIOR AUTHORIZATION REQUEST AND DENIAL:**

A prior authorization request was submitted for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 for Ms. Priya Nair. The request was denied by HealthFirst Utilization Management on 2024-10-25 (Denial Reason Code MED-003) stating that "Member does not meet conservative treatment requirements under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. Physical therapy: 5 weeks completed. Standard pathway requires 6 weeks minimum. Member has not completed the required minimum conservative treatment."

---

**II. APPEAL BASIS:**

The treating provider, Dr. James Chen, appealed the denial on the following grounds:
1.  The plan incorrectly applied Section 2.2 (standard pathway); the myelopathy pathway (Section 2.2A) should apply.
2.  The member has a confirmed myelopathy diagnosis, supported by bilateral UMN signs and cord compression on MRI.
3.  The myelopathy pathway (Section 2.2A) requires a minimum of 4 weeks of physical therapy, and the member completed 5 weeks, exceeding this requirement.
4.  Under the correct myelopathy pathway, all criteria are met.

---

**III. REVIEW FINDINGS:**

This appeal was reviewed by an expert health plan appeals reviewer applying the three-source hierarchy: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.

**A. Clinical Presentation and Diagnosis:**
Ms. Priya Nair is a 56-year-old female with a confirmed diagnosis of Cervical Myelopathy at C5-C6. This diagnosis is supported by:
*   **Imaging:** MRI dated 2024-09-01 demonstrating "C5-C6 moderate central cord compression." (No T2 signal change/myelomalacia noted).
*   **Neurological Exam:** Dated 2024-09-05, revealing "Bilateral Hoffman signs. Bilateral hyperreflexia upper extremities. Tandem gait mildly impaired. Bilateral hand clumsiness." These are consistent with upper motor neuron signs.
*   **Diagnosis Confirmation:** Neurologist consultation on 2024-09-12 confirming "Clinical picture consistent with cervical myelopathy. Bilateral UMN signs are pathognomonic. Diagnosis: cervical myelopathy, primary." (ICD-10: G35.89 cervical myelopathy).

Conservative treatment attempted included:
*   **Physical Therapy:** 5 weeks (15 sessions) completed under a myelopathy protocol. Documented "No improvement in UMN signs." PT was halted by the treating physician due to the diagnosis and lack of improvement.
*   **Pharmacotherapy:** Baclofen 10mg TID for 3 months (minimal spasticity relief) and Methylprednisolone dose pack for 3 weeks (temporary improvement only).
*   **Interventional:** No interventional procedures were performed, as the treating physician documented this as not indicated due to neurological urgency (cord compression with myelopathy requiring surgical evaluation).

**B. Review of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

1.  **Diagnosis (Section 2.1):** The member has a confirmed primary diagnosis of Cervical Myelopathy (2.1(b)), meeting this criterion.
2.  **Conservative Treatment (Section 2.2):**
    *   The denial stated that Section 2.2 (standard pathway, 6 weeks PT) was not met.
    *   However, Plan Criteria Section 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY — states: "For members with confirmed cervical myelopathy (criterion 2.1(b)), the conservative treatment requirement is MODIFIED as follows: Physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks minimum. Interventional treatment requirement (2.2(c)) is WAIVED if treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses risk of neurological deterioration. Pharmacotherapy requirement (2.2(b)) remains in full."
    *   **Application of Myelopathy Pathway:**
        *   **Physical Therapy:** Member completed 5 weeks, exceeding the 4-week minimum requirement for myelopathy. This criterion is met.
        *   **Interventional Treatment:** Waived as treating physician documented neurological urgency. This criterion is met.
        *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Requires a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. Baclofen was used for 3 months (meets). Methylprednisolone was used for 3 weeks (does not meet the 4-week minimum). Therefore, under a strict reading of the plan's *myelopathy exception*, the pharmacotherapy component was not fully met.

**C. Review of California Regulatory Requirements:**

1.  **CHSC § 1374.31(b) - Procedural Adequacy of Denial:** The denial notice was procedurally adequate, specifying the criteria cited (Section 2.2) and the reason for denial. The issue lies in the *incorrect application* of the criteria, not the lack of criteria.
2.  **CIC § 10169.5 - Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This state law explicitly prohibits conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where:
    *   **(a)(1) Physician documents PT contraindicated or unlikely to benefit:** The treating physician halted PT after 5 weeks, noting "No improvement in UMN signs" and given the myelopathy diagnosis, indicating PT was unlikely to provide further benefit for the myelopathic symptoms. This condition is met.
    *   **(a)(2) Member completed PT and reached functional plateau:** The member completed 5 weeks of PT with "No improvement in UMN signs." The treating physician's decision to halt therapy due to lack of improvement constitutes reaching a functional plateau, particularly for neurological symptoms of myelopathy which are not expected to resolve with PT. This condition is met.
    *   **(a)(3) Objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address:** The member's MRI demonstrates "C5-C6 moderate central cord compression," which is objective structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot resolve. This condition is met.
    *   **Conclusion:** Under California Insurance Code § 10169.5, the plan is prohibited from conditioning approval on further physical therapy.
3.  **DMHC APL 22-014 - Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions:**
    *   Section II.1 mandates that plans SHALL treat cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression as urgent. The member's condition meets this classification.
    *   Section II.2 states plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when: (a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia, or progressive herniation on serial imaging), (b) Treating physician documents conservative treatment failure, AND (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines.
        *   While the MRI did not show myelomalacia (thus not meeting this specific waiver trigger in (a)), the treating physician documented conservative failure (b), and surgical intervention is indicated under clinical guidelines (c, reviewed below).
        *   The APL reinforces the principle that plans should not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards for such conditions.
4.  **CHSC § 1374.32(b) - Standard of Review for IMR:** This provision mandates that if a service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, it SHALL be approved regardless of the plan's more restrictive internal coverage criteria.

**D. Review of Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

1.  **Diagnosis and Treatment Distinction:** Clinical guidelines emphasize that myelopathy and radiculopathy are fundamentally different diseases requiring different treatment algorithms. Applying radiculopathy-derived conservative treatment requirements to myelopathy is not supported by evidence (Source 3, Section 1.1).
2.  **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy:**
    *   For moderate-severe myelopathy, physical therapy "does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement" and is "NOT recommended as primary treatment for severe myelopathy" (Source 3, Section 2.1).
    *   AO Spine Guidelines (Source 3, Section 2.1) state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients... may result in neurological deterioration."
    *   There is no evidence supporting a defined minimum duration of conservative treatment before surgery for myelopathy (Source 3, Section 2.2).
3.  **Surgical Indications (AANS/CNS 2023):** The member meets **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION (surgery recommended)** criteria:
    *   (a) Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI (confirmed by diagnosis, UMN signs, and MRI findings).
    *   (b) Progressive neurological deficit despite conservative treatment (confirmed by no improvement in UMN signs despite 5 weeks of PT and minimal response to pharmacotherapy).
    *   While myelomalacia (c) is not present, its absence does not negate the other Tier 1 indicators.
4.  **Conclusion:** Clinical guidelines strongly support surgical intervention for the member's condition and explicitly state that mandatory PT duration for moderate-severe myelopathy is not evidence-based and may be harmful.

---

**IV. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND DISPOSITION:**

The HealthFirst denial was based on a misapplication of its own criteria, failing to apply the myelopathy exception (Section 2.2A). Even if the myelopathy exception were applied, the plan's pharmacotherapy requirement was not fully met. However, applying the established hierarchy:

1.  **State Law (CIC § 10169.5) overrides Plan Criteria:** California law explicitly prohibits requiring further physical therapy given the member's objective structural neurological compromise (cord compression), lack of benefit from PT (no improvement in UMN signs), and functional plateau. This renders the plan's PT duration requirement, whether 6 weeks or 4 weeks, inapplicable.
2.  **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS, AO Spine) overrides Plan Criteria:** Generally accepted clinical standards for moderate to severe cervical myelopathy with cord compression strongly recommend surgical decompression and explicitly state that mandatory or prolonged physical therapy is not evidence-based and may cause neurological deterioration. This directly conflicts with and overrides the plan's conservative treatment duration requirements (PT and implicitly, any conservative treatment that delays indicated surgery).
3.  **CHSC § 1374.32(b) reinforces this:** The service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, and therefore, under California law, must be approved irrespective of the plan's more restrictive internal criteria.

Although the plan's specific pharmacotherapy duration requirement was not fully met, the overarching state law and clinical evidence against delaying surgery for myelopathy with cord compression, combined with the documentation of conservative failure regarding the myelopathic symptoms, indicate that enforcing this specific duration would be contrary to state law and accepted medical practice. The primary reason for denial was PT duration, which is demonstrably overridden.

Therefore, the initial denial was incorrect both in its application of the plan's criteria and, more importantly, because the criteria themselves are superseded by California state law and generally accepted clinical standards for this condition.

---

**V. DETERMINATION:**

The denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) for Ms. Priya Nair is **OVERTURNED**.

---

**VI. WARRANT:**

The denial is OVERTURNED because HealthFirst Health Plan incorrectly applied its own clinical criteria (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, standard pathway) by failing to apply the myelopathy exception (Section 2.2A) to a member with a confirmed primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy and objective cord compression. Under the correct myelopathy exception, the member completed 5 weeks of physical therapy, exceeding the 4-week minimum.

More significantly, California state law (CIC § 10169.5) prohibits a health plan from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy where, as in this case, there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (cord compression) that PT cannot address, where the treating physician documents PT is unlikely to benefit, and where a functional plateau has been reached.

Furthermore, generally accepted standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021) classify moderate to severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression as a TIER 1 strong indication for surgical intervention. These clinical guidelines explicitly state that there is no high-quality evidence supporting mandatory physical therapy prior to surgery for such patients, and that delaying surgery may result in neurological deterioration. Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), services meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice SHALL be approved regardless of a plan's more restrictive internal criteria.

Therefore, the service is medically necessary and should be approved.
