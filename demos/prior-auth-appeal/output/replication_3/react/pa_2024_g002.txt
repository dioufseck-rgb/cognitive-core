**1. BACKGROUND & DENIAL REASON:**

The member, Priya Nair, age 56, was denied prior authorization for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6. The initial denial, dated 2024-10-25, cited HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) Section 2.2, stating the member had completed 5 weeks of physical therapy (PT), which did not meet the standard pathway's minimum 6-week requirement.

The treating physician, Dr. James Chen, MD (Neurosurgery), appealed this denial, asserting that the myelopathy pathway (Section 2.2A) of the plan's criteria should apply, which requires only 4 weeks of PT. The appeal also confirmed a primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy with bilateral upper motor neuron (UMN) signs and cord compression on MRI, and that under the correct pathway, all criteria were met.

---

**2. CLINICAL RECORD SUMMARY:**

*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Confirmed Cervical Myelopathy C5-C6.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-01):** Moderate central cord compression at C5-C6. No T2 signal change or myelomalacia noted. Bilateral foraminal stenosis.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-09-05):** Bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia in upper extremities, mildly impaired tandem gait, bilateral hand clumsiness. These findings confirm upper motor neuron involvement consistent with myelopathy.
*   **Diagnosis Confirmation:** Neurologist consultation (2024-09-12) confirmed cervical myelopathy as the primary diagnosis, noting bilateral UMN signs are pathognomonic.
*   **Conservative Treatment Summary:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 5 weeks (15 sessions) completed under a myelopathy protocol. Treating physician halted PT due to no improvement in UMN signs and the confirmed myelopathy diagnosis.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Baclofen 10mg TID for 3 months (minimal spasticity relief); Methylprednisolone dose pack for 3 weeks (temporary improvement only).
    *   **Interventional:** Waived by Dr. Chen, who documented that interventional procedures were not indicated due to cord compression with myelopathy requiring surgical evaluation.

**3. APPLICATION OF PLAN CRITERIA (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**

Upon review of the clinical record against CC-SPINE-2024:

*   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:**
    *   The member has a confirmed primary diagnosis of Cervical Myelopathy C5-C6, meeting Section 2.1(b), supported by MRI showing cord compression and neurological exam findings of UMN signs. **(MET)**
*   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
    *   The initial denial incorrectly applied the standard pathway (Section 2.2) requiring 6 weeks of PT.
    *   The member's confirmed primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy triggers **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY**.
        *   **Physical Therapy:** The myelopathy pathway reduces the PT requirement to a **minimum of 4 weeks**. The member completed 5 weeks of PT. **(MET)**

The member received Baclofen for 3 months (meets requirement) and Methylprednisolone for 3 weeks (does not meet the 4-week minimum requirement for the second agent). **(NOT MET by plan criteria's strict wording)**
*   **2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:**
    *   Current MRI demonstrates structural pathology (moderate central cord compression C5-C6) consistent with symptoms and proposed surgical level. **(MET)**
*   **2.4 PHYSICIAN DOCUMENTATION:**
    *   Documentation provided includes attestation of conservative treatment completion and failure (PT halted, minimal drug efficacy), rationale for surgery, functional limitations, and risks/alternatives. **(MET)**

**Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** While the primary denial reason (PT duration under standard pathway) was incorrect, a strict application of the myelopathy pathway's pharmacotherapy criteria reveals that only one of the two required agents (Baclofen) met the 4-week minimum duration. This means the member did not strictly satisfy the pharmacotherapy requirement under HealthFirst's own criteria.

**4. APPLICATION OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK:**

The review must consider California state laws and regulations, which supersede plan criteria when more permissive or protective of the member.

*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):**
    *   This law explicitly prohibits conditioning surgical approval on PT completion where:
        *   **(a)(1) Physician documents PT unlikely to benefit:** Dr. Chen halted PT at 5 weeks due to "no improvement in UMN signs" and the myelopathy diagnosis. This demonstrates PT was deemed unlikely to benefit. **(Applies)**
        *   **(a)(3) Objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address:** Cervical myelopathy with moderate central cord compression constitutes objective structural neurological compromise that PT cannot resolve. **(Applies)**
    *   The law further states that a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy "shall not be applied mechanically." The plan's denial based solely on PT duration (even if corrected to 4 weeks) is a mechanical application directly prohibited by this statute.
    *   **Conclusion:** Under state law, the plan's PT duration requirement cannot be used to deny authorization for this member.

*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):**
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** The DMHC classifies cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression as a condition with potential for irreversible harm, requiring plans to treat such cases as urgent. Priya has confirmed myelopathy with moderate central cord compression.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** This APL states plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when:
        *   (a) Objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (cord compression). **(Applies)**
        *   (b) Treating physician documents conservative treatment has been attempted and failed. Dr. Chen documented lack of improvement in UMN signs with PT, and pharmacotherapy provided only minimal/temporary relief. **(Applies)**
        *   (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines. As detailed below, it is. **(Applies)**
    *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** Plans "shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice."
    *   **Conclusion:** This APL strongly indicates that overly strict conservative treatment requirements, including minimum durations for pharmacotherapy or PT, cannot be applied to delay surgery for myelopathy when conservative treatment has failed and surgery is clinically indicated.

*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard of Review):**
    *   This statute mandates that if a service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice," it "SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." This provision allows clinical standards to override plan criteria. **(Applies)**

**5. APPLICATION OF CLINICAL STANDARDS (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

*   **Diagnosis:** Priya's presentation (confirmed cervical myelopathy, objective cord compression, bilateral UMN signs: Hoffman, hyperreflexia, gait disturbance, hand clumsiness) clearly distinguishes her condition as myelopathy, which requires a different treatment algorithm than radiculopathy.
*   **Surgical Indication:** Per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), Priya's condition meets **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION** for surgery:
    *   Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI.
    *   Progressive neurological deficit despite conservative treatment (no improvement in UMN signs, minimal relief from pharmacotherapy).
*   **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy:**
    *   AO Spine Guidelines (2021) state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
    *   NASS Guidelines (2020) recommend surgical decompression as the primary treatment for moderate to severe myelopathy.
    *   These guidelines do not establish specific minimum durations for pharmacotherapy trials for myelopathy but emphasize the limited efficacy of conservative treatment and the risks of delaying surgery.
*   **Conclusion:** Generally accepted clinical standards strongly advocate for surgical intervention in cases of confirmed moderate to severe cervical myelopathy with cord compression and UMN signs where conservative treatment has failed. These standards explicitly caution against delaying surgery to complete arbitrary durations of conservative therapy, as this may lead to irreversible neurological deterioration.

**6. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND DISPOSITION:**

The hierarchy of authority is **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.**

1.  **Initial Denial Error:** The initial denial was procedurally flawed by misapplying the plan's own criteria, specifically by referencing the standard pathway (6 weeks PT) instead of the myelopathy exception pathway (4 weeks PT). The member completed 5 weeks of PT, thus exceeding the applicable 4-week requirement.
2.  **Pharmacotherapy Discrepancy:** While a strict interpretation of the plan's myelopathy pathway for pharmacotherapy (2 agents for 4+ weeks each) reveals that one agent (Methylprednisolone) was only given for 3 weeks, this was not the basis of the original denial.
3.  **State Law Override:**
    *   **CIC § 10169.5** explicitly overrides any plan requirement for PT completion in this case due to the documented neurological compromise (myelopathy with cord compression) and the treating physician's assessment that PT was unlikely to benefit/had reached its plateau regarding UMN signs.
    *   **DMHC APL 22-014** mandates that for myelopathy with objective cord compression, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when conservative failure is documented and surgery is clinically indicated. This applies to the entire conservative treatment requirement, including pharmacotherapy. The APL and clinical guidelines highlight the urgency of myelopathy and the risks of delayed surgery.
    *   **CHSC § 1374.32(b)** empowers independent medical reviewers to approve services that meet generally accepted standards of medical practice, even if plan criteria are more restrictive.
4.  **Clinical Standard Override:** The clinical standards from AANS/CNS, NASS, and AO Spine unequivocally categorize the member's condition as a strong indication for surgery and strongly advise against delaying surgery for extended conservative treatment trials in moderate-severe myelopathy, directly contradicting the mechanical application of minimum conservative treatment durations.

**Disposition:** **OVERTURN**

**Warrant:**
The denial must be OVERTURNED for the following reasons:

1.  **Erroneous Application of Plan Criteria:** The initial denial was based on an incorrect application of HealthFirst's own CC-SPINE-2024 policy. The Myelopathy Pathway (Section 2.2A), which applies to the member's primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, requires a minimum of 4 weeks of physical therapy, not 6. The member completed 5 weeks of physical therapy, thereby meeting this specific criterion.
2.  **State Law Mandate (CIC § 10169.5):** California Insurance Code § 10169.5 prohibits conditioning surgical approval on completion of physical therapy where there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address (which applies to myelopathy with cord compression) or where the treating physician documents PT is unlikely to benefit. Both conditions are met in this case. The plan's mechanical application of a minimum PT duration is therefore unlawful.
3.  **State Law Mandate (DMHC APL 22-014):** California Department of Managed Health Care All-Plan Letter 22-014 explicitly directs plans not to require completion of a full course of conservative treatment for cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression when the treating physician documents conservative treatment failure and surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines. This applies to the member's condition and documented treatment course.
4.  **Overriding Clinical Standards (CHSC § 1374.32(b)):** Leading clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) classify the member's condition (confirmed moderate-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and UMN signs) as a Tier 1 strong indication for surgical intervention. These guidelines explicitly state there is no high-quality evidence supporting mandatory physical therapy duration prior to surgery for myelopathy and warn that delaying surgery may result in neurological deterioration. These generally accepted standards of medical practice override any more restrictive plan criteria, including the technical non-compliance with the pharmacotherapy duration, as the overall conservative treatment has clearly failed to improve the myelopathy and further delay is not clinically indicated.

Therefore, despite the technical non-compliance with the pharmacotherapy duration under plan criteria, the higher authority of California state law and generally accepted clinical standards dictate that surgical intervention is medically necessary and should be approved without further delay.

---
**FINAL DECISION:** The prior authorization denial for ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) for Priya Nair (M-G002) is **OVERTURNED**. Authorization is approved.
