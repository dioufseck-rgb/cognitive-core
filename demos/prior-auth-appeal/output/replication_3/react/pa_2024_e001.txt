**I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

This appeal concerns the denial of a two-level anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 and C6-C7. Upon review of the clinical record, plan criteria, California state law, and generally accepted clinical standards, it is determined that the C5-C6 level meets the criteria for surgical intervention due to documented cervical radiculopathy and objective neurological deficit that has failed conservative treatment. However, the C6-C7 level does not demonstrate sufficient pathology, objective neurological deficit, or failure of conservative treatment to warrant surgical intervention based on either HealthFirst's plan criteria or generally accepted clinical standards.

Therefore, the request for a **two-level ACDF (C5-C6 and C6-C7)** as submitted is **UPHELD** in its denial, as the C6-C7 level does not meet medical necessity criteria. However, a **single-level ACDF at C5-C6 is medically necessary and should be approved.**

---

**II. REASONING FOR DISPOSITION**

This determination applies the three-source hierarchy: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.

**A. Clinical Presentation Analysis:**

1.  **C5-C6 Level:**
    *   **Imaging:** MRI dated 2024-09-15 shows "large disc herniation, moderate-severe right C6 nerve root compression, foraminal stenosis."
    *   **Neurological Exam:** Dated 2024-09-20 reveals "right bicep weakness 3+/5, bicep reflex absent right, right C6 dermatomal numbness and tingling." These findings are consistent with **cervical radiculopathy**.
    *   **Conservative Treatment:**
        *   8 weeks of physical therapy completed, with C6 symptoms persisting.
        *   4 months of Pregabalin and 3 months of Naproxen used, providing partial C6 relief.
        *   A right C6 epidural was performed, providing good C6 relief (70%) that fully returned in 4 weeks, indicating temporary effect and failure of interventional therapy.
    *   **Conclusion for C5-C6:** The member demonstrates clear objective evidence of cervical radiculopathy with significant neurological deficit (motor weakness) that has failed an adequate course of conservative treatment.

2.  **C6-C7 Level:**
    *   **Imaging:** MRI dated 2024-09-15 shows "small disc bulge, mild foraminal narrowing, no nerve root compression identifiable, no cord contact."
    *   **Neurological Exam:** Dated 2024-09-20 notes "right tricep reflex slightly diminished. No definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms. Physician notes mild C7 symptoms possibly present but not clearly documented."
    *   **Conservative Treatment:**
        *   8 weeks of physical therapy completed. PT note at week 8 indicates "C7 symptoms: minimal and possibly resolving."
        *   Pharmacotherapy (Pregabalin, Naproxen) was used, but C7-specific contribution to relief is uncertain.
        *   No C7-specific interventional procedure was performed.
    *   **Conclusion for C6-C7:** The evidence for pathology at C6-C7 is minimal, with no identifiable nerve root compression on imaging and no definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms. The mild, possibly resolving symptoms, coupled with the lack of a C7-specific interventional treatment trial, indicate that surgical criteria are not met for this level. There is no evidence of myelopathy (spinal cord compression or myelomalacia) at either level.

**B. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

1.  **C5-C6 Level:**
    *   **2.1 Diagnosis:** Meets 2.1(a) (cervical radiculopathy) and 2.1(c) (cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit) based on MRI findings and right C6 weakness. **(Pass)**
    *   **2.2 Conservative Treatment:**
        *   2.2(a) Physical therapy: 8 weeks completed, exceeding the 6-week minimum, with C6 symptoms persisting. **(Pass)**
        *   2.2(b) Pharmacotherapy: Pregabalin (4 months) and Naproxen (3 months) meet the 4-week minimum for two agents. **(Pass)**
        *   2.2(c) Interventional: A right C6 epidural was performed. **(Pass)**
    *   **2.3 Imaging:** MRI consistent with symptoms. **(Pass)**
    *   **Conclusion for C5-C6:** This level meets all of HealthFirst's medical necessity criteria for surgical authorization.

2.  **C6-C7 Level:**
    *   **2.1 Diagnosis:** Does not meet 2.1(a) or 2.1(c). Imaging shows no identifiable nerve root compression, and the neurological exam lacks definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms or objective neurological deficit. A "slightly diminished tricep reflex" is insufficient to meet the definition of radiculopathy or objective neurological deficit for surgical criteria without corroborating imaging findings of compression. **(Fail)**
    *   **2.2 Conservative Treatment:**
        *   2.2(c) Interventional: No C7-specific interventional treatment was performed. The C6 epidural was not directed at C7 pathology. **(Fail)**
    *   **2.3 Imaging:** Imaging findings (small disc bulge, mild foraminal narrowing, no nerve root compression identifiable) are not consistent with a surgical indication given the lack of correlating definitive symptoms. **(Fail)**
    *   **Conclusion for C6-C7:** This level fails to meet multiple HealthFirst medical necessity criteria.

**C. Application of California Regulatory Framework:**

1.  **Procedural Adequacy (CHSC § 1374.31(b)):** The initial denial notice specified the clinical reason for the denial (C6-C7 not meeting criteria) and implicitly cited relevant criteria by referring to "pathology consistent with surgical criteria" for C5-C6 and "mild changes only" for C6-C7. This is procedurally adequate.
2.  **Myelopathy/Urgent Conditions (DMHC APL 22-014):** This APL is not applicable as the member's presentation does not involve cervical myelopathy, nor is there objective evidence of cord compression or myelomalacia.
3.  **Physical Therapy as Prerequisite (CIC § 10169.5):** For the C5-C6 level, the 8 weeks of PT with persistent symptoms and objective motor weakness could qualify under 10169.5(a)(2) (functional plateau) or (a)(3) (structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address), supporting that further PT is not required. For the C6-C7 level, symptoms were "minimal and possibly resolving," which does not indicate a functional plateau of *persistent symptoms* warranting surgery, nor objective structural compromise.

**D. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

1.  **C5-C6 Level:** The member's persistent radiculopathy with objective motor weakness (3+/5) after an adequate trial of conservative treatment meets the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) TIER 2 criteria for ACDF (specifically 2(b) "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and 2(c) "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care"). The clinical standards support surgical intervention for C5-C6.
2.  **C6-C7 Level:** This level does not meet any TIER 1 or TIER 2 surgical indications in the AANS/CNS guidelines. The imaging findings of a "small disc bulge, mild foraminal narrowing" without identifiable nerve root compression, combined with "no definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms" and minimal, possibly resolving C7 symptoms, fall under TIER 3 ("Imaging findings without correlating symptoms"). Furthermore, the lack of a C7-specific interventional procedure means a full conservative trial for this specific level's minimal symptoms has not been completed per generally accepted standards where symptoms are not progressive or severe.
3.  **Adjacent Level Surgery (Appeal Basis):** The argument that "Adjacent levels should be addressed simultaneously to prevent accelerated degeneration" is not supported by current clinical guidelines for fusing a non-pathological or minimally symptomatic level lacking independent surgical indications. While adjacent segment disease is a recognized phenomenon, prophylactic fusion of an otherwise non-surgical segment is not a generally accepted standard of medical practice.

**E. Conflict Resolution & Overall Conclusion:**

*   The initial denial's assessment that "C5-C6 level shows pathology consistent with surgical criteria" is affirmed by this review, as C5-C6 meets both plan criteria and clinical standards.
*   The initial denial's assessment that "C6-C7 shows mild changes only" and thus does not meet criteria for surgical intervention is also affirmed. C6-C7 fails to meet HealthFirst's criteria (Section 2.1 Diagnosis, 2.2(c) Interventional, 2.3 Imaging) and also fails to meet generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS TIER 1 or TIER 2 indications).
*   Since the requested procedure is a *two-level fusion* (ACDF C5-C6 AND C6-C7), and one of those levels (C6-C7) does not meet medical necessity criteria, the requested *two-level procedure as a whole* cannot be approved.
*   California law (CHSC § 1374.32(b)) mandates that services meeting generally accepted standards shall be approved regardless of more restrictive plan criteria. In this instance, both plan criteria and clinical standards align regarding the lack of medical necessity for the C6-C7 level. No state law or clinical standard overrides this finding to mandate approval of the C6-C7 level.

---

**III. DISPOSITION**

The denial of the **two-level ACDF (C5-C6 and C6-C7)** is **UPHELD**.

However, HealthFirst Health Plan is directed to **APPROVE** a **single-level ACDF at C5-C6** for Victor Patel.

**Warrant:**
The member's clinical presentation at the C5-C6 level, including objective right C6 radiculopathy, significant motor weakness (3+/5), and documented failure of an adequate course of conservative treatment (8 weeks PT, 4 months pharmacotherapy, single C6 epidural with temporary relief), meets both HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Sections 2.1(a)(c), 2.2, 2.3) and generally accepted clinical standards for ACDF (AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines TIER 2(b)(c)). Furthermore, California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(3) supports that the existing structural neurological compromise at C5-C6 renders further physical therapy unlikely to address the condition, making surgical intervention appropriate.

Conversely, the C6-C7 level does not demonstrate medical necessity for surgical intervention. Imaging shows only a small disc bulge and mild foraminal narrowing without identifiable nerve root compression, and the neurological exam lacks definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms, showing only a "slightly diminished tricep reflex." The C7 symptoms were noted as "minimal and possibly resolving" during physical therapy, and no C7-specific interventional treatment was performed. These findings fail to meet HealthFirst's criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Sections 2.1, 2.2(c), 2.3) and do not meet generally accepted clinical standards for ACDF (AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines TIER 1 or TIER 2). The appeal basis regarding adjacent level degeneration does not override the fundamental lack of independent surgical indication for the C6-C7 level based on current clinical evidence. Therefore, the denial of the two-level procedure is appropriate, but the medically necessary single-level ACDF at C5-C6 should be approved.
