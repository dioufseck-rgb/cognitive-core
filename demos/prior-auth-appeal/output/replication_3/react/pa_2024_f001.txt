**Warrant:**

The initial prior authorization request for an ACDF C6-C7 (CPT 22551) for member Dorothy James was denied on 2024-11-02 by Dr. Alan Foster, MD (MED-003) due to "Physical therapy requirement not met. Plan received PT records from Valley Physical Therapy documenting 4 weeks of treatment. Member claims 8 total weeks but second provider (Mountain PT) records not received by plan. Cannot verify claimed treatment without complete records."

Upon review of the submitted appeal, including the previously unreceived records from Mountain Physical Therapy, the following determination is made:

1.  **Resolution of Initial Denial Reason (Plan Criteria):** The primary reason for the initial denial was administrative, citing incomplete records regarding physical therapy. The appeal directly addresses this by submitting the records from Mountain Physical Therapy, which document an additional 4 weeks of treatment. Therefore, the member has completed a total of 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy across two providers (Valley Physical Therapy: 4 weeks; Mountain Physical Therapy: 4 weeks). This duration (8 weeks) exceeds HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) Section 2.2(a) which requires a "minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy." Thus, the plan's stated duration requirement is now met.

2.  **Compliance with State Law – CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Requirement):**
    *   **Functional Plateau:** The clinical record clearly documents that the member reached a functional plateau during physical therapy. Valley Physical Therapy noted "Moderate improvement then plateau," and Mountain Physical Therapy documented "No improvement in neurological symptoms. Plateau documented by PT at discharge." California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2) explicitly states that a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." This provision overrides any plan requirement for further physical therapy.
    *   **Structural Neurological Compromise:** The clinical record demonstrates objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. The MRI reveals a "C6-C7 large disc herniation, bilateral C7 nerve root compression, bilateral foraminal stenosis, mild cord contact without compression." The neurological exam confirms "Bilateral C7 dermatomal symptoms: bilateral tricep weakness 4/5, bilateral tricep reflex diminished, bilateral finger tingling." Physical therapy at Mountain PT specifically noted "No improvement in neurological symptoms." CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) dictates that a plan cannot require PT completion when "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." This condition is met.

3.  **Adherence to Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine):**
    *   **Myelopathy/Myeloradiculopathy Features:** While the treating diagnosis is "Cervical radiculopathy C6-C7, bilateral," the presence of bilateral C7 dermatomal symptoms, bilateral triceps weakness (4/5), diminished bilateral tricep reflexes, and "mild cord contact" on MRI points towards a more complex condition often termed myeloradiculopathy, or myelopathy with associated radiculopathy. The AANS/CNS and AO Spine guidelines emphasize that myelopathy and radiculopathy require different treatment algorithms and that applying radiculopathy-derived conservative treatment requirements to myelopathy patients is a "category error."
    *   **Efficacy of PT for Neurological Symptoms:** Clinical guidelines, including the AO Spine Guidelines (2021) and Cochrane Review (2021), state that physical therapy has limited efficacy for moderate-severe myelopathy and specifically does "NOT produce meaningful functional improvement" for neurological symptoms. They also highlight that "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients... may result in neurological deterioration." The member's documented lack of improvement in neurological symptoms during PT is consistent with this understanding.
    *   **Surgical Indication:** Per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), surgical indications are met under Tier 1(b) ("Progressive neurological deficit despite conservative treatment" – patient's bilateral weakness and diminished reflexes persist despite 8 weeks PT) and Tier 2(b) & (c) ("Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care").

**Conclusion:**

The initial denial was based on a documentation deficiency that has been rectified. With the complete records, the member has not only met HealthFirst's 6-week minimum physical therapy requirement (having completed 8 weeks) but has also demonstrated a clear functional plateau, particularly regarding neurological symptoms. Crucially, California State Law (CIC § 10169.5) explicitly prohibits plans from denying surgery based on unmet PT requirements when a functional plateau has been reached or when structural neurological compromise exists that PT cannot address. Furthermore, generally accepted clinical standards support surgical intervention for patients with persistent, functionally impairing bilateral neurological deficits that have not responded to an adequate course of conservative treatment.

Applying the hierarchy of authority (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria), both state law and clinical standards dictate that the requested ACDF is medically necessary and should be approved.

**The prior authorization denial is hereby OVERTURNED.**

The requested procedure (ACDF C6-C7, CPT 22551) is approved for Dorothy James.
