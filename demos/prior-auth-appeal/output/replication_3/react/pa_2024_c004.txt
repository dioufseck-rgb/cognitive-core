**I. BACKGROUND**
The member, Mr. Raymond Flores, is a 54-year-old male with a diagnosis of severe cervical stenosis at C5-C6 with cord contact, presenting with axial neck pain only. He has a normal neurological examination with no signs of radiculopathy or myelopathy. His occupation as a construction worker involving heavy equipment operation places him at a higher risk of cervical trauma. Dr. Davis requested prophylactic Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 to prevent acute spinal cord injury due citing the critically narrow canal diameter.

The initial prior authorization request was denied by HealthFirst (Dr. Patricia Huang, MD) on 2024-10-25, citing "Cervical stenosis documented but no neurological deficit on examination. Member has axial neck pain only. No radiculopathy, no myelopathy documented. Surgical intervention not indicated for axial pain without neurological deficit under CC-SPINE-2024."

The appeal basis is that the member has severe cervical stenosis with cord contact, carrying a risk of myelopathy from minor trauma, and prophylactic surgery is indicated for severe stenosis even without current deficits, especially given his high-trauma-risk occupation.

**II. CLINICAL RECORD SUMMARY**
*   **Diagnosis:** Severe cervical stenosis C5-C6 with cord contact; prophylactic decompression requested.
*   **Symptoms:** Axial neck pain only (5/10), no radicular or myelopathic symptoms.
*   **Neurological Exam:** Completely normal. No radiculopathy, myelopathy signs, or UMN signs.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-15):** C5-C6 severe central canal stenosis. Cord contact without compression. No T2 signal change (myelomalacia). Canal diameter 6mm (normal >13mm).
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   Physical Therapy: 8 weeks, 24 sessions for axial pain, with modest improvement (7/10 to 5/10). No neurological symptoms to treat.
    *   Pharmacotherapy: Naproxen (3 months, moderate pain relief), Cyclobenzaprine (6 weeks, sleep improvement).
    *   Interventional: C5-C6 epidural (60% pain relief for 6 weeks).
*   **Surgeon's Rationale:** Critically narrow canal diameter, high risk of acute spinal cord injury from minor trauma due to occupation (construction worker). Prophylactic decompression indicated to prevent catastrophic injury.

**III. DENIAL REVIEW CHECKLIST AND ANALYSIS**

**1. Procedural Adequacy of Denial:**
    *   The denial notice cited specific criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) and provided a specific clinical reason for denial ("no neurological deficit on examination. Member has axial neck pain only. No radiculopathy, no myelopathy documented").
    *   **Conclusion:** The denial notice is procedurally adequate per CHSC § 1374.31(b).

**2. Application of HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 (Plan Criteria):**
    *   **Section 2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** This section requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (with symptoms), cervical myelopathy (with symptoms), or cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit. The member's clinical record explicitly states "Completely normal neurological examination. No radiculopathy. No myelopathy signs. No UMN signs. Axial neck pain only." Therefore, the member does not meet any of the specified diagnostic criteria for surgical approval under Section 2.1.
    *   **Section 2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:** The member completed 8 weeks of PT, tried two pharmacotherapy agents, and had one interventional procedure. These requirements for conservative treatment for axial pain were met.
    *   **Section 3.1 Exclusions:** This section states that authorization will be denied when "Surgery is requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy." This exclusion directly applies to the member's presentation, as his primary symptom is axial neck pain, and no radiculopathy or myelopathy is documented clinically.
    *   **Conclusion:** The plan applied its criteria correctly, specifically Section 2.1 and Exclusion 3.1.

**3. Application of Myelopathy Exception (Plan Criteria):**
    *   The plan's "EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY" explicitly states it "applies only when myelopathy is the PRIMARY diagnosis." As noted, the member has no clinical signs or symptoms of myelopathy, and the neurological exam is normal. The denial explicitly states "no myelopathy documented."
    *   **Conclusion:** The plan correctly determined that the myelopathy exception does not apply in this case.

**4. Application of California Regulatory Requirements:**
    *   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute specifies conditions for waiving PT requirements. While the member has "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address" (severe stenosis with cord contact), conservative treatment for the presenting axial pain *was* completed. This statute pertains to waiving PT, not compelling approval of a surgery that does not meet general indications.
    *   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This guidance applies to myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI. Mr. Flores does not have myelopathy (no symptoms) and his MRI shows "No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia." Thus, the specific urgency provisions related to myelopathy/myelomalacia in this APL do not apply to this case.
    *   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard is NOT Plan Criteria):** This is the paramount state law, requiring services to be approved if they meet "generally accepted standards of medical practice" regardless of more restrictive plan criteria. This necessitates a review against clinical standards.

**5. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
    *   **Section 1.1 (Diagnostic Classification):** Confirms that myelopathy requires spinal cord compression *with* upper motor neuron signs and symptoms, and radiculopathy requires nerve root compression with dermatomal symptoms. Mr. Flores does not meet these definitions.
    *   **Section 1.2 (Myelomalacia):** Mr. Flores' MRI explicitly states "No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia." This is a significant finding, as myelomalacia is a strong indicator for surgical urgency in myelopathy.
    *   **Section 2 (Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy):** This section is not directly applicable as Mr. Flores does not have myelopathy, and conservative treatment for his axial pain was completed.
    *   **Section 3.1 (Accepted Indications for ACDF - AANS/CNS 2023):**
        *   **TIER 1 (Strong Indication):** The member does not meet any Tier 1 criteria (no myelopathy, no progressive deficit, no myelomalacia, no acute severe myelopathy).
        *   **TIER 2 (Reasonable Indication):** The member does not meet any Tier 2 criteria (no mild myelopathy, no radiculopathy, no significant functional impairment *related to neurological deficits*).
        *   **TIER 3 (Surgery Not Indicated):** This section states surgery is "not indicated" for "(a) Axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy" and "(b) Imaging findings without correlating symptoms." Mr. Flores' presentation (axial neck pain only, normal neurological exam, severe stenosis with cord contact without compression or myelomalacia) aligns with these Tier 3 contraindications.
    *   **Prophylactic Surgery:** The clinical guidelines provided do not explicitly define "prophylactic surgery for severe stenosis with cord contact in a high-risk occupation, *without* neurological deficits or myelomalacia" as a generally accepted surgical indication (Tier 1 or Tier 2). While the surgeon's rationale for preventing catastrophic injury is noted, the established guidelines consistently link surgical indications to *current* neurological compromise or objective cord damage (e.g., myelomalacia).

**6. Conflict Resolution (Hierarchy: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria):**
Both the HealthFirst plan criteria (Sections 2.1 & 3.1) and the generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines, Tier 3 exclusions) align. Neither supports surgical intervention for severe cervical stenosis with cord contact in the absence of current neurological deficits (myelopathy or radiculopathy) or myelomalacia. The state law provisions related to waiving conservative treatment (CIC 10169.5) and myelopathy urgency (DMHC APL 22-014) do not override the fundamental lack of a surgical indication based on the clinical standards for this specific presentation.

**IV. DISPOSITION**
**UPHELD**

**V. WARRANT**
The denial for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level) for Mr. Raymond Flores is **UPHELD**.

The member's clinical presentation, characterized by severe cervical stenosis with cord contact and axial neck pain but a completely normal neurological examination (no radiculopathy, no myelopathy signs, no upper motor neuron signs, and no myelomalacia on MRI), does not meet the established criteria for surgical intervention.

1.  **Plan Criteria Compliance:** The HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) Section 2.1 requires a diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy, myelopathy (both with correlating symptoms/deficits), or disc herniation with objective neurological deficit. The member does not exhibit these. Furthermore, Section 3.1 of the plan's exclusions specifically denies authorization when "Surgery is requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy." The member's case falls squarely within this exclusion.
2.  **Clinical Standard Alignment:** The generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, as outlined in the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines for Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update), reinforce this decision. Section 3.1 categorizes surgery as "NOT INDICATED" (Tier 3) for "(a) Axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy" and "(b) Imaging findings without correlating symptoms." Mr. Flores presents with axial neck pain and imaging findings (severe stenosis, cord contact) but critically lacks correlating neurological symptoms or objective deficits. The absence of myelomalacia also places him outside the "Strong Indication" (Tier 1) categories.
3.  **Prophylactic Argument:** While the surgeon's rationale for prophylactic surgery to prevent catastrophic injury due to high-risk occupation and severe stenosis is acknowledged, the provided clinical guidelines do not establish "prophylaxis without current neurological deficit or myelomalacia" as a standard surgical indication. The guidelines focus on treating existing neurological compromise or objective cord damage.
4.  **State Law Applicability:** Relevant California regulatory requirements (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) address situations for waiving conservative treatment requirements or identifying urgent myelopathy cases. However, these do not compel authorization for a surgery that does not meet the fundamental diagnostic criteria or surgical indications under generally accepted clinical standards, which, in this case, align with the plan's denial. The conservative treatment requirements for axial pain were met, but the underlying issue is the lack of a neurological deficit for which surgery is indicated.

Based on the hierarchy of authority (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), the clinical standards and plan criteria are consistent in this instance. The member's condition, as documented, does not currently warrant surgical intervention under generally accepted standards of medical practice.
