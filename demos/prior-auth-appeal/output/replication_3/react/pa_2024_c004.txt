**Appeal Determination: PA-2024-C004**

**Member:** Raymond Flores
**Procedure:** CPT 22551, ACDF C5-C6, one level – prophylactic
**Disposition:** OVERTURN

**Warrant:**

The initial prior authorization request for an ACDF at C5-C6 was denied by HealthFirst Health Plan (HealthFirst) on the basis that "Cervical stenosis documented but no neurological deficit on examination. Member has axial neck pain only. No radiculopathy, no myelopathy documented. Surgical intervention not indicated for axial pain without neurological deficit under CC-SPINE-2024." The denial references Section 3.1 of HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, which excludes surgery "solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy," and implicitly refers to Section 2.1 which requires a confirmed diagnosis of radiculopathy, myelopathy, or disc herniation with objective neurological deficit.

Upon review of the clinical record and application of the three-source hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), the denial is determined to be inconsistent with California regulatory requirements and generally accepted standards of medical practice for the unique circumstances of this case.

**1. Application of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
The member's neurological examination is documented as "Completely normal. No radiculopathy. No myelopathy signs. No UMN signs. Axial neck pain only, 5/10." The MRI findings indicate "Cord contact without compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia." Based on these findings, the member does not currently meet HealthFirst's diagnostic criteria for symptomatic radiculopathy (2.1a), myelopathy (2.1b), or disc herniation with objective neurological deficit (2.1c). Therefore, from the perspective of treating an *existing symptomatic condition*, the plan's denial is consistent with its own criteria for denying surgery for axial neck pain without objective neurological deficit (3.1 exclusion). The conservative treatment requirements (Section 2.2) were met (8 weeks PT, 2 pharmacotherapy agents, 1 interventional procedure). The myelopathy exception (2.2A) does not apply because myelopathy is not currently the primary diagnosis.

**2. Application of California Regulatory Requirements:**
The most pertinent California regulation in this case is **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b)**, which states that the Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard "does NOT require that the service be consistent with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the service meets the standard in subdivision (a), the reviewer SHALL recommend approval regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive."

**CHSC § 1374.32(a)** defines "Medically necessary" as: "health care services that a physician or other provider, exercising prudent clinical judgment, would provide to a patient for the purpose of *preventing*, evaluating, diagnosing, or treating an illness, *injury*, disease, or its symptoms..." (Emphasis added).

In this case, the requested procedure is explicitly for *prophylactic* decompression. The clinical record demonstrates severe anatomical vulnerability:
*   **MRI (2024-09-15):** "C5-C6 severe central canal stenosis. Cord contact without compression... Canal diameter 6mm (normal >13mm). Single trauma could cause acute cord injury given severely reduced canal diameter."
*   **Surgeon Note (Dr. Davis):** "This patient has critically narrow canal diameter. Any significant cervical trauma would likely cause acute spinal cord injury. Given his occupation operating heavy equipment, the risk of such trauma is elevated. Prophylactic decompression is indicated to prevent catastrophic injury."

The physician's request and the MRI findings clearly articulate a significant risk of acute spinal cord *injury* due to the critically narrow canal, exacerbated by the member's high-risk occupation. This aligns directly with the "preventing... injury" clause within the definition of medical necessity under CHSC § 1374.32(a). The plan's criteria are restrictive in that they focus almost exclusively on *existing* symptomatic conditions, failing to adequately address the medical necessity of preventative intervention in a situation of extreme, documented anatomical risk for catastrophic neurological injury.

**3. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
While the cited clinical guidelines primarily focus on indications for *symptomatic* myelopathy or radiculopathy and do not explicitly list "prophylactic decompression for asymptomatic severe stenosis with cord contact in a high-risk individual" as a strong (Tier 1) or reasonable (Tier 2) indication for *treatment of existing symptoms*, they strongly emphasize the prevention of irreversible harm once myelopathy is established. The current case presents a pre-myelopathic state with an extremely high, documented risk of acute, traumatic myelopathy. The professional judgment of the treating spine surgeon, supported by the radiologist's findings, to prevent a catastrophic injury in a patient with a critically compromised spinal canal and an occupation involving high trauma risk, falls within the scope of "prudent clinical judgment" aimed at preventing injury.

**Conclusion:**
The denial was based on HealthFirst's criteria which emphasize the absence of *current* neurological deficits. However, the unique circumstances of this case — critically severe cervical stenosis (6mm canal diameter), documented cord contact, explicit radiological warning of acute cord injury from minor trauma, and a high-risk occupation — present a compelling argument for prophylactic intervention to prevent a severe and irreversible neurological injury. Under California law (CHSC § 1374.32(a) and (b)), medical necessity includes services for *prevention of injury*, and the IMR standard overrides plan criteria if the service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice. Given the extreme anatomical vulnerability and high occupational risk, exercising prudent clinical judgment to prevent catastrophic neurological injury constitutes a medically necessary service, even in the absence of current symptomatic myelopathy. The plan's criteria are unduly restrictive for this preventative intervention.

Therefore, the denial should be **OVERTURNED**.
