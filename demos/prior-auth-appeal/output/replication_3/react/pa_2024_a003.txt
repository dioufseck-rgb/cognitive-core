**I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

This appeal reviews the denial of prior authorization for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 for member Patricia Osei. The initial denial was based on unfulfilled conservative treatment requirements, specifically insufficient physical therapy duration and absence of an epidural steroid injection.

Upon comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst Plan Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory requirements (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014), and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021), it has been determined that the initial denial was inconsistent with both HealthFirst's own criteria (when correctly applied) and superseding state law and clinical standards.

**Disposition: OVERTURN DENIAL**

---

**II. CLINICAL SUMMARY**

Patricia Osei is a 55-year-old member diagnosed with progressive cervical myelopathy at C4-C5 with myelomalacia. Her neurological exam revealed significant upper motor neuron signs including bilateral lower extremity spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor skills, and a positive Lhermitte sign, all consistent with spinal cord compression. An MRI dated 2024-08-20, performed with a pacemaker-compatible protocol, confirmed severe central cord compression at C4-C5 with T2 signal change (myelomalacia) and noted progressive cord injury.

Conservative treatment included 5 weeks (15 sessions) of physical therapy, which was discontinued due to pacemaker precautions limiting certain modalities and the physician's determination that further PT was not addressing neurological progression. Pharmacotherapy included Baclofen for 3 months (partial relief of spasticity) and two courses of Methylprednisolone taper (temporary improvement). A cervical epidural steroid injection was not performed due to a documented medical contraindication: the member has a permanent pacemaker, rendering fluoroscopy-guided injections unsafe per anesthesiology consultation.

---

**III. REVIEW AND ANALYSIS**

The appeal was reviewed against the established hierarchy of authority: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.

**A. Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)**

The initial denial cited failure to meet Section 2.2 conservative treatment requirements (6 weeks PT, 1 interventional procedure). However, the plan's own criteria include a specific **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) \u2014 MYELOPATHY PATHWAY**, which applies when myelopathy is the primary diagnosis.

1.  **Diagnosis (Section 2.1):** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (2.1(b)) with supporting MRI. **Met.**
2.  **Conservative Treatment (Section 2.2 and Exception 2.2(A)):**
    *   **Physical Therapy (2.2(a)):** The myelopathy exception reduces the requirement to a minimum of 4 weeks. The member completed 5 weeks (15 sessions). This **meets** the modified requirement. Furthermore, the physician documented that further PT was not addressing neurological progression, indicating a functional plateau.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2(b)):** The member completed trials of Baclofen (3 months) and Methylprednisolone taper. This **meets** the requirement for two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks.
    *   **Interventional Treatment (2.2(c)):** The myelopathy exception waives this requirement if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration. The clinical record clearly documents that a fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injection is medically **contraindicated** due to the member's permanent pacemaker, per anesthesiology consultation. This **meets** the waiver provision of the myelopathy exception.
3.  **Imaging (Section 2.3):** Current MRI confirms severe central cord compression C4-C5 with myelomalacia. **Met.**
4.  **Physician Documentation (Section 2.4):** Assumed met; no contrary information.

**Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The initial denial was a misapplication of HealthFirst's own Clinical Coverage Criteria. The member *does* meet the conservative treatment requirements when the specific "Myelopathy Pathway" exception (Section 2.2A) is correctly applied.

**B. California Regulatory Requirements**

1.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This APL directly addresses cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia.
    *   Section II.1 states that myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm.
    *   Section II.2 mandates that plans **SHALL NOT** require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia), the treating physician documents conservative failure, and surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. The presence of myelomalacia specifically supports waiver of standard conservative requirements.
    *   The member's diagnosis of progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia, documented neurological progression despite conservative efforts, and a strong surgical indication directly trigger the waiver provisions of this APL.
    *   Section II.3 prohibits plans from applying internal criteria more restrictive than generally accepted standards for such conditions.
2.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute prohibits conditioning approval of surgery on PT completion where:
    *   (a)(1) The physician documents PT is unlikely to benefit: The treating physician determined further PT was "not addressing neurological progression."
    *   (a)(2) The member reached a functional plateau: The cessation of PT due to lack of addressing neurological progression indicates a functional plateau for the *myelopathic* symptoms.
    *   (a)(3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address: The MRI findings of severe central cord compression with T2 signal change (myelomalacia) unequivocally meet this criterion. Myelomalacia signifies existing cord damage that PT cannot reverse.

**Conclusion on State Law:** The denial is in direct conflict with both DMHC APL 22-014 and CIC § 10169.5. California state law explicitly overrides the plan's standard conservative treatment requirements for this member's condition.

**C. Clinical Standards of Medical Practice**

1.  **Diagnosis and Urgency:** AANS/CNS (2023), NASS (2020), and AO Spine (2021) guidelines clearly distinguish myelopathy from radiculopathy, emphasizing that myelopathy involves spinal cord compression with upper motor neuron signs and carries a risk of progressive neurological deficit. Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) is identified as a strong indicator of existing cord injury, increased risk of permanent deficit, and a factor contributing to surgical urgency.
2.  **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy:** The clinical guidelines state there is **no high-quality evidence** supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for moderate to severe cervical myelopathy, especially with myelomalacia. The 6-week PT requirement is derived from radiculopathy evidence, not myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete an arbitrary PT trial is not supported by evidence and may result in neurological deterioration (AO Spine 2021).
3.  **Surgical Indication:** The member's presentation \u2014 progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and objective neurological deficits \u2014 meets **TIER 1 \u2014 STRONG INDICATION** for surgical intervention under AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023).

**Conclusion on Clinical Standards:** Generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice strongly support surgical intervention for this member's condition and do not advocate for mandatory prolonged conservative treatment, particularly physical therapy, in the presence of myelomalacia.

**D. Conflict Resolution (Hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria)**

The denial relies on the standard Section 2.2 of HealthFirst's criteria, failing to apply the appropriate Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2A). Even if the plan's standard criteria were applied, they are rendered superseded by:
*   California state law (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014), which mandates waiver of conservative treatment in cases of myelopathy with myelomalacia and prohibits mechanical application of PT duration requirements when neurological compromise is present.
*   Generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS, AO Spine), which classify this condition as urgent, a strong surgical indication, and contraindicate extended conservative trials.

The plan's denial is inconsistent with its own criteria when correctly applied, and definitively conflicts with superseding state law and clinical standards.

---

**IV. DISPOSITION**

The prior authorization denial for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C4-C5, one level) for member Patricia Osei is **OVERTURNED**.

---

**V. WARRANT**

The denial is overturned because the initial determination misapplied HealthFirst's own Clinical Coverage Criteria and is in direct conflict with California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice for cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia.

1.  **Misapplication of Plan Criteria:** The HealthFirst Plan Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2A (Myelopathy Exception Pathway), explicitly modifies conservative treatment requirements for members with confirmed cervical myelopathy as a primary diagnosis. This exception reduces the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks (the member completed 5 weeks) and waives the interventional treatment requirement if the treating physician documents a medical contraindication. The clinical record clearly documents both that Patricia Osei's primary diagnosis is myelopathy and that a cervical epidural steroid injection is medically contraindicated due to her permanent pacemaker. Therefore, the member satisfies the plan's conservative treatment requirements under its own myelopathy exception.
2.  **Violation of California State Law:**
    *   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014** (September 2022) mandates that for conditions like cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia (which presents a risk of irreversible harm), plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia), the physician documents conservative failure, and surgery is indicated. Patricia Osei's MRI confirms myelomalacia and progressive cord injury, and her physician documented that conservative therapy was not addressing neurological progression.
    *   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5** (effective January 1, 2023) prohibits conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy completion when (a)(3) "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The documented myelomalacia and severe central cord compression directly meet this criterion, rendering the plan's 6-week PT requirement inapplicable.
3.  **Inconsistency with Clinical Standards:** Generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS Guidelines for Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy 2023 Update, AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guideline 2021) classify moderate-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia as a **TIER 1 strong surgical indication**. These guidelines explicitly state there is "no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy" and warn that "delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." The plan's denial conflicts with these evidence-based recommendations.

Based on the hierarchy of authority, California state law and generally accepted clinical standards supersede the plan's more restrictive criteria, and the plan's internal criteria were misapplied. Therefore, the requested ACDF at C4-C5 is medically necessary and approved.
