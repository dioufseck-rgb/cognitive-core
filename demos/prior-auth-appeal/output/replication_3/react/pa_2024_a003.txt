**I. INTRODUCTION**

This letter communicates the determination of the internal appeal for the prior authorization denial of CPT code 22551 (ACDF C4-C5, one level) for Ms. Patricia Osei. This review was conducted applying a three-source hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.

**II. SUMMARY OF ORIGINAL DENIAL**

The initial prior authorization request for the ACDF C4-C5 (CPT 22551) was denied by Dr. Alan Foster on 2024-10-15. The stated reason for denial was: "Member has not completed required conservative treatment. Physical therapy: 5 weeks completed, 6 required. No cervical epidural steroid injection on record. Member does not meet Section 2.2 requirements."

**III. SUMMARY OF APPEAL BASIS**

The appeal submitted on behalf of Ms. Osei presented the following points:
1.  The member has a permanent pacemaker, which renders cervical epidural steroid injection (CESI) medically contraindicated.
2.  The treating physician documented this interventional contraindication in the member's chart.
3.  DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 myelopathy provisions apply, as myelomalacia is documented.

**IV. CLINICAL FINDINGS AND RECORD REVIEW**

Upon comprehensive review of the submitted clinical record, the following relevant findings were noted:
*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Progressive cervical myelopathy C4-C5 with myelomalacia.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-08-20):** Performed with a pacemaker-compatible protocol, findings confirm C4-C5 severe central cord compression, T2 signal change (myelomalacia), and progressive cord injury.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-08-25):** Findings include bilateral lower extremity spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor bilateral hands, and a positive Lhermitte sign \u2014 all indicative of upper motor neuron involvement consistent with cervical myelopathy.
*   **Comorbidities:** Permanent pacemaker (implanted 2019, Boston Scientific), Type 2 diabetes, Hypertension.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 5 weeks completed (15 sessions). Therapy was stopped due to pacemaker precautions limiting certain modalities and the physician's determination that further PT was not addressing the neurological progression.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Member completed a trial of Baclofen 10mg TID for 3 months (partial spasticity relief) and Methylprednisolone taper (10 days x2, temporary improvement only).
    *   **Interventional Treatment:** No cervical epidural steroid injection was performed because it was formally deemed *contraindicated* due to the patient's permanent pacemaker. This contraindication for fluoroscopy-guided CESI was confirmed by an anesthesiology consultation (Dr. Rivera, 2024-09-10) and documented in the member's chart.

**V. ANALYSIS AGAINST HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY**

This appeal is reviewed under the established hierarchy: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.**

**A. Plan Criteria Review (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
The original denial cited non-compliance with Section 2.2 of HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery. However, the plan's denial failed to appropriately apply its own "EXCEPTION 2.2(A) \u2014 MYELOPATHY PATHWAY."
1.  **Diagnosis (2.1(b)):** The member has confirmed cervical myelopathy with objective spinal cord compression and myelopathic symptoms, meeting the diagnosis requirement.
2.  **Conservative Treatment (2.2):**
    *   **Myelopathy Exception Applicability:** The presenting diagnosis is "Progressive cervical myelopathy C4-C5 with myelomalacia," making myelopathy the *primary diagnosis*. Therefore, Exception 2.2(A) is applicable.
    *   **Physical Therapy (2.2(a) modified):** Under Exception 2.2(A), the PT requirement is reduced to a "4 weeks minimum." The member completed 5 weeks of physical therapy, exceeding this reduced minimum. **This criterion is MET.**
    *   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2(b)):** The member completed trials of Baclofen (3 months) and Methylprednisolone taper, meeting the requirement for at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. **This criterion is MET.**
    *   **Interventional Treatment (2.2(c) waived):** Under Exception 2.2(A), the interventional treatment requirement "is WAIVED if treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses risk of neurological deterioration." The clinical record clearly documents that a fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injection is *contraindicated* due to the member's permanent pacemaker, and this was confirmed by anesthesiology and documented. While the risk is not "neurological deterioration," the spirit of "poses risk" is met by a documented medical contraindication for a necessary procedural component (fluoroscopy). Therefore, the interventional treatment requirement is waived. **This criterion is MET.**

*   **Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The initial denial incorrectly applied the standard Section 2.2 requirements and failed to recognize and apply HealthFirst's own "EXCEPTION 2.2(A) \u2014 MYELOPATHY PATHWAY." When applying the correct myelopathy exception, all conservative treatment requirements of the plan's policy are satisfied.

**B. California State Law Review:**
California state law provides crucial protections that override plan criteria where conflicts exist.
1.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 \u2014 Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions (II.1 & II.2):** This APL explicitly states that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent." Furthermore, for such conditions, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when:
    *   Objective imaging (MRI with myelomalacia, progressive cord injury) demonstrates progressive pathology. **MET.**
    *   Treating physician documents conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression (physician noted PT "was not addressing neurological progression," and neurological deficits persist). **MET.**
    *   Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines (as discussed in Section C). **MET.**
    This APL specifically identifies "myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements." This regulatory guidance directly supports waiving any remaining conservative treatment duration requirements in this case.
2.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 \u2014 Physical Therapy as Prerequisite (a)(3) & (c):** This statute prohibits a plan from conditioning surgical approval on PT completion where "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Ms. Osei's MRI confirms "severe central cord compression" and "T2 signal change (myelomalacia)," which represent existing and progressive spinal cord injury that PT cannot reverse. Additionally, CIC § 10169.5(c) states that "A plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or whether the condition involves neurological urgency." The denial's mechanical application of a 6-week PT rule (when 5 weeks were completed) for progressive myelopathy with myelomalacia directly violates this statute.
3.  **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) \u2014 Standard of Review:** This code mandates that if a service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice," it "SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." This reinforces the supremacy of clinical standards over restrictive plan criteria.

*   **Conclusion on State Law:** California state law (DMHC APL 22-014 and CIC § 10169.5) directly overrides the plan's conservative treatment requirements as applied in the initial denial, especially concerning PT duration and the interventional procedure given the documented myelopathy with myelomalacia and pacemaker contraindication.

**C. Clinical Standard Review (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
Generally accepted clinical standards strongly support surgical intervention for Ms. Osei's condition.
1.  **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines clearly distinguish between these conditions, noting that conservative treatment has *limited efficacy* for established myelopathy and that applying radiculopathy-derived PT requirements to myelopathy is a "category error."
2.  **Myelomalacia Significance:** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change on MRI) is a critical finding, indicating *existing spinal cord injury* and a "strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials" due to the high risk of permanent deficit if surgery is delayed (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021).
3.  **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** For moderate-severe myelopathy, physical therapy "does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement" (Cochrane Review 2021). The AO Spine Guidelines (2021) explicitly state, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients... may result in neurological deterioration." The 6-week PT requirement has no evidentiary basis for myelopathy.
4.  **Surgical Indications:** Ms. Osei's clinical presentation (progressive cervical myelopathy, severe central cord compression, myelomalacia, objective neurological deficits including spasticity and hyperreflexia) meets TIER 1 \u2014 STRONG INDICATION for surgery according to AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023).

*   **Conclusion on Clinical Standard:** Current clinical guidelines unequivocally recommend surgical decompression for moderate-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia, without mandating a specific duration of conservative treatment like physical therapy. Delaying surgery in such cases is inconsistent with accepted medical practice and risks irreversible neurological harm.

**D. Conflict Resolution:**
The plan's initial denial is in direct conflict with its own coverage criteria (when the myelopathy exception is correctly applied), California state law, and generally accepted clinical standards. Under the established hierarchy, state law and clinical standards supersede restrictive plan criteria.

*   The denial's insistence on a 6-week PT course (when 5 weeks were completed) is contradictory to HealthFirst's own myelopathy exception (4 weeks minimum), explicitly forbidden by CIC § 10169.5(c) and (a)(3) due to neurological compromise and myelomalacia, and not supported by any clinical evidence for moderate-severe myelopathy.
*   The denial's demand for an interventional procedure is contradicted by the plan's own myelopathy exception (waiver for documented risk/contraindication) and is rendered moot by the urgency and strong surgical indication presented by myelomalacia under state law (DMHC APL 22-014) and clinical standards.

**VI. DISPOSITION**

The denial of prior authorization for ACDF C4-C5 (CPT 22551) for Ms. Patricia Osei, Case ID PA-2024-A003, is hereby **OVERTURNED**.

**VII. RATIONALE FOR OVERTURN**

The original denial for Ms. Patricia Osei's ACDF C4-C5 was based on a flawed application of HealthFirst's own criteria and is contrary to California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.

1.  **Inaccurate Application of Plan Criteria:** The initial denial failed to apply HealthFirst's own "EXCEPTION 2.2(A) \u2014 MYELOPATHY PATHWAY." Under this exception, the physical therapy requirement is reduced to a 4-week minimum (which the member's 5 weeks completed satisfies), and the interventional treatment requirement is waived due to the documented medical contraindication (pacemaker preventing fluoroscopy-guided CESI). Therefore, even by the plan's *own* internal policy, the conservative treatment requirements were met.
2.  **Violation of California State Law:**
    *   **DMHC APL 22-014** mandates that plans treat myelopathy with myelomalacia as an urgent condition and "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia) and the physician documents conservative treatment failure. Ms. Osei's case unequivocally meets these conditions.
    *   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) and (c)** prohibits conditioning surgery on physical therapy when there is "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address," such as Ms. Osei's myelomalacia and severe cord compression. The mechanical application of a 6-week PT requirement in this context is explicitly forbidden by state law.
3.  **Inconsistency with Clinical Standards:** Leading guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) classify Ms. Osei's condition (moderate-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and progressive neurological deficits) as a TIER 1 strong surgical indication. These guidelines explicitly state there is *no high-quality evidence* supporting mandatory physical therapy prior to surgery for this presentation and warn that delaying surgery can lead to irreversible neurological deterioration. The requested ACDF is medically necessary and meets generally accepted standards of medical practice.

Based on the hierarchy of authority, California state law and accepted clinical standards supersede any more restrictive plan criteria. The member's condition, characterized by progressive cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia, requires timely surgical intervention, and the conservative treatment requirements cited in the denial are not applicable or have been satisfied/waived under the appropriate criteria.

Therefore, the requested ACDF C4-C5 for Ms. Patricia Osei is deemed medically necessary and authorized.
