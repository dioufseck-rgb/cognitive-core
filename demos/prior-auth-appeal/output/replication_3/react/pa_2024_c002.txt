Dear Mr. Santos, Dr. Wong, and HealthFirst Appeals Department,

This letter serves as the final appeal determination for the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 for Mr. David Santos, which was initially denied on 2024-10-05. An expert health plan appeals reviewer has thoroughly reviewed the submitted clinical documentation, the HealthFirst clinical coverage criteria, applicable California state laws and regulations, and generally accepted clinical standards.

---

### CASE SUMMARY

Mr. David Santos, a 63-year-old member, requested prior authorization for ACDF C6-C7 due to a presenting diagnosis of mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy. Imaging shows C6-C7 moderate cord compression with T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the cord, and right C7 foraminal stenosis. Neurological examination reveals both right C7 radicular symptoms and objective myelopathic signs including bilateral hyperreflexia (right > left), positive right Hoffman, equivocal left Hoffman, and mildly wide-based gait. Conservative treatment included 6 weeks of physical therapy (myelopathy protocol), 3 months of Pregabalin, 2 months of Naproxen, and one right C7 epidural steroid injection. The physical therapy and interventional procedure showed no improvement in bilateral neurological signs or hyperreflexia.

The initial denial, issued by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, on 2024-10-05, stated:

However, member's symptoms are primarily right-sided (radicular) with only mild bilateral components. Primary diagnosis remains radiculopathy. Myelopathy exception requires myelopathy as primary diagnosis." (Reason Code: MED-003)

### REVIEW AGAINST CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK

The denial and appeal have been reviewed in the context of California state law and regulations, which supersede plan criteria where conflicts exist.

1.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 – Prior Authorization Requirements for Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions:**
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** This regulation states that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent unless compelling clinical evidence supports otherwise."
        *   **Application:** Mr. Santos's MRI clearly shows "moderate cord compression" and "T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within cord." This directly triggers DMHC APL 22-014. HealthFirst is therefore **required to treat this case as urgent**, regardless of whether his subjective pain is predominantly radicular. The presence of myelomalacia is a critical objective finding of existing spinal cord injury.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** For conditions where delay risks irreversible harm (like Mr. Santos's myelopathy with myelomalacia), plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment... when: (a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia...), AND (b) Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression, AND (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines."
        *   **Application:**
            *   (a) Met: "T2 signal change (myelomalacia)" is objective evidence of progressive pathology.
            *   (b) Met: 6 weeks of "myelopathy protocol" PT resulted in "No improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms." The epidural had "No change in bilateral hyperreflexia." This documents failure of conservative treatment to address the myelopathic signs.
            *   (c) Met: As detailed below, surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted guidelines.
        *   **Further, APL 22-014 explicitly states:** "The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements even where a full course has not been completed, when the treating physician documents urgency."
        *   **Conclusion:** The plan's denial, which hinges on a determination of "primary diagnosis" for conservative treatment requirements, is directly contradicted by DMHC APL 22-014. The objective finding of myelomalacia with myelopathy mandates an urgent approach and permits waiver of standard conservative requirements, rendering HealthFirst's restrictive "primary diagnosis" clause in its myelopathy exception invalid under state law for this case.

2.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 – Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:**
    *   This section states that a plan "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where: (3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
        *   **Application:** Mr. Santos's MRI finding of "T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within cord" is clear objective evidence of structural neurological compromise. Myelomalacia signifies existing cord injury that physical therapy cannot reverse or adequately address.
    *   Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) states that PT completion cannot be required where "the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." The physical therapy note explicitly states "No improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms" after 6 weeks. This indicates a functional plateau regarding the neurological and radicular symptoms relevant to the surgery.
    *   **Conclusion:** CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) and (a)(2) prohibit HealthFirst from using the physical therapy requirement as a basis for denial, even if it had not been "completed" to their satisfaction, which it arguably was for the myelopathic symptoms.

3.  **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) – Standard of Review for IMR:**
    *   This critical provision dictates that for medical necessity, if a service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice," approval "SHALL be recommended regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive."
    *   **Application:** As demonstrated below, Mr. Santos's condition meets generally accepted standards for surgical intervention. Therefore, HealthFirst's more restrictive criteria, such as the "primary diagnosis" clause in its myelopathy exception, cannot be used to deny coverage.

### REVIEW AGAINST CLINICAL STANDARD (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)

1.  **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy Distinction:** The clinical record clearly indicates cervical myelopathy. While radicular symptoms are present, the objective findings of bilateral hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman signs, gait disturbance, and critically, T2 signal change (myelomalacia) on MRI unequivocally establish myelopathy. The denial's assertion that radiculopathy is the "primary diagnosis" is not supported by the objective neurological findings. Myelomalacia indicates actual cord injury.

2.  **Significance of Myelomalacia:** All major spine societies (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) recognize myelomalacia (T2 cord signal change) as a strong indicator of existing spinal cord injury, a predictor of permanent deficit if surgery is delayed, and a strong surgical indication. It is considered a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials.

3.  **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy:**
    *   **AO Spine (2021) states:** "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
    *   The 6-week PT requirement often found in payer policies is primarily derived from radiculopathy evidence, not myelopathy. Applying it to myelopathy is considered a category error by clinical experts.

4.  **Surgical Indications for ACDF (CPT 22551):**
    *   Per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), Mr. Santos meets **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION (surgery recommended)** criteria due to:
        *   (a) Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI.
        *   (c) Myelomalacia on MRI (cord signal change).
    *   The clinical standard dictates that patients with confirmed cervical myelopathy, myelomalacia on MRI, and objective neurological deficits (Hoffman sign, hyperreflexia, gait disturbance) meet strong surgical indications. Requiring completion of a minimum course of physical therapy in this setting is inconsistent with accepted standards of medical practice and may risk further neurological deterioration.

### CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND APPEAL DETERMINATION

The HealthFirst denial of prior authorization for ACDF C6-C7 is based on a restrictive interpretation of its own internal criteria, specifically the requirement for myelopathy to be the "primary diagnosis" for its myelopathy exception to apply. This interpretation is directly contradicted by California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of care.

Applying the hierarchy of authority (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):

1.  **State Law Overrides Plan Criteria:**
    *   DMHC APL 22-014 mandates that cases with myelopathy, cord compression, and myelomalacia on MRI **shall be treated as urgent**, and plans **shall not require completion of a full course of conservative treatment**. This directly overrides HealthFirst's "primary diagnosis" clause, as the objective presence of myelomalacia itself justifies waiver of conservative requirements regardless of symptom "primacy."
    *   CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) prohibits denying surgery based on physical therapy requirements when objective structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) is present and PT cannot address it. Furthermore, the clinical record demonstrates a functional plateau for the myelopathic symptoms after 6 weeks of PT, aligning with CIC § 10169.5(a)(2).

2.  **Clinical Standard Overrides Plan Criteria:**
    *   AANS/CNS (2023) and AO Spine (2021) guidelines clearly indicate that myelopathy with myelomalacia is a TIER 1 strong surgical indication. These guidelines explicitly state that delaying surgery to complete extended conservative treatment, particularly physical therapy, is not supported by evidence and may lead to neurological deterioration. The plan's requirement for myelopathy to be the "primary" diagnosis or for a rigid conservative treatment pathway is more restrictive than these generally accepted standards.

In accordance with CHSC § 1374.32(b), the service, being medically necessary under generally accepted standards of medical practice, must be approved regardless of the plan's more restrictive internal criteria. Mr. Santos clearly exhibits objective signs of cervical myelopathy with confirmed myelomalacia, which necessitates prompt surgical intervention to prevent irreversible neurological damage. His conservative treatment has failed to improve the myelopathic signs.

### DISPOSITION

**OVERTURN**

### WARRANT

The denial of prior authorization for ACDF C6-C7 for Mr. David Santos is **OVERTURNED**.

The plan's denial was based on its determination that cervical myelopathy was not the "primary diagnosis," thereby precluding the application of its myelopathy exception which would reduce physical therapy requirements. This interpretation is in direct conflict with California state regulations and generally accepted clinical guidelines for managing cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia.

1.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014** requires HealthFirst to treat cases with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI as urgent. This regulation explicitly allows for the waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements when myelomalacia is present and surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines (Source 2, Part II.1 & II.2). Mr. Santos's MRI unequivocally shows myelomalacia with moderate cord compression. The plan's "primary diagnosis" clause cannot supersede this regulatory mandate.
2.  **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)** prohibits health plans from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy where there is "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Myelomalacia represents existing spinal cord damage that cannot be reversed by physical therapy (Source 2, Part III). Furthermore, the 6 weeks of PT showed "No improvement in bilateral neurological signs," indicating a functional plateau as per CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) and Clinical Standard, Section 5 (Source 2, Part III; Source 3, Section 5).
3.  **Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)** classify moderate-severe myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia as a TIER 1 strong surgical indication. These guidelines explicitly state that delaying surgery for an extended course of conservative treatment, especially physical therapy, is not supported by evidence and may increase the risk of irreversible neurological deterioration (Source 3, Section 1.2, 2.1, 2.3, 3.1, 3.2). The clinical record confirms objective myelopathic signs (bilateral hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman, gait disturbance) alongside imaging findings of myelomalacia and cord compression.

Therefore, Mr. Santos's requested ACDF C6-C7 is medically necessary under generally accepted standards of medical practice and falls within the scope of services required to be covered under California law, despite the plan's more restrictive internal criteria.

**Authorization for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C6-C7, one level) for David Santos is hereby APPROVED.**
