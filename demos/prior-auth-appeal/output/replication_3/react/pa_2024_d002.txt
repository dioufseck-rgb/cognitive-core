**I. BACKGROUND**

This constitutes the formal appeal determination for the prior authorization denial of an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 for Mr. Michael Green. The initial request, submitted by Dr. Sarah Kim, was denied by HealthFirst on 2024-11-08 (reviewer Dr. Patricia Huang) under reason code MED-003, citing failure to meet plan criteria. The appeal basis highlights several procedural defects and the member's inability to evaluate the denial without the referenced criteria.

**II. CLINICAL SUMMARY**

Mr. Michael Green, a 57-year-old male, presents with cervical radiculopathy at C5-C6 with functional impairment.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-09-20):** Reveals a large C5-C6 disc herniation with moderate left C6 nerve root compression. Importantly, there is **no cord contact** and no evidence of myelomalacia.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-09-25):** Documents left C6 dermatomal numbness, diminished left bicep reflex, and left bicep weakness graded 4/5. These findings are consistent with radiculopathy.
*   **Conservative Treatment History:**
    *   **Physical Therapy (PT):** Completed 7 weeks (21 sessions). PT notes explicitly document a "plateau at week 6" with persistent radicular symptoms.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Trialed Gabapentin 600mg TID for 4 months (partial relief) and Naproxen 500mg BID for 3 months (inadequate relief).
    *   **Interventional:** Received a left C6 epidural on 2024-10-28, which provided 40% relief, but symptoms returned to baseline within 3 weeks.
*   **Primary Diagnosis:** Based on the clinical record, the primary diagnosis is cervical **radiculopathy**, not myelopathy. Myelopathy requires evidence of spinal cord compression and specific upper motor neuron signs or myelomalacia, which are not present here.

**III. DENIAL REVIEW AND ANALYSIS**

As an expert health plan appeals reviewer, I have applied the three-source hierarchy: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.

**A. Procedural Adequacy of Denial (State Law - CHSC § 1374.31(b))**

The initial denial notice contained significant procedural defects:
1.  **Missing Criteria Document:** The denial referenced an attached criteria document, but no document was attached, and both the member and provider confirmed non-receipt.
2.  **Missing IMR Notice:** The denial notice failed to include information about the member's right to an Independent Medical Review (IMR).

**CHSC § 1374.31(b) states that a denial notice failing to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is "DEFECTIVE" and "shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."** The failure to provide the referenced criteria document directly violates this provision, rendering the denial procedurally inadequate. This alone constitutes grounds for overturning the denial.

**B. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)**

Assuming, for the purpose of a comprehensive review, that a procedurally adequate denial had been issued, I will now assess the request against HealthFirst's own published criteria (CC-SPINE-2024).

1.  **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy C5-C6 with objective neurological deficit and imaging demonstrating consistent structural pathology (large disc herniation, moderate C6 nerve root compression). **This criterion is MET.**
2.  **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **(a) Physical therapy:** Requires a minimum of 6 weeks. The member completed 7 weeks of PT, with documented functional plateau at week 6. **This criterion is MET.**
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** Requires a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The member trialed Gabapentin for 4 months and Naproxen for 3 months. **This criterion is MET.**
    *   **(c) Interventional treatment:** Requires one of the specified interventions. The member received a left C6 epidural. **This criterion is MET.**
    *   **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY:** This exception is for confirmed cervical myelopathy. As established, the member has cervical radiculopathy with no cord contact or myelopathic signs/symptoms. Therefore, **this exception does not apply**, and the standard conservative treatment requirements for radiculopathy were correctly referenced (and, as shown above, were met).

**C. California Regulatory Requirements (State Law)**

Beyond the procedural defects, California state law provides specific guidance that may supersede plan criteria.

1.  **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This law prohibits plans from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where, among other reasons, "(2) The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
    *   Mr. Green completed 7 weeks of PT, and the PT notes clearly document a "plateau at week 6" with persistent radicular symptoms.
    *   **This state law provision directly applies.** Even if HealthFirst were to argue that the PT duration was somehow insufficient despite meeting their 6-week minimum, state law mandates that a functional plateau waives further mechanical PT requirements.

2.  **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This All-Plan Letter primarily addresses myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia. Since Mr. Green's diagnosis is radiculopathy with "no cord contact" or myelomalacia, the specific urgency provisions of this APL for myelopathy do not directly apply to this case. However, the APL reinforces that "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice."

**D. Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)**

Generally accepted clinical standards support surgical intervention for persistent radiculopathy after a failed course of conservative treatment.
*   The AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) identify "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care" as **TIER 2 (Reasonable Indication) for surgery.**
*   Mr. Green has undergone 7 weeks of PT (with plateau), 4 months of Gabapentin, 3 months of Naproxen, and an epidural that provided only temporary relief, all while experiencing persistent left C6 dermatomal numbness, diminished reflex, and 4/5 bicep weakness. This clinical picture clearly falls within the "reasonable indication" for surgery under generally accepted guidelines.
*   The clinical evidence base distinguishes radiculopathy from myelopathy, noting that the 6-week PT requirement is typically derived from radiculopathy studies, and there is no evidence to support delaying surgery for myelopathy. For radiculopathy, 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment is generally considered an adequate trial before surgery. Mr. Green has surpassed this with documented failure.

**IV. CONFLICT RESOLUTION (HIERARCHY: State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria)**

1.  **State Law vs. Plan Criteria:** The denial is procedurally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a state law violation that alone necessitates overturning. Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) directly prohibits denying care based on PT duration when a functional plateau has been reached, which it has in this case. State law overrides any conflicting or misapplied plan criteria.
2.  **Clinical Standard vs. Plan Criteria:** Even if the plan's criteria were deemed more restrictive than state law permits, the clinical standard supports surgical intervention for Mr. Green's condition given the failure of robust conservative treatment and persistent neurological deficits. CHSC § 1374.32(b) emphasizes that services meeting generally accepted standards should be approved regardless of more restrictive plan criteria.
3.  **Plan's Own Criteria Application:** Independent of state law and clinical standards, the plan's denial appears to be an incorrect interpretation or application of its *own* published criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), as the member demonstrably meets all listed requirements for radiculopathy.

Based on the comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's published criteria, applicable California state law, and generally accepted clinical standards, the denial of prior authorization for ACDF C5-C6 for Mr. Michael Green **MUST BE OVERTURNED.**
