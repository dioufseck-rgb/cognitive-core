**HEALTHFIRST HEALTH PLAN — PRIOR AUTHORIZATION APPEAL DETERMINATION**

**Case ID:** PA-2024-A001
**Member:** Diana Torres, M-112233
**Procedure:** ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551)
**Date of Review:** 2024-11-08

---

**I. BACKGROUND**

Ms. Diana Torres, a 61-year-old member, requested prior authorization for an ACDF at C5-C6. The request was denied by HealthFirst Utilization Management on 2024-11-01 (initial level review). The denial cited "MED-003" and stated the member completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy (PT), did not meet the 6-week PT requirement under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, and only one pharmacotherapy agent was trialed.

The treating neurosurgeon, Dr. James Kim, appealed the denial, asserting that the member has severe myelomalacia on MRI, which under California law prohibits the PT requirement when structural neurological compromise is present. Dr. Kim further declared PT contraindicated due to documented cord signal change and invoked regulatory override under CIC 10169.5(a)(3).

---

**II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND DIAGNOSIS**

The clinical record indicates a primary diagnosis of **Progressive cervical myelopathy C5-C6 with myelomalacia**. Ms. Torres presented with a 4-month history of bilateral hand clumsiness and gait instability. Neurological examination revealed significant upper motor neuron signs including bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia in all extremities, positive Babinski reflex bilaterally, and documented gait ataxia. MRI findings from 2024-08-15 confirmed severe central cord compression C5-C6 with T2 hyperintensity within the cord (myelomalacia), indicating existing spinal cord injury.

Conservative treatment consisted of 2 weeks (6 sessions) of physical therapy, which was discontinued by the treating physician due to symptom exacerbation, and a 2-week course of Methylprednisolone taper, which provided only temporary mild improvement. Dr. Kim explicitly documented that PT was contraindicated due to myelomalacia and the risk of irreversible cord injury.

---

**III. REVIEW OF DENIAL AGAINST HEALTHFIRST PLAN CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)**

The denial cited HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 for conservative treatment requirements.

1.  **Diagnosis (2.1):** The member meets criterion 2.1(b) for cervical myelopathy with objective neurological deficits and supporting MRI.
2.  **Conservative Treatment (2.2):**
    *   **Physical Therapy (2.2(a)):** The standard pathway requires 6 weeks of PT. The member completed 2 weeks. The plan's myelopathy exception (2.2(A)) reduces this to 4 weeks minimum. Even with the exception, the member did not meet the stated duration.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2(b)):** The standard pathway requires 2 agents for 4+ weeks each. The member trialed one agent (Methylprednisolone taper for 2 weeks). This requirement was not met.
    *   **Interventional (2.2(c)):** The standard pathway requires one interventional treatment. The plan's myelopathy exception (2.2(A)) waives this if the treating physician documents risk. No interventional treatment was performed, and it was not cited in the denial, suggesting it may have been implicitly waived or not applicable.
3.  **Myelopathy Exception (2.2(A)):** The plan's denial did not explicitly state whether it applied its own myelopathy exception. By citing the "6 weeks" standard pathway, it appears the plan either did not apply the myelopathy exception's reduced 4-week PT requirement or determined the member still failed to meet even the reduced requirement. However, the plan's myelopathy exception (2.2(A)) itself states that the pharmacotherapy requirement "remains in full."

**Conclusion based solely on Plan Criteria:** Based strictly on the written criteria, even with its own myelopathy exception, the member technically did not meet the full duration of PT (2/4 weeks) or the pharmacotherapy requirement (1/2 agents). Therefore, the denial *would be upheld* if only plan criteria were considered.

---

**IV. REVIEW AGAINST CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK (State Law - Highest Authority)**

California law provides specific protections and overrides for prior authorization denials, especially in cases of neurological compromise.

1.  **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):**
    This statute directly prohibits an insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy under several circumstances. Two of these apply directly to this case:
    *   **§ 10169.5(a)(1):** "The member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition." Dr. Kim's letter (2024-10-15) explicitly states, "PT is contraindicated in this patient. Myelomalacia is present on initial MRI. Continuing PT risks irreversible cord injury." This provision **is met**.
    *   **§ 10169.5(a)(3):** "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The MRI finding of "Severe central cord compression C5-C6. T2 hyperintensity within cord (myelomalacia)" constitutes objective evidence of structural neurological compromise. This provision **is met**.
    *   **§ 10169.5(c):** "A plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or whether the condition involves neurological urgency under subdivision (a)(3)." This provision directly addresses and invalidates the plan's mechanical application of a minimum PT duration in this case.

2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):**
    This APL provides further guidance for time-sensitive neurological conditions:
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** It states that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent." This description precisely matches Ms. Torres's clinical presentation.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** This section states that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization" when specific conditions are met, including:
        *   (a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (e.g., myelomalacia): **MET** (MRI shows myelomalacia).
        *   (b) Treating physician documents conservative treatment failure and surgical indication: **MET** (Dr. Kim documented PT caused symptom exacerbation, discontinued it, and stated surgery is urgently indicated).
        *   (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines: **MET** (as will be discussed under clinical standards).
    *   The APL specifically identifies "the presence of myelomalacia on MRI as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements even where a full course has not been completed, when the treating physician documents urgency." This provision directly addresses and negates the plan's conservative treatment requirements for Ms. Torres.
    *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for conditions identified in this guidance. Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs."

**Conclusion based on State Law:** California law, specifically CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014, explicitly prohibits the denial of this authorization based on the failure to complete a minimum duration of physical therapy or a full course of conservative treatment, given the member's diagnosis of cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and the treating physician's documentation. These state laws supersede the plan's more restrictive internal criteria.

---

**V. REVIEW AGAINST CLINICAL STANDARD (Generally Accepted Medical Practice)**

The clinical evidence base from leading spine societies (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) strongly supports the treating physician's position.

1.  **Distinction between Myelopathy and Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines emphasize that myelopathy and radiculopathy are distinct conditions requiring different treatment algorithms. The evidence base for conservative treatment, including defined PT durations, primarily applies to radiculopathy, not myelopathy. Applying radiculopathy-derived PT requirements to myelopathy patients is considered a "category error." (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.1)
2.  **Myelomalacia Significance:** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change on MRI) is a critical finding, indicating existing spinal cord injury. It is a "strong predictor of permanent deficit if surgery delayed" and a "strong surgical indication, contraindication to extended conservative trial." (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.2)
3.  **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** For moderate-severe myelopathy (as in this case, mJOA likely 8-12 given UMN signs and gait instability), physical therapy "does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement." Delaying surgery for a PT trial "is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1)
4.  **Conservative Treatment Duration:** There is "no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy." The 6-week PT requirement in many payer policies is explicitly stated to derive from radiculopathy evidence, "NOT myelopathy evidence." (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.2)
5.  **Surgical Indications:** Ms. Torres meets **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION** for surgery per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) due to:
    *   Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI.
    *   Progressive neurological deficit despite conservative treatment (symptoms worsened during PT).
    *   Myelomalacia on MRI.
    *   Objective neurological deficits (Hoffman, hyperreflexia, gait ataxia).
    The clinical standard "does NOT require completion of a minimum course of physical therapy as a prerequisite to surgery for this presentation." (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.2)
6.  **Risk of Delay:** Delaying surgery for moderate-severe myelopathy, particularly with myelomalacia, is associated with decreased likelihood of neurological recovery and worse outcomes. (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.3)

**Conclusion based on Clinical Standard:** Generally accepted standards of medical practice strongly recommend surgical intervention for Ms. Torres's condition and explicitly do not support requiring a minimum duration of physical therapy or an extended conservative trial given the diagnosis of moderate-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia. The plan's criteria are inconsistent with these standards.

---

**VI. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND DISPOSITION**

Applying the three-source hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria):

1.  **Plan Criteria:** The denial aligns with the *letter* of the plan's general conservative treatment requirements, though it appears to misapply them in the context of myelopathy or its own myelopathy exception's pharmacotherapy clause.
2.  **State Law:** California Insurance Code § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014 directly and explicitly override the plan's conservative treatment requirements for this specific clinical scenario (myelopathy with myelomalacia and physician documentation of contraindication/urgency).
3.  **Clinical Standard:** Peer-reviewed clinical guidelines and evidence-based medicine from leading spine societies unequivocally support surgical intervention and explicitly contradict the plan's requirement for a minimum duration of physical therapy or an extended conservative trial for this presentation.

**Resolution:** State law and clinical standards are in agreement and directly contradict the basis for the plan's denial. Under the established hierarchy, state law and generally accepted clinical standards govern, making the plan's internal criteria for conservative treatment inapplicable in this case.

---

**VII. DISPOSITION**

**OVERTURN**

---

**VIII. WARRANT**

The HealthFirst Health Plan's denial for ACDF C5-C6 for Ms. Diana Torres is hereby **OVERTURNED**. The denial was based on the member's failure to complete a minimum of 6 weeks of physical therapy and trial two pharmacotherapy agents, as per HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2.

This denial is in direct conflict with California state law and generally accepted standards of medical practice for the treatment of cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia, which mandate approval of the requested service.

Specifically:
1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3)** explicitly prohibits health plans from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy when:
    *   The treating physician documents that PT is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member (Dr. Kim's letter confirms PT was contraindicated due to risk of irreversible cord injury with myelomalacia).
    *   The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address (MRI shows severe central cord compression and T2 hyperintensity/myelomalacia).
2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (II.2)** mandates that plans shall not require a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite for surgical authorization when objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia present), the treating physician documents conservative treatment failure, and surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. The APL specifically identifies myelomalacia on MRI as a finding supporting the waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements.
3.  **Clinical Standards of Medical Practice** (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) classify Ms. Torres's condition (moderate-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and upper motor neuron signs) as a **TIER 1 strong indication for surgical intervention**. These guidelines explicitly state that requiring a minimum duration of physical therapy for this presentation is not supported by evidence and delaying surgery may result in irreversible neurological deterioration. The clinical evidence base differentiates myelopathy from radiculopathy, noting that conservative treatment guidelines for radiculopathy do not apply to myelopathy.
4.  The plan's application of its internal conservative treatment criteria (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2) is more restrictive than, and directly contradicted by, both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of care. Per **CHSC § 1374.32(b)** and **DMHC APL 22-014 (II.3)**, where such conflicts exist, the higher authority of state law and clinical standards must govern the decision.

Given the clear and present structural neurological compromise with myelomalacia, the documented contraindication to further PT, and the strong surgical indication under generally accepted medical guidelines, the requested ACDF at C5-C6 is medically necessary. The denial for insufficient conservative treatment is therefore inconsistent with legal and clinical requirements and must be overturned.
