**I. SUMMARY OF ORIGINAL DENIAL**
The initial prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 was denied by HealthFirst Utilization Management (Dr. Alan Foster, MD) on 2024-11-01. The reason for denial (Reason Code MED-003) stated the member failed to meet conservative treatment requirements under HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2. Specifically, the denial cited:
1.  Completion of only 2 weeks of physical therapy, whereas the standard pathway requires a minimum of 6 weeks.
2.  Pharmacotherapy requirement not met, with only one agent trialed.

---

**II. APPEAL BASIS**
The appeal submitted on behalf of Ms. Torres asserts that the denial should be overturned based on the following:
1.  Member has severe myelomalacia on MRI, which, under California law, prohibits a physical therapy requirement when structural neurological compromise is present.
2.  The treating physician declares physical therapy contraindicated due to documented cord signal change (myelomalacia).
3.  Regulatory override under California Insurance Code (CIC) 10169.5(a)(3) makes the plan's conservative treatment criteria irrelevant in this specific clinical context.

---

**III. CLINICAL FINDINGS AND BACKGROUND**
Ms. Diana Torres, a 61-year-old female, presents with progressive cervical myelopathy at C5-C6.

*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Progressive cervical myelopathy C5-C6 with myelomalacia.
*   **Initial Presentation (2024-08-10):** 4-month history of bilateral hand clumsiness and gait instability. Neurological examination revealed upper motor neuron signs including bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia bilateral upper and lower extremities, and positive Babinski bilateral with documented gait ataxia.
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-08-15):** Findings of severe central cord compression at C5-C6 with T2 hyperintensity within the spinal cord (myelomalacia).
*   **Conservative Treatment Trial:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** Initiated 2024-09-01, discontinued 2024-09-14 after 2 weeks (6 sessions). The treating physician (Dr. Kim) documented that PT was causing symptom exacerbation and declared it contraindicated, issuing a written order to discontinue.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Trial of Methylprednisolone taper for 2 weeks, which resulted in temporary mild improvement but no sustained benefit. No other pharmacotherapy agents were trialed.
*   **Physician Declaration (Dr. Kim, 2024-10-15):** Explicitly stated that PT is contraindicated due to myelomalacia on MRI and the risk of irreversible cord injury. Dr. Kim asserted that CIC 10169.5(a)(3) applies and surgery is urgently indicated.

---

**IV. ANALYSIS**

This appeal determination applies the three-source hierarchy: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.**

**A. Assessment of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)**

1.  **Diagnosis (2.1):** The member clearly meets the diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (2.1(b)) with supporting MRI demonstrating severe central cord compression and myelomalacia. **(MET)**
2.  **Conservative Treatment (2.2):**
    *   **Physical Therapy (2.2(a)):** The plan's standard pathway requires 6 weeks of PT. The myelopathy exception (2.2(A)) reduces this to 4 weeks. The member completed only 2 weeks. **(NOT MET by plan's criteria/exception duration)**
    *   **Pharmacotherapy (2.2(b)):** The plan requires a trial of at least two agents for 4+ weeks each. The member trialed only one agent (Methylprednisolone taper) for 2 weeks. **(NOT MET by plan's criteria/exception)**
    *   **Interventional Treatment (2.2(c)):** The plan's myelopathy exception waives this if the treating physician documents risk of neurological deterioration. While Dr. Kim documented risk for PT, he did not explicitly do so for interventional treatment. This was not the primary basis for the denial, however.

**Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** Based solely on the explicit duration requirements within HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024, including its myelopathy exception, the member technically did not meet the specified conservative treatment durations for physical therapy and pharmacotherapy.

**B. Assessment of California Regulatory Requirements (State Law - Highest Authority)**

1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This state law explicitly limits a plan's ability to require physical therapy as a condition for surgical approval.
    *   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1):** Prohibits conditioning approval on PT where the "treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition." Dr. Kim explicitly documented PT was contraindicated due to myelomalacia and caused symptom exacerbation. **(MET)**
    *   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3):** Prohibits conditioning approval on PT where "the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The MRI finding of severe central cord compression with T2 hyperintensity (myelomalacia) constitutes objective evidence of structural neurological compromise. **(MET)**
    *   **CIC § 10169.5(c):** States that a "plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or whether the condition involves neurological urgency under subdivision (a)(3)." This directly overrides the plan's mechanical application of a 4- or 6-week PT requirement for this member.

2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 — Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions:** This guidance specifically addresses prior authorization for time-sensitive neurological conditions.
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** States that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent..." Ms. Torres's clinical presentation precisely matches this description. **(MET)**
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** For urgent conditions where delay risks irreversible harm, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when: (a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia...), AND (b) Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression, AND (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines for the condition."
        *   (a) Myelomalacia on MRI is present. **(MET)**
        *   (b) PT was attempted, symptoms worsened, and physician documented contraindication. Pharmacotherapy provided only temporary mild improvement. This constitutes attempted and failed conservative treatment. **(MET)**
        *   (c) As will be discussed in Section C, surgical intervention for myelopathy with myelomalacia is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines.
        *   The APL further clarifies that "the presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements even where a full course has not been completed, when the treating physician documents urgency." Dr. Kim documented urgency. **(MET)**
    *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** This section explicitly states, "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for conditions identified in this guidance. Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs."

**Conclusion on State Law:** California state law, specifically CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014, directly overrides HealthFirst's conservative treatment requirements for physical therapy and the broader "full course of conservative treatment" for this member's condition of cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia. The law considers PT contraindicated and explicitly states that such conditions should be treated as urgent, waiving full conservative treatment requirements.

**C. Assessment of Clinical Standard (Higher Authority than Plan Criteria)**

1.  **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020):** Clinical guidelines emphasize that myelopathy and radiculopathy are distinct conditions with different treatment algorithms. Applying radiculopathy-derived conservative treatment requirements to myelopathy is a "category error" not supported by evidence.
2.  **Myelomalacia Significance (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021):** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) indicates existing spinal cord injury, is a strong predictor of permanent deficit if surgery is delayed, and is considered a "strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials."
3.  **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy (AO Spine 2021, Cochrane 2021):** For moderate-severe myelopathy (which Ms. Torres has), physical therapy does "NOT produce meaningful functional improvement." AO Spine Guidelines (2021) explicitly state, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
4.  **Conservative Treatment Duration (NASS 2020):** "No randomized controlled trials support a defined minimum duration of conservative treatment before surgery" for myelopathy. The 6-week PT requirement is derived from radiculopathy evidence.
5.  **Surgical Indications (AANS/CNS 2023):** Ms. Torres's presentation (confirmed cervical myelopathy, myelomalacia on MRI, objective neurological deficits including UMN signs and gait disturbance) meets "TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION (surgery recommended)."

**Conclusion on Clinical Standard:** Generally accepted clinical standards strongly support surgical intervention for Ms. Torres's condition and do not support requiring a minimum duration of physical therapy or other conservative treatment components that would delay surgery in the presence of myelomalacia and progressive myelopathy.

---

**V. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND DISPOSITION**

The plan's denial is based on unmet conservative treatment requirements (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2). However, this conflicts directly with California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.

*   **State Law Overrides Plan Criteria:** CIC § 10169.5 directly prohibits the plan from requiring physical therapy in this case due to the treating physician's documented contraindication and the objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia). DMHC APL 22-014 further mandates that plans treat such conditions as urgent and waive full conservative treatment requirements when myelomalacia is present and surgery is indicated. This applies to both the PT and pharmacotherapy requirements.
*   **Clinical Standard Overrides Plan Criteria:** Clinical guidelines from AANS/CNS, AO Spine, and NASS unequivocally support urgent surgical intervention for moderate-severe myelopathy with myelomalacia and explicitly state that delaying surgery for a mandatory PT course is not supported by evidence and carries a risk of neurological deterioration. These clinical standards are explicitly stated by DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3 and CHSC § 1374.32(b) to override more restrictive plan criteria.

Therefore, the plan's internal criteria regarding conservative treatment cannot be applied in this case due to the higher authority of state law and prevailing clinical standards. The member meets the criteria for urgent surgical intervention.

---

**VI. DETERMINATION**

The denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6, one level) for member Diana Torres is **OVERTURNED**.

**Warrant:**
The denial by HealthFirst Health Plan for failure to meet conservative treatment requirements, specifically the duration of physical therapy and the number of pharmacotherapy agents, is not supported when applying the required hierarchy of authority (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria).

1.  **State Law (CIC § 10169.5):** California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3) explicitly prohibit conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy when the treating physician documents it as contraindicated due to neurological compromise (myelomalacia) or unlikely to benefit. Both conditions are met in this case, as documented by Dr. Kim and confirmed by MRI findings. This directly overrides HealthFirst's 4-week or 6-week PT requirement (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2).
2.  **State Law (DMHC APL 22-014):** The Department of Managed Health Care's All-Plan Letter 22-014 mandates that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI be treated as an urgent condition. For such conditions, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" as a prerequisite for surgical authorization, especially when imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (myelomalacia), conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression, and surgery is indicated by generally accepted clinical guidelines. The member's case fulfills all these conditions, thereby overriding the plan's requirements for both physical therapy and the number/duration of pharmacotherapy agents.
3.  **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021, NASS 2020):** Generally accepted clinical guidelines classify cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and objective neurological deficits (such as those presented by Ms. Torres) as a TIER 1 strong indication for surgical intervention. These guidelines explicitly state that there is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgery for moderate to severe cervical myelopathy, and delaying surgery in such patients may result in irreversible neurological deterioration. Myelomalacia is recognized as a sign of existing cord injury and a contraindication to extended conservative trials. The plan's criteria for conservative treatment are therefore inconsistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice for this urgent and severe condition.

In conclusion, the plan's denial is based on criteria that are overridden by California state law and contradict generally accepted clinical standards for the management of cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia. The requested ACDF C5-C6 for Ms. Diana Torres is medically necessary.
