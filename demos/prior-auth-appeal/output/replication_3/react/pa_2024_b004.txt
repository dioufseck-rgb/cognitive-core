### APPEAL DETERMINATION: **OVERTURN**

---

### REVIEW FINDINGS & RATIONALE:

This appeal was reviewed applying the three-source hierarchy of authority: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria**.

**1. Primary Clinical Presentation:**
The clinical record describes "Acute cervical radiculopathy C5-C6 with functional impairment." MRI findings confirm "C5-C6 large disc herniation with moderate right C6 nerve root compression. Foraminal stenosis. No cord contact. No myelomalacia." Neurological exam shows "Right C6 dermatomal numbness, bicep reflex diminished right, grip strength 4/5 right. Fine motor testing: unable to perform violin fingering tasks." Based on the domain knowledge provided (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.1), these findings are consistent with **Cervical Radiculopathy**, not Myelopathy (which would involve spinal cord compression, upper motor neuron signs, bilateral symptoms, and potentially myelomalacia). The initial denial correctly identified the condition as radiculopathy.

**2. Review of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

*   **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** **MET.** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of Cervical Radiculopathy (2.1a) with supporting MRI consistent with symptoms.
*   **2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:** **MET.** Current MRI within 6 months demonstrates relevant structural pathology.
*   **2.4 PHYSICIAN DOCUMENTATION:** Appears to be **MET** based on the appeal basis and requesting physician's recommendation.
*   **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT (CORE OF DENIAL):**
    *   **(a) Physical therapy:** The plan requires a "minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy." The clinical record clearly states "6 weeks intensive PT" (18 sessions) was completed.
        *   **Finding:** The initial denial's rationale that "Member's symptoms have been present for only 6 weeks. Plan criteria require at least 6 weeks of symptoms before surgical consideration" is a **misapplication and misinterpretation** of HealthFirst's own criteria. Plan criterion 2.2(a) requires 6 weeks of *physical therapy*, not 6 weeks of *symptoms before surgical consideration*. The member *completed* the required 6 weeks of PT. The denial suggests the 6-week *symptom duration* is the barrier, despite 6 weeks of *failed intensive PT* occurring during that time.
    *   **(b) Pharmacotherapy:** **MET.** Member trialed Methylprednisolone (6 weeks) and Gabapentin (5 weeks), meeting the "at least two... for minimum 4 weeks each" requirement.
    *   **(c) Interventional treatment:** **MET.** Member received a Right C6 epidural.
    *   **Myelopathy Exception (2.2A):** This exception does not apply, as the member's diagnosis is radiculopathy.

**3. Application of California Regulatory Requirements (State Law - HIGHEST AUTHORITY):**

*   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This state law directly addresses the plan's denial regarding the "premature" nature of the surgical request due to duration of conservative treatment.
    *   **Subdivision (a)(2) applies:** A plan cannot condition approval on completion of physical therapy where "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." The clinical record unequivocally states, "Functional plateau documented at 6 weeks. No improvement in fine motor deficits or radicular symptoms. OT confirms inability to perform professional duties." This directly aligns with the legal exception.
    *   **Subdivision (a)(3) applies:** A plan cannot condition approval on physical therapy where "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The MRI shows "C5-C6 large disc herniation with moderate right C6 nerve root compression" causing motor weakness and significant functional impairment (unable to perform violin fingering tasks). Physical therapy cannot resolve this structural compression.
    *   **Subdivision (c) is violated:** This subdivision states, "A plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or whether the condition involves neurological urgency under subdivision (a)(3)." The plan's denial is a mechanical application of a minimum duration, completely disregarding the documented functional plateau and neurological compromise.
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Conservative Treatment Exceptions):** While this APL focuses on myelopathy, its underlying principle for "conditions where delay of treatment risks irreversible harm" is relevant. The member's severe, career-ending functional impairment (loss of fine motor control essential for a professional violinist) due to neurological deficit makes this a time-sensitive issue where further delay of effective treatment (surgery) risks irreversible harm to their livelihood. The physician has documented conservative failure, and surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines.
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This law dictates that services meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. As detailed below, the service meets clinical standards.

**4. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine - SECOND HIGHEST AUTHORITY):**

*   **Functional Plateau:** The clinical record states "Functional plateau documented at 6 weeks. No improvement in fine motor deficits or radicular symptoms." This aligns precisely with the clinical standard definition of functional plateau: "less than 10% improvement in validated functional outcome measure over 3 consecutive therapy sessions, OR therapist documentation that patient has reached maximum therapeutic benefit" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 5).
*   **Surgical Indications for Radiculopathy:** According to AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), ACDF is a **TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION** for:
    *   (b) "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx." The member has had 6 weeks of intensive, failed conservative treatment with persistent symptoms.
    *   (c) "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care." The member's inability to perform violin fingering tasks due to right hand weakness is a clear, career-ending "significant functional impairment" not responsive to extensive conservative care.
*   **Conservative Treatment Duration:** While guidelines often mention 6-12 weeks for radiculopathy, once a functional plateau is reached and severe functional impairment persists, continued *ineffective* conservative treatment is not clinically necessary. The assertion in the denial about "high rate of spontaneous resolution" is less applicable here given the objective motor weakness and documented failure of multiple conservative modalities to improve motor function. Delaying surgery in this specific context (professional musician with career-ending deficit) is not supported by the evidence and risks permanent disability (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.3).

**5. Conflict Resolution & Conclusion:**
The initial denial, based on the mechanical application of a 6-week symptom duration requirement, directly conflicts with:
1.  **HealthFirst's own plan criteria (2.2a):** The member *completed* the required 6 weeks of physical therapy. The denial's interpretation of "6 weeks of symptoms before surgical consideration" is erroneous.
2.  **California State Law (CIC 10169.5(a)(2), (a)(3), and (c)):** The law explicitly prohibits denial based on needing further PT when a functional plateau has been reached and/or structural neurological compromise exists that PT cannot address. Both conditions are met in this case.
3.  **Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:** The member's significant functional impairment, unresponsive to a robust 6-week conservative trial and reaching a functional plateau, constitutes a reasonable indication for surgery per AANS/CNS guidelines. Continued delay is not supported.

Therefore, applying the hierarchy of authority (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria), the state law and clinical standards override the plan's misapplied internal criteria and the denial's flawed reasoning.

---

### DISPOSITION: **OVERTURN**

**Warrant:** The prior authorization denial is **OVERTURNED**. The member has met all conservative treatment duration requirements per plan criteria and has reached a documented functional plateau despite 6 weeks of intensive physical therapy, two pharmacotherapy agents, and an epidural injection, with persistent and career-ending motor weakness. The denial's reasoning that surgery is "premature" based on a misinterpretation of plan criteria and mechanical application of treatment duration directly violates California Insurance Code § 10169.5 and is contrary to generally accepted standards of medical practice for a patient with significant functional impairment due to cervical radiculopathy. Surgical authorization for ACDF C5-C6 is medically necessary and approved.
