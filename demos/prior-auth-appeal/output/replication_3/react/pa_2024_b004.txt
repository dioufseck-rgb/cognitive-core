## PRIOR AUTHORIZATION APPEAL DETERMINATION LETTER

**Case ID:** PA-2024-B004
**Member:** Kevin Watkins (M-889900)
**Plan:** HealthFirst PPO Gold — Group Plan 77441
**Procedure:** ACDF C5-C6, one level (CPT 22551)
**Date of Denial:** 2024-11-10
**Reviewer:** Appeals Department, HealthFirst Health Plan

**Date of Appeal Determination:** 2024-11-20

---

**I. BACKGROUND**

A prior authorization request was submitted by Dr. Sarah Martinez for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 for Mr. Kevin Watkins, a 41-year-old professional violinist diagnosed with acute cervical radiculopathy C5-C6 with functional impairment. The initial request was denied on November 10, 2024, on the basis that the "surgical request is premature" as "Member's symptoms have been present for only 6 weeks," with the denial stating that "Plan criteria require at least 6 weeks of symptoms before surgical consideration" and noting "Recent onset radiculopathy has a high rate of spontaneous resolution." The denial notice also stated, "Member meets conservative treatment requirements."

The member's appeal asserts that the member is a professional musician whose right hand weakness is career-ending, all conservative treatment was completed and failed within the 6-week period, a functional plateau was reached at 6 weeks, and the physician recommends surgery given the functional impairment.

---

**II. CLINICAL RECORD REVIEW**

Mr. Watkins presented with acute cervical radiculopathy C5-C6 and significant functional impairment. His occupation as a professional violinist makes right-hand function essential to his livelihood.

*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-10-01):** Revealed a C5-C6 large disc herniation with moderate right C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis. Crucially, the MRI **did not show spinal cord contact or myelomalacia**, which would indicate myelopathy.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-10-01):** Documented right C6 dermatomal numbness, diminished right bicep reflex, 4/5 right grip strength, and inability to perform violin fingering tasks, with functional impairment confirmed by occupational therapy. This is consistent with radiculopathy.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** Member completed 6 weeks of intensive PT (18 sessions). The record explicitly states a "Functional plateau documented at 6 weeks. No improvement in fine motor deficits or radicular symptoms. OT confirms inability to perform professional duties."
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Included Methylprednisolone (6 weeks, temporary partial relief) and Gabapentin (5 weeks, partial pain relief, no motor improvement).
    *   **Interventional:** A right C6 epidural steroid injection (2024-10-28) provided only "25% temporary relief, no improvement in motor deficit, returned to baseline in 2 weeks."

---

**III. DENIAL REVIEW AND APPLICABLE CRITERIA**

This review applies the hierarchy of authority: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.**

**1. Primary Clinical Presentation:**
Based on the clinical record (MRI findings of nerve root compression without cord contact/myelomalacia, and neurological exam demonstrating dermatomal symptoms/reflex changes without upper motor neuron signs), the primary diagnosis is **Cervical Radiculopathy**, not Myelopathy. This distinction is critical as the plan's myelopathy exception and certain state/clinical guidelines specific to myelomalacia do not directly apply.

**2. Procedural Adequacy of Denial (CHSC § 1374.31(b)):**
The denial notice specified a reason ("premature" due to 6 weeks of symptoms, referring to plan criteria for conservative treatment duration). While the reasoning is flawed as outlined below, it is not procedurally defective as it provided a specific clinical reason.

**3. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
*   **2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** Member meets criterion 2.1(a) (Cervical radiculopathy) and 2.1(c) (Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit). **MET.**
*   **2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:**
    *   (a) Physical therapy: Member completed 6 weeks of intensive PT. The plan's criteria specify a *minimum* of 6 weeks. **MET.**
    *   (b) Pharmacotherapy: Member completed Methylprednisolone (6 weeks) and Gabapentin (5 weeks). Both meet the 4-week minimum for two agents. **MET.**
    *   (c) Interventional: Member completed one cervical epidural steroid injection. **MET.**
*   **Plan's Denial Reasoning vs. Plan Criteria:** The denial stated, "Member meets conservative treatment requirements" but then deemed the request "premature" because "Member's symptoms have been present for only 6 weeks." This creates an internal contradiction. HealthFirst's own policy (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a)) requires a *minimum* of 6 weeks of PT. Once this minimum is met and conservative treatment has failed (as documented), the plan's criteria do not explicitly state a *further* waiting period before surgery can be considered. The denial's interpretation that 6 weeks is "premature" directly misapplies its own policy's *minimum* requirement. The Myelopathy Exception (2.2(A)) does not apply as this is a radiculopathy case.

**4. Application of California Regulatory Requirements:**
*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):**
    *   This state law is highly relevant. It states that a health insurer **shall not condition approval** of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where:
        *   **(a)(2) "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."** The clinical record explicitly documents 6 weeks of intensive PT, a "Functional plateau documented at 6 weeks," and "No improvement in fine motor deficits or radicular symptoms." The occupational therapist confirmed inability to perform professional duties. This directly satisfies CIC § 10169.5(a)(2).
        *   **(a)(3) "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."** While this is radiculopathy, the objective neurological deficit (grip strength 4/5, inability to perform violin tasks) with functional impairment that PT failed to improve, falls under this category of PT being unable to address the core neurological deficit.
    *   **(c) CIC § 10169.5:** "A plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or whether the condition involves neurological urgency under subdivision (a)(3)." The plan's denial applied its 6-week minimum *mechanically* by arguing it was "premature" at 6 weeks, effectively ignoring the documented functional plateau. This violates state law.
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:** This APL primarily addresses myelopathy with myelomalacia, which is not present here. However, it reiterates the principle (II.3) that "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice."
*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This provision dictates that if a service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, it **shall be approved regardless** of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.

**5. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
*   **Functional Plateau:** Clinical standards define a functional plateau as <10% improvement over 3 sessions OR therapist documentation of maximum benefit reached (AANS/CNS 2023, Section 5). The member's clinical record, including OT documentation of no improvement in fine motor deficits or radicular symptoms despite intensive PT, clearly meets this definition.
*   **ACDF Indications for Radiculopathy:** The AANS/CNS guidelines (2023, Section 3.1) indicate surgery as a "TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION" for:
    *   (b) "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx"
    *   (c) "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care"
*   Mr. Watkins has persistent radiculopathy symptoms after 6 weeks of failed intensive conservative treatment and has a significant, career-ending functional impairment that did not respond to therapy. While the clinical standard provides a 6-12 week range for conservative treatment for radiculopathy (AANS/CNS 2023, Section 4), the presence of a documented functional plateau and severe functional impairment, especially for a professional musician, supports surgical intervention at this stage. Further delay for more conservative treatment would be inconsistent with the documented failure and plateau.

**6. Conflict Resolution (Hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria):**
The plan's denial, which deems the request "premature" at 6 weeks despite the member having met the plan's *minimum* PT duration and having reached a documented functional plateau, directly conflicts with **California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2) and (c)**. This state law explicitly prohibits conditioning approval on further physical therapy when a functional plateau has been reached. Furthermore, the plan's denial is internally inconsistent and misapplies its own minimum PT requirement. The clinical standard also supports surgical intervention when a significant functional impairment persists and a functional plateau has been reached after an adequate conservative trial.

Therefore, the plan's denial is superseded by California state law.

---

**IV. DISPOSITION**

Based on the comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst Health Plan's clinical criteria, California state laws and regulations, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, the prior authorization denial for ACDF C5-C6 for Mr. Kevin Watkins should be **OVERTURNED**.

**Warrant:**

1.  **Misapplication of Plan Criteria:** The initial denial stated that the member "meets conservative treatment requirements" but then deemed the request "premature" because "symptoms have been present for only 6 weeks." This is contradictory. HealthFirst's own Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a)) requires a *minimum* of 6 weeks of physical therapy, which the member completed. There is no provision in the plan criteria requiring *more than* 6 weeks of symptoms or conservative treatment once the minimum is met and treatment has failed.
2.  **Violation of California State Law (CIC § 10169.5):** California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2) explicitly prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of surgery on further physical therapy when "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." The clinical record clearly documents that Mr. Watkins completed 6 weeks of intensive PT, reached a "Functional plateau," and had "No improvement in fine motor deficits or radicular symptoms," with an Occupational Therapist confirming inability to perform professional duties. The plan's implicit requirement for more than 6 weeks, despite this plateau, violates this state law (CIC § 10169.5(c)).
3.  **Consistency with Clinical Standards:** While 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment is generally considered for radiculopathy, clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, Section 3.1, TIER 2(c)) identify "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care" as a reasonable indication for surgery. The member's career-ending inability to perform fine motor tasks for his profession, coupled with objective neurological deficits and a documented functional plateau, indicates that further conservative treatment is unlikely to provide benefit and aligns with generally accepted clinical standards supporting surgical intervention at this juncture (AANS/CNS 2023, Section 5).

Therefore, the denial is contrary to California state law, which takes precedence over plan criteria, and is inconsistent with the plan's own criteria and generally accepted clinical standards.
