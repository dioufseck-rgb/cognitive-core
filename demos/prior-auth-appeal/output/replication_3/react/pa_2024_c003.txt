## HealthFirst Prior Authorization Appeal Determination

**Case ID:** PA-2024-C003
**Member:** Helen Brooks (M-112244), Age 49
**Plan:** HealthFirst PPO Gold — Group Plan 77441, California (2024 plan year)
**Procedure:** ACDF C4-C5, one level (CPT 22551)
**Requested By:** Dr. James Park, MD — Neurosurgery
**Denial Date:** 2024-11-20
**Reviewer:** Appeals Reviewer, HealthFirst Medical Department
**Appeal Level:** First Level Internal Appeal

### **Disposition:** OVERTURNED

### **Warrant:**

The prior authorization denial for an anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 for member Helen Brooks is **OVERTURNED**. The denial was based on an incorrect application of the plan's own criteria, and furthermore, the plan's requirements are superseded by both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards for the management of cervical myelopathy.

**Reasoning:**

1.  **Confirmed Diagnosis of Cervical Myelopathy:**
    The clinical record unequivocally supports a diagnosis of cervical myelopathy. Ms. Brooks presents with objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia in the upper extremities, and subtle balance impairment on tandem gait. These clinical findings are consistent with spinal cord compression, which is documented on MRI at C4-C5 with cord contact. This presentation fully meets HealthFirst's own diagnostic criteria for cervical myelopathy (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(b)). The plan's reviewer's dispute regarding the "clinical significance" of the T2 signal change on MRI does not negate the clear clinical presentation of myelopathy supported by objective neurological signs and cord compression. The second radiologist's opinion confirming "early myelomalacia" further strengthens the diagnosis of myelopathy.

2.  **Incorrect Application of HealthFirst's Myelopathy Exception:**
    HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY) explicitly modifies conservative treatment requirements for members with confirmed cervical myelopathy. Specifically, it states that the "Interventional treatment requirement (2.2(c)) is WAIVED if treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses risk of neurological deterioration." In this case, the treating neurosurgeon, Dr. Park, documented that epidural steroid injections were not performed due to "myelopathy concern — epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated in cord compression." This documentation directly triggers the waiver provision within HealthFirst's own myelopathy exception.
    The initial denial stated, "Myelopathy exception does not apply" based on the plan radiologist's interpretation of T2 signal change. However, as established above, the diagnosis of myelopathy is confirmed by clinical signs and cord compression, regardless of the nuanced interpretation of myelomalacia. Therefore, the plan **incorrectly applied its own criteria** by failing to activate the myelopathy exception, which would have waived the interventional treatment requirement.

3.  **State Law Overrides Plan Criteria:**
    *   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Prior Authorization Requirements for Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions):** This California state guidance is directly applicable. It stipulates that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" when objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (such as cord signal change/myelomalacia) and surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines (DMHC APL 22-014, II.2). The second radiologist's report confirming "early myelomalacia" provides objective imaging evidence of cord injury. The APL specifically identifies the presence of myelomalacia as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements. The APL also states that where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs (DMHC APL 22-014, II.3).
    *   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute prohibits conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy where "[t]he condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address" (CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)). Cervical myelopathy, with its inherent spinal cord compression and upper motor neuron involvement, clearly falls under this provision. Ms. Brooks completed 8 weeks of physical therapy with "no improvement in UMN signs," indicating that for her myelopathic condition, physical therapy reached a functional plateau regarding the neurological symptoms it cannot address, as described in clinical standards (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 5).

4.  **Generally Accepted Clinical Standards Support Surgical Intervention and Waive Further Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine (2023 Update), NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), and AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guideline (2021):** These authoritative guidelines categorize moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression (and especially with myelomalacia) as a **TIER 1 strong surgical indication** (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.1).
    *   The guidelines unequivocally state that there is **no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy** (AO Spine 2021, Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1). Applying conservative treatment requirements derived from radiculopathy to myelopathy patients is considered a "category error" not supported by evidence (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 1.1).
    *   Furthermore, these guidelines caution against delaying surgery, noting that "Delaying surgery in such patients... may result in neurological deterioration" and that "Myelomalacia on preoperative MRI is the strongest predictor of incomplete neurological recovery postoperatively" (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.3). The treating physician's decision to avoid interventional procedures due to risk of neurological deterioration in cord compression is consistent with clinical standards.

**Conclusion:**

Based on the comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's own criteria, California state law, and generally accepted clinical standards, the denial of prior authorization for ACDF at C4-C5 is not supported. Ms. Brooks meets the diagnostic criteria for cervical myelopathy, and the plan's own myelopathy exception should have been applied, waiving the interventional procedure requirement. Moreover, California state law and clinical guidelines take precedence over the plan's criteria in this instance, indicating that further conservative treatment is neither medically necessary nor appropriate and may pose a risk of harm.

**Therefore, the denial is OVERTURNED, and the requested ACDF C4-C5 (CPT 22551) is deemed medically necessary and approved.**
