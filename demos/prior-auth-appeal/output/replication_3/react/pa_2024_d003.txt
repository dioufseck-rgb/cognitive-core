**WARRANT FOR DISPOSITION**

The denial of prior authorization for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C6-C7, one level) for member Jessica Turner is hereby **OVERTURNED**. This determination is based on a critical procedural defect in the denial notice, which violates California state law, and further, on substantive conflicts between the plan's criteria and both state law and generally accepted clinical standards of care for cervical radiculopathy.

**I. Procedural Defect in Denial Notice (State Law Violation)**

The primary and immediate basis for this overturn is the plan's failure to issue a procedurally adequate denial notice as mandated by California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a)(2) and (b).

*   **Lack of Written Criteria and Specific Reason:** The denial explicitly states that the "written_denial_basis" was "Verbal only — peer-to-peer call," and that "Detailed criteria review provided verbally during peer-to-peer call." CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2) requires that a plan SHALL "Include in the denial notice: the specific criteria applied, the specific clinical reason for the denial, and notice of IMR rights." Communicating these details verbally during a peer-to-peer discussion does not satisfy the requirement for a *written* denial notice.
*   **Absence of IMR Notice:** The clinical record explicitly states "imr_notice_included: false." This directly violates CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2), which mandates the inclusion of IMR rights in the denial notice.
*   **Legal Consequence:** CHSC § 1374.31(b) unequivocally states: "A denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is DEFECTIVE and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines." This defect renders the denial invalid from its inception, necessitating an **OVERTURN** to allow for a compliant review process.

**II. Substantive Conflicts with State Law and Clinical Standards**

Even if the procedural defects were remedied, a substantive review reveals likely conflicts with state law and generally accepted clinical standards, which would also challenge the validity of the denial.

*   **Diagnosis:** The member's presenting diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy C6-C7, confirmed by imaging (C6-C7 disc herniation, right C7 nerve root compression, moderate foraminal stenosis) and neurological exam (right C7 dermatomal numbness and tingling, diminished tricep reflex, grip 4+/5). This meets the HealthFirst Plan Criteria Section 2.1(a) for Cervical Radiculopathy. There is no evidence of cervical myelopathy (e.g., cord compression, myelomalacia, upper motor neuron signs), therefore the plan's myelopathy exception (Section 2.2(A)) is not applicable, nor are myelopathy-specific state guidance like DMHC APL 22-014 regarding myelomalacia.

*   **Physical Therapy Requirement (Conflict with State Law):**
    *   **Plan Criteria:** HealthFirst Plan Criteria Section 2.2(a) requires a "minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy." The member completed 5 weeks (15 sessions).
    *   **State Law Override:** California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2) states that a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
    *   **Application:** The member completed a course of physical therapy (5 weeks), and the treating physician explicitly "declares plateau in appeal letter." Per CIC § 10169.5(b), a "functional plateau" is determined by the treating physical therapist *or physician*. The physician's declaration in the appeal letter constitutes sufficient documentation under state law. Therefore, the plan cannot deny based solely on the duration of physical therapy, as state law overrides the plan's 6-week minimum in this circumstance.

*   **Pharmacotherapy and Interventional Treatment Requirements (Conflict with Clinical Standard):**
    *   **Plan Criteria:** HealthFirst Plan Criteria Section 2.2(b) requires "at least two... for minimum 4 weeks each" pharmacotherapy agents, and Section 2.2(c) requires "One of the following interventional treatments." The member used Meloxicam (3 months, meets 4-week duration) but Prednisone (2 weeks, does not meet 4-week duration), so only one pharmacotherapy agent met criteria. No interventional treatments were performed.
    *   **Clinical Standard:** The AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines for Cervical Myelopathy (which also addresses radiculopathy) Section 3.1, Tier 2, states that surgery is a "Reasonable Indication" for "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx." This guideline provides a broad timeframe for conservative treatment but does not mandate specific numbers or types of pharmacotherapy agents or interventional procedures.
    *   **Conflict:** With 5 weeks of PT (with a physician-declared plateau) and 3 months of NSAID therapy, the member has undergone a substantial course of conservative treatment. The plan's specific, quantitative requirements for two pharmacotherapy agents meeting a 4-week minimum *each* and a mandatory interventional procedure may be considered more restrictive than the "generally accepted standards of medical practice" for an adequate conservative trial for radiculopathy, as outlined by AANS/CNS. Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), services meeting generally accepted standards of medical practice shall be approved, regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.

**III. Conclusion**

The denial by HealthFirst PPO Gold for CPT 22551 is procedurally defective under California law and is also subject to substantive challenges based on state law overrides and conflicts with generally accepted clinical standards.

**Therefore, the denial is OVERTURNED.** The plan must rescind the current denial and process the prior authorization request in a manner consistent with California law and generally accepted standards of medical practice. If the plan intends to issue a denial, it must do so with a fully compliant written notice, including specific clinical reasons, criteria, and IMR rights.
