**DISPOSITION: OVERTURN DENIAL**

**WARRANT:**

The denial for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C6-C7, one level) for member Jessica Turner is hereby OVERTURNED for the following reasons, applying the hierarchy of State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria:

**1. Procedural Defects in the Denial Notice (Violation of State Law):**
The initial denial notice is procedurally defective and in violation of California State Law.
*   **CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2) and (b):** This statute mandates that a plan SHALL provide written notice of a denial, including "the specific criteria applied, the specific clinical reason for the denial, and notice of IMR rights." It further states that a denial notice failing to specify the clinical criteria or specific clinical reason "is DEFECTIVE and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."
*   **Case Specifics:** The denial explicitly states that the "Detailed criteria review provided verbally during peer-to-peer call" and "Written denial basis: Verbal only — peer-to-peer call." Furthermore, the record indicates "imr_notice_included: false." This directly contradicts the requirements of CHSC § 1374.31. A verbal peer-to-peer discussion cannot substitute for a formal, written denial notice that includes specific criteria and IMR rights.
*   **Conclusion:** The plan's failure to provide a written denial notice detailing the specific criteria and clinical reasons, along with the omission of IMR notice, constitutes a fundamental procedural defect that, by state law, renders the denial invalid.

**2. State Law Overrides Plan's Physical Therapy Requirement:**
Even if the denial had been procedurally sound, the plan's application of its physical therapy (PT) duration requirement is overridden by state law.
*   **Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a)):** Requires a "minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy." The member completed 5 weeks of PT.
*   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3):** This state law prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where "the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
*   **Case Specifics:** The member's clinical record documents cervical radiculopathy with C6-C7 disc herniation, right C7 nerve root compression, and a diminished tricep reflex. These findings represent objective structural neurological compromise. Physical therapy can aid in symptom management but cannot reverse a mechanical nerve root compression or restore a diminished reflex. Additionally, the treating physician declared that the member reached a functional plateau, which aligns with CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) and (2) as further justification for not requiring additional PT.
*   **Conclusion:** Due to the presence of objective structural neurological compromise, the plan's strict 6-week PT requirement is superseded by CIC § 10169.5, rendering this aspect of the denial invalid.

**3. Clinical Presentation and Adequacy of Conservative Treatment (Clinical Standard):**
The member's primary clinical presentation is cervical radiculopathy, not myelopathy, as evidenced by unilateral symptoms (right C7 dermatomal numbness/tingling), nerve root compression on MRI, and lower motor neuron signs (diminished tricep reflex). Myelopathy-specific exceptions in plan criteria and state/clinical guidelines (e.g., DMHC APL 22-014) are not directly applicable here.
*   **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020):** Surgical intervention for radiculopathy is considered a reasonable option (TIER 2 indication) for patients with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment or significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care.
*   **Case Specifics:** The member has undergone 5 weeks of physical therapy (declared plateau by physician) and pharmacotherapy for 3 months (Meloxicam, with partial relief) and 2 weeks (Prednisone, temporary improvement). While an interventional procedure (Plan Criteria 2.2(c)) was not performed, the overall conservative trial, combined with objective neurological findings and persistent symptoms, approaches the "adequate conservative trial" timeframe often cited in clinical guidelines for radiculopathy. Given the state law override of the PT duration, the absence of an interventional procedure alone is insufficient to uphold the denial, particularly in light of the significant procedural defects.

**Summary of Conflicts and Resolution:**
The primary basis for this overturn is the severe procedural defect in the denial process, which directly violates California State Law (CHSC § 1374.31) and renders the original denial invalid. Furthermore, the plan's reliance on a strict 6-week PT requirement is overridden by CIC § 10169.5 due to objective neurological compromise. While the member did not fully meet all components of the plan's conservative treatment criteria (specifically the interventional procedure requirement and the duration for one pharmacotherapy agent), the overriding state law violations and the overall clinical picture of persistent radiculopathy symptoms after a substantial course of conservative management support overturning the denial.

**Final Determination:**
Based on the foregoing, the denial of prior authorization for CPT 22551 for Jessica Turner is **OVERTURNED**. The requested service is determined to be medically necessary based on the current clinical evidence and relevant California state laws.
