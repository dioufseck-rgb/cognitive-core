**I. SUMMARY OF REQUEST AND DENIAL**

Catherine Mills, a 53-year-old member, requested prior authorization for a three-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C3-C4, C4-C5, and C5-C6 for multi-level cervical spondylosis with myeloradiculopathy.

HealthFirst Health Plan initially denied the request for the C3-C4 and C5-C6 levels (Reason Code MED-003), stating that "Review of imaging and clinical record supports surgical intervention at one level (C4-C5) only. C3-C4 and C5-C6 do not demonstrate pathology of sufficient severity to warrant surgical intervention at this time." The denial implicitly approved the C4-C5 level.

The member's appeal asserts that all three levels are symptomatic, the treating surgeon has documented reasoning for a three-level fusion, and conservative treatment has been completed for all levels.

As an expert health plan appeals reviewer, I have reviewed the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024), relevant California regulatory frameworks (CIC, CHSC, DMHC APL 22-014), and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021).

**IV. DETAILED REVIEW AND ANALYSIS**

**A. Procedural Adequacy of Denial Notice (CHSC § 1374.31(b))**
The initial denial notice specified "MED-003" and provided a clinical reason (insufficient pathology for C3-C4 and C5-C6). While it did not explicitly cite the plan's criteria document or detail IMR rights, the reason provided is sufficiently specific to allow for appeal. Thus, the denial is considered procedurally adequate for the purpose of this review.

**B. Application of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)**

1.  **Diagnosis Requirement (2.1):** The member clearly meets criteria for cervical myelopathy (2.1b) at C4-C5 and cervical radiculopathy (2.1a) at C5-C6, with objective neurological deficits (2.1c) and supporting MRI within 6 months. This criterion is satisfied for C4-C5 and C5-C6.
2.  **Conservative Treatment Requirement (2.2):**
    *   The member completed 9 weeks of PT (exceeding the standard 6 weeks), 3-4 months of two pharmacotherapy agents (exceeding the 4-week minimum), and one interventional procedure.
    *   **Myelopathy Exception (2.2A):** Given the clear evidence of moderate-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and upper motor neuron signs at C4-C5, myelopathy is a primary diagnosis. The plan's exception should apply, reducing PT to 4 weeks (met) and waiving interventional treatment if physician documents risk (not documented, but interventional treatment was pursued anyway). The pharmacotherapy requirement (met) remains. The member's conservative treatment exceeds the requirements of the myelopathy exception.
    *   Therefore, the conservative treatment requirements under the plan's criteria, including the myelopathy exception, have been met for the primary condition.
3.  **Imaging Requirement (2.3) and Pathology Severity (Denial Reason 3.3):**
    *   **C4-C5:** The MRI demonstrates severe cord compression and myelomalacia, fully consistent with myelopathy. This level was implicitly approved by the plan, and is medically necessary.
    *   **C5-C6:** The MRI shows moderate disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression, and foraminal stenosis. This is consistent with the left C6 weakness and diminished reflex on exam and persistent radicular symptoms. The plan's denial based on "insufficient severity" for this level is reviewed against clinical standards.
    *   **C3-C4:** The MRI shows mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing. The neurological exam notes neck pain but no specific radicular pattern at this level. The plan's denial for "insufficient severity" for this level is reviewed against clinical standards.

**C. Application of California Regulatory Requirements (State Law)**

1.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):**
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** The member has myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia at C4-C5. DMHC mandates such cases be treated as urgent due to potential for irreversible harm.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** For conditions risking irreversible harm (like myelopathy with myelomalacia), plans SHALL NOT require full conservative treatment when objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia indicates existing cord injury/progressive risk), treating physician documents conservative failure (PT notes: "Myelopathic symptoms stable but not improved. C5-C6 radicular symptoms persist"), and surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. Myelomalacia specifically supports waiver of standard conservative requirements.
    *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** Plans shall not apply internal criteria more restrictive than generally accepted standards.

2.  **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):**
    *   **(a)(3) Objective evidence of structural neurological compromise:** At C4-C5, severe cord compression with myelomalacia constitutes structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. At C5-C6, moderate nerve root compression with objective C6 weakness also falls under this.
    *   **(a)(2) Functional plateau:** The member completed 9 weeks of PT, and symptoms for both myelopathy and C5-C6 radiculopathy persisted/did not improve. This constitutes reaching a functional plateau, as defined clinically (less than 10% improvement or therapist documented maximum benefit).
    *   This state law overrides any plan requirement for a minimum number of weeks of PT in this specific clinical scenario, as the member has either reached a functional plateau or has objective neurological compromise.

3.  **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard is NOT plan criteria):** This critical provision mandates that if a service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, it SHALL be approved, regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.

**D. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)**

1.  **Myelopathy (C4-C5):** The member has confirmed moderate-severe cervical myelopathy (bilateral UMN signs, gait disturbance, severe cord compression, myelomalacia).
    *   AANS/CNS 2023 (Section 3.1) considers myelomalacia on MRI and moderate-severe myelopathy with objective cord compression as TIER 1 strong surgical indications.
    *   AO Spine 2021 (Section 2.1) explicitly states no high-quality evidence supports mandatory PT before surgery for moderate-severe myelopathy, and delaying surgery may result in deterioration.
    *   The plan's implicit approval of C4-C5 aligns with these strong clinical standards.

2.  **Radiculopathy (C5-C6):** The member has objective C6 nerve root compression, C6 weakness, and persistent radicular symptoms after 9 weeks of PT, 4 months of pharmacotherapy, and an epidural.
    *   AANS/CNS 2023 (Section 3.1) categorizes radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment as a TIER 2 reasonable surgical indication. The member has exceeded the duration for conservative treatment, and symptoms persist.
    *   The plan's denial based on "insufficient severity" for C5-C6 is more restrictive than generally accepted clinical standards when objective neurological deficits and failed conservative treatment are present, especially in the context of adjacent myelopathy.

3.  **C3-C4:** The MRI shows only mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing, and the neurological exam notes neck pain but no specific radicular pattern or myelopathy attributable to this level.
    *   AANS/CNS 2023 (Section 3.1) considers imaging findings without correlating symptoms as a TIER 3 (surgery not indicated) scenario. Surgery for axial neck pain alone without radiculopathy or myelopathy is also not indicated.
    *   For this level, the plan's denial based on "insufficient severity" aligns with generally accepted clinical standards.

**V. CROSS-SOURCE CONFLICT RESOLUTION**

*   **For C4-C5 (Myelopathy):** The plan's implicit approval is consistent with State Law (DMHC APL 22-014, CIC 10169.5) and Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS TIER 1). No conflict.
*   **For C5-C6 (Radiculopathy):** The plan's denial for "insufficient severity" conflicts with both State Law and Clinical Standards.
    *   **State Law:** CIC 10169.5 mandates waiver of PT requirements due to functional plateau and neurological compromise. DMHC APL 22-014 prohibits plans from applying criteria more restrictive than generally accepted standards.
    *   **Clinical Standards:** AANS/CNS considers persistent radiculopathy with neurological deficit after adequate conservative treatment (met here) a reasonable surgical indication. The presence of moderate disc herniation, nerve root compression, and weakness with persistent symptoms is not "insufficient severity" by generally accepted standards.
    *   **Resolution:** State law and clinical standards override the plan's restrictive interpretation. Surgical intervention for C5-C6 is medically necessary.
*   **For C3-C4:** The plan's denial for "insufficient severity" aligns with Clinical Standards, which do not support surgery for mild imaging findings without correlating neurological symptoms. No conflict.

**VII. WARRANT FOR DETERMINATION**

1.  **C4-C5 (Myelopathy) — APPROVED:** The member has clear, objective evidence of moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy at C4-C5, including severe cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI, with corresponding upper motor neuron signs (bilateral hyperreflexia, Hoffman, gait disturbance) and bilateral C5 weakness. Per **AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines (Section 3.1)**, myelomalacia and moderate-to-severe myelopathy are TIER 1 strong surgical indications. **DMHC APL 22-014 (II.1 & II.2)** explicitly identifies such myelopathy with myelomalacia as an urgent condition warranting surgical intervention without requiring a full course of conservative treatment. The plan implicitly approved this level, which is consistent with all applicable standards.

2.  **C5-C6 (Radiculopathy) — APPROVED:** The member demonstrates objective evidence of C6 nerve root compression, moderate disc herniation, left C6 weakness (4-/5), and diminished left bicep reflex. She has completed an extensive course of conservative treatment, including 9 weeks of physical therapy, 4 months of pharmacotherapy, and an epidural, with persistent radicular symptoms ("C5-C6 radicular symptoms persist"). This satisfies the definition of a functional plateau and failed conservative management for radiculopathy (**CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) & (b)** and **Clinical Standard Section 5**). **AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines (Section 3.1)** consider persistent radiculopathy with symptoms after 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment a TIER 2 reasonable surgical indication. The plan's denial based on "insufficient severity" is more restrictive than these generally accepted standards and is overridden by **CHSC § 1374.32(b)** and **DMHC APL 22-014 (II.3)**.

3.  **C3-C4 — UPHELD (DENIED):** The imaging for C3-C4 shows only mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing. The neurological exam notes neck pain but no specific radicular pattern or myelopathic signs attributable to this level. Per **AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines (Section 3.1, TIER 3)**, surgery is not indicated for imaging findings without correlating symptoms or for axial neck pain alone. The plan's denial based on "insufficient severity" for this level is consistent with generally accepted clinical standards.

Therefore, surgical intervention is medically necessary for decompression and fusion at the C4-C5 and C5-C6 levels but not at C3-C4.
