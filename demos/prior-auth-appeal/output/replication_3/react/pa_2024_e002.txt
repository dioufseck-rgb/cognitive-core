**I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

This appeal concerns the denial of a three-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) for Catherine Mills. The initial denial approved surgery at C4-C5 but denied C3-C4 and C5-C6 due to "insufficient severity."

Upon review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California state law (CIC, CHSC, DMHC APL), and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine), the determination is:

*   **OVERTURNED** for levels C4-C5 and C5-C6.
*   **UPHELD** for level C3-C4.

Therefore, the requested three-level ACDF (CPT 22551) is partially overturned. **Authorization for a two-level ACDF at C4-C5 and C5-C6 is hereby approved.**

---

**II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND APPELLANT'S BASIS**

Catherine Mills, a 53-year-old female, presents with multi-level cervical spondylosis with documented myeloradiculopathy.

**Key Clinical Findings:**
*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Multi-level cervical spondylosis with myeloradiculopathy.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-10-05):** Bilateral hyperreflexia, bilateral Hoffman sign, wide-based gait (consistent with myelopathy). Bilateral C5 deltoid weakness (4/5) and left C6 bicep weakness (4-/5) with diminished left bicep reflex (consistent with radiculopathy).
*   **Imaging (MRI, 2024-10-01):**
    *   **C3-C4:** Mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing. No clear clinical correlation.
    *   **C4-C5:** Large disc herniation, severe cord compression, **T2 signal change (myelomalacia)**.
    *   **C5-C6:** Moderate disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression, foraminal stenosis.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   Physical Therapy: 9 weeks (27 sessions). Myelopathic symptoms stable but not improved; C5-C6 radicular symptoms persist.
    *   Pharmacotherapy: Baclofen (3 months, minimal spasticity relief), Pregabalin (4 months, partial pain relief).
    *   Interventional: Epidural (modest improvement in radicular symptoms, no change in myelopathy signs).

**Appellant's Appeal Basis:**
1.  All three levels are symptomatic.
2.  Treating surgeon has documented reasoning for three-level fusion.
3.  Conservative treatment completed for all levels.

**III. REGULATORY AND CLINICAL FRAMEWORK**

Per California law, the hierarchy of authority for appeal review is: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.**

**A. California Regulatory Requirements:**

1.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):**
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** Explicitly states "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent."
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** For urgent conditions (like myelopathy with myelomalacia), plans SHALL NOT require full conservative treatment if objective imaging shows progressive pathology (e.g., myelomalacia), treating physician documents conservative failure, and surgery is indicated by guidelines. Myelomalacia specifically supports waiver of standard conservative requirements.
    *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** Plans shall not apply internal criteria more restrictive than generally accepted standards for conditions identified in this guidance.

2.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Prerequisite):**
    *   (a)(3) A plan cannot condition approval of surgery on PT completion where "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Myelomalacia with cord compression falls squarely under this provision.
    *   (a)(2) A plan cannot condition approval where "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." The PT summary notes "Myelopathic symptoms stable but not improved" and "C5-C6 radicular symptoms persist," indicating a functional plateau for the primary neurological issues.

3.  **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):**
    *   The Independent Medical Review standard (and by extension, internal appeals informed by the same principles) does NOT require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the service meets "generally accepted standards of medical practice," it SHALL be approved, even if plan criteria are more restrictive.

**B. HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**

*   **Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement):** Member has confirmed Cervical Myelopathy (2.1(b)) and Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit (2.1(c)).
*   **Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement):**
    *   Standard pathway requires 6 weeks PT, 2 pharmacotherapy agents (4+ weeks), 1 interventional procedure.
    *   **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY:** PT reduced to 4 weeks minimum. Interventional waived if physician documents risk. This exception applies when myelopathy is the PRIMARY diagnosis. Given the myelopathic signs and myelomalacia, this is the primary diagnosis. The member completed 9 weeks of PT (exceeding both standard and myelopathy-exception minimums), 2 pharmacotherapy agents (exceeding duration), and an interventional procedure. All conservative treatment requirements, even the modified ones, are met.
*   **Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement):** Current MRI demonstrates pathology consistent with symptoms at C4-C5 and C5-C6.

**C. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**

*   **Cervical Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** These are distinct conditions with different treatment algorithms. Applying radiculopathy-derived conservative treatment requirements to myelopathy is not supported by evidence (AANS/CNS Section 1.1).
*   **Myelomalacia Significance:** Myelomalacia (T2 signal change) indicates existing cord injury, higher risk of permanent deficit, reduced recovery, and surgical urgency. It is a strong surgical indication and a contraindication to extended conservative trials (AANS/CNS Section 1.2).
*   **Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy:**
    *   Physical therapy does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement for moderate-severe myelopathy (AANS/CNS Section 2.1).
    *   No high-quality evidence supports mandatory PT duration prior to surgery for moderate-severe myelopathy. Delaying surgery may result in neurological deterioration (AO Spine quote, AANS/CNS Section 2.1, 2.2).
    *   Each month of surgical delay for moderate-severe myelopathy is associated with decreased likelihood of neurological recovery (AANS/CNS Section 2.3).
*   **Surgical Indications for ACDF (AANS/CNS 2023, Section 3.1):**
    *   **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION:** Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI; Myelomalacia on MRI; Progressive neurological deficit despite conservative treatment.
    *   **TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION:** Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative treatment.

**IV. ANALYSIS AND DETERMINATION**

**A. Level C4-C5 (Myelopathy with Myelomalacia):**

The denial implicitly approved surgery at C4-C5, stating "Review of imaging and clinical record supports surgical intervention at one level (C4-C5) only." However, it is crucial to understand the strong justification for this level within the appeals context.

1.  **Clinical Record:** Member has severe cord compression and **myelomalacia** at C4-C5, coupled with clear myelopathic signs (bilateral hyperreflexia, bilateral Hoffman, wide-based gait, bilateral C5 weakness).
2.  **Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):** This level meets HealthFirst's diagnosis and imaging requirements for myelopathy. The conservative treatment requirements, even under the myelopathy exception, were exceeded (9 weeks PT, 2 pharmacotherapy agents for 3-4 months each, 1 epidural). Despite this, myelopathic symptoms did not improve.
3.  **State Law (DMHC APL 22-014, CIC 10169.5):** Due to the presence of myelomalacia and severe cord compression, this case is classified as urgent under DMHC APL 22-014. This APL explicitly states that standard conservative treatment requirements SHALL NOT be required for such conditions. Furthermore, CIC 10169.5(a)(3) overrides PT requirements where there is structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address.
4.  **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023):** The combination of moderate-severe myelopathy, severe cord compression, and myelomalacia constitutes a TIER 1 (strong) surgical indication. Clinical guidelines strongly recommend against delaying surgery for extended conservative trials in such cases due to the risk of irreversible harm and poor neurological recovery.

**Conclusion for C4-C5: Consistent with the initial approval, but crucially supported by multiple overriding authorities.**

**B. Level C5-C6 (Radiculopathy):**

The initial denial stated C5-C6 did not "demonstrate pathology of sufficient severity to warrant surgical intervention at this time."

1.  **Clinical Record:** MRI shows moderate disc herniation, moderate left C6 nerve root compression, and foraminal stenosis. Neurological exam reveals objective C6 level left bicep weakness (4-/5) and diminished left bicep reflex. Conservative treatment (9 weeks PT, 3-4 months pharmacotherapy, epidural with "modest improvement in radicular symptoms") failed to resolve the persistent C5-C6 radicular symptoms.
2.  **Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):** This level meets diagnosis and imaging criteria (2.1(a) Cervical radiculopathy; 2.1(c) Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit; 2.3 Imaging consistent). The conservative treatment, as detailed above, was completed, with symptoms persisting. The denial's assertion of "insufficient severity" is not aligned with the objective clinical findings of nerve root compression and correlating weakness that failed conservative management.
3.  **State Law (CHSC § 1374.32(b); DMHC APL 22-014, II.3):** California law mandates approval if the service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, even if the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive regarding "severity." The plan's interpretation of "sufficient severity" appears to be more restrictive than clinical standards for persistent radiculopathy.
4.  **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023):** Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment is a TIER 2 (reasonable) indication for surgery. This member has completed 9 weeks of PT, prolonged pharmacotherapy, and an epidural, with persistent objective weakness and symptoms. Addressing this level concurrently with the myelopathy at C4-C5 is consistent with accepted multi-level spinal surgery principles.

**Conclusion for C5-C6: OVERTURNED.** The clinical evidence of persistent radiculopathy with objective findings and failure of an adequate course of conservative treatment meets generally accepted standards for surgical intervention.

**C. Level C3-C4:**

The initial denial stated C3-C4 did not "demonstrate pathology of sufficient severity to warrant surgical intervention at this time."

1.  **Clinical Record:** MRI shows only "mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing." The neurological exam noted "C4 level: unclear \u2014 neck pain with C3-C4 palpation but no specific radicular pattern," and the PT summary noted "C3-C4 contribution unclear."
2.  **Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):** Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement) mandates imaging demonstrates pathology consistent with symptoms. Here, the clinical correlation to the mild imaging findings is explicitly unclear.
3.  **Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023):** Surgical indications require objective pathology consistent with clinical symptoms. Without clear radicular or myelopathic symptoms specifically correlating to the mild C3-C4 imaging findings, surgical intervention at this level is not supported by generally accepted standards.

**Conclusion for C3-C4: UPHELD.** The clinical record lacks sufficient objective evidence and clear symptomatic correlation to support surgical intervention at this level.

---

**V. FINAL DISPOSITION**

Based on the comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria, California state law, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial denial for a three-level ACDF is **PARTIALLY OVERTURNED**.

**Authorization is approved for a two-level ACDF (CPT 22551) at levels C4-C5 and C5-C6.**

**Warrant:**
The member's presentation of moderate-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia at C4-C5 represents an urgent condition strongly indicated for surgery by generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023 TIER 1) and warrants an override of standard conservative treatment requirements per California state law (DMHC APL 22-014, CIC 10169.5). The C5-C6 level demonstrates objective radiculopathy (moderate nerve root compression, C6 weakness) which has persisted despite an extensive and adequate course of conservative treatment, meeting generally accepted clinical standards for surgical intervention (AANS/CNS 2023 TIER 2). California law (CHSC 1374.32(b)) requires approval where services meet generally accepted standards, irrespective of more restrictive plan criteria. However, surgical intervention at C3-C4 is not supported by the clinical record, as imaging findings are mild and clinical correlation is explicitly unclear, thus not meeting generally accepted standards for surgical necessity.
