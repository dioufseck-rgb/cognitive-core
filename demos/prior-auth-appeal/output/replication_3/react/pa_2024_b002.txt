**I. BACKGROUND**

Mr. Thomas Reed, a 52-year-old member, requested prior authorization for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 for persistent cervical radiculopathy. The request was initially denied by HealthFirst citing that the member did not meet criteria, specifically that physical therapy (PT) was "not yet completed to functional plateau," despite imaging showing disc herniation and mild cord contact without measurable compression, and no myelopathy findings on exam.

The member's appeal asserts that 7 weeks of PT have been completed with a documented failure to improve, a functional plateau has been reached per the PT discharge summary, and the surgeon believes ACDF is indicated for persistent radiculopathy.

**II. CLINICAL RECORD REVIEW**

1.  **Presenting Diagnosis:** Cervical radiculopathy C6-C7, persistent after conservative treatment.
2.  **Imaging (MRI 2024-08-01):** C6-C7 disc herniation with *mild cord contact but no measurable compression*. *No cord signal change (no myelomalacia)*. Mild right C7 foraminal stenosis.
3.  **Neurological Exam (2024-08-05):** Right C7 dermatomal numbness, diminished right tricep reflex. *No bilateral findings. No upper motor neuron signs*. Pain 7/10 at rest.
4.  **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 7 weeks (21 sessions) completed. The PT discharge summary explicitly states "functional plateau reached – patient has not improved over final 3 sessions. Radicular symptoms persist unchanged."
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Pregabalin 150mg BID for 4 months (partial pain relief, radicular symptoms persist); Meloxicam 15mg for 3 months (inadequate).
    *   **Interventional:** Right C7 epidural steroid injection (2024-09-20) resulted in 30% improvement lasting 3 weeks, then returned to baseline.

**III. APPLICATION OF REVIEW HIERARCHY**

As an expert health plan appeals reviewer, I will apply the three-source hierarchy: **State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria.**

**A. Clinical Presentation Analysis**

Based on the clinical record:
*   The absence of bilateral symptoms, upper motor neuron signs (Hoffman, hyperreflexia), and cord signal change (myelomalacia) confirms that the primary diagnosis is **cervical radiculopathy**, not cervical myelopathy. The denial reviewer correctly identified "No myelopathy findings on exam."
*   Therefore, the myelopathy-specific exceptions in plan criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(A)) and state regulatory guidance (DMHC APL 22-014 Sections II.1, II.2 regarding myelomalacia/cord compression) **do not directly apply** as primary indicators for this case. However, general principles of state law and clinical standards remain pertinent.

**C. Application of California State Law**

*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — PHYSICAL THERAPY AS PREREQUISITE:**
    *   This law explicitly states that a health insurer **shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... (2) The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms.**
    *   Furthermore, **(c) A plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or whether the condition involves neurological urgency...**
    *   **Finding:** The clinical record clearly indicates that Mr. Reed completed a course of physical therapy (7 weeks) and reached a functional plateau with persistent radicular symptoms. The plan's denial, based on the assertion that PT was "not yet completed to functional plateau," directly contravenes CIC § 10169.5. This state law takes precedence over any conflicting plan criteria.
*   **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) — Standard of Review for IMR:**
    *   This code mandates that services meeting "generally accepted standards of medical practice" SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. This provides a secondary layer of review.
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014:** While primarily focused on myelopathy, Section II.3 states: "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice... Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs."

**D. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)**

*   **Cervical Radiculopathy Treatment:** The clinical guidelines recognize ACDF as a reasonable option for cervical radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after an adequate trial of conservative treatment that has failed to provide adequate relief.
*   **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Section 3.1 — TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION:** This tier includes "(b) Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and "(c) Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care."
    *   **Finding:** Mr. Reed has completed 7 weeks of PT (well within 6-12 weeks), 4 months of neuropathic medication, 3 months of NSAIDs, and an epidural, yet continues to experience persistent radiculopathy and significant pain (7/10 at rest). This constitutes an adequate and failed conservative trial, aligning with Tier 2 indications for surgery.
*   **Functional Plateau Definition (Clinical Standard):** The clinical standard defines functional plateau as "<10% improvement... over 3 consecutive therapy sessions, OR Therapist documentation that patient has reached maximum therapeutic benefit."
    *   **Finding:** The PT's documentation ("patient has not improved over final 3 sessions," "functional plateau reached") fully aligns with this clinical definition.

**IV. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND CONCLUSION**

1.  **Misapplication of Plan Criteria:** The plan's initial denial reason ("PT not yet completed to functional plateau") directly contradicts the clinical documentation. The member completed 7 weeks of PT, and the PT discharge summary explicitly states a functional plateau was reached, which aligns with HealthFirst's own definition of functional plateau.
2.  **Violation of State Law:** California Insurance Code § 10169.5 prohibits plans from denying surgery based on a PT requirement when a functional plateau has been reached after a course of therapy. The plan's denial is a direct violation of this state law, which holds superior authority to plan criteria.
3.  **Consistency with Clinical Standard:** The clinical record demonstrates that Mr. Reed meets generally accepted clinical guidelines for surgical intervention for persistent cervical radiculopathy after an adequate and failed conservative treatment course.

Based on the hierarchy of authority (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria), the factual inaccuracies in the denial reason, the misapplication of the plan's own criteria, and the direct violation of California state law, the initial denial is invalid.

**V. DISPOSITION**

**OVERTURN**

**VI. WARRANT**

The prior authorization denial for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C6-C7) for member Thomas Reed (M-667788) is **OVERTURNED** for the following reasons:

1.  **Misapplication of HealthFirst Plan Criteria:** The HealthFirst Plan's denial stated that "PT not yet completed to functional plateau." This is contradicted by the clinical record, which explicitly documents 7 weeks of physical therapy completion with a discharge summary confirming that a "functional plateau reached – patient has not improved over final 3 sessions." This documentation satisfies HealthFirst's own definition of "functional plateau" as per HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a) Note.
2.  **Violation of California State Law (CIC § 10169.5):** California Insurance Code § 10169.5 (a)(2) and (c) prohibit health insurers from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy when the member has completed a course of PT and the treating physician documents that a functional plateau has been reached without adequate resolution of symptoms. Mr. Reed's case directly meets this statutory exception, overriding any plan-specific duration requirements that would ignore a documented functional plateau.
3.  **Consistency with Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:** Mr. Reed's presentation of persistent cervical radiculopathy (pain 7/10, right C7 dermatomal numbness, diminished tricep reflex) after an adequate and failed course of conservative treatment (7 weeks PT to functional plateau, 4 months neuropathic medication, 3 months NSAIDs, and a temporary epidural response) aligns with Tier 2 surgical indications outlined in the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines for Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023), Section 3.1(b) and (c).

The service is medically necessary based on a correct application of HealthFirst's own criteria, compliance with California state law, and consistency with generally accepted standards of medical practice.
