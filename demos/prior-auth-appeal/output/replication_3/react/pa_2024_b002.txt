**I. Case Summary**

Mr. Thomas Reed, a 52-year-old member, was initially denied prior authorization for an ACDF C6-C7 (CPT 22551). The denial, issued on 2024-10-20, cited that the "Member does not meet criteria for ACDF. Imaging shows C6-C7 disc herniation with mild cord contact but no measurable compression. No myelopathy findings on exam. PT not yet completed to functional plateau."

The appeal challenges the denial based on documentation that 7 weeks of physical therapy (PT) were completed, a functional plateau was reached per PT discharge summary, and the surgeon's clinical judgment that ACDF is indicated for persistent radiculopathy.

---

**II. Clinical Record Review**

The clinical record provided for review indicates the following pertinent information:

*   **Presenting Diagnosis:** Cervical radiculopathy C6-C7, persistent after conservative treatment.
*   **Imaging (MRI, 2024-08-01):** C6-C7 disc herniation with mild cord contact but no measurable compression. No cord signal change or myelomalacia. Mild right C7 foraminal stenosis.
*   **Neurological Exam (2024-08-05):** Right C7 dermatomal distribution numbness. Right tricep reflex diminished. No bilateral findings, no upper motor neuron signs. Pain reported as 7/10 at rest.
*   **Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 7 weeks (21 sessions) completed. The PT discharge summary explicitly states "functional plateau reached – patient has not improved over final 3 sessions. Radicular symptoms persist unchanged."
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Pregabalin 150mg BID for 4 months (partial pain relief, radicular symptoms persist); Meloxicam 15mg for 3 months (inadequate response).
    *   **Interventional:** Right C7 epidural steroid injection on 2024-09-20, resulting in 30% improvement lasting 3 weeks, then return to baseline symptoms.

---

**III. Analysis based on Hierarchy of Authority: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria**

**A. Primary Clinical Presentation:**
Based on the neurological exam findings (unilateral dermatomal numbness, diminished reflex, absence of bilateral findings or upper motor neuron signs) and imaging (C7 foraminal stenosis), the member's primary clinical presentation is **Cervical Radiculopathy**. The absence of spinal cord compression, myelopathy findings on exam, or myelomalacia on MRI confirms that cervical myelopathy is not the primary diagnosis, a finding consistent with the initial denial. This distinction is critical as treatment algorithms and conservative treatment requirements differ significantly for radiculopathy versus myelopathy (Domain Knowledge, Clinical Reference Summary Section 1.1).

**B. Review against HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024):**
1.  **2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:**
    *   Criterion 2.1(a) (Cervical radiculopathy) is met. Imaging and clinical exam confirm right C7 radiculopathy.
2.  **2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:**
    *   **2.2(a) Physical therapy:** Requires a minimum of 6 weeks. Member completed 7 weeks (21 sessions).
    *   **2.2(b) Pharmacotherapy:** Requires a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. Member completed 4 months of Pregabalin and 3 months of Meloxicam, meeting this criterion.
    *   **2.2(c) Interventional:** Requires one treatment. Member completed a Right C7 epidural steroid injection, meeting this criterion.
    *   **Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A):** This exception is not applicable as the member's primary diagnosis is radiculopathy, not myelopathy (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 Note).

    The initial denial stated, "PT not yet completed to functional plateau." However, HealthFirst's own "Functional plateau" definition (from Domain Knowledge) states it is reached when there is "<10% improvement over 3 sessions OR therapist documents maximum benefit reached." The clinical record, specifically the PT discharge summary, explicitly states, "functional plateau reached – patient has not improved over final 3 sessions. Radicular symptoms persist unchanged."
    **Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** The plan's criteria for the *duration* and *types* of conservative treatment were met. The initial denial's assertion that PT was "not yet completed to functional plateau" is directly contradicted by the documented clinical record, which aligns with HealthFirst's *own definition* of a functional plateau. Therefore, the plan misapplied its own criteria in the initial denial.

**C. Review against California State Law:**
1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2) — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This law states that a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
    **Conclusion on CIC § 10169.5:** The member completed 7 weeks of physical therapy, and the PT discharge summary explicitly documents that a functional plateau was reached, with persistent radicular symptoms. This directly satisfies the conditions of CIC § 10169.5(a)(2). Therefore, even if HealthFirst's criteria *could* be interpreted to require further PT, state law prohibits such a denial once a functional plateau is documented. State law explicitly supersedes any plan criteria to the contrary (CIC 10169.5(c)).
2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014:** This guidance primarily addresses myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia, requiring urgent treatment and potentially waiving conservative treatment requirements. As the member's diagnosis is radiculopathy without myelopathy or myelomalacia, this specific APL is not directly applicable to this case.

**D. Review against Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
1.  **Conservative Treatment for Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines indicate that conservative treatment is typically effective for radiculopathy, and a trial of 6-12 weeks is standard (Clinical Reference Summary Section 3.1 Tier 2(b)). The member completed 7 weeks of PT, 4 months of pharmacotherapy, and an epidural, demonstrating an adequate trial.
2.  **Functional Plateau:** The clinical standard defines a functional plateau as "<10% improvement in validated functional outcome measure...over 3 consecutive therapy sessions, OR Therapist documentation that patient has reached maximum therapeutic benefit" (Clinical Reference Summary Section 5). The PT discharge summary's statement that the patient "has not improved over final 3 sessions" and that symptoms persist unchanged directly meets this clinical definition.
3.  **Surgical Indication:** The AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) list "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care" as TIER 2 (Reasonable Indication) for surgery (Clinical Reference Summary Section 3.1). The member's ongoing 7/10 pain, numbness, and diminished reflex after a comprehensive conservative trial and documented functional plateau fit this indication.
**Conclusion on Clinical Standard:** The clinical standard supports surgical intervention for persistent radiculopathy after an adequate course of conservative treatment where a functional plateau has been reached. The member's clinical presentation and treatment history align with these generally accepted standards.

**IV. Conflict Resolution and Final Determination**

Applying the three-source hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria):

1.  **Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Record:** The initial denial stated PT was "not yet completed to functional plateau." This directly contradicts the clinical record, which explicitly documents that a functional plateau was reached after 7 weeks of PT, in alignment with HealthFirst's own definition of functional plateau. Therefore, HealthFirst misapplied its own criteria.
2.  **State Law Override:** California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2) explicitly prohibits conditioning surgical approval on further physical therapy when a functional plateau has been reached and documented. The clinical record demonstrates this condition has been met. This state law takes precedence over any plan criteria or interpretation suggesting otherwise.
3.  **Clinical Standard Alignment:** The member's presentation of persistent radiculopathy after an adequate conservative trial (7 weeks PT, 4 months pharmacotherapy, epidural) and documented functional plateau meets generally accepted clinical standards for ACDF (AANS/CNS TIER 2 indication).

Based on the documented evidence, the initial denial was inconsistent with both HealthFirst's own clinical coverage criteria (when correctly interpreted against the clinical record) and, more importantly, with California state law and generally accepted clinical standards.

---

**V. Disposition**

The denial for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C6-C7, one level) for Mr. Thomas Reed is **OVERTURNED**.

**Warrant:**
The denial was based on the premise that physical therapy had not been completed to a functional plateau. However, the provided clinical record explicitly documents that 7 weeks of physical therapy were completed, and the physical therapist's discharge summary clearly states that a "functional plateau reached – patient has not improved over final 3 sessions." This finding directly satisfies HealthFirst's own definition of a functional plateau (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 and Domain Knowledge). Furthermore, California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(2) explicitly prohibits a health plan from denying surgery on the basis of requiring further physical therapy when a member has completed a course of PT and a functional plateau has been documented by the treating physician/therapist. Finally, the member's diagnosis of persistent cervical radiculopathy after 7 weeks of PT, 4 months of pharmacotherapy, and an epidural injection, with documented functional plateau, meets the "reasonable indication" for surgery under generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023, TIER 2 indication). The hierarchy of authority dictates that state law and clinical standards supersede the plan's initial denial rationale.
