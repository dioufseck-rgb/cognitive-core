**DISPOSITION: OVERTURN**

**WARRANT AND JUSTIFICATION:**

Upon comprehensive review of the submitted appeal, the member's clinical record, HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria, applicable California state law, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, the initial prior authorization denial for CPT 22551 is **OVERTURNED**. The requested service is determined to be medically necessary.

The original denial cited non-compliance with conservative treatment requirements (MED-003), specifically the minimum 6 weeks of physical therapy, and determined the myelopathy exception was inapplicable because "cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy" was listed as the primary diagnosis. This determination is flawed based on the following findings:

1.  **Primary Clinical Presentation of Myelopathy Overrides Billing Code:**
    *   The member's clinical presentation unequivocally demonstrates Cervical Spondylotic Myelopathy, not solely radiculopathy. The clinical record documents bilateral hand weakness, coordination problems, positive bilateral Hoffman sign, bilateral hyperreflexia, and impaired tandem gait. These are classic upper motor neuron (UMN) signs and myelopathic symptoms. (Clinical record: "initial_presentation" notes, "neurological_exam" findings; Clinical Standard Knowledge: Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy classification).
    *   Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) on 2024-07-10 confirms C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate cord compression and, critically, T2 signal change within the cord consistent with early myelomalacia. Myelomalacia is a strong indicator of existing spinal cord injury. (Clinical record: "imaging" MRI findings; Clinical Standard Knowledge: Section 1.2 Myelomalacia; DMHC APL 22-014, Section II.1).
    *   The treating physician explicitly noted a "BILLING DISCREPANCY," stating the ICD-10 code M47.812 (spondylosis with radiculopathy) was an "administrative artifact" and that narrative notes consistently describe cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia. For clinical decision-making, the comprehensive clinical documentation and objective findings supersede an administrative billing code.
    *   Therefore, the plan's rationale for not applying its Myelopathy Exception (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2A) due to a billing code discrepancy, rather than the documented clinical reality of myelopathy, is medically unsound.

2.  **State Law Mandates Waiver of Physical Therapy Requirement:**
    *   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1) and (3)**: This state law mandates that a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where:
        *   (1) The treating physician documents PT is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit. In this case, the treating physician halted PT after myelomalacia was confirmed and provided a letter stating further PT is contraindicated. (Clinical record: "conservative_treatment" PT summary).
        *   (3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. The presence of myelomalacia with moderate cord compression on MRI fulfills this criterion. (Clinical record: "imaging" MRI findings).
    *   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, Section II.2**: This regulatory guidance explicitly states that plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (like myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents conservative failure, and surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. Myelomalacia is specifically identified as supporting waiver of standard conservative requirements.

3.  **Clinical Standards Support Surgical Intervention and Waive Extended Conservative Treatment:**
    *   **AANS/CNS (2023) and AO Spine (2021) Guidelines**: These authoritative clinical guidelines classify moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI as a TIER 1 STRONG surgical indication. (Clinical Reference Summary: Section 3.1, TIER 1; Section 3.2).
    *   For moderate-severe myelopathy, physical therapy does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement, and there is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgery. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by evidence and may result in neurological deterioration. (Clinical Reference Summary: Section 2.1, 2.2, 2.3).
    *   The plan's 6-week PT requirement (and even the 4-week myelopathy exception for PT) is derived from radiculopathy evidence and represents a "category error" when applied to myelopathy, particularly with myelomalacia. (Clinical Standard Knowledge; Clinical Reference Summary: Section 1.1, 2.2, 4).
    *   The member's 4 weeks of PT, followed by a physician-documented halt due to myelomalacia and contraindication for further PT, along with trials of pharmacotherapy and an epidural injection with minimal relief of neurological deficits, adequately demonstrates failure of conservative treatment given the underlying myelopathy and myelomalacia. (Clinical record: "conservative_treatment" summary).

4.  **Hierarchy of Authority:**
    *   Per the established hierarchy (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria), both California state law (CIC §10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) explicitly override HealthFirst's internal criteria regarding the physical therapy duration requirement and the rigid application of the "primary diagnosis" for myelopathy when clear clinical evidence dictates otherwise.
    *   The standard of review for medical necessity (CHSC § 1374.32(b)) mandates approval if the service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.

**CONCLUSION:**

Marcus Webb clearly presents with cervical spondylotic myelopathy, confirmed by objective clinical findings, neurological exam, and MRI demonstrating cord compression with myelomalacia. The treating physician appropriately halted conservative treatment, citing myelomalacia and contraindication for further PT. Both California state law and prevailing clinical standards of care strongly support surgical intervention in this scenario and explicitly contraindicate delaying surgery to complete an arbitrary duration of physical therapy. The plan's reliance on a billing code to deny the myelopathy exception, and its failure to apply state-mandated waivers for conservative treatment, are in direct conflict with higher authorities.

Therefore, the requested ACDF C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is medically necessary and the denial is overturned.
