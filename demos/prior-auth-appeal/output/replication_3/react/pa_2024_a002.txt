**OVERVIEW OF DENIAL:**
HealthFirst PPO Gold denied prior authorization for ACDF C6-C7 (CPT 22551) based on reason code MED-003. The stated reason was that the member completed 4 weeks of physical therapy, falling short of the standard pathway's 6-week minimum. The denial also asserted that the myelopathy exception (which reduces PT to 4 weeks) did not apply because the treating physician's most recent note listed 'cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy' (ICD-10 M47.812) as the primary diagnosis, rather than myelopathy.

**MEMBER'S APPEAL BASIS:**
The member's appeal asserts that:
1.  Myelomalacia is documented on MRI, triggering regulatory override regardless of diagnosis labeling.
2.  The treating physician used 'cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy' as a billing code, but clinical notes clearly document myelopathy.
3.  DMHC APL 22-014 mandates waiver of conservative treatment when myelomalacia is present.

**REVIEWER'S ANALYSIS AND APPLICATION OF HIERARCHY (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria):**

1.  **Primary Clinical Presentation:**
    The clinical record unequivocally demonstrates that Mr. Webb's primary condition is Cervical Spondylotic Myelopathy (CSM), not solely radiculopathy. Key findings supporting this include:
    *   **Neurological Exam:** Bilateral grip weakness (3/5), positive Hoffman sign bilateral, bilateral hyperreflexia, and impaired tandem gait. These are classic upper motor neuron (UMN) signs and symptoms of myelopathy (Clinical Record: Neurological Exam; Clinical Standard: Section 1.1).
    *   **Imaging (MRI):** C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate cord compression and "T2 signal change within cord consistent with early myelomalacia." Myelomalacia signifies existing spinal cord injury and is a strong indicator of myelopathy (Clinical Record: Imaging; Clinical Standard: Section 1.2).
    *   **Treating Physician's Documentation:** Despite an ICD-10 billing code for radiculopathy (M47.812) on referrals, the treating physician's narrative notes consistently describe cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia. The physician explicitly states the billing code is an "administrative artifact" and not reflective of the clinical diagnosis (Clinical Record: Presenting Diagnosis, Initial Presentation, Diagnosis Note).
    *   **Conclusion:** The clinical presentation aligns with HealthFirst's own definition of Cervical Myelopathy (Plan Criteria: Section 2.1(b)).

2.  **Application of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
    *   **Diagnosis Requirement (2.1):** Mr. Webb meets criterion 2.1(b) for Cervical Myelopathy based on the clinical findings detailed above.
    *   **Conservative Treatment Requirement (2.2):**
        *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Mr. Webb completed a trial of Gabapentin (3 months) and Naproxen (2 months), satisfying criterion 2.2(b).
        *   **Interventional Treatment:** Mr. Webb underwent a cervical epidural injection, satisfying criterion 2.2(c).
        *   **Physical Therapy (PT):** Mr. Webb completed 4 weeks of physical therapy.
    *   **Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A):** The plan's denial incorrectly determined that Mr. Webb did not qualify for the myelopathy exception.
        *   The exception reduces the PT requirement to 4 weeks minimum and waives the interventional requirement if risk is documented.
        *   The plan denied the exception based on the "primary diagnosis" being listed as radiculopathy via a billing code. This is a misapplication of the plan's own criteria, as the *clinical record* clearly establishes myelopathy as the primary condition. The "NOTE" in 2.2(A) regarding "primary diagnosis" should refer to the clinical presentation and physician's assessment, not merely a billing code.
        *   Had the myelopathy exception been correctly applied based on clinical evidence, Mr. Webb would have met the 4-week PT requirement.

3.  **Application of California Regulatory Requirements:**
    California state law provides explicit overrides to the plan's denial reasoning:
    *   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This statute prohibits conditioning surgery on completion of physical therapy where:
        *   (a)(1) The treating physician has documented PT as contraindicated or unlikely to benefit. In this case, the treating physician halted PT after myelomalacia was confirmed and documented that further PT was contraindicated (Clinical Record: Conservative Treatment).
        *   (a)(3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. Mr. Webb's MRI showing cord compression with myelomalacia (cord signal change) constitutes objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (Clinical Record: Imaging; CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)).
        *   Therefore, per CIC § 10169.5, HealthFirst cannot require Mr. Webb to complete additional physical therapy.
    *   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This All-Plan Letter directly addresses Mr. Webb's situation:
        *   **II.1:** Identifies cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI as a condition with potential for irreversible harm, requiring urgent treatment. Mr. Webb meets this classification.
        *   **II.2:** Mandates that plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when (a) objective imaging shows progressive pathology (myelomalacia), (b) the physician documents conservative failure, and (c) surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines. All three conditions are met in Mr. Webb's case (Clinical Record: Imaging, Conservative Treatment; Clinical Standard: Section 3). The APL specifically identifies myelomalacia as a finding supporting waiver of standard conservative treatment.
        *   **II.3:** States that plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. The plan's rigid adherence to a billing code and its requirement for further PT are more restrictive than generally accepted standards for myelopathy with myelomalacia.
    *   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) (IMR Standard):** This statute clarifies that the IMR standard is based on "generally accepted standards of medical practice" and that services meeting this standard SHALL be approved regardless of the plan's more restrictive internal criteria.

4.  **Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
    *   **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines emphasize the critical distinction between these conditions, stating that myelopathy and radiculopathy require fundamentally different treatment algorithms (Clinical Standard: Section 1.1).
    *   **Significance of Myelomalacia:** Myelomalacia is a strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials due to the high risk of permanent deficit if surgery is delayed (Clinical Standard: Section 1.2).
    *   **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** For moderate-severe myelopathy, physical therapy does NOT produce meaningful functional improvement. There is "no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy." Delaying surgery in such patients is not supported by evidence and may result in neurological deterioration (Clinical Standard: Section 2.1, 2.2, 2.3).
    *   **Surgical Indication:** Mr. Webb's presentation (moderate-severe myelopathy, objective cord compression, myelomalacia, and objective neurological deficits) meets TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION for surgery per AANS/CNS guidelines (Clinical Standard: Section 3.1, 3.2).

5.  **Conflict Resolution:**
    Applying the hierarchy (State law > Clinical standard > Plan criteria), the plan's denial is directly contradicted:
    *   The plan's insistence on a 6-week PT course (standard pathway) or its rigid interpretation of the "primary diagnosis" for the myelopathy exception is overridden by both California law (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) and established clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine).
    *   State law (CIC § 10169.5) explicitly permits waiver of PT requirements when further PT is contraindicated or objective neurological compromise (like myelomalacia) is present.
    *   DMHC APL 22-014 mandates waiver of conservative treatment when myelomalacia is present and classifies Mr. Webb's condition as urgent. It further states that plan criteria cannot be more restrictive than generally accepted standards.
    *   Clinical guidelines unanimously support early surgical intervention for moderate-severe myelopathy with myelomalacia and do not support mandatory PT as a prerequisite.

**DISPOSITION:**
**OVERTURN**

**WARRANT AND RATIONALE:**
The prior authorization denial for Marcus Webb's ACDF C6-C7 is **OVERTURNED**. The denial was based on an incorrect application of the plan's own criteria, which was then directly superseded by both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.

1.  **Misapplication of Plan Criteria:** The plan's initial denial incorrectly classified Mr. Webb's condition as primarily radiculopathy due to a billing code (M47.812), thereby refusing to apply its own Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A). The extensive clinical documentation, including bilateral UMN signs, gait impairment, cord compression, and specifically, myelomalacia on MRI, unequivocally establishes a diagnosis of cervical myelopathy. The treating physician explicitly clarified the billing code as an "administrative artifact," rendering the plan's reliance on it for diagnostic classification medically unsound (Clinical Record: Presenting Diagnosis, Diagnosis Note; Plan Criteria: Section 2.2(A) Note).
2.  **Override by California State Law:**
    *   **CIC § 10169.5:** This statute prohibits a health plan from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy when the treating physician documents that PT is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit (which was done here), or when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. Myelomalacia on MRI, indicating existing cord damage, falls directly under the latter provision (Clinical Record: Conservative Treatment, Imaging; CIC § 10169.5(a)(1), (a)(3)).
    *   **DMHC APL 22-014:** This All-Plan Letter specifically identifies cervical myelopathy with cord compression and myelomalacia as an urgent condition requiring waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements where physician documentation supports it. Mr. Webb's case perfectly aligns with this guidance, compelling the plan to waive the full conservative treatment period (Clinical Record: Imaging, Conservative Treatment; DMHC APL 22-014: II.1, II.2).
3.  **Override by Clinical Standard:** Generally accepted standards of medical practice from authoritative bodies (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) strongly recommend surgical intervention for moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy, particularly when myelomalacia is present. These guidelines explicitly state that there is no evidence to support a mandatory physical therapy trial for this condition and that delaying surgery in the presence of myelomalacia can lead to irreversible neurological deterioration (Clinical Standard: Section 1.2, 2.1, 2.3, 3.2). The plan's requirement for additional physical therapy, even if through its myelopathy exception, is inconsistent with these standards.
4.  **Hierarchy of Authority:** Per the established hierarchy, California State Law and Clinical Standards supersede the plan's internal criteria when they are more restrictive. Both state law and clinical guidelines support the immediate approval of surgery in this case, overriding the plan's denial (DMHC APL 22-014: II.3; CHSC § 1374.32(b)).

Based on the comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's own criteria, California state law, and generally accepted clinical guidelines, Marcus Webb's request for ACDF C6-C7 is medically necessary and should be approved without further delay.
