**APPEAL DETERMINATION LETTER**

**Case ID:** PA-2024-G003
**Member:** Bernard Achebe
**Plan:** HealthFirst PPO Gold — Group Plan 77441
**Procedure:** ACDF C5-C6, one level (CPT 22551)
**Date of Initial Denial:** 2024-11-05

**Disposition:** UPHOLD DENIAL

**Warrant:**

The denial for prior authorization of CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) for Mr. Bernard Achebe is **UPHELD**. While the initial denial notification from HealthFirst was procedurally deficient by not explicitly addressing the plan's specific myelopathy exception, the objective clinical evidence does not support overturning the denial for lack of completed conservative treatment at this time, even when applying the correct plan criteria and considering relevant state laws and clinical standards.

**Analysis:**

1.  **Member's Clinical Presentation:**
    *   Mr. Achebe, 61, presents with a diagnosis of **mild cervical myelopathy C5-C6** (mJOA score 15/18).
    *   Objective neurological findings include bilateral Hoffman signs and mild hyperreflexia. However, the patient's hand grip strength is 4+/5 bilateral (no frank weakness), tandem gait is intact, and Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) are fully independent.
    *   Symptoms have been **stable for 6 months**.
    *   MRI dated 2024-10-01 shows moderate central cord compression at C5-C6, but **importantly, explicitly notes "No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia."**
    *   Conservative treatment thus far includes 3 weeks of physical therapy (9 sessions) and a 6-week trial of one pharmacotherapy agent (Baclofen), which provided mild spasticity improvement. No interventional procedures have been attempted. Dr. Osei halted PT citing "myelopathy concern."
    *   The treating neurologist, Dr. Kwame Osei, declares "surgical urgency" and "contraindication to further delay," stating the condition is "severe and progressive" with risk of "irreversible neurological deterioration."

2.  **Review of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
    *   **2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** Confirmed cervical myelopathy with MRI showing structural pathology is met.
    *   **2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement (Standard Pathway):** The initial denial correctly identified that the member has not met the standard pathway requirements of 6 weeks PT, 2 pharmacotherapy agents for 4+ weeks each, and 1 interventional procedure.
    *   **2.2(A) Myelopathy Exception:** This exception modifies the conservative treatment requirements for cervical myelopathy:
        *   Physical therapy is reduced to a **4-week minimum** (instead of 6). Mr. Achebe completed 3 weeks.
        *   Pharmacotherapy remains at **two agents** for a minimum of 4 weeks each. Mr. Achebe completed a trial of only one agent.
        *   Interventional treatment is waived if the physician documents risk of neurological deterioration. Dr. Osei's letter could satisfy this condition, potentially waiving the interventional requirement.
    *   **Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** Even applying the plan's less restrictive Myelopathy Exception, Mr. Achebe has not completed the required 4 weeks of physical therapy or a trial of two pharmacotherapy agents. Therefore, the procedure would still be denied under HealthFirst's internal criteria.

3.  **Review of California Regulatory Framework:**
    *   **CHSC § 1374.31(b) (Denial Notice Adequacy):** The initial denial notice is procedurally deficient as it failed to explicitly reference and apply the plan's specific "Myelopathy Exception (Section 2.2A)" for Mr. Achebe's primary diagnosis. While this constitutes a defect, it does not change the outcome of the appeal in this specific instance, as the denial would still stand even with the correct criteria applied.
    *   **DMHC APL 22-014 (Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions):** This guidance states plans shall not require full conservative treatment when *objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia, or progressive herniation on serial imaging)* AND the treating physician documents conservative failure AND surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines.
        *   Crucially, Mr. Achebe's MRI explicitly states **"No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia."** The APL specifically identifies myelomalacia as a key factor supporting waiver of conservative treatment due to urgency.
        *   Despite Dr. Osei's declaration of "progressive" deterioration, the clinical record objectively states "symptom duration: 6 months, stable," directly contradicting the claim of progression.
        *   Therefore, the objective criteria for overriding conservative treatment based on urgency under APL 22-014 are not met in this case.
    *   **CIC § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):** This code prohibits conditioning surgery approval on PT completion where: (1) PT is contraindicated/unlikely to benefit, OR (2) member reached a functional plateau, OR (3) condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address.
        *   For *mild, stable myelopathy without myelomalacia*, it cannot be objectively concluded that PT is entirely "unlikely to benefit" or that structural compromise cannot be addressed by *any* PT (especially given the plan's modified, reduced PT requirement). The objective findings do not support the level of urgency or futility that would completely waive conservative treatment under this statute for this patient.

4.  **Review of Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
    *   **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** The distinction is recognized, and applying radiculopathy-derived PT requirements to myelopathy is not supported. However, HealthFirst's plan *does* have a myelopathy exception.
    *   **Myelomalacia:** The clinical guidelines consistently identify myelomalacia (T2 signal change) as a strong indicator for surgical urgency and a contraindication to extended conservative trials due to existing cord injury and risk of permanent deficit. The **absence of myelomalacia** in Mr. Achebe's MRI significantly reduces the clinical urgency for immediate surgery.
    *   **Conservative Treatment for Mild Myelopathy:** For *mild cervical myelopathy* (mJOA score > 12), clinical guidelines state that "conservative treatment may be considered as initial management, with close monitoring for progression" (NASS 2020). Surgery for mild myelopathy with stable symptoms is considered a TIER 2 ("reasonable option") *after an adequate conservative trial* (AANS/CNS 2023).
    *   The guidelines challenge mandatory PT for *moderate to severe* myelopathy but do not universally waive the concept of an "adequate conservative trial" for *mild, stable* myelopathy without features indicating acute progression or significant cord damage (e.g., myelomalacia). Mr. Achebe's completion of 3 weeks of PT and one pharmacotherapy agent does not constitute an "adequate conservative trial" by generally accepted standards for a mild, stable condition.
    *   The treating neurologist's declaration of "severe and progressive" and "immediate surgical intervention" is not objectively supported by the clinical findings of mild, stable myelopathy and the absence of myelomalacia or other signs of acute deterioration.

**Conclusion:**

Based on the objective clinical evidence, Mr. Achebe presents with mild cervical myelopathy that has been stable for 6 months, and without signs of myelomalacia or objective progression. While the initial denial was procedurally flawed by not specifically invoking the plan's myelopathy exception, applying this exception still finds the member has not completed the required conservative treatment (4 weeks PT and 2 pharmacotherapy agents).

The objective findings do not meet the criteria under California state law (DMHC APL 22-014, CIC 10169.5) or generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) that would necessitate a complete waiver of conservative treatment for immediate surgical intervention. For mild, stable myelopathy without myelomalacia or acute progression, an "adequate conservative trial" remains an appropriate initial management strategy. Mr. Achebe has not completed this.

Therefore, the denial is **UPHELD**. HealthFirst is strongly advised to provide a revised denial letter to the member that explicitly addresses the plan's specific myelopathy exception (Section 2.2A of CC-SPINE-2024) and clearly outlines the remaining conservative treatment requirements for the member to meet for future authorization consideration.
