### **Appeal Determination: OVERTURN**

---

### **Warrant for Determination**

The denial for revision ACDF C5-C6 for Mr. George Harris is hereby **OVERTURNED** based on a misapplication of HealthFirst's own clinical criteria, and because California state law mandates approval under the documented clinical circumstances, overriding any conflicting plan criteria.

1.  **Misapplication of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024):**
    The initial denial explicitly stated, "Revision criteria require documentation of hardware failure, pseudarthrosis, or new pathology at previously treated level. Documentation submitted does not confirm hardware failure." However, the submitted clinical record, specifically the CT scan dated 2024-09-15, clearly states findings of "incomplete bridging bone formation. Lucency around hardware suggesting pseudarthrosis. Findings consistent with failed fusion/pseudarthrosis." The plan's initial denial focused solely on the absence of "hardware failure" while completely disregarding the *confirmed presence of "pseudarthrosis"*, which is listed as an equally valid criterion for revision surgery in its own denial language. This constitutes a direct misapplication and misinterpretation of the plan's stated clinical criteria for revision surgery. Per CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is defective; in this instance, the denial selectively interpreted the criteria and ignored documented evidence directly meeting another stated criterion.

2.  **State Law Overrides Conservative Treatment Requirements (CIC § 10169.5):**
    Even if the plan intended to deny based on insufficient conservative treatment (though not explicitly stated in the denial reason, it is often an implicit requirement), California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 directly addresses physical therapy as a prerequisite for surgical approval. This statute provides clear exceptions that are met in Mr. Harris's case:
    *   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1):** "The member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition." The treating physician's note after 8 weeks of PT states, "No improvement — treating physician notes PT cannot address structural hardware/fusion failure." This directly meets this exception.
    *   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2):** "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms." Mr. Harris completed 8 weeks of PT, and the physician documented "No improvement," indicating a functional plateau. This aligns with clinical standards defining a functional plateau (Section 5, Clinical Reference Summary).
    *   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3):** "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The CT scan confirms "pseudarthrosis" at C5-C6, which is an objective structural failure of the prior fusion causing C6 radiculopathy (bicep weakness 4/5 right, diminished bicep reflex, C6 dermatomal numbness). This is a structural neurological compromise that PT cannot resolve.
    Therefore, per CIC § 10169.5(c), HealthFirst's internal 6-week PT requirement cannot be applied mechanically, and the conservative treatment criteria are satisfied under state law.

3.  **Adherence to Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:**
    The clinical record demonstrates that Mr. Harris has symptomatic pseudarthrosis at C5-C6 following a prior ACDF, with objective neurological deficits (right C6 radiculopathy worse than preoperative baseline) that have not responded to a documented trial of conservative treatment. This presentation meets generally accepted clinical standards for surgical intervention.
    *   The AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) categorize radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after an adequate conservative trial and significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care as a "TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION" for surgery (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 3.1).
    *   Mr. Harris completed 8 weeks of physical therapy, 4 months of Naproxen, 3 months of Gabapentin, and an epidural steroid injection, all with minimal to no improvement in neurological symptoms, and with physician documentation explaining the inefficacy due to structural failure. This constitutes an adequate and failed conservative trial for the underlying structural issue.
    Per CHSC § 1374.32(b), if the service meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, it SHALL be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.

### **Conclusion**

The initial denial by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, was procedurally flawed by misinterpreting the submitted diagnostic evidence against the plan's own stated criteria. Furthermore, California state law (CIC § 10169.5) explicitly overrides any internal plan requirements for physical therapy when the conditions described in Mr. Harris's clinical record are met. The requested revision ACDF is medically necessary, clinically appropriate, and meets generally accepted standards of medical practice for symptomatic pseudarthrosis with persistent radiculopathy after failed conservative management.

Therefore, the prior authorization for CPT 22551 (Revision ACDF C5-C6) is **OVERTURNED**, and the procedure is approved.
