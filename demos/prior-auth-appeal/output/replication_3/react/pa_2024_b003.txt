**I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

This appeal concerns the prior authorization denial for a C3-C4 Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) for member Angela Morrison. The initial denial (2024-10-01) cited insufficient pathology on MRI at C3-C4 to support surgical intervention (Reason Code MED-003).

Upon comprehensive review of the clinical record, appeal basis, HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory framework, and generally accepted clinical standards, it is determined that the denial should be **OVERTURNED**. The primary reason for this determination is that the initial denial was based on incomplete imaging information, which has been clarified by a subsequent CT myelogram demonstrating clear surgical pathology. The member also meets all other applicable plan criteria and generally accepted clinical standards for surgical intervention.

---

**II. CASE BACKGROUND**

Ms. Angela Morrison, a 67-year-old member, presented with adjacent segment disease at C3-C4 following a prior C4-C5 ACDF in 2019. Her current symptoms include new onset (since 2023) left C4 dermatomal numbness, deltoid weakness (3+/5 left), and diminished bicep reflex on the left.

The initial prior authorization request was denied, citing that while conservative treatment requirements were met, the MRI showed only "mild degenerative changes" and "insufficient pathology at C3-C4 to support surgical intervention."

The appeal basis highlights:
1.  Completion of conservative treatment (acknowledged by the initial denial).
2.  Surgeon's clinical assessment of C3-C4 as the symptomatic level.
3.  Confirmation of compression via CT myelogram not visible on MRI due to hardware artifact.

---

**III. CLINICAL RECORD REVIEW AND CRITERIA APPLICATION**

**A. Clinical Presentation (Radiculopathy vs. Myelopathy):**
*   **Clinical Record Findings:** Neurological exam (2024-08-20) details "Left C4 dermatomal distribution: shoulder and lateral arm numbness, deltoid weakness 3+/5 left, bicep reflex diminished left."
*   **Analysis:** These symptoms are classic indicators of **cervical radiculopathy** (nerve root compression), characterized by unilateral dermatomal distribution, pain, sensory change, and weakness without upper motor neuron signs (Hoffman, hyperreflexia, gait disturbance). The clinical record does not provide evidence of myelopathy.
*   **Source:** Domain Knowledge, Clinical Reference Summary (Section 1.1).

**B. HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) Review:**

1.  **2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **Criterion:** "Member must have a confirmed diagnosis of... (a) Cervical radiculopathy — nerve root compression with radicular symptoms (pain, numbness, weakness in dermatomal distribution) OR (c) Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit."
    *   **Clinical Record:** Neurological exam confirms left C4 radiculopathy. The CT myelogram identifies "moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression confirmed. Significant foraminal stenosis."
    *   **Finding:** **MET.** The member has objective evidence of cervical radiculopathy correlating to symptoms and imaging.

2.  **2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **Criterion:** Requires 6 weeks PT, 2 pharmacotherapy agents (4+ weeks each), and 1 interventional treatment. (Myelopathy exception 2.2(A) is not applicable as the primary diagnosis is radiculopathy).
    *   **Clinical Record:**
        *   **Physical Therapy:** 10 weeks (30 sessions), with "No improvement in left C4 radicular symptoms." (Exceeds 6-week minimum).
        *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Pregabalin 300mg BID for 6 months (partial relief) and Duloxetine 60mg for 3 months (minimal benefit). (Meets requirement for 2 agents, 4+ weeks each).
        *   **Interventional:** Left C4 Epidural (2024-09-25) provided good initial relief (60%), returning to baseline by week 3. (Meets requirement for 1 interventional treatment).
    *   **Finding:** **MET.** The member has completed a full and documented course of conservative treatment for radiculopathy with persistent symptoms. The initial denial acknowledged this.

3.  **2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:**
    *   **Criterion:** "Current imaging (within 6 months) must demonstrate structural pathology consistent with the member's symptoms and proposed surgical level."
    *   **Clinical Record:**
        *   MRI (2024-08-15): "SIGNIFICANT ARTIFACT from C4-C5 hardware limiting evaluation. Mild degenerative signal changes visible. Cannot exclude moderate compression due to artifact." This was the basis for the initial denial.
        *   CT myelogram (2024-09-10): "performed due to MRI artifact limitation. C3-C4: moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression confirmed. Significant foraminal stenosis. MRI underestimated pathology due to hardware artifact from prior fusion."
    *   **Finding:** **MET.** The CT myelogram, performed specifically to overcome the limitations of the MRI, clearly demonstrates "moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression" and "significant foraminal stenosis" at the proposed surgical level, which is entirely consistent with the member's C4 radicular symptoms. The initial denial's assessment of imaging was incomplete and incorrect due to reliance solely on the limited MRI.

4.  **2.4 PHYSICIAN DOCUMENTATION:**
    *   **Criterion:** Requires physician attestation of conservative treatment completion, clinical rationale for surgery, description of functional limitations, and surgical risks/alternatives.
    *   **Clinical Record:** The appeal basis and clinical notes provided attest to conservative treatment completion, surgeon's rationale for the symptomatic level, and functional deficits.
    *   **Finding:** **MET.**

**IV. REGULATORY AND CLINICAL STANDARD REVIEW**

**A. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:**
*   **Relevant Provision:** This code states that a plan shall not condition approval of surgery on PT completion where: (a)(2) "The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms;" OR (a)(3) "The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
*   **Analysis:** The member completed 10 weeks of PT with "No improvement in left C4 radicular symptoms." This clearly demonstrates reaching a "functional plateau" per CIC § 10169.5(a)(2). Additionally, the CT myelogram confirming "moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression" represents "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise" that physical therapy cannot resolve, as per CIC § 10169.5(a)(3).
*   **Source:** CIC § 10169.5.

**B. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) — IMR Standard:**
*   **Relevant Provision:** "The IMR standard does NOT require that the service be consistent with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the service meets the standard in subdivision (a), the reviewer SHALL recommend approval regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive." This emphasizes that services meeting "generally accepted standards of medical practice" should be approved.
*   **Analysis:** As demonstrated below, the requested surgery meets generally accepted standards.

**C. Clinical Standard Knowledge (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021):**
*   **Diagnostic Classification:** The member's presentation of unilateral C4 dermatomal weakness, numbness, and diminished reflex, combined with objective imaging of nerve root compression, clearly defines **cervical radiculopathy**.
*   **Conservative Treatment for Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines (NASS 2020) indicate conservative treatment is typically effective for radiculopathy, with a trial period of 6-12 weeks often recommended before considering surgery.
*   **Surgical Indications for ACDF:** Per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), ACDF for radiculopathy falls under **TIER 2 — REASONABLE INDICATION (surgery reasonable option)**, specifically for: (b) "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx" and (c) "Significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care."
*   **Analysis:** The member has undergone 10 weeks of PT with no improvement, 3-6 months of pharmacotherapy with partial/minimal benefit, and an epidural injection providing only temporary relief. These persistent symptoms and functional limitations (3+/5 weakness) after an adequate conservative trial, coupled with objective imaging of nerve root compression, meet the generally accepted clinical indications for surgery.
*   **Source:** Clinical Reference Summary (Sections 1.1, 2.1, 3.1, 4).

**D. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 — Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions:**
*   **Relevant Provision:** While this APL primarily focuses on myelopathy, it generally states that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment... when: (b) Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression, AND (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines for the condition."
*   **Analysis:** Although the member has radiculopathy (not myelopathy), the principle that failed conservative treatment (as documented by "No improvement in left C4 radicular symptoms" and temporary relief from other modalities) warrants surgical consideration under generally accepted guidelines is applicable.
*   **Source:** DMHC APL 22-014 (Section II.2).

**V. CONFLICT RESOLUTION (State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria)**

The initial denial was based on a perceived lack of sufficient pathology on imaging. However, the subsequent CT myelogram directly refutes this, providing clear evidence of "moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression" and "significant foraminal stenosis" at the symptomatic level. This updated imaging directly addresses and satisfies HealthFirst's Imaging Requirement (CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.3).

All other HealthFirst criteria for cervical spine surgery (Diagnosis 2.1, Conservative Treatment 2.2, Physician Documentation 2.4) are also met.

Furthermore, the requested procedure is well within generally accepted standards of medical practice for cervical radiculopathy with persistent symptoms and objective nerve root compression after a failed trial of conservative treatment, as outlined in the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines. State law (CIC § 10169.5) explicitly permits surgical approval when a functional plateau has been reached or objective structural compromise exists, which is the case here.

Therefore, there is no conflict between the plan's criteria (when properly applied with complete information), state law, or clinical standards. The initial denial was based on an incomplete assessment of the imaging.

**VI. DISPOSITION**

The prior authorization denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C3-C4) for member Angela Morrison (M-778899) is **OVERTURNED**.

**WARRANT:**
The initial denial for lack of sufficient pathology on imaging is demonstrably false given the findings of the CT myelogram dated 2024-09-10. This CT myelogram, performed specifically to overcome artifact limitations of the prior MRI, confirms "moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression" and "significant foraminal stenosis" at C3-C4, which correlates precisely with the member's documented left C4 radicular symptoms (numbness, 3+/5 weakness, diminished reflex). This imaging fully satisfies HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement).

Additionally, the member has met all other HealthFirst criteria, including diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (Section 2.1) and completion of a comprehensive conservative treatment course for 10 weeks of physical therapy (with documented "no improvement"), 3-6 months of pharmacotherapy (with partial/minimal benefit), and an interventional epidural injection (with only temporary relief) (Section 2.2). This constitutes a failed conservative trial with persistent, functionally limiting symptoms.

Surgical intervention for persistent cervical radiculopathy with objective nerve root compression after failed conservative management is a generally accepted standard of medical practice (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023, TIER 2). Moreover, California law (CIC § 10169.5) explicitly prohibits denial based on physical therapy requirements when a functional plateau has been reached or objective structural neurological compromise exists, both of which are present in this case.

Based on the complete clinical record, adherence to HealthFirst's own criteria, and alignment with California regulatory requirements and generally accepted clinical standards, the requested ACDF at C3-C4 is medically necessary.
