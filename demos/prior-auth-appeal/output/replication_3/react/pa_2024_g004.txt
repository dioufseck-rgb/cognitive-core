**I. INTRODUCTION**
This letter serves as the appeal determination for the prior authorization denial of CPT code 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) for member Stephanie Oduya. The initial request was denied on October 15, 2024, by HealthFirst Independent Reviewer Dr. Maria Rivera, MD, citing non-completion of the conservative treatment pathway and absence of medical necessity criteria for surgical intervention at this time. An appeal was submitted by Dr. Patrick Walsh, the treating surgeon, on October 18, 2024. This review applies HealthFirst's internal clinical coverage criteria, relevant California state regulations, and generally accepted clinical standards, prioritizing sources according to the hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria.

**II. CLINICAL SUMMARY**
Ms. Stephanie Oduya, a 44-year-old female, presents with a diagnosis of right-sided cervical radiculopathy at C5-C6. Her MRI, dated 2024-09-10, shows a mild to moderate disc herniation at C5-C6 with right C6 nerve root contact, mild right foraminal narrowing, but explicitly no measurable nerve root compression, no cord contact, no cord compression, no T2 signal change, and no myelomalacia. Her neurological examination on 2024-09-15 documented right shoulder and neck pain (6/10) and mild right C6 dermatomal tingling, with no objective weakness (grip strength 5/5 bilateral), no upper motor neuron signs, and symmetric bicep reflexes (2+). Functionally, she is working full-time with no reported activity limitations.

Conservative treatment to date includes:
*   **Physical Therapy (PT):** 4 weeks, 12 sessions, with "mild improvement in pain" but no neurological deficits to track. Dr. Walsh elected to halt PT.
*   **Pharmacotherapy:** Naproxen 500mg BID for 8 weeks, with "moderate pain relief."
*   **Interventional Procedures:** None reported.

**III. DENIAL RATIONALE (INITIAL REVIEW)**
The initial denial, issued by Dr. Maria Rivera, MD (HealthFirst Independent Reviewer, Board-certified neurosurgeon), stated that imaging showed mild-moderate disc herniation with no cord compression and no neurological deficit on examination, and that the conservative treatment pathway had not been completed. Therefore, the request did not meet medical necessity criteria for surgical intervention at this time.

**IV. APPEAL BASIS**
Dr. Walsh's appeal disputes Dr. Rivera's interpretation, emphasizes his 25 years of spine surgery experience and direct patient examination, asserts that the patient requires surgery now to prevent permanent disability, and states that his clinical judgment as the treating surgeon should take precedence.

**V. REVIEW METHODOLOGY & FINDINGS**

**A. Primary Clinical Presentation: Radiculopathy vs. Myelopathy**
*   **Clinical Record:** The MRI explicitly states "No cord contact. No cord compression. No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia." The neurological exam shows "No weakness. No UMN signs." Symptoms are unilateral C6 dermatomal tingling.
*   **Domain Knowledge & Clinical Standard (Section 1.1, 1.2):** Myelopathy requires spinal cord compression, upper motor neuron signs, bilateral symptoms, and/or gait disturbance, often with myelomalacia. Radiculopathy involves nerve root compression with unilateral dermatomal symptoms and no/minimal UMN signs.
*   **Finding:** Based on the objective clinical findings, the patient's primary diagnosis is **cervical radiculopathy**, not cervical myelopathy. This distinction is critical as treatment algorithms and conservative treatment requirements differ significantly for these two conditions.

**B. Application of HealthFirst Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)**
1.  **2.1 Diagnosis Requirement:** The patient has cervical radiculopathy with nerve root contact and correlating symptoms. This broadly meets criteria 2.1(a).
2.  **2.2 Conservative Treatment Requirement:**
    *   **2.2(a) Physical Therapy:** Plan requires a minimum of 6 weeks. The patient completed 4 weeks. **(NOT MET)**
    *   **2.2(b) Pharmacotherapy:** Plan requires at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The patient utilized only one agent (Naproxen) for 8 weeks. **(NOT MET)**
    *   **2.2(c) Interventional Treatment:** Plan requires one interventional procedure. None were performed. **(NOT MET)**
3.  **2.2(A) Myelopathy Exception:** This exception modifies conservative treatment requirements for *confirmed cervical myelopathy*. As determined above, the patient does not have myelopathy. **(NOT APPLICABLE)**
*   **Finding:** The patient did not meet the conservative treatment requirements (Section 2.2) of the HealthFirst plan criteria.

**C. Application of California Regulatory Framework**
1.  **CHSC § 1374.31(b) — Denial Notice Adequacy:** The denial notice specified "Conservative treatment pathway not completed" and "no cord compression and no neurological deficit," providing a specific clinical reason. This is deemed procedurally adequate.
2.  **DMHC APL 22-014 — Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions:** This APL provides exceptions for conservative treatment where objective imaging shows progressive pathology (e.g., myelomalacia) for conditions risking irreversible harm (e.g., myelopathy with cord compression).
    *   **Clinical Record:** The patient's MRI shows no cord compression, no myelomalacia, and no progressive pathology (only one MRI provided). Her symptoms are mild (no weakness, full-time work).
    *   **Finding:** The conditions for waiver under DMHC APL 22-014 are **not met**. This case does not fall under the "time-sensitive neurological conditions" category (II.1, II.2) described by the APL.
3.  **CIC § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This statute prohibits conditioning surgery on PT completion when specific criteria are met:
    *   **(a)(1) PT contraindicated or unlikely to benefit:** Dr. Walsh elected to halt PT, but did not document that *further* PT was contraindicated or unlikely to benefit for radiculopathy, especially given "mild improvement in pain."
    *   **(a)(2) Functional plateau reached:** Only 4 weeks of PT were completed (less than plan's 6-week minimum), and there is no documentation by the therapist or physician, meeting the clinical definition (e.g., <10% improvement over 3 sessions), that a functional plateau was reached. The patient's functional status remains robust ("working full-time, no activity limitations").
    *   **(a)(3) Objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address:** The patient has radiculopathy with nerve root contact, but no cord compression, myelomalacia, or significant neurological deficit (no weakness). For radiculopathy, PT is a recognized treatment, and the objective findings do not suggest PT *cannot* address the condition.
    *   **Finding:** The conditions for waiver of the PT requirement under CIC § 10169.5 are **not met**.

**D. Application of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)**
1.  **Conservative Treatment for Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 2.1, 2.2, Section 4 Summary Table) indicate that physical therapy is strongly evidenced for radiculopathy, with a typical minimum duration of 6-12 weeks before considering surgery. The plan's 6-week PT requirement for radiculopathy is consistent with this evidence base.
2.  **Surgical Indications for Radiculopathy (Section 3.1):**
    *   The patient's condition (mild radiculopathy, no weakness, working full-time) does not meet TIER 1 (strong indication) criteria, which are reserved for myelopathy or progressive neurological deficit.
    *   For TIER 2(b) ("Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative tx"), the patient has persistent symptoms but has **not completed the 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment** recommended by guidelines.
    *   The patient's presentation aligns more closely with TIER 3(c) ("Mild radiculopathy not yet trialed on conservative treatment"), for which surgery is not indicated.
3.  **Risk of Delay:** For radiculopathy, the risk of delay associated with a conservative treatment trial is generally low (Clinical Reference Summary, Section 4 Summary Table), in contrast to moderate-severe myelopathy.
*   **Finding:** Generally accepted clinical standards support the need for a more comprehensive trial of conservative treatment (6-12 weeks of PT and other modalities) for radiculopathy prior to surgical intervention, especially in a case with mild symptoms and no objective signs of neurological deterioration or cord compromise.

**E. Conflict Resolution (Hierarchy: State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria)**
No direct conflict exists among the three sources in this case.
*   **HealthFirst Plan Criteria:** The denial is consistent with the plan's criteria due to non-completion of conservative treatment (PT, pharmacotherapy, interventional).
*   **California State Law:** California regulations (DMHC APL 22-014, CIC § 10169.5) do not provide an exception for the conservative treatment requirements for this member's specific clinical presentation (cervical radiculopathy without myelopathy or severe/progressive neurological compromise).
*   **Clinical Standard:** Clinical guidelines support and reinforce the need for a conservative treatment trial (6-12 weeks) for radiculopathy before considering surgery. The objective clinical findings do not support an urgent need for surgery that would bypass standard conservative care for radiculopathy.
*   **Treating Surgeon's Arguments:** While Dr. Walsh's experience and direct examination are noted, his assertion for immediate surgery and the claim of impending permanent disability are not supported by the objective clinical findings (mild symptoms, no weakness, no cord compression, no myelomalacia) or by generally accepted medical necessity guidelines for cervical radiculopathy.

**VI. DISPOSITION**

**UPHOLD** the denial.

**VII. WARRANT**
The denial for anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF C5-C6) for member Stephanie Oduya is upheld. The patient's clinical presentation, characterized by cervical radiculopathy with mild symptoms (right C6 dermatomal tingling, 6/10 pain, no weakness, working full-time) and objective imaging showing no cord compression, no myelomalacia, and no progressive pathology, does not meet the medical necessity criteria for surgical intervention at this time.

Specifically, the patient has not completed the conservative treatment pathway as outlined in HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2. The record shows only 4 weeks of physical therapy (6 weeks required), one pharmacotherapy agent (two required), and no interventional procedures (one required).

California state regulations, including DMHC APL 22-014 and CIC § 10169.5, which provide exceptions to conservative treatment requirements in specific urgent or unresponsive clinical scenarios, are not applicable here. The patient's condition does not involve myelopathy, myelomalacia, or objective evidence of neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Furthermore, there is no documentation from the treating physician or therapist indicating that physical therapy is contraindicated, or that a functional plateau has been reached according to accepted definitions.

Finally, generally accepted clinical standards from the AANS/CNS, NASS, and AO Spine consistently recommend a trial of 6-12 weeks of conservative treatment for cervical radiculopathy prior to surgical consideration. The current clinical findings do not support an urgent surgical indication, and the risk of delaying surgery for completion of conservative therapy for this presentation of radiculopathy is considered low. Therefore, the plan's denial is consistent with its internal criteria, aligns with California state law, and is supported by generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.
