**I. BACKGROUND**

Robert Chang, a 38-year-old male, presented with acute onset bilateral arm weakness and difficulty walking, progressing over 72 hours, consistent with acute cervical myelopathy. Emergency MRI revealed severe C5-C6 central cord compression with extensive T2 hyperintensity (myelomalacia) throughout the cord, indicating acute cord injury. A neurological exam showed bilateral arm weakness (2/5), leg weakness (3/5), hyperreflexia, bilateral Babinski signs, and urinary urgency, consistent with an incomplete spinal cord syndrome. The treating neurosurgeon, Dr. Elena Marchetti, requested emergency authorization for ACDF, declaring physical therapy (PT) absolutely contraindicated due to the risk of converting an incomplete cord syndrome to a complete cord syndrome, citing extensive myelomalacia.

HealthFirst PPO Gold initially denied the request, citing failure to complete required conservative treatment (0 weeks PT, no documented pharmacotherapy or interventional procedures) as per CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2.

---

**II. HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY**

This appeal review applies the following hierarchy of authority:
1.  **State Law** (California Insurance Code (CIC), California Health & Safety Code (CHSC), DMHC All-Plan Letters (APLs))
2.  **Clinical Standard** (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine Guidelines)
3.  **Plan Criteria** (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)

Where sources conflict, the higher authority governs.

---

**III. REVIEW OF DENIAL AND APPEAL BASIS**

The plan's denial was based on the member not completing the conservative treatment requirements outlined in CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. Specifically, the denial noted 0 weeks of PT, no documented pharmacotherapy (implying failure to meet the 4-week, two-agent requirement), and no interventional procedures.

The appeal basis asserts that the member presented with acute myelopathy, PT was contraindicated due to immediate harm, MRI showed severe cord compression with extensive myelomalacia, and this constitutes an emergency surgical situation to which CIC 10169.5(a)(3) applies.

---

**IV. ANALYSIS AGAINST HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY**

**A. Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)**

1.  **Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1):** The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (Section 2.1(b)) with objective imaging (severe C5-C6 cord compression, myelomalacia) and classic myelopathic symptoms. **MET.**
2.  **Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2):** This is the basis for the denial.
    *   **Standard Pathway:** The member has not completed the standard 6 weeks of PT, 2 pharmacotherapy agents for 4+ weeks each, or 1 interventional procedure.
    *   **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY:** This exception reduces the PT requirement to a minimum of 4 weeks and waives the interventional treatment if the physician documents risk. The pharmacotherapy requirement remains in full.
        *   **PT:** Even with the myelopathy exception, the plan requires a minimum of 4 weeks of PT. The member has 0 weeks documented.
        *   **Interventional:** The physician did not specifically document that interventional treatment posed a risk of neurological deterioration.
        *   **Pharmacotherapy:** The member received IV methylprednisolone for 24 hours, which does not meet the "two agents for minimum 4 weeks each" requirement.
    *   **Conclusion on Plan Criteria:** Based *solely* on a literal reading of HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 (even with the myelopathy exception), the conservative treatment requirements are not fully met.

**B. State Law (California Regulatory Framework)**

California state laws and regulations directly address the requirements for conservative treatment, particularly in time-sensitive neurological conditions.

1.  **CIC § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:** This statute prohibits health insurers from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where:
    *   **(a)(1) Physician documents PT contraindicated or unlikely to benefit:** Dr. Marchetti explicitly documented that "PT is absolutely contraindicated and would be dangerous. Initiating PT would risk converting incomplete cord syndrome to complete cord syndrome." **This condition is MET.**
    *   **(a)(3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address:** The MRI clearly shows "Severe C5-C6 central cord compression. Extensive T2 hyperintensity throughout cord (extensive myelomalacia)." Myelomalacia signifies existing spinal cord injury due to structural compression, which PT cannot resolve. **This condition is MET.**
    *   **Conclusion on CIC § 10169.5:** Because both conditions (a)(1) and (a)(3) are met, California law **prohibits** HealthFirst from denying surgery based on the lack of PT completion. This explicitly overrides the plan's 4-week PT requirement.

2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 — Myelopathy and Urgent Conditions:**
    *   **Section II.1 — Myelopathy Classification:** The DMHC states that "cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Plans SHALL treat such cases as urgent." Mr. Chang's presentation directly meets this classification.
    *   **Section II.2 — Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** For conditions risking irreversible harm, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when: (a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia...), AND (b) Treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and has failed to arrest progression, AND (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines."
        *   (a) MRI shows "Extensive T2 hyperintensity throughout cord (extensive myelomalacia)." **MET.**
        *   (b) Dr. Marchetti's declaration documents the urgency and contraindication to *initiating* conservative treatment, effectively documenting that it would fail or cause harm. **MET.**
        *   (c) As discussed below under Clinical Standard, surgery is indicated. **MET.**
        *   The APL further clarifies that "The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding that may support waiver of standard conservative treatment requirements even where a full course has not been completed, when the treating physician documents urgency."
    *   **Section II.3 — Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** The APL mandates that "Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for conditions identified in this guidance. Where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted standards, the clinical standard governs."
    *   **Conclusion on DMHC APL 22-014:** This APL directly supports waiving *all* full conservative treatment requirements (PT, pharmacotherapy, interventional) for this urgent myelopathy case with myelomalacia.

**C. Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)**

1.  **Diagnostic Classification (Section 1.1 & 1.2):** The clinical record unequivocally establishes a diagnosis of acute, moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy, not radiculopathy. The presence of myelomalacia (T2 signal change) indicates existing cord injury, higher risk of permanent deficit with delay, and is a strong indication for surgical intervention, acting as a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials.
2.  **Evidence on Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy (Section 2.1 & 2.2):**
    *   Physical therapy is not effective for moderate-severe myelopathy and is generally *not recommended* as a primary treatment.
    *   AO Spine Guidelines (2021) state, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration." This directly contradicts the plan's 4-week PT requirement.
    *   There is no evidence supporting a defined minimum duration of conservative treatment before surgery for myelopathy; the 6-week (or 4-week myelopathy exception) PT requirement in payer policies is derived from radiculopathy evidence and is a "category error" when applied to myelopathy.
3.  **Risk of Delay (Section 2.3):** Delaying surgery in the presence of myelomalacia and progressive neurological deficits significantly increases the risk of permanent neurological deficit and reduces the likelihood of recovery.
4.  **Surgical Indications (Section 3.1 & 3.2):** Mr. Chang's presentation meets multiple TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION criteria for ACDF per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023):
    *   (a) Moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI.
    *   (c) Myelomalacia on MRI (cord signal change).
    *   (d) Acute severe myelopathy with risk of permanent deficit.
    The clinical standard for this presentation does not require completion of a minimum course of physical therapy or other conservative measures as a prerequisite to surgery.
5.  **Conclusion on Clinical Standard:** Current clinical guidelines unequivocally support immediate surgical intervention for acute, severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and rapidly progressive deficits. Delaying surgery for a trial of PT or prolonged pharmacotherapy in this setting is not supported by evidence and is considered harmful.

---

**V. CONFLICT RESOLUTION AND DETERMINATION**

The denial was based on HealthFirst's internal criteria requiring conservative treatment. However, the plan's criteria (even its myelopathy exception for 4 weeks of PT and full pharmacotherapy) are directly contradicted and superseded by both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice.

*   **State Law Preemption:** CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3) explicitly state that a plan cannot condition surgery on PT completion when PT is contraindicated or when there is objective neurological compromise (both present here). DMHC APL 22-014 further mandates that plans shall not require a full course of conservative treatment in urgent myelopathy cases with myelomalacia where surgery is indicated. These state laws directly override HealthFirst's conservative treatment requirements.
*   **Clinical Standard Preemption:** The clinical record meets Tier 1 (strong) indications for surgery per AANS/CNS guidelines. Clinical evidence from AO Spine, NASS, and Cochrane Review clearly states that conservative treatment, especially PT, is not indicated for moderate-severe myelopathy and delaying surgery may cause irreversible harm, particularly in the presence of myelomalacia. The plan's requirements are more restrictive than generally accepted medical practice, which is prohibited by DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3 and CHSC § 1374.32(b).

The denial notice itself was procedurally adequate in citing criteria and reason (CHSC § 1374.31(b)). However, the *application* of those criteria was inappropriate given the higher authority of state law and clinical standards.

---

**VI. DISPOSITION**

The prior authorization denial for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) for Robert Chang (M-445566) is **OVERTURNED**.

**Warrant:**
The denial, based on the non-completion of conservative treatment requirements under HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, is in direct conflict with both California state law and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice for acute cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia.

1.  **State Law Override:** Pursuant to California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3), HealthFirst is prohibited from denying the surgical procedure based on the absence of physical therapy. The treating physician documented PT as "absolutely contraindicated and would be dangerous" due to the risk of converting an incomplete spinal cord syndrome to a complete one, and the MRI shows "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise" (severe cord compression with extensive myelomalacia) that PT cannot address. Furthermore, DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.2 mandates that plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment" for urgent myelopathy cases with myelomalacia where surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted guidelines.
2.  **Clinical Standard Override:** The member's acute presentation with rapidly progressive neurological deficits, severe cord compression, and extensive myelomalacia meets multiple Tier 1 "strong indication" criteria for immediate surgical decompression per AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023). Leading spine societies (AO Spine, NASS) confirm that conservative treatment, particularly physical therapy, is not indicated for moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy, and delaying surgery in such cases is not supported by evidence and risks neurological deterioration. The plan's conservative treatment requirements are more restrictive than these generally accepted standards, which is not permissible under DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3 and CHSC § 1374.32(b).

Therefore, based on the hierarchy of authority, the state law and clinical standards supersede the plan's criteria, requiring approval of the requested procedure.
