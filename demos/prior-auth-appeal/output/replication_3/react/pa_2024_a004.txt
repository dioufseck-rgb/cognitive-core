### **INTRODUCTION**

This determination addresses the appeal submitted by Robert Chang regarding the denial of prior authorization for an ACDF C5-C6 surgical procedure. The initial denial was based on the member's failure to complete conservative treatment requirements as outlined in HealthFirst's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. This review applies the established hierarchy of authority: **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria**.

### **CASE SUMMARY**

Mr. Robert Chang, a 38-year-old male, presented with acute onset bilateral arm weakness and difficulty walking, diagnosed as acute cervical myelopathy with rapidly progressive deficits, requiring emergency surgical intervention. An MRI on 2024-11-10 revealed severe C5-C6 central cord compression with extensive T2 hyperintensity (myelomalacia), indicating acute cord injury. His neurological exam showed significant motor deficits, hyperreflexia, and bilateral Babinski.

Dr. Elena Marchetti, Neurosurgery, requested an emergency authorization for ACDF C5-C6. The request was denied by HealthFirst on 2024-11-18, citing unmet conservative treatment requirements (0 weeks of physical therapy, no pharmacotherapy for the required duration, no interventional procedures) per CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2.

The appeal argues that:
1.  The member presented with acute myelopathy, and physical therapy was contraindicated due to the risk of immediate harm and converting an incomplete cord syndrome to a complete one.
2.  MRI showed severe cord compression with extensive myelomalacia, confirming an acute cord injury.
3.  This is an emergency surgical situation, and California Insurance Code (CIC) 10169.5(a)(3) applies, waiving conservative treatment requirements.

### **CLINICAL FINDINGS**

*   **Diagnosis:** Acute cervical myelopathy with rapidly progressive deficits, deemed a surgical emergency.
*   **Symptoms:** Acute onset bilateral arm weakness (2/5), bilateral leg weakness (3/5), difficulty walking, hyperreflexia, bilateral Babinski, urinary urgency (incomplete spinal cord syndrome).
*   **Imaging (MRI 2024-11-10):** Severe C5-C6 central cord compression, extensive T2 hyperintensity (myelomalacia) throughout the cord, consistent with acute cord injury.
*   **Conservative Treatment Attempted:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** 0 weeks. Treating physician declared PT "absolutely contraindicated" and "dangerous" due to extensive myelomalacia and risk of neurological deterioration.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** IV methylprednisolone administered for 24 hours in the ER as per acute spinal cord injury protocol.
    *   **Interventional Procedures:** None.
*   **Physician Rationale:** Dr. Marchetti explicitly stated the patient has an acute incomplete cervical cord syndrome with extensive myelomalacia, making PT contraindicated. She declared this an emergency situation where delaying surgery risks permanent complete paralysis, citing CIC 10169.5(a)(3).

### **ANALYSIS**

This review systematically applies the hierarchy of authority to determine the medical necessity and coverage of the requested procedure.

#### **1. Review of Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)**

*   **Section 2.1 DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENT:** Met. Member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (2.1(b)) supported by MRI findings.
*   **Section 2.2 CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT REQUIREMENT:** This is the basis of the denial.
    *   **Standard Pathway:** Requires 6 weeks PT, 2 pharmacotherapy agents for 4+ weeks, and 1 interventional procedure. **Not met.**
    *   **EXCEPTION 2.2(A) — MYELOPATHY PATHWAY:** Reduces PT to 4 weeks minimum and waives interventional if risk is documented. Pharmacotherapy remains in full.
        *   PT: 0 weeks. **Not met.**
        *   Pharmacotherapy: 24 hours of one agent. **Not met.**
        *   Interventional: Waived if risk documented. While Dr. Marchetti did not explicitly state interventional treatment posed a risk, her declaration of a "surgical emergency" and risk of permanent paralysis due to delay implicitly argues against any delaying conservative treatment. However, strictly applying the exception, it's not explicitly met for interventional, and pharmacotherapy requirements are certainly not met.
*   **Section 2.3 IMAGING REQUIREMENT:** Met. MRI demonstrates severe C5-C6 compression and myelomalacia consistent with symptoms.
*   **Section 2.4 PHYSICIAN DOCUMENTATION:** Largely met, providing rationale, functional limitations, and surgical risks. The "attestation that conservative treatment has been completed" is not met, as it was deemed contraindicated.

*   **Conclusion based *solely* on Plan Criteria:** The plan's initial denial, based on the unmet conservative treatment requirements (even with the myelopathy exception for PT and pharmacotherapy), was technically accurate according to its *own internal criteria*.

#### **2. Review of California Regulatory Requirements (State Law)**

California state law supersedes HealthFirst's internal criteria where conflicts exist.

*   **CIC § 10169.5 — PHYSICAL THERAPY AS PREREQUISITE (Effective: 2023-01-01):**
    This statute prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where specific circumstances apply.
    *   **(a)(1) Physician documents PT contraindicated or unlikely to benefit:** Dr. Marchetti explicitly documented that "PT is absolutely contraindicated and would be dangerous. Delaying surgery risks permanent complete paralysis." This directly engages and satisfies this exception.
    *   **(a)(3) Objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address:** The MRI demonstrates "Severe C5-C6 central cord compression" and "Extensive T2 hyperintensity... (extensive myelomalacia)." Myelomalacia signifies existing spinal cord damage and is a structural compromise that physical therapy cannot reverse or alleviate. This directly engages and satisfies this exception.
    *   **(c) Mechanical application of PT duration:** The plan's mechanical application of a 4-6 week PT minimum is explicitly prohibited by this section in cases involving neurological urgency.
    *   **Finding:** CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3) **override** the plan's physical therapy requirement for this member. The plan **cannot** deny based on the lack of physical therapy.

*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 — MYELOPATHY AND URGENT CONDITIONS (Issued: 2022-09):**
    This guidance specifically addresses prior authorization for time-sensitive neurological conditions.
    *   **II.1 Myelopathy Classification:** States that cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI **SHALL** be treated as urgent due to potential for irreversible harm. This perfectly matches Mr. Chang's presentation.
    *   **II.2 Conservative Treatment Exceptions:** Mandates that plans **SHALL NOT** require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when:
        *   **(a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change, myelomalacia):** Mr. Chang's MRI shows "extensive myelomalacia" on initial presentation.
        *   **(b) Treating physician documents conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression:** Dr. Marchetti's declaration of PT as "absolutely contraindicated" and the need for "emergency authorization" due to risk of "permanent complete paralysis" indicates that delaying for any conservative trial would be detrimental and has effectively "failed" as a viable initial treatment strategy.
        *   **(c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines:** (Addressed in Clinical Standards below, and is affirmative).
        *   The APL specifically notes that myelomalacia on MRI **may support waiver** of standard conservative treatment requirements when urgency is documented by the physician. Dr. Marchetti documented urgency.
    *   **II.3 Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard:** Reaffirms that plans shall not apply internal criteria more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice.
    *   **Finding:** DMHC APL 22-014 **mandates a waiver** of the full conservative treatment requirements (including PT, pharmacotherapy duration, and interventional procedures) for this urgent myelopathy case with myelomalacia and physician-documented urgency.

*   **CHSC § 1374.32(b) — STANDARD OF REVIEW FOR IMR:** While applicable to IMR, this provision establishes the principle that services meeting "generally accepted standards of medical practice" **SHALL** be approved regardless of the plan's more restrictive internal criteria. This principle guides internal appeal review as well.

#### **3. Review of Clinical Standard (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021)**

Generally accepted clinical standards provide robust support for Mr. Chang's requested surgery and contradict the plan's conservative treatment requirements.

*   **Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy:** Clinical guidelines clearly differentiate cervical myelopathy as a more severe condition involving spinal cord compression, requiring different treatment algorithms. Applying radiculopathy-derived conservative treatment protocols (like 6-week PT) to myelopathy is a "category error."
*   **Myelomalacia:** This finding on MRI signifies existing spinal cord injury and is universally recognized as a **strong indication for surgical intervention and a contraindication to extended conservative treatment trials.** It is a predictor of permanent deficit if surgery is delayed.
*   **Physical Therapy for Myelopathy:** The evidence base (Cochrane Review 2021, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) indicates that for moderate-to-severe myelopathy, physical therapy does **not** produce meaningful functional improvement and is **not recommended as primary treatment**. AO Spine (2021) explicitly states, "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients... may result in neurological deterioration." This strongly supports Dr. Marchetti's assessment that PT is contraindicated and dangerous.
*   **Surgical Indication:** Mr. Chang's presentation (confirmed cervical myelopathy, myelomalacia on MRI, objective neurological deficits with rapidly progressive symptoms) meets **TIER 1 — STRONG INDICATION** for surgery according to AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023). These guidelines do not require a minimum course of PT as a prerequisite for surgery in this scenario.
*   **Risk of Delay:** Clinical evidence consistently shows that delaying surgical treatment for moderate-severe myelopathy is associated with decreased likelihood of neurological recovery and worse outcomes.

#### **4. Conflict Resolution**

*   The plan's denial is based on the member's failure to meet the conservative treatment requirements of CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2.
*   However, both California state law (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014) and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine) explicitly and unequivocally state that these conservative treatment requirements, particularly for physical therapy, **are not applicable and should be waived** for a patient presenting with acute, moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia and rapidly progressive neurological deficits.
*   The physician's declaration of PT contraindication and the presence of myelomalacia directly engage the legal exceptions. The clinical urgency and lack of evidence for conservative treatment in this specific myelopathic presentation further align with the state law's intent.
*   Applying the hierarchy, **State Law > Clinical Standard > Plan Criteria**, the state law and clinical standards dictate that the plan's criteria for conservative treatment cannot be applied to deny this request. The plan's denial directly conflicts with higher authorities.

### **CONCLUSION AND DETERMINATION**

Based on a thorough review of the clinical record, HealthFirst's clinical coverage criteria, applicable California state law, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, the initial denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) for Mr. Robert Chang should be **OVERTURNED**.

### **RATIONALE**

The denial of prior authorization for ACDF C5-C6 was based on the member's failure to complete conservative treatment requirements under HealthFirst's CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. This determination finds the denial to be inconsistent with California state law and generally accepted standards of medical practice for the following reasons:

1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1) & (a)(3) Applies:** The treating neurosurgeon, Dr. Marchetti, explicitly documented that physical therapy is "absolutely contraindicated and would be dangerous" and that delaying surgery risks "permanent complete paralysis." This satisfies CIC § 10169.5(a)(1). Furthermore, the MRI demonstrates "severe C5-C6 central cord compression" with "extensive T2 hyperintensity (myelomalacia)," which constitutes objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address, satisfying CIC § 10169.5(a)(3). Therefore, the plan cannot condition approval on the completion of physical therapy.
2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 Mandates Waiver of Conservative Treatment:** This APL specifically identifies cervical myelopathy with cord compression and myelomalacia as an urgent condition that plans **shall treat as urgent** and for which plans **shall not require a full course of conservative treatment**. Mr. Chang's MRI shows myelomalacia, and his physician documented urgency, aligning perfectly with the APL's criteria for waiving standard conservative requirements. The plan's application of CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 is more restrictive than this state regulatory guidance.
3.  **Inconsistency with Generally Accepted Clinical Standards:** Major spine society guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021) classify acute, moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia as a **Tier 1 strong surgical indication**. These guidelines explicitly state that there is **no high-quality evidence** supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgery for this condition, and delaying surgery may lead to **neurological deterioration**. The 6-week (or even 4-week) PT requirement in the plan's criteria is derived from radiculopathy evidence and is inappropriate for myelopathy, representing a "category error." The 24-hour IV steroid treatment is a standard acute intervention, but the requirement for "two pharmacotherapy agents for 4+ weeks each" is similarly not supported for this acute, urgent myelopathic presentation.
4.  **CHSC § 1374.32(b) Principle:** The service requested meets generally accepted standards of medical practice, and under California law, such services shall be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.

The plan's denial fails to account for the urgency of Mr. Chang's myelopathy, the contraindication of conservative treatment (particularly physical therapy) given his myelomalacia and rapidly progressive deficits, and specific state laws and clinical guidelines designed to protect patients in such time-sensitive neurological conditions.
