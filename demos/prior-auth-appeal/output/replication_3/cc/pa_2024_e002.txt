I. CODING CORRECTION AND COMPLIANCE DIRECTIVE
The initial prior authorization request was submitted for "CPT 22551 x3" (three units of the primary ACDF code). Under standard American Medical Association (AMA) CPT coding guidelines, CPT 22551 is a single-level primary code and cannot be billed in multiple units for a single operative session. Multi-level fusions must be coded using the primary code CPT 22551 for the first level, and the add-on code CPT 22552 for each additional level. 

Approving "CPT 22551 x3" would constitute a severe coding and billing error, violating federal and state billing compliance standards and resulting in improper billing of three primary procedures instead of one primary and two add-on procedures. To ensure administrative and billing compliance, HealthFirst has restructured the authorization to approve CPT 22551 x1 (C4-C5) and CPT 22552 x1 (C5-C6), while upholding the denial of the C3-C4 level (which would have been represented by a second unit of CPT 22552).

II. CLINICAL RATIONALE BY LEVEL

1. C4-C5 LEVEL: OVERTURNED (APPROVED under CPT 22551 x1)
- Clinical Presentation & Imaging: The patient's MRI dated 2024-10-01 demonstrates a large disc herniation at C4-C5 causing severe spinal cord compression and T2 signal change within the spinal cord, which is diagnostic of myelomalacia. The neurological exam dated 2024-10-05 confirms objective upper motor neuron signs of cervical myelopathy, including bilateral hyperreflexia, bilateral Hoffman sign, and a wide-based gait.
- Plan Criteria Analysis: Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(b), the patient has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy. Under Section 2.2 Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway), the conservative treatment requirement is modified: the physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks, and the interventional treatment requirement is waived if it poses a risk of neurological deterioration. The patient completed 9 weeks of physical therapy and 4 months of pharmacotherapy (Pregabalin and Baclofen), fully satisfying the plan's criteria.
- Regulatory Override & Clinical Standards: Under California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI is a time-sensitive, urgent condition with potential for irreversible harm. Plans are prohibited from requiring a full course of conservative treatment when myelomalacia is present. Furthermore, California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) prohibits conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (such as severe cord compression and myelomalacia) that physical therapy cannot address. Generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines, 2023; AO Spine Guidelines, 2021) state that moderate-to-severe myelopathy with myelomalacia is a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) for immediate surgical decompression, and delaying surgery to complete conservative trials is a clinical error that risks permanent neurological deficit. Therefore, surgical decompression at C4-C5 is medically necessary.

2. C5-C6 LEVEL: OVERTURNED (APPROVED under CPT 22552 x1)
- Clinical Presentation & Imaging: The MRI dated 2024-10-01 demonstrates a moderate disc herniation at C5-C6 with moderate left C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis. The neurological exam dated 2024-10-05 reveals objective lower motor neuron deficits localizing to the C6 nerve root, specifically left bicep weakness (graded 4-/5) and a diminished left bicep reflex.
- Plan Criteria Analysis: Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(a) and (c), the patient has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy and cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit. Under Section 2.2, the patient completed a comprehensive course of conservative treatment: (a) 9 weeks of physical therapy (exceeding the 6-week minimum), (b) pharmacotherapy with Pregabalin (4 months) and Baclofen (3 months) (exceeding the 2-drug, 4-week minimum), and (c) a cervical epidural steroid injection on 2024-10-20.
- Resolution of Conservative Treatment Efficacy: The initial reviewer suggested that because the epidural steroid injection on 2024-10-20 resulted in "modest improvement in radicular symptoms," conservative treatment was successful and had not failed. However, the physical therapy summary notes that "C5-C6 radicular symptoms persist" and the neurological exam confirms persistent left bicep weakness (4-/5) and a diminished reflex. Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2), a health plan cannot condition approval on further physical therapy when the patient has completed a course of PT and reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms. Under Section 5 of the Clinical Reference Summary, a patient who achieves partial pain reduction but no improvement in objective neurological deficits (weakness, reflex changes) after a comprehensive therapy course has reached a functional plateau. Generally accepted clinical standards (NASS Guidelines, 2020; AANS/CNS Guidelines, 2023) state that persistent radicular symptoms with objective motor weakness (4-/5 bicep weakness) despite a 9-week conservative trial constitute a Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) for surgical decompression. Denying surgery for C5-C6 because of "modest improvement" from an injection ignores the persistence of objective motor weakness and the failure of conservative therapy to resolve the neurological deficit, exposing the patient to progressive nerve root injury. Therefore, surgical decompression at C5-C6 is medically necessary.

3. C3-C4 LEVEL: UPHELD (DENIED)
- Clinical Presentation & Imaging: The MRI dated 2024-10-01 demonstrates only a mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing at C3-C4. The neurological exam dated 2024-10-05 shows no specific radicular pattern or objective neurological deficit localizing to the C3-C4 level (noting only "neck pain with C3-C4 palpation"). The physical therapy summary explicitly states that the C3-C4 contribution is "unclear."
- Plan Criteria Analysis: Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 3.1, surgical authorization is excluded when requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy. Under Section 3.3, surgery is excluded when imaging does not demonstrate pathology at the proposed level consistent with the clinical presentation.
- Clinical Standards: Generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS Guidelines, 2023) classify surgery for mild imaging findings without correlating symptoms or for axial neck pain alone as Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated). There is no clinical evidence that a fusion at C3-C4 would benefit the patient, and performing an unnecessary adjacent-level fusion exposes the patient to significant risks, including adjacent segment disease and pseudarthrosis. Therefore, the denial of the C3-C4 level is upheld.

III. PROCEDURAL COMPLIANCE NOTE
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a) and (b), a health plan's denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the denial. The initial denial notice dated 2024-11-25 failed to cite the specific sections of the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) applied and failed to provide a detailed clinical explanation for why the C5-C6 level was denied despite documented motor weakness. This procedural defect has been corrected in this final appeal determination.

IV. NOTICE OF INDEPENDENT MEDICAL REVIEW (IMR) RIGHTS
Because a portion of your request (the C3-C4 level) has been upheld as denied, you have the right to request an Independent Medical Review (IMR) through the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC).

What is an Independent Medical Review?
An IMR is an evaluation of your case by independent, board-certified doctors who are not associated with HealthFirst. If the IMR reviewer decides that the denied service is medically necessary, HealthFirst is legally required to approve and cover the service. The IMR process is free to you.

How to Apply for IMR:
You may apply for an IMR if HealthFirst's decision has been upheld, modified, or delayed. You must submit your application to the DMHC within 180 days of receiving this letter. 

To apply, complete the Independent Medical Review/Complaint Form envelope provided with this letter or apply online at the DMHC website: www.dmhc.ca.gov. You may also contact the DMHC Help Center at 1-888-466-2219 for assistance.
