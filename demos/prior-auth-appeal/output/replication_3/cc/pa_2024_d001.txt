Dear Ms. White,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of the appeal filed on October 18, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial for the requested single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551). 

After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical documentation, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, and applicable California statutory and regulatory mandates, HealthFirst has OVERTURNED the initial denial. The requested surgical procedure is hereby AUTHORIZED.

This determination is based on two independent, legally binding grounds under California law: (1) a procedural defect in the initial denial notice that results in an expired statutory decision window and subsequent "Deemed Approved" status, and (2) a statutory waiver of the plan's physical therapy requirements due to objective structural neurological compromise.

I. PROCEDURAL DEFECTS AND DEEMED APPROVAL (CHSC § 1374.31(b) & CHSC § 1367.01)

The initial denial notice issued on October 5, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, was procedurally defective under California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b). 

Specifically, CHSC § 1374.31(b) mandates that any medical necessity denial notice must:
1. Specify the exact clinical criteria applied (including specific section numbers).
2. Provide the specific clinical reasons for the denial.
3. Include the mandatory notice of the member's right to an Independent Medical Review (IMR).

The initial denial notice dated October 5, 2024, failed on all counts: it contained only a generic reference to plan criteria without citing any section numbers or specific criteria, and it completely omitted the mandatory IMR rights notice.

Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria or clinical reasons is defective and "shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."

Because the October 5, 2024 notice is legally void, no valid denial was issued. Under CHSC § 1367.01 and California Code of Regulations (CCR) Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1, a health plan must issue a prior authorization decision within five (5) business days of receipt of all information. Because the statutory decision window has expired without a legally valid denial being issued, the requested service is DEEMED APPROVED under CHSC § 1367.01 and CCR Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1.

An internal health plan appeals department does not possess the jurisdictional authority to "remand" a case to allow the plan to reissue a compliant denial notice. Under California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) regulations, an internal appeal must result in a final determination (either Uphold or Overturn) within 30 days. Consequently, the procedural defect cannot be retroactively cured, and the appeal must be resolved with an OVERTURN.

II. CLINICAL MERITS AND STATUTORY WAIVER OF CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT (CIC § 10169.5)

In addition to the procedural deemed approval, the denial must be overturned on its clinical merits under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5.

The initial denial was based on the patient's failure to meet HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2, which requires a minimum of six (6) weeks of supervised physical therapy, a trial of two pharmacotherapy agents for four (4) weeks each, and at least one interventional treatment (such as an epidural steroid injection). The clinical record shows the patient completed four (4) weeks of physical therapy (12 sessions) and a six-week trial of one pharmacotherapy agent (Ibuprofen 800mg TID), with no interventional treatments.

However, the patient's clinical presentation includes objective structural neurological compromise. A neurological examination on August 20, 2024, documented:
- Right C7 dermatomal numbness.
- Objective tricep motor weakness graded at 4/5 on the right.
- Diminished right tricep reflex.
An MRI dated August 15, 2024, confirmed a corresponding C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate right C7 nerve root compression.

Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), a health plan or insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... [t]he condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Active motor weakness (4/5 tricep strength) and reflex deficits resulting from moderate nerve root compression constitute objective structural neurological compromise. Physical therapy cannot decompress the compressed nerve root or restore motor function in the presence of active structural compromise.

Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(c) prohibits plans from mechanically applying a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy (such as the plan's 6-week requirement) without reference to whether the condition involves neurological urgency or structural compromise.

Because the patient has documented objective structural neurological compromise, the plan's 6-week physical therapy requirement is waived by operation of state law under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3).

III. CONCLUSION

Because the initial denial notice was procedurally void under CHSC § 1374.31(b), the request is deemed approved under CHSC § 1367.01. Additionally, because the patient exhibits objective structural neurological compromise, the physical therapy requirement is waived under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3).

Accordingly, the denial is OVERTURNED, and the requested Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is fully authorized.
