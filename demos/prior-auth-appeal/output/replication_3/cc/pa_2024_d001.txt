Dear Ms. White and Dr. Lee,

The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the first-level internal appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551). 

After evaluating the clinical records, the plan's internal coverage criteria, and the applicable California regulatory framework, HealthFirst has determined that the initial prior authorization denial dated October 5, 2024, must be OVERTURNED. The requested surgical procedure is hereby DEEMED APPROVED. 

This determination is based on two independent, legally binding grounds under California law: (1) a procedural defect in the initial denial notice that triggers a statutory deemed approval, and (2) a substantive statutory waiver of the plan's physical therapy requirements due to documented objective structural neurological compromise.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I. PROCEDURAL ANALYSIS & STATUTORY DEEMED APPROVAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Under California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a), when a health plan issues a prior authorization denial based on medical necessity, it is mandated to provide a written notice that specifies the exact clinical criteria applied, the specific clinical reasons for the denial, and a clear notice of the member's right to an Independent Medical Review (IMR).

The initial denial notice issued by HealthFirst on October 5, 2024, failed to meet these statutory standards. Specifically:
1. The notice did not cite the specific clinical criteria applied, providing only a generic reference to "plan criteria" without a section number or specific criterion text.
2. The notice failed to include the mandatory IMR rights notice.

Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is legally "defective and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."

Because the October 5, 2024 notice is legally treated as if no denial was issued, HealthFirst failed to issue a valid decision within the mandatory 5-business-day decision timeframe for standard prior authorizations under CHSC § 1367.01 and California Code of Regulations (CCR) Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1. 

Under CHSC § 1367.01 (Part IV.3 of the California Regulatory Framework), if a plan fails to issue a timely, legally compliant decision within the required timeframe, the request is subject to a mandatory "DEEMED APPROVAL" for the period of the delay. Consequently, because the plan's procedural defects invalidated the initial denial, the surgery is deemed approved by operation of law.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
II. SUBSTANTIVE CLINICAL ANALYSIS & STATUTORY PT WAIVER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The plan's initial denial was substantively based on the member's failure to complete the 6-week physical therapy (PT) requirement outlined in HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(a). The clinical record indicates that the member completed 4 weeks (12 sessions) of supervised PT.

However, the clinical record also documents that the member has objective structural neurological compromise. Specifically:
1. An MRI dated August 15, 2024, demonstrates a C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate right C7 nerve root compression.
2. A neurological examination dated August 20, 2024, documents objective motor weakness (right tricep strength graded at 4/5), right C7 dermatomal numbness, and a diminished right tricep reflex.

Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3), a health insurer is legally prohibited from conditioning the approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where "the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(c) explicitly prohibits plans from mechanically applying internal requirements for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy without reference to whether the condition involves neurological urgency under subdivision (a)(3).

Because Ms. White's clinical presentation involves objective structural neurological compromise (moderate C7 nerve root compression with corresponding 4/5 tricep motor weakness and diminished reflex), the plan's internal 6-week PT requirement is legally waived under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3). The mechanical application of the 6-week PT requirement to deny this request was substantively invalid under California law.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
III. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION DETAILS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Because the initial denial notice was procedurally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b), triggering a statutory deemed approval under CHSC § 1367.01, and because the clinical basis for the denial violated the physical therapy waiver mandates of CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), the denial is OVERTURNED.
