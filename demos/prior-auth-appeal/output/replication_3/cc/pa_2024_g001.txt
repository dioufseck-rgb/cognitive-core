Dear Mr. Reyes and Dr. Okonkwo,

HealthFirst Health Plan’s Appeals Department has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at the C6-C7 level (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical documentation, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, we have determined that the initial denial issued on November 1, 2024, was based on a factual error and must be OVERTURNED. 

The prior authorization request for CPT 22551 is hereby APPROVED.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The initial denial of prior authorization was based on the assertion that the member did not meet the conservative treatment requirements under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. Specifically, the denial letter stated that the member completed only three (3) weeks of physical therapy (PT), failing to meet the six (6) week minimum, and that a functional plateau was not documented. 

A review of the complete clinical record submitted with the original request reveals that this factual claim is incorrect. The member completed a total of nine (9) weeks of supervised physical therapy across two different providers: three (3) weeks at Valley Physical Therapy and six (6) weeks at Capital Physical Therapy. The discharge summary from Capital Physical Therapy explicitly documents that a functional plateau was reached. Furthermore, the member has completed all other conservative treatment requirements, including trials of two pharmacotherapy agents and an interventional epidural steroid injection. Because the member satisfies all clinical and regulatory criteria, the denial is overturned.

II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION & DIAGNOSTIC REQUIREMENTS (CC-SPINE-2024 SECTION 2.1)
Under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1, authorization for cervical spinal surgery requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy, cervical myelopathy, or cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit, supported by current imaging (within 6 months).
* Diagnosis: The member has a documented diagnosis of persistent, right-sided C6-C7 cervical radiculopathy (Clinical Record: Presenting Diagnosis).
* Imaging: An MRI of the cervical spine dated August 20, 2024, demonstrates a large C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate right C7 nerve root compression and confirmed right foraminal stenosis. There is no cord contact or myelomalacia (Clinical Record: Imaging MRI_1). This imaging was performed within six months of the request, satisfying Section 2.1 and Section 2.3.
* Neurological Exam: A neurological examination dated August 25, 2024, confirms objective neurological deficits correlating with the imaging findings, including right C7 dermatomal numbness, a diminished right tricep reflex, and right-sided grip strength of 4/5 (Clinical Record: Neurological Exam).

III. CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT EVALUATION (CC-SPINE-2024 SECTION 2.2)
Section 2.2 of the plan criteria requires a documented course of conservative treatment consisting of physical therapy, pharmacotherapy, and an interventional treatment.

1. Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)):
   * Plan Requirement: Minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy directed toward the cervical condition with documented functional outcomes.
   * Initial Denial Claim: The reviewer asserted the member completed only 3 weeks of PT and that a functional plateau was not documented (Denial Info).
   * Appeal Findings: The clinical record demonstrates that the member completed a total of nine (9) weeks (27 sessions) of supervised physical therapy (Clinical Record: Physical Therapy Summary):
     - Valley Physical Therapy: June 1, 2024, to June 21, 2024 (3 weeks, 9 sessions). Outcome: Partial improvement, symptoms persist (Clinical Record: Physical Therapy Provider 1).
     - Capital Physical Therapy: July 8, 2024, to August 16, 2024 (6 weeks, 18 sessions). Outcome: Functional plateau reached. The discharge summary explicitly states: "Maximum therapeutic benefit achieved. No further improvement expected from continued PT." (Clinical Record: Physical Therapy Provider 2).
   * Conclusion: The member completed 9 weeks of PT, exceeding the 6-week minimum. A functional plateau was explicitly documented. The initial reviewer failed to count the records from Capital Physical Therapy, which were submitted with the original request.

2. Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)):
   * Plan Requirement: Trial of at least two specified agents (NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
   * Appeal Findings: The clinical record documents the following trials (Clinical Record: Pharmacotherapy):
     - Gabapentin (Neuropathic agent): 600mg TID for 4 months. Outcome: Partial pain relief, radicular symptoms persist.
     - Naproxen (NSAID): 500mg BID for 3 months. Outcome: Inadequate relief.
   * Conclusion: The member completed trials of two qualifying agents for well over the 4-week minimum, satisfying Section 2.2(b).

3. Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)):
   * Plan Requirement: At least one interventional treatment (cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point injection series).
   * Appeal Findings: The member underwent a right C7 cervical epidural steroid injection on September 15, 2024. The procedure provided 35% temporary relief, with symptoms returning to baseline within 3 weeks (Clinical Record: Interventional).
   * Conclusion: The interventional requirement is fully satisfied.

IV. REGULATORY COMPLIANCE & STATE LAW (CALIFORNIA INSURANCE CODE § 10169.5)
The initial denial is in direct violation of California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5. Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms. 

Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(c) prohibits plans from mechanically applying an internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau. By ignoring the Capital Physical Therapy records and the documented functional plateau, and mechanically denying the request based on an incorrect 3-week count, the plan violated California statutory requirements.

V. CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARD
According to the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Disorders (2023 Update) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), surgical decompression via ACDF is a Tier 2 "Reasonable Indication" for patients presenting with cervical radiculopathy, objective neurological deficits (such as diminished reflexes and motor weakness), and persistent symptoms that have failed to respond to an appropriate course of conservative management (typically 6 to 12 weeks). 

The member's clinical course—consisting of 9 weeks of physical therapy, 4 months of neuropathic medication, 3 months of NSAIDs, and a cervical epidural steroid injection without lasting relief—demonstrates exhaustive conservative management. Proceeding with surgical intervention is highly appropriate and aligned with generally accepted standards of medical practice.

VI. DISPOSITION & AUTHORIZATION DETAILS
Based on the clear factual error in the initial review and the member's full compliance with CC-SPINE-2024, California Insurance Code § 10169.5, and national clinical guidelines, the initial denial is OVERTURNED.
