FINAL APPEAL DETERMINATION: OVERTURN INITIAL DENIAL

Dear Mr. Reyes and Dr. Okonkwo,

The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the first-level appeal filed on November 14, 2024, regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C6-C7 level (CPT 22551) at UCSF Medical Center. 

After a comprehensive review of the clinical documentation, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has determined that the initial prior authorization denial issued on November 1, 2024, was based on a factual error. Accordingly, the initial decision is OVERTURNED, and the requested surgical procedure is APPROVED.

I. BACKGROUND AND INITIAL DENIAL REASON
On November 1, 2024, HealthFirst Utilization Management issued a denial of the prior authorization request for CPT 22551 under Clinical Coverage Criteria Document CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. The denial, reviewed by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, asserted that the member completed only 3 weeks of physical therapy (PT), failing to meet the plan's 6-week minimum requirement, and that a functional plateau was not documented. The denial also stated that "all other criteria remain unmet."

II. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND FACTUAL CORRECTION
A detailed review of the clinical record submitted with the original prior authorization request reveals that the initial denial's factual premise was incorrect. The member has fully satisfied all clinical and conservative treatment requirements under CC-SPINE-2024:

1. Diagnosis and Imaging Requirements (CC-SPINE-2024 Sections 2.1 & 2.3):
   - Clinical Presentation: The clinical record documents a presenting diagnosis of persistent, right-sided C6-C7 cervical radiculopathy. A neurological examination on August 25, 2024, revealed objective neurological deficits, including right C7 dermatomal numbness, a diminished right tricep reflex, and diminished right grip strength (4/5). This satisfies Section 2.1(a) (Cervical radiculopathy with nerve root compression and radicular symptoms) and Section 2.1(c) (Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit).
   - Imaging: An MRI dated August 20, 2024 (within the required 6-month window), demonstrated a large C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate right C7 nerve root compression and confirmed right foraminal stenosis. This satisfies Section 2.3 (Current imaging demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms).

2. Conservative Treatment Requirements (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2):
   - Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): The initial reviewer counted only the 3 weeks of PT completed at Valley Physical Therapy (June 1, 2024, to June 21, 2024; 9 sessions). However, the records submitted with the original request also included documentation from Capital Physical Therapy, where the member completed an additional 6 weeks of supervised PT (July 8, 2024, to August 16, 2024; 18 sessions). In total, the member completed 9 weeks (27 sessions) of supervised physical therapy directed at the cervical spine, which exceeds the plan's 6-week minimum requirement.
   - Functional Plateau: The Capital Physical Therapy discharge summary explicitly documents a functional plateau, stating: "Maximum therapeutic benefit achieved. No further improvement expected from continued PT." This directly refutes the denial's claim that a functional plateau was not documented, satisfying Section 2.2(a).
   - Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): The member completed trials of two qualifying agents for at least 4 weeks each:
     - Gabapentin (neuropathic agent): 600mg TID for 4 months, with partial pain relief but persistent radicular symptoms.
     - Naproxen (NSAID): 500mg BID for 3 months, with inadequate response.
     This satisfies Section 2.2(b), which requires a trial of at least two qualifying agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
   - Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): The member underwent a right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 15, 2024, which provided 35% temporary relief before symptoms returned to baseline within 3 weeks. This satisfies Section 2.2(c).

III. REGULATORY COMPLIANCE AND HIERARCHY OF STANDARDS
Under California law, which supersedes any conflicting internal health plan guidelines, the mechanical application of a physical therapy week-count requirement to deny medically necessary surgery is strictly prohibited:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5:
   This statute dictates that a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy if the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating provider documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)). Because the member completed 9 weeks of PT and reached a documented functional plateau, the plan's mechanical denial based on an incorrect week count violates California law.

2. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b):
   A denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reason for the denial. Because the initial denial was based on a clear factual error (overlooking the Capital PT records that were submitted with the original request), the denial notice is procedurally defective under California law.

IV. CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARDS
According to generally accepted clinical standards, including the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020) and the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023), surgical decompression via ACDF is a Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) intervention for patients with cervical radiculopathy who exhibit persistent radicular pain, objective neurological deficits (such as diminished reflexes and 4/5 grip strength), and structural nerve root compression on MRI that has failed to respond to an exhaustive course of conservative management (including physical therapy, pharmacotherapy, and epidural injections). The member's clinical presentation and treatment history fully align with these clinical standards.

V. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION
Based on the clinical documentation, the member has met all criteria under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, California Insurance Code § 10169.5, and generally accepted clinical standards. The initial denial was a factual error resulting from a failure to review the complete medical record submitted.

Therefore, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED.
