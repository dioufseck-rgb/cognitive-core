Dear Mr. Santos,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its clinical and regulatory review of the first-level appeal submitted on your behalf by Dr. Lisa Wong, MD, regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C6-C7 level (CPT 22551). 

After a comprehensive review of your clinical records, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California statutory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has determined that the original denial issued on October 5, 2024, was in error. Accordingly, the denial is OVERTURNED, and the requested surgical procedure is APPROVED.

Below is a detailed explanation of the clinical, contractual, and regulatory bases for this determination.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND MEDICAL HISTORY
The clinical records submitted demonstrate a mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy presentation at the C6-C7 level, characterized by both severe radicular pain and objective, progressive upper motor neuron (myelopathic) deficits:
1. Imaging: A cervical spine MRI dated September 1, 2024, reveals moderate spinal cord compression at the C6-C7 level accompanied by T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord. It also demonstrates right C7 foraminal stenosis that is more prominent than the left.
2. Neurological Examination: A physical and neurological examination conducted on September 5, 2024, documented:
   - Radicular findings: Right tricep weakness (graded 4/5), right finger numbness, and severe right-sided pain rated 8/10.
   - Myelopathic findings: Bilateral hyperreflexia (right greater than left), a positive right Hoffman sign, an equivocal left Hoffman sign, and a mildly wide-based gait.
3. Conservative Treatment History:
   - Physical Therapy: You completed 6 weeks (18 sessions) of supervised physical therapy utilizing a myelopathy protocol. The physical therapist documented no improvement in either your bilateral neurological signs or your right-sided radicular symptoms.
   - Pharmacotherapy: You completed a 3-month trial of Pregabalin (150mg BID) which yielded only partial right-sided pain relief, and a 2-month trial of Naproxen (500mg BID) which was inadequate.
   - Interventional Treatment: You underwent a right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 28, 2024, which provided 50% temporary relief of right-sided pain that fully returned within 3 weeks, with no change in your bilateral hyperreflexia.

II. PLAN CRITERIA COMPLIANCE ANALYSIS
The original denial issued by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, on October 5, 2024, cited reason code MED-003, stating that the "Myelopathy exception applied but criteria not met" because your symptoms were primarily right-sided (radicular) and the primary diagnosis remained radiculopathy. The reviewer asserted that the myelopathy exception requires myelopathy to be the primary diagnosis.

A rigorous review of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (Document: CC-SPINE-2024) reveals that the plan misapplied its own criteria:
1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): You have a confirmed diagnosis of both cervical radiculopathy (meeting Section 2.1(a) via nerve root compression, tricep weakness, and finger numbness) and cervical myelopathy (meeting Section 2.1(b) via cord compression, hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign, and gait disturbance).
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): The plan's standard criteria require:
   - (a) Supervised physical therapy for a minimum of 6 weeks.
   - (b) Trial of at least two specified pharmacotherapy agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
   - (c) At least one interventional treatment (such as an epidural steroid injection).
Your clinical records document that you completed 6 weeks of physical therapy, trialed two medications (Pregabalin for 3 months and Naproxen for 2 months), and received a right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 28, 2024. 

Therefore, you fully satisfied the standard conservative treatment requirements under Section 2.2. The plan's reviewer erred by evaluating your request solely under the "Myelopathy Exception 2.2(A)" pathway and denying it based on the "primary diagnosis" restriction. Because you met the standard, non-modified conservative treatment criteria, the "primary diagnosis" restriction in the exception clause is irrelevant.

III. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS AND CALIFORNIA REGULATORY OVERRIDES
Under the hierarchy of authority, California statutory provisions and generally accepted clinical standards supersede any restrictive internal plan criteria. Even if you had not completed the standard conservative treatment requirements, California law prohibits the plan from denying this request:
1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3): This statute mandates that a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the condition involves "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Your MRI objectively documents moderate spinal cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the cord. Physical therapy cannot decompress the spinal cord or resolve myelomalacia. Under CIC § 10169.5(c), the plan's mechanical application of physical therapy requirements is prohibited in the presence of such structural compromise.
2. California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014: The DMHC has determined that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a time-sensitive neurological condition with potential for irreversible harm. Under Section II.2 of APL 22-014, plans are prohibited from requiring a full course of conservative treatment when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as cord signal change/myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression. Furthermore, Section II.3 mandates that where a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted clinical standards, the clinical standard governs.

IV. GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, the standard for medical necessity is governed by generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersede restrictive internal plan criteria:
1. Surgical Indications: According to the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update) and the AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021), the presence of moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia) on MRI constitutes a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) for surgical decompression.
2. Inefficacy of Conservative Care: The North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020) and the Cochrane Review (Nikolaidis et al., 2021) establish that conservative treatment, including physical therapy, has limited to no efficacy in arresting the progression of moderate-to-severe myelopathy. 
3. Risk of Delay: Clinical evidence demonstrates that delaying surgical decompression in patients with documented myelomalacia is associated with a high risk of irreversible neurological deterioration and permanently poorer functional outcomes. Requiring extended conservative trials in this clinical scenario is contrary to safe, evidence-based medical practice.

V. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION DETAILS
Based on the clinical documentation of mixed cervical myelopathy-radiculopathy, your full compliance with the plan's standard conservative treatment criteria (Section 2.2), the statutory protections of CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) and DMHC APL 22-014, and generally accepted clinical standards, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED.
