I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The prior authorization denial for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is hereby OVERTURNED. A comprehensive review of the clinical record, HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California statutory and regulatory mandates, and generally accepted clinical guidelines demonstrates that the requested surgical procedure is medically necessary and must be authorized.

The plan's initial denial was based on the assertion that the "Myelopathy exception" did not apply because the member's primary diagnosis was radiculopathy rather than myelopathy. This determination was clinically and legally defective for two independent reasons:
1. Standard Criteria Met: The member fully completed and failed the plan's standard conservative treatment requirements under Section 2.2, including 6 weeks of physical therapy, two trials of pharmacotherapy, and a cervical epidural steroid injection. Therefore, the member did not require the "Myelopathy exception" to qualify for surgery.
2. Regulatory Override: Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, a health plan cannot condition surgical approval on physical therapy when there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (specifically, T2 signal change/myelomalacia on MRI). The plan's restrictive "primary diagnosis" limitation is superseded by California law and generally accepted clinical standards.

II. CLINICAL HISTORY AND PRESENTATION
Mr. David Santos is a 63-year-old male with a mixed presentation of cervical myelopathy and radiculopathy.
- Clinical Symptoms & Exam (September 5, 2024): The member exhibits a predominant right C7 radicular distribution, including right tricep weakness (4/5), right finger numbness, and severe right-sided pain (8/10). Crucially, the neurological exam also documents objective upper motor neuron (myelopathic) findings: bilateral hyperreflexia (right greater than left), a positive right Hoffman sign, an equivocal left Hoffman sign, and a mildly wide-based gait.
- Objective Imaging (September 1, 2024): A cervical MRI demonstrates moderate cord compression at C6-C7 accompanied by T2 signal change (myelomalacia) within the spinal cord, as well as prominent right C7 foraminal stenosis.
- Conservative Treatment History:
  - Physical Therapy: Completed 6 weeks (18 sessions) of a supervised myelopathy protocol. The treating physical therapist documented no improvement in bilateral neurological signs or right-sided radicular symptoms.
  - Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 3-month trial of Pregabalin (150mg BID) with only partial right-sided pain relief, and a 2-month trial of Naproxen (500mg BID) with inadequate response.
  - Interventional Treatment: Received a right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 28, 2024, which provided 50% temporary relief of right-sided pain that fully returned within 3 weeks, with no change in bilateral hyperreflexia.

III. ANALYSIS OF PLAN CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2, the requested ACDF is fully authorized as the member satisfies all standard criteria:
1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): The member has a confirmed diagnosis of both cervical radiculopathy (Section 2.1(a)) and cervical myelopathy (Section 2.1(b)), supported by an MRI within 6 months demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms.
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): The plan's denial incorrectly assumed the member needed to qualify under "Exception 2.2(A) — Myelopathy Pathway" and denied the request because myelopathy was not documented as the "primary" diagnosis. However, the member fully satisfied the standard, non-modified conservative treatment requirements:
   - Section 2.2(a) (Physical Therapy): Completed 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy directed at the cervical spine.
   - Section 2.2(b) (Pharmacotherapy): Completed trials of two neuropathic/NSAID agents (Pregabalin for 3 months, Naproxen for 2 months), exceeding the 4-week minimum.
   - Section 2.2(c) (Interventional): Completed a right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 28, 2024.
Because the standard conservative treatment requirements were fully met, the plan's denial based on the "primary diagnosis" restriction of the myelopathy exception is internally inconsistent and clinically invalid.

IV. ANALYSIS OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK (STATE LAW OVERRIDE)
Even if the member had not completed the standard conservative treatment, California law prohibits the plan from denying this request:
1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3): This statute explicitly mandates that a health insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... [t]he condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Mr. Santos's MRI of September 1, 2024, objectively documents moderate cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia). Myelomalacia represents structural spinal cord injury that physical therapy cannot address. Under CIC § 10169.5(c), the plan's mechanical application of its physical therapy requirements is unlawful.
2. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014: The Department of Managed Health Care has determined that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm. Under Section II.2, plans shall not require completion of conservative treatment when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (cord signal change/myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression.
3. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b): Under the Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard, medical necessity is determined based on generally accepted standards of medical practice, and "does NOT require that the service be consistent with the plan's internal coverage criteria." The plan's restrictive internal guideline requiring myelopathy to be the "primary" diagnosis cannot override state law or clinical standards.

V. ANALYSIS OF CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARDS
Generally accepted clinical guidelines strongly support immediate surgical decompression for patients presenting with cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia:
1. AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023): Surgical decompression is a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) recommendation for patients with moderate-to-severe myelopathy and objective cord compression or myelomalacia (cord signal change) on MRI.
2. AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021): "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
3. NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020): Recommend surgical decompression as the primary treatment for moderate-to-severe myelopathy. Mr. Santos's clinical presentation—including bilateral hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign, and gait disturbance—indicates established myelopathic pathology.
4. Clinical Significance of Myelomalacia: T2 signal change (myelomalacia) indicates active spinal cord injury. Continued compression poses an imminent risk of permanent, irreversible neurological deficits. Delaying surgery to enforce conservative treatment is clinically contraindicated.

VI. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS (HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY)
In accordance with the established hierarchy of authority (State Law > Clinical Standards > Plan Criteria):
1. The plan's internal restriction in CC-SPINE-2024 (requiring myelopathy to be the "primary" diagnosis to waive conservative treatment) directly conflicts with CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) and DMHC APL 22-014, which prohibit conditioning surgery on physical therapy when structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) is present. State law supersedes the plan's internal criteria.
2. The plan's denial conflicts with generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine, NASS), which recognize myelomalacia as an indicator of surgical urgency and a contraindication to delayed surgical intervention. Clinical standards supersede restrictive plan criteria.
3. The plan's denial is internally inconsistent, as it failed to recognize that the member met the standard conservative treatment criteria under Section 2.2, rendering the "primary diagnosis" dispute moot.

VII. DECISION AND AUTHORIZATION
The prior authorization denial for ACDF C6-C7 (CPT 22551) at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center, requested by Dr. Lisa Wong, MD, is OVERTURNED.

HealthFirst is directed to immediately approve and authorize the requested procedure. Under CHSC § 1367.01 and CCR Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1, this authorization is effective immediately.
