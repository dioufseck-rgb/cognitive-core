The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its clinical and regulatory review of the first-level appeal submitted on November 5, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551). 

After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California statutory and regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has determined that the initial prior authorization denial was issued in error. Accordingly, the initial denial is hereby OVERTURNED, and the requested surgical procedure is APPROVED.

I. SUMMARY OF INITIAL DENIAL AND BASIS FOR APPEAL
On October 20, 2024, HealthFirst initially denied the prior authorization request for ACDF C6-C7 (CPT 22551) under Reason Code MED-003. The denial notice stated: "Member does not meet criteria for ACDF. Imaging shows C6-C7 disc herniation with mild cord contact but no measurable compression. No myelopathy findings on exam. PT not yet completed to functional plateau."

The appeal submitted on November 5, 2024, challenged this decision on the following grounds:
1. The member completed 7 weeks of physical therapy (PT) with documented failure to improve.
2. A functional plateau was explicitly reached and documented in the PT discharge summary.
3. The requesting surgeon, Dr. Karen Liu, MD, maintains that ACDF is clinically indicated for persistent, medically refractory C7 radiculopathy.

II. CLINICAL RECORD REVIEW
The clinical evidence submitted for review includes:
1. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) of the Cervical Spine (dated August 1, 2024): Demonstrates a C6-C7 disc herniation with mild cord contact without measurable compression, no cord signal change, no myelomalacia, and mild right C7 foraminal stenosis.
2. Neurological Examination (dated August 5, 2024): Documents right C7 dermatomal distribution numbness, a diminished right tricep reflex, no bilateral findings, no upper motor neuron signs, and a baseline pain score of 7/10 at rest.
3. Conservative Treatment Documentation:
   - Physical Therapy: Completed 7 weeks (21 sessions) of supervised physical therapy. The PT discharge summary explicitly states that a functional plateau was reached, noting that the patient failed to improve over the final 3 sessions, and radicular symptoms persist unchanged.
   - Pharmacotherapy: Documented trials of Pregabalin 150mg BID for 4 months (neuropathic agent) with only partial pain relief, and Meloxicam 15mg daily for 3 months (NSAID) with inadequate response.
   - Interventional Treatment: Right C7 epidural steroid injection performed on September 20, 2024, which provided 30% temporary pain relief lasting 3 weeks before symptoms returned to baseline.

III. PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)
The initial reviewer, Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, evaluated this case under a cervical myelopathy framework, citing a lack of measurable cord compression and myelopathy findings. However, the clinical record establishes that the primary presenting diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, not myelopathy. Under the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery (CC-SPINE-2024), the patient fully satisfies the requirements for the cervical radiculopathy pathway:

1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): Satisfied. The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy under Section 2.1(a), defined as nerve root compression with radicular symptoms (pain, numbness, weakness in a dermatomal distribution). The clinical record documents right C7 dermatomal numbness, a diminished right tricep reflex, and severe pain (7/10), which correlates anatomically with the C6-C7 disc herniation and right C7 foraminal stenosis shown on the MRI dated August 1, 2024.
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): Satisfied.
   - Section 2.2(a) (Physical Therapy): The member completed 7 weeks (21 sessions) of supervised PT, exceeding the plan's 6-week minimum.
   - Section 2.2(b) (Pharmacotherapy): The member completed a 4-month trial of Pregabalin (neuropathic agent) and a 3-month trial of Meloxicam (NSAID), satisfying the requirement for at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
   - Section 2.2(c) (Interventional Treatment): The member underwent a right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 20, 2024, satisfying the requirement for at least one interventional procedure.
3. Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement): Satisfied. The MRI dated August 1, 2024 (within 6 months of the request) demonstrates structural pathology (C6-C7 disc herniation and right C7 foraminal stenosis) consistent with the member's right C7 radicular symptoms and the proposed surgical level.
4. Section 2.4 (Physician Documentation): Satisfied. Dr. Karen Liu, MD, provided the necessary clinical rationale, documenting persistent radiculopathy despite exhaustive conservative care, functional limitations, and surgical risks.

The initial denial's assertion that "PT was not yet completed to functional plateau" represents a direct factual error that contradicts the PT discharge summary, which explicitly documents a functional plateau.

IV. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY COMPLIANCE ANALYSIS
Under California law, which governs this HealthFirst PPO Gold plan, the initial denial is non-compliant on multiple statutory grounds:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):
   - CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician or therapist documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms.
   - CIC § 10169.5(c) mandates that a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau.
   - The initial denial's demand for further physical therapy despite the documented functional plateau at 7 weeks (21 sessions) directly violates CIC § 10169.5.

2. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 (Standard of Review for IMR):
   - CHSC § 1374.32(b) establishes that the standard for medical necessity is based on generally accepted standards of medical practice and does not require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria. Under CHSC § 1374.32(c), internal criteria shall not be the sole or determinative basis for a decision if the service is clinically appropriate and effective under generally accepted standards.

3. DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 (Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard):
   - Part II.3 mandates that plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. Where a conflict exists, the clinical standard governs.

V. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND GENERALLY ACCEPTED STANDARDS OF MEDICAL PRACTICE
Generally accepted standards of medical practice, as compiled in the Clinical Reference Summary, support surgical intervention for this patient:

1. AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Disorders (2023):
   - Under Section 3.1, the patient's clinical presentation meets Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) for surgical decompression (ACDF C6-C7). Tier 2(b) explicitly recommends surgery for "Radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6-12 weeks conservative treatment" where there is anatomical correlation on imaging.
2. Definition of Functional Plateau (Clinical Standard Section 5):
   - A functional plateau is defined as a failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement after a course of therapy, specifically noted as less than 10% improvement over 3 consecutive sessions. The physical therapist's discharge summary explicitly confirms this plateau, noting no improvement over the final 3 sessions and unchanged radicular symptoms.
3. Risk of Delay:
   - Clinical evidence indicates that requiring additional conservative treatment for a patient who has already reached a functional plateau and exhibits objective neurological deficits (diminished reflex, dermatomal numbness) is clinically inappropriate, offers no therapeutic benefit, and unnecessarily delays effective treatment.

VI. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS AND FINAL DISPOSITION
Applying the established hierarchy of authority (California State Law > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Plan Internal Criteria):

1. State Law (CIC § 10169.5) strictly prohibits the plan from denying surgery to demand further physical therapy once a functional plateau has been documented. This statutory mandate overrides any contrary interpretation of the plan's internal criteria.
2. Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines 2023) confirm that ACDF is medically necessary and clinically indicated for persistent C7 radiculopathy following a failed 7-week course of conservative therapy with correlating MRI findings of C6-C7 disc herniation and foraminal stenosis.
3. Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) are fully satisfied when the correct clinical pathway (cervical radiculopathy) is applied. The initial reviewer's application of myelopathy imaging thresholds (demanding cord compression) to a radiculopathy patient was a clinical mismatch.

Because the clinical record demonstrates full compliance with the plan's radiculopathy criteria, and because the initial denial's physical therapy objection is both factually incorrect and legally prohibited under California law, the initial denial cannot be sustained.

DECISION:
The prior authorization denial for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED. The procedure is APPROVED.
