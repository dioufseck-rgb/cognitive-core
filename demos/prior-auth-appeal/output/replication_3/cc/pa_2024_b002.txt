Dear Mr. Reed and Dr. Liu,

The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial decision to deny coverage has been OVERTURNED. 

The requested procedure is APPROVED. This authorization is valid for 90 days from the date of this letter, expiring on February 6, 2025, in accordance with HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria Section 3.5.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The initial prior authorization denial issued on October 20, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, was based on two primary findings: (1) that imaging showed mild cord contact without measurable compression or myelopathy, and (2) that physical therapy (PT) had not been completed to a functional plateau. 

Upon appeal review, this denial is overturned due to a clear factual error in the initial review and a misapplication of clinical criteria:
1. Factual Error regarding Physical Therapy: The clinical record demonstrates that the member completed 7 weeks (21 sessions) of supervised physical therapy, and the PT discharge summary explicitly documents that a functional plateau was reached, with no improvement over the final 3 sessions.
2. Misapplication of Diagnostic Criteria: The initial reviewer evaluated the request under myelopathy-related standards (noting a lack of cord compression or myelopathy findings). However, the member's primary presenting diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy. Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, cord compression and myelopathy are not required to authorize surgery for a primary diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy.
3. Statutory Non-Compliance: Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5, a health plan is prohibited from conditioning surgical approval on further physical therapy once a functional plateau has been documented.

II. CLINICAL HISTORY AND PRESENTING PRESENTATION
Mr. Thomas Reed is a 52-year-old male with a history of persistent, severe right-sided C6-C7 cervical radiculopathy. 
- Clinical Symptoms & Exam (Neurological Exam dated August 5, 2024): The member presents with right C7 dermatomal distribution numbness, a diminished right tricep reflex, and a rest pain score of 7/10. No upper motor neuron signs or bilateral findings are present, confirming the absence of myelopathy.
- Imaging (MRI dated August 1, 2024): Demonstrates a C6-C7 disc herniation with mild cord contact (without measurable compression, cord signal change, or myelomalacia) and mild right C7 foraminal stenosis. This structural pathology directly correlates with the member's right C7 dermatomal symptoms and diminished tricep reflex.
- Conservative Treatment History:
  - Physical Therapy: Completed 7 weeks (21 sessions) of supervised physical therapy directed at the cervical spine. The PT discharge summary explicitly notes that a functional plateau was reached, with the patient's radicular symptoms remaining unchanged and showing no improvement over the final 3 sessions.
  - Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 4-month trial of Pregabalin (150mg BID) with only partial pain relief and persistent radicular symptoms, and a 3-month trial of Meloxicam (15mg) which was inadequate.
  - Interventional Treatment: Received a right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 20, 2024, which provided 30% temporary pain relief for 3 weeks before symptoms returned to baseline.

III. EVALUATION OF HEALTHFIRST CLINICAL COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
The requested ACDF C6-C7 (CPT 22551) fully satisfies all medical necessity criteria outlined in Section 2 of CC-SPINE-2024:

The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy under Section 2.1(a), defined as nerve root compression with radicular symptoms (documented right C7 dermatomal numbness and pain) and cervical disc herniation with an objective neurological deficit under Section 2.1(c) (documented diminished right tricep reflex). The MRI dated August 1, 2024, was performed within 6 months of the request and demonstrates structural pathology (C6-C7 disc herniation and right C7 foraminal stenosis) consistent with these symptoms.

   - Section 2.2(a) (Physical Therapy): The member completed 7 weeks of supervised PT, exceeding the plan's 6-week minimum requirement.
   - Section 2.2(b) (Pharmacotherapy): The member completed trials of two required classes for more than 4 weeks each: Pregabalin (neuropathic agent, 4 months) and Meloxicam (NSAID, 3 months).
   - Section 2.2(c) (Interventional Treatment): The member completed a right C7 epidural steroid injection on September 20, 2024.

IV. EVALUATION OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY REQUIREMENTS
Under the California regulatory framework, the initial denial is legally non-compliant and must be overturned:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite): Under subdivision (a)(2), a health plan shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the member has completed a course of PT and the treating physician or physical therapist documents that the member reached a "functional plateau" without adequate resolution of symptoms. Subdivision (b) defines a functional plateau as a documented failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement. The initial denial's assertion that "PT [was] not yet completed to functional plateau" directly contradicts the PT discharge summary and violates CIC § 10169.5 by mechanically applying a PT requirement despite documented plateau.
2. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b) (Defective Denial Notice): Because the initial denial relied on a factual error (stating PT was not completed to a plateau when the clinical record explicitly documented a plateau), the denial notice is procedurally defective.
3. CHSC § 1374.32 (Standard of Review for Independent Medical Review): Under California law, "medically necessary" services are those that a prudent physician would provide in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice. Subdivision (b) dictates that the clinical standard of care governs, regardless of whether a plan's internal criteria are more restrictive. As demonstrated below, the requested surgery meets generally accepted clinical standards.

V. EVALUATION OF CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND STANDARDS OF CARE
The requested procedure is highly supported by generally accepted standards of medical practice:

1. AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy and Radiculopathy (2023 Update): Under Section 3.1, Mr. Reed's clinical presentation meets Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) for surgical intervention. Surgery is recommended for patients with cervical radiculopathy who have persistent, severe symptoms (pain 7/10, objective sensory and reflex deficits) and correlating imaging findings after an adequate trial of conservative treatment (6-12 weeks). Mr. Reed has failed 7 weeks of PT, 4 months of neuropathic medication, and an epidural steroid injection.
2. Definition of Functional Plateau (Clinical Standard Section 5): A functional plateau is clinically defined as a failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement over 3 consecutive therapy sessions. The PT discharge summary explicitly notes that Mr. Reed did not improve over his final 3 sessions, satisfying the clinical definition of a functional plateau.

VI. RESOLUTION OF CROSS-SOURCE CONFLICTS
The initial denial created a conflict by applying myelopathy-related imaging expectations (requiring measurable cord compression or myelopathy findings) to a radiculopathy patient, and by asserting that PT was incomplete. 

Applying the established hierarchy of authority (California State Law > Clinical Standards > Plan Criteria):
1. State Law Governs PT Requirements: Under CIC § 10169.5, the documented functional plateau in the PT discharge summary legally supersedes any plan-level interpretation of PT completion. The plan cannot require further conservative therapy.
2. Clinical Standards Govern Diagnostic Expectations: Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) and CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1, a patient with radiculopathy does not require spinal cord compression or myelomalacia to qualify for surgery. The presence of a C6-C7 disc herniation with correlating right C7 nerve root deficits (numbness, diminished tricep reflex) is a complete and sufficient indication for a single-level ACDF. The initial reviewer's focus on "mild cord contact but no measurable compression" is clinically irrelevant to a radiculopathy diagnosis.

VII. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION
Based on the clinical evidence, the member has met all diagnostic, imaging, and conservative treatment requirements under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024. Furthermore, California law (CIC § 10169.5) prohibits the plan from denying coverage based on further physical therapy requirements. 

Accordingly, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED, and the request for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is APPROVED.
