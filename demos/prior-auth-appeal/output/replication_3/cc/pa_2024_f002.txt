Dear Mr. Clark and Dr. Nguyen,

The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical records, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial decision to deny coverage has been OVERTURNED. The requested surgical procedure is APPROVED.

I. BACKGROUND AND INITIAL DENIAL REASON
On October 10, 2024, HealthFirst initially denied prior authorization for the requested ACDF C5-C6 procedure under Reason Code MED-003. The sole basis for the denial was an apparent discrepancy in the physical therapy (PT) documentation. The plan noted that records from Riverside Physical Therapy documented 6 weeks of therapy, while the initial records submitted for Coastal Physical Therapy documented only 4 weeks, creating an unresolved conflict regarding the total duration of conservative treatment. The plan required verification of the claimed PT duration prior to approval.

On October 25, 2024, an appeal was submitted clarifying that the initial Coastal PT submission was incomplete because the final 2 weeks of records had been faxed separately. The complete 6-week records for Coastal PT have now been enclosed and verified.

II. CLINICAL HISTORY AND EVIDENCE REVIEWED
The clinical record submitted for review establishes the following:
1. Diagnosis and Clinical Presentation: The patient is a 46-year-old male with a primary diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy at C5-C6 with significant functional impairment.
2. Objective Neurological Deficits: A neurological examination dated August 20, 2024, documents objective deficits, including right C6 dermatomal numbness, 4/5 motor weakness in the right bicep, an absent right bicep reflex, and a positive right Spurling's sign.
3. Diagnostic Imaging: An MRI of the cervical spine dated August 15, 2024, demonstrates a large disc herniation at C5-C6 causing severe right C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis.
4. Conservative Treatment History:
   - Physical Therapy:
     - Riverside Physical Therapy (April 1, 2024 – May 10, 2024): 6 weeks, 18 sessions. Complete records received. Outcome: Documented functional plateau with persistent symptoms.
     - Coastal Physical Therapy (June 1, 2024 – July 12, 2024): 6 weeks, 18 sessions. Complete records now received. Outcome: No improvement in neurological deficits; therapist documented a functional plateau.
     - Total PT: 12 weeks (36 sessions) of supervised physical therapy.
   - Interventional Treatment: A right C6 epidural steroid injection was performed on August 28, 2024, resulting in 60% temporary relief, with symptoms returning to baseline within 3 weeks.
   - Pharmacotherapy:
     - Gabapentin 600mg TID (three times daily) for 5 months, yielding partial relief.
     - Duloxetine 60mg daily for 3 months, yielding partial neuropathic benefit.

III. ANALYSIS OF PLAN CRITERIA COMPLIANCE
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2, authorization for cervical spinal surgery requires satisfaction of the following:
1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1(a)): Met. The patient has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with correlating MRI evidence of severe right C6 nerve root compression and objective neurological deficits (4/5 bicep weakness, absent reflex).
2. Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): Met. The plan requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. The undisputed records from Riverside Physical Therapy independently document 6 weeks (18 sessions) of completed therapy with a documented functional plateau. Furthermore, the newly submitted complete records from Coastal Physical Therapy document an additional 6 weeks (18 sessions), totaling 12 weeks of supervised PT.
3. Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): Met. The patient underwent a right C6 epidural steroid injection on August 28, 2024, satisfying the requirement for at least one interventional treatment.
4. Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): Technically Non-Compliant under Plan Criteria.
   - Section 2.2(b) requires a trial of at least "two of the following" categories of medications for a minimum of 4 weeks each: NSAIDs/COX-2 inhibitors, Muscle relaxants, Neuropathic agents, or Oral corticosteroids.
   - The patient's records document trials of Gabapentin (5 months) and Duloxetine (3 months). Both medications are classified as neuropathic agents under the same bullet point in Section 2.2(b).
   - Because the patient trialed two medications from the same category (neuropathic agents) rather than two different categories, the pharmacotherapy requirement is technically not met under a strict, literal reading of the plan's internal criteria.

IV. REGULATORY AND CLINICAL STANDARD OVERRIDE
While the patient's pharmacotherapy trial does not strictly comply with the plan's internal criteria, California law and generally accepted clinical standards dictate that the denial must be overturned.

1. Primacy of Clinical Standards over Plan Criteria (California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32):
   - Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the standard of review for medical necessity does not require consistency with a health plan's internal coverage criteria. If a requested service is medically necessary under generally accepted standards of medical practice, the plan's more restrictive internal criteria are superseded.
   - Generally accepted clinical guidelines, including the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020) and the American Association of Neurological Surgeons / Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Joint Section Guidelines (2023), state that surgical decompression (ACDF) is indicated for patients with cervical radiculopathy who exhibit persistent symptoms and objective neurological deficits (such as 4/5 motor weakness and reflex loss) after an adequate trial of conservative care (typically 6 to 12 weeks).
   - The patient's conservative trial is exceptionally robust, consisting of 12 weeks of supervised physical therapy (double the plan's requirement), a targeted cervical epidural steroid injection, and two distinct neuropathic medications (Gabapentin and Duloxetine) trialed for 5 and 3 months respectively.
   - Requiring this patient—who has severe, MRI-confirmed nerve root compression and objective motor weakness—to undergo an additional 4-week trial of a different class of medication (such as NSAIDs or muscle relaxants) is clinically inappropriate, lacks clinical evidence support, and poses an unnecessary risk of progressive, irreversible neurological deficit. Under clinical standards, the patient's conservative trial is highly robust and clinically sufficient. Therefore, under CHSC § 1374.32, the clinical standard governs, and the conservative treatment requirement is satisfied.

2. Procedural Defect in Denial Notice (California Health & Safety Code § 1374.31(b)):
   - Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a health plan's denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the denial. Any reason not raised in the initial denial is procedurally waived for the purposes of the appeal.
   - The plan's initial denial on October 10, 2024, was based solely on the physical therapy documentation discrepancy (MED-003). The plan did not raise any objection regarding the pharmacotherapy trial.
   - Because the member has fully resolved the sole stated reason for denial by submitting the complete Coastal PT records, and because the Riverside PT records independently satisfied the 6-week requirement, the plan cannot introduce a new, technical criteria-based objection (the pharmacotherapy class discrepancy) for the first time on appeal. Raising this new issue now constitutes a procedural defect under CHSC § 1374.31(b).

V. DISPOSITION
Based on the resolution of the physical therapy documentation discrepancy, the clinical robustness of the patient's conservative treatment under generally accepted clinical standards (NASS 2020; AANS/CNS 2023), and the regulatory requirements of California Health & Safety Code §§ 1374.31(b) and 1374.32, the prior authorization denial for ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED.

The procedure is approved. This authorization is valid for 90 days from the date of this letter (expiring January 26, 2025) per Section 3.5 of the Clinical Coverage Criteria.
