Dear Ms. Kamau and Dr. Osei,

The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) at UCSF Medical Center. After a comprehensive clinical and regulatory evaluation of the member's medical records, the plan's internal coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), and applicable California statutory mandates, HealthFirst has OVERTURNED the initial denial. The requested surgical procedure is APPROVED.

I. BACKGROUND AND INITIAL DENIAL ANALYSIS
On November 20, 2024, HealthFirst Utilization Management issued a denial of the prior authorization request for a single-level ACDF C5-C6. The sole basis for the denial was the member's prior authorization history—specifically, a denial in May 2024 for a more extensive three-level cervical fusion (C4-C7). The initial reviewer, Dr. Alan Foster, MD, asserted that the member's cervical pathology had not materially changed since the prior denial and that the current request did not warrant reconsideration.

Upon administrative and clinical review, this denial basis is determined to be procedurally and substantively flawed:
1. Unauthorized Denial Basis: Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, prior authorization history is not an authorized clinical criterion or exclusion. Each prior authorization request must be evaluated independently on its own clinical merits.
2. Procedural Defect under California Law: Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), a denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the denial. Because the initial denial notice failed to apply any valid clinical criteria from CC-SPINE-2024 and relied entirely on an unauthorized administrative basis (prior PA history), the denial notice is legally DEFECTIVE.
3. Factual Mischaracterization: The May 2024 denial was for a three-level fusion (C4-C7) because the C4-C5 and C6-C7 levels did not meet criteria. The prior reviewer explicitly noted at that time that a single-level C5-C6 fusion would have been approved. The current request is for that exact single-level C5-C6 procedure, supported by new clinical evidence of progressive deterioration.

II. STRICT VERIFICATION AGAINST PLAN CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
A rule-by-rule evaluation of the member's clinical record against CC-SPINE-2024 reveals that while the core clinical indications are met, there are specific internal criteria non-conformances and documentation gaps.

1. Section 2.1: Diagnosis Requirement — MET
The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy C5-C6 with progressive nerve root compression and radicular symptoms (right C6 dermatomal numbness, absent right bicep reflex, and worsening right bicep weakness graded at 3+/5). This is supported by a current MRI dated October 15, 2024, demonstrating a large C5-C6 disc herniation with moderate-to-severe right C6 nerve root compression and new foraminal stenosis, representing an interval increase in size from the prior MRI dated April 20, 2024.

2. Section 2.2(a): Physical Therapy Requirement — MET (With Acknowledged Discrepancy)
- Plan Criteria: Minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy directed toward the cervical condition.
- Clinical Record: The member completed an 8-week supervised PT course (24 sessions).
- Discrepancy Acknowledgment: The clinical change summary contains a factual contradiction, stating the PT course was completed in "October 2024," whereas the physical therapy summary states it was completed "July-September 2024." To resolve this discrepancy, HealthFirst relies on the primary physical therapy records, which confirm the course was completed between July and September 2024. This timeline is clinically logical as it preceded the October 15, 2024 MRI and the October 20, 2024 neurological exam.
- Functional Plateau: The physical therapy records explicitly document a functional plateau: "no further improvement expected, neurological deficit persistent and worsening." Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2), a health plan cannot condition surgical approval on further physical therapy once a functional plateau is documented.

3. Section 2.2(b): Pharmacotherapy Duration Requirement — NON-CONFORMANT
- Plan Criteria: Trial of at least two covered agents (NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
- Clinical Record: The member trialed Gabapentin 600mg TID for 6 months (MET). However, the second agent was "Methylprednisolone dose pack x2" for "2 courses."
- Non-Conformance: A standard oral corticosteroid dose pack is a 6-day tapered course. Two courses equal approximately 12 days of therapy, which fails to meet the mandatory 4-week (28-day) minimum required under Section 2.2(b). Because the primary diagnosis is radiculopathy, the myelopathy conservative treatment exceptions under Section 2.2(A) do not apply.

4. Section 2.2(c): Interventional Treatment Requirement — MET
The member completed a right C6 epidural steroid injection on November 5, 2024, which provided 50% relief for 2 weeks before symptoms returned to baseline.

5. Section 2.3: Imaging Requirement — MET
The current MRI (October 15, 2024) was performed within 6 months of the request and demonstrates structural pathology (large C5-C6 disc herniation with moderate-to-severe right C6 nerve root compression) directly correlating with the member's right C6 radiculopathy symptoms.

6. Section 2.4: Physician Documentation — PARTIALLY NON-CONFORMANT
- Plan Criteria: Requires treating physician attestation of conservative treatment (2.4(a)), clinical rationale (2.4(b)), description of functional limitations (2.4(c)), and a "Statement of surgical risks and alternatives considered" (2.4(d)).
- Non-Conformance: The clinical record contains detailed documentation for 2.4(a), (b), and (c), but lacks the mandatory "Statement of surgical risks and alternatives considered" required under Section 2.4(d).

III. RESOLUTION OF NON-CONFORMANCES VIA CALIFORNIA REGULATORY OVERRIDES
Although the clinical record exhibits technical non-conformance with the plan's internal criteria regarding pharmacotherapy duration (Section 2.2(b)) and physician documentation (Section 2.4(d)), California regulatory mandates compel an OVERTURN of the denial.

1. Clinical Standard Override of Pharmacotherapy Duration (CHSC § 1374.32)
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), the standard for medical necessity on review is governed by generally accepted standards of medical practice, not a plan's more restrictive internal criteria.
- Clinical Inappropriateness of Plan Criteria: Requiring a continuous 4-week (28-day) trial of oral corticosteroids is clinically inappropriate and contrary to standard medical practice. Prolonged oral corticosteroid therapy carries severe systemic risks, including adrenal suppression, avascular necrosis of the femoral head, and severe metabolic disturbances. Standard clinical practice guidelines (AANS/CNS and NASS) utilize short-course corticosteroid dose packs (typically 6 to 12 days) to treat acute radicular flares.
- Clinical Standard Met: The member has documented progressive neurological deficits (worsening right bicep weakness to 3+/5 and an absent bicep reflex) and correlating progressive MRI findings. Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), surgical decompression is strongly indicated (Tier 2) for radiculopathy with persistent symptoms and progressive neurological deficits after 6–12 weeks of conservative treatment (satisfied here by 8 weeks of PT, 6 months of Gabapentin, and an epidural injection).
- Regulatory Resolution: Because the plan's internal 4-week corticosteroid requirement is more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice and poses unnecessary clinical risk, CHSC § 1374.32 and DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 dictate that the clinical standard governs. The member's conservative pharmacotherapy trial is clinically sufficient.

2. Resolution of Missing Physician Documentation (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
The omission of the Section 2.4(d) risk/benefit statement is a documentation gap. However, because HealthFirst's initial denial notice was procedurally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b) (failing to cite any valid clinical criteria or identify this documentation omission, and instead relying on an unauthorized prior PA history basis), the plan is legally precluded from raising this post-hoc administrative omission to uphold the denial on appeal. The objective clinical necessity of the procedure is fully established by the medical records, and the procedural defect of the initial denial requires that the clinical merits govern.

IV. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION DETAILS
The clinical evidence demonstrates that Theresa Kamau has progressive C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy with objective, worsening neurological deficits (3+/5 bicep weakness, absent reflex) and correlating progressive MRI findings. She has exhausted appropriate conservative therapies, including an 8-week course of physical therapy resulting in a documented functional plateau, 6 months of neuropathic medication, and a cervical epidural steroid injection.

While the request did not strictly conform to the plan's internal 4-week corticosteroid duration or include a formal risk statement, California regulatory frameworks (CHSC §§ 1374.31 and 1374.32) override these restrictive internal criteria in favor of generally accepted clinical standards. Delaying surgery to enforce an clinically inappropriate 4-week steroid trial or to reject the request based on a prior, unrelated PA history would violate California law and place the member at risk of irreversible neurological damage.

Accordingly, the denial is OVERTURNED, and the requested procedure is APPROVED.
