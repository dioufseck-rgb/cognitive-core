The initial notice failed to cite the plan's specific clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) or provide a detailed clinical analysis of the C6-C7 level.

To fully cure this procedural defect and protect the member's rights, HealthFirst has conducted a comprehensive, de novo clinical and regulatory review on appeal under CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2). SECTION 2 — CLINICAL FINDINGS AND CRITERIA APPLICATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
HealthFirst evaluates requests for cervical spinal procedures under Clinical Coverage Criteria document CC-SPINE-2024 (Effective January 1, 2024). 

1. C5-C6 LEVEL (CPT 22551) — OVERTURNED (APPROVED)
De novo review of the clinical record demonstrates that the C5-C6 level fully satisfies all medical necessity criteria under CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2:
- Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1): The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy. The MRI dated September 15, 2024, reveals a large disc herniation with moderate-to-severe right C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis. The neurological exam dated September 20, 2024, documents correlating objective deficits: right bicep weakness (3+/5), an absent right bicep reflex, and right C6 dermatomal numbness and tingling.
- Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2): The member completed 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy (24 sessions) with persistent C6 symptoms, satisfying Section 2.2(a). Pharmacotherapy requirements under Section 2.2(b) were met with a 4-month trial of Pregabalin (150mg BID) and a 3-month trial of Naproxen (500mg BID). Interventional requirements under Section 2.2(c) were met via a right C6 epidural steroid injection on October 15, 2024, which provided 70% temporary relief before symptoms fully returned.

Because the C5-C6 level meets all plan criteria and aligns with California's medical necessity standard under CHSC § 1374.32, the denial of CPT 22551 is OVERTURNED and APPROVED.

2. C6-C7 LEVEL (CPT 22552) — REMANDED
Review of the clinical record demonstrates that the C6-C7 level does not currently meet the medical necessity criteria under CC-SPINE-2024:
- Imaging Requirement (Section 2.3 & Exclusion 3.3): The MRI dated September 15, 2024, shows only a small disc bulge and mild foraminal narrowing at C6-C7. No nerve root compression is identifiable, and there is no spinal cord contact or signal change.
- Clinical Correlation (Section 2.1): The neurological exam dated September 20, 2024, notes a slightly diminished right tricep reflex, but no definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms. Furthermore, physical therapy records at week 8 document that C7-related symptoms were minimal and possibly resolving.
- Conservative Treatment (Section 2.2): No C7-specific interventional treatment (such as a selective nerve root block) has been performed to isolate this level as a primary pain generator.

SECTION 3 — CLINICAL REBUTTAL: ADJACENT SEGMENT DEGENERATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
In the appeal submission, the treating physician (Dr. Lisa Park) argued that both levels must be addressed simultaneously to prevent accelerated adjacent segment degeneration (ASD) at C6-C7.

Under generally accepted standards of medical practice, including the North American Spine Society (NASS) 2020 Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines and the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023), prophylactic fusion of a mildly degenerated, asymptomatic, or clinically non-correlative level is not clinically indicated. 

Biomechanical and clinical literature demonstrates that extending a fusion to an asymptomatic level:
1. Increases overall biomechanical stiffness of the cervical spine, which shifts greater mechanical stress to the next adjacent segment (C7-T1), thereby accelerating degeneration at that next level.
2. Exposes the patient to unnecessary surgical risks, including increased rates of pseudarthrosis (non-union), longer operative times, and greater blood loss, without any documented clinical benefit.

Generally accepted standards dictate that cervical fusion must be strictly limited to symptomatic levels with clear objective clinical and radiographic correlation. Prophylactic fusion to "protect" adjacent segments is unsupported by high-quality clinical evidence. Therefore, the C6-C7 level cannot be approved on the basis of adjacent segment protection.

SECTION 4 — SURGICAL PLAN MODIFICATION & CPT 22552 LIMITATIONS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
HealthFirst acknowledges that CPT 22552 is an add-on code representing the second level of an ACDF and cannot be performed or authorized independently of the primary code (CPT 22551). Under California law, forcing a surgeon to perform a single-level fusion when a two-level fusion was planned could be construed as an impermissible clinical modification of a surgical plan.

HealthFirst explicitly clarifies that it is not modifying the surgical plan or practicing medicine. The final clinical decision of whether to proceed with a single-level C5-C6 ACDF, or to defer surgery entirely until further diagnostic evidence is obtained for the C6-C7 level, rests solely with the treating surgeon (Dr. Lisa Park) and the patient (Victor Patel).

By approving CPT 22551 and remanding C6-C7, the plan secures immediate authorization for the medically necessary portion of the procedure, while providing a clear, structured pathway to authorize the second level if supporting clinical evidence is developed.

Upon receipt of this objective clinical evidence, the C6-C7 level will be re-evaluated for immediate authorization.
