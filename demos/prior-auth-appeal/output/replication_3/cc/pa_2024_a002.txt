Dear Mr. Webb and Dr. Chen,

The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals Department has completed its review of the appeal regarding the prior authorization denial for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California statutory mandates, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has OVERTURNED the initial denial. Prior authorization for the requested procedure is hereby APPROVED.

I. BACKGROUND AND INITIAL DENIAL
On November 5, 2024, HealthFirst initially denied prior authorization for CPT 22551 under Reason Code MED-003. The denial was based on the plan's Clinical Coverage Criteria (Document: CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2), which requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. The reviewer, Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, noted that the member had completed only 4 weeks of physical therapy. Furthermore, the plan determined that the member did not qualify for the "Myelopathy Pathway Exception" (Section 2.2(A)), which reduces the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks, because the primary diagnosis listed on the physical therapy referrals and the most recent billing form was "cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy" (ICD-10 M47.812) rather than cervical myelopathy.

II. CLINICAL RECORD AND EVIDENCE EVALUATED
The clinical record contains objective evidence of severe, progressive structural neurological compromise:
1. Clinical Presentation (July 1, 2024): The patient presented with bilateral hand weakness, coordination problems, and upper motor neuron (UMN) signs.
2. Neurological Examination (July 1, 2024): Documented bilateral grip weakness (3/5), bilateral positive Hoffman sign, bilateral upper extremity hyperreflexia, and impaired tandem gait.
3. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) (July 10, 2024): Demonstrated a C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate spinal cord compression and T2 signal change within the cord consistent with early myelomalacia.
4. Conservative Treatment History:
   - Physical Therapy: Completed 4 weeks (12 sessions). Dr. Chen halted further physical therapy after the MRI confirmed myelomalacia, documenting in writing that further physical therapy was contraindicated due to the risk of neurological deterioration.
   - Pharmacotherapy: Gabapentin 300mg TID for 3 months (provided partial pain relief but no improvement in weakness) and Naproxen 500mg BID for 2 months (inadequate response), satisfying Section 2.2(b).
   - Interventional Treatment: Cervical epidural steroid injection on September 15, 2024 (minimal improvement, no change in motor deficits), satisfying Section 2.2(c).
5. Billing Discrepancy: The ICD-10 code M47.812 (cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy) was utilized on physical therapy referrals. However, Dr. Chen's narrative clinical notes consistently describe cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia, clarifying that the billing code was an administrative artifact.

III. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS & HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY
To resolve the conflict between the plan's internal criteria and the clinical/regulatory realities of this case, we apply the established hierarchy of authority: California State Law > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Plan's Internal Coverage Criteria.

1. CALIFORNIA STATE LAW OVERRIDE (CONTROLLING AUTHORITY)
Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a), a health insurer is prohibited from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy under several specific circumstances, two of which are directly applicable here:
   - CIC § 10169.5(a)(3): Physical therapy cannot be mandated when the member's condition involves "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The patient's MRI of July 10, 2024, provides objective evidence of moderate spinal cord compression and early myelomalacia (T2 signal change), which is a severe structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot resolve.
   - CIC § 10169.5(a)(1): Physical therapy cannot be mandated when the treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is "contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition." Dr. Chen explicitly documented that further physical therapy was contraindicated after myelomalacia was confirmed.
   - DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014: This regulatory mandate states that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm. Under Section II.2, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization" when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression.
   - CIC § 10169.5(c): Explicitly prohibits the mechanical application of a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy without reference to neurological urgency under subdivision (a)(3).

Therefore, California law mandates the waiver of any remaining physical therapy requirements. The plan's mechanical enforcement of a 6-week physical therapy trial is a direct violation of CIC § 10169.5 and APL 22-014.

2. GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS (SECONDARY AUTHORITY)
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), medical necessity determinations must align with generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersede restrictive internal plan criteria.
   - AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023): The patient's clinical presentation (bilateral hand weakness, hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign, impaired tandem gait) combined with MRI evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia meets Tier 1 (Strong Indication) criteria for surgical decompression.
   - AO Spine Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021): Explicitly state that there is "no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy." The guidelines warn that delaying surgery to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by evidence and "may result in neurological deterioration."
   - NASS Clinical Guidelines (2020): Recommend surgical decompression as the primary treatment for moderate to severe myelopathy, noting that conservative treatment has limited efficacy and poses a risk of irreversible cord damage when myelomalacia is present.

The clinical evidence demonstrates that requiring additional physical therapy for a patient with active myelomalacia is clinically inappropriate, lacks scientific support, and exposes the patient to a high risk of permanent neurological deficit.

3. PLAN CLINICAL COVERAGE CRITERIA (TERTIARY AUTHORITY)
Even under a strict reading of the plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), the denial was improper:
   - Section 2.1(b) (Diagnosis Requirement): The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, supported by objective clinical findings (gait disturbance, bilateral upper extremity weakness, hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign) and a current MRI demonstrating structural pathology consistent with these symptoms.
   - Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway Exception): This exception reduces the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks. The member completed exactly 4 weeks (12 sessions) of physical therapy.
   - The Billing Discrepancy: The plan's initial denial relied on the administrative billing code (M47.812) to deny the Myelopathy Pathway Exception. However, clinical narrative notes consistently documented cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia. Under clinical and regulatory standards, the clinical narrative and objective imaging findings (MRI showing myelomalacia) govern over administrative billing codes. The patient clinically qualified for the Myelopathy Pathway Exception, and the 4 weeks of completed physical therapy were sufficient.

IV. CONCLUSION
The prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED. The requested single-level ACDF at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is medically necessary and is hereby approved.
