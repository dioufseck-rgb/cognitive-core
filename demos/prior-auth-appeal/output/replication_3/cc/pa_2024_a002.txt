APPEAL DETERMINATION: OVERTURNED

Dear Mr. Webb and Dr. Chen,

The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the prior authorization appeal for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) to be performed at UCSF Medical Center. After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California statutory and regulatory mandates, and generally accepted clinical standards of care, the initial prior authorization denial issued on November 05, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED. 

The requested surgical procedure is APPROVED.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The initial denial of prior authorization was based on a mechanical application of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2, which requires a minimum of 6 weeks of physical therapy. The plan determined that the member completed only 4 weeks of physical therapy and did not qualify for the Myelopathy Pathway Exception (Section 2.2(A)) because the primary diagnosis code listed on the physical therapy referral and billing forms was "cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy" (ICD-10 M47.812) rather than cervical myelopathy.

Upon appellate review, this denial is overturned because it violates California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5, California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, and California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014. Under California law, a health plan cannot condition surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy when there is objective clinical and imaging evidence of structural neurological compromise (specifically, spinal cord compression and myelomalacia) that physical therapy cannot address, or when the treating physician documents that further physical therapy is contraindicated. Furthermore, generally accepted clinical standards of care, including guidelines from the American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS) and the Congress of Neurological Surgeons (CNS), establish that moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia is a Tier 1 strong indication for urgent surgical decompression, and delaying surgery to complete physical therapy poses a high risk of irreversible neurological harm.

II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND HISTORY
According to the clinical record, Mr. Marcus Webb is a 47-year-old male who presented on July 01, 2024, with a clinical history of bilateral hand weakness, coordination problems, and upper motor neuron (UMN) signs. 
- Neurological Examination (dated July 01, 2024): Documented bilateral grip weakness of 3/5, bilateral positive Hoffman sign, bilateral upper extremity hyperreflexia, and an impaired tandem gait.
- Imaging (MRI dated July 10, 2024): Demonstrated a C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate spinal cord compression and T2 signal change within the spinal cord consistent with early myelomalacia.
- Conservative Treatment History:
  1. Physical Therapy: Completed 4 weeks (12 sessions). Dr. Sarah Chen halted physical therapy after the MRI confirmed myelomalacia, documenting in writing that further physical therapy is contraindicated due to the risk of neurological deterioration.
  2. Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 3-month trial of Gabapentin 300mg TID (resulting in partial pain relief but no improvement in weakness) and a 2-month trial of Naproxen 500mg BID (inadequate response), satisfying the plan's requirement for two agents trialed for a minimum of 4 weeks each under Section 2.2(b).
  3. Interventional Treatment: Underwent a cervical epidural steroid injection on September 15, 2024, which resulted in minimal improvement and no change in motor deficits, satisfying Section 2.2(c).

III. PLAN CLINICAL CRITERIA ANALYSIS
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1, a member must have a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (2.1(a)), cervical myelopathy (2.1(b)), or cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit (2.1(c)). Mr. Webb's clinical presentation of gait disturbance, bilateral upper extremity weakness, hyperreflexia, and a positive Hoffman sign satisfies the diagnostic criteria for cervical myelopathy under Section 2.1(b).

The initial denial cited Section 2.2, stating that the member failed to complete the standard 6-week physical therapy course. The plan refused to apply the Myelopathy Pathway Exception (Section 2.2(A))—which reduces the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks—because the primary billing code used on the physical therapy referral was "cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy" (ICD-10 M47.812).

This appellate review finds that the plan's mechanical reliance on the administrative billing code to deny the exception is improper. The clinical narrative notes consistently describe cervical myelopathy and objective myelomalacia on MRI. Dr. Chen's documentation notes that the billing code was an administrative artifact. Under Section 2.1(b) of the plan's criteria, the clinical findings themselves confirm the diagnosis of cervical myelopathy. Because the member has confirmed cervical myelopathy, the Myelopathy Pathway Exception (Section 2.2(A)) applies, reducing the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks. Since the member completed 4 weeks of physical therapy, the conservative treatment requirements under Section 2.2 are fully satisfied.

IV. REGULATORY OVERRIDE AND CALIFORNIA LAW
Even if the plan's internal criteria did not permit the Myelopathy Pathway Exception due to the billing code discrepancy, California statutory and regulatory mandates override the plan's internal guidelines:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy Prerequisites:
   - Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Mr. Webb's MRI demonstrates moderate cord compression and T2 signal change (myelomalacia), which constitutes objective structural neurological compromise.
   - Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(1), physical therapy cannot be mandated when the treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated. Dr. Chen explicitly documented that further physical therapy was halted and contraindicated after myelomalacia was confirmed.
   - Under CIC § 10169.5(c), a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the condition involves neurological urgency.

2. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 — Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions:
   - Section II.1 of APL 22-014 states that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated, and plans must treat such cases as urgent.
   - Section II.2 prohibits plans from requiring a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as cord signal change/myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed or is contraindicated. The presence of myelomalacia on MRI is specifically identified as a finding supporting the waiver of standard conservative treatment.
   - Section II.3 mandates that plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice.

3. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) — Standard of Review:
   - CHSC § 1374.32(b) establishes that the standard for medical necessity is based on generally accepted standards of medical practice, and a plan's internal coverage criteria shall not be the sole or determinative basis for a decision if those criteria are more restrictive than the clinical standard.

V. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND STANDARD OF CARE ANALYSIS
Generally accepted clinical standards of care strongly support immediate surgical intervention for Mr. Webb's presentation, rendering the plan's 6-week physical therapy requirement clinically inappropriate:

1. AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update):
   - Under Section 3.1, Mr. Webb's clinical presentation meets Tier 1 — Strong Indication (surgery recommended) due to moderate-to-severe myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia (cord signal change) on MRI. The guidelines do not require or recommend conservative treatment trials for patients with objective upper motor neuron deficits and myelomalacia.

2. AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):
   - The guidelines state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."

3. Cochrane Review (2021) & NASS Guidelines (2020):
   - These systematic reviews demonstrate that physical therapy has limited to no efficacy for moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy (mJOA score 8-12 or lower) and does not produce meaningful functional improvement. Applying physical therapy requirements derived from radiculopathy studies to a patient with spinal cord compression and myelomalacia is a clinical category error.
   - Preoperative myelomalacia is the strongest predictor of incomplete neurological recovery, and each month of delay in surgical decompression is associated with a decreased likelihood of neurological recovery (Section 2.3).

VI. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS
In resolving the conflict between the plan's mechanical application of its internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) and the governing authorities, the Appeals Department applies the established regulatory hierarchy: California State Law and Regulations > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards of Care > Plan Internal Criteria.

The plan's initial denial anchored on an administrative billing code (M47.812) to deny the myelopathy exception and enforce a 6-week physical therapy trial. However, under CHSC § 1374.32(b) and DMHC APL 22-014, the objective clinical findings of spinal cord compression, myelomalacia, and severe upper motor neuron deficits govern the medical necessity determination, not the administrative billing code. Under CIC § 10169.5, the plan is legally prohibited from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of additional physical therapy.

VII. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION
Based on the clinical evidence demonstrating cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia, and in accordance with California Insurance Code § 10169.5, DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014, and the AANS/CNS clinical guidelines, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED.
