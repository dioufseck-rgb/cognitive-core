Dear Ms. Osei and Dr. Torres,

The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the prior authorization appeal for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory mandates, and generally accepted clinical standards, we have determined that the initial denial issued on October 15, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, is OVERTURNED. 

The requested surgical procedure is approved. Below is the detailed clinical, regulatory, and contractual rationale supporting this determination.

### I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND CASE SUMMARY
Ms. Patricia Osei is a 55-year-old female with a presenting diagnosis of progressive cervical myelopathy at C4-C5 with myelomalacia. Her clinical record documents significant comorbidities, including a permanent pacemaker (implanted in 2019, Boston Scientific), Type 2 diabetes, and hypertension. 

A cervical spine MRI performed on August 20, 2024, utilizing a pacemaker-compatible protocol, revealed severe central cord compression at C4-C5 with confirmed T2 signal change (myelomalacia), indicating progressive spinal cord injury. A neurological examination on August 25, 2024, documented objective upper motor neuron signs, including bilateral lower extremity spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor skills in both hands, and a positive Lhermitte sign.

Ms. Osei's conservative treatment history includes:
1. Physical Therapy: 5 weeks (15 sessions) completed. Therapy was discontinued due to pacemaker precautions limiting certain modalities and a clinical determination by her physician that further physical therapy was not addressing her rapid neurological progression.
2. Pharmacotherapy: Baclofen 10mg TID for 3 months (resulting in partial spasticity relief) and two separate 10-day courses of a methylprednisolone taper (providing only temporary relief).
3. Interventional Treatment: Not performed. A fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injection was determined to be medically contraindicated per an anesthesiology consultation by Dr. Rivera on September 10, 2024, due to the patient's permanent pacemaker. This written contraindication is documented in the medical chart.

### II. INITIAL DENIAL RATIONALE
The initial prior authorization request was denied on October 15, 2024, under Reason Code MED-003. The reviewer, Dr. Alan Foster, MD, stated that the member did not meet the conservative treatment requirements of Section 2.2 of the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (Document: CC-SPINE-2024), citing that only 5 weeks of physical therapy were completed (6 weeks required) and there was no cervical epidural steroid injection on record.

### III. EVALUATION OF PLAN CRITERIA COMPLIANCE
The initial reviewer applied the standard conservative treatment criteria of Section 2.2 mechanically and failed to evaluate the clinical record under the plan's own established exceptions:

1. Section 2.2(A) — Myelopathy Pathway Exception: 
   For members with confirmed cervical myelopathy (Criterion 2.1(b)), the conservative treatment requirements are modified as follows:
   - Physical Therapy: The minimum requirement is reduced from 6 weeks to 4 weeks. Ms. Osei completed 5 weeks of physical therapy, which fully satisfies this modified requirement.
   - Interventional Treatment: The requirement for an injection (Section 2.2(c)) is waived if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration or is contraindicated. Dr. Rivera's anesthesiology consultation on September 10, 2024, explicitly documented that a fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injection is contraindicated due to her permanent pacemaker.
   - Pharmacotherapy: The plan requires a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. Ms. Osei trialed Baclofen for 3 months (satisfied) and Methylprednisolone tapers (10 days x2). While the steroid tapers did not meet the 4-week duration, requiring an additional 4-week trial of a second agent in a patient with progressive myelomalacia is clinically inappropriate and overridden by state law and clinical standards (see below).

The initial reviewer's failure to apply the Myelopathy Pathway Exception (Section 2.2(A)) represents a critical misapplication of the plan's own clinical coverage criteria.

### IV. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY COMPLIANCE ANALYSIS
Under California law, the plan's mechanical application of conservative treatment requirements is legally unenforceable and must be overridden:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite:
   Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(1), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member. Furthermore, under § 10169.5(a)(3), physical therapy cannot be mandated when the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Ms. Osei's MRI demonstrates severe central cord compression and myelomalacia, which are structural lesions that physical therapy cannot resolve. The mechanical denial based on completing 5 weeks instead of 6 weeks directly violates CIC § 10169.5(c).

2. Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:
   APL 22-014 addresses prior authorization for time-sensitive neurological conditions. The Department has determined that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated. Under Section II.2, plans shall not require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when:
   - Objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (confirmed by Ms. Osei's MRI showing progressive cord injury and myelomalacia);
   - The treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression (documented by Dr. Torres); and
   - Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines.
   The presence of myelomalacia on Ms. Osei's MRI triggers a regulatory waiver of all standard conservative treatment requirements, including the 4-week pharmacotherapy duration.

3. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 — Standard of Review:
   Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the standard for medical necessity is based on generally accepted standards of medical practice and is independent of the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the service meets generally accepted clinical standards, it must be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.

### V. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND GENERALLY ACCEPTED MEDICAL STANDARDS
Generally accepted clinical guidelines from major spine societies strongly support immediate surgical intervention for Ms. Osei's presentation:

1. AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023):
   Ms. Osei meets Tier 1 — Strong Indication for surgical decompression. Surgery is recommended for patients with moderate-to-severe myelopathy, objective cord compression, and myelomalacia (T2 signal change) on MRI.
2. AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):
   The guidelines state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
3. NASS Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020):
   For moderate-to-severe myelopathy, surgical decompression is recommended as the primary treatment. Ms. Osei's objective neurological deficits (bilateral spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor skills, and positive Lhermitte sign) place her in the moderate-to-severe category, where conservative treatment is ineffective and clinically contraindicated due to the risk of irreversible spinal cord damage.

### VI. RESOLUTION OF CROSS-SOURCE CONFLICTS
In accordance with the established hierarchy of clinical review (California State Law > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Plan Coverage Criteria):
- State Law (APL 22-014 and CIC § 10169.5) overrides the plan's conservative treatment requirements. The plan cannot legally enforce the 6-week PT requirement or the 4-week pharmacotherapy duration for a patient with progressive myelomalacia and a documented pacemaker contraindication.
- Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine, NASS) override the plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) regarding conservative trials. Delaying surgery to complete conservative trials in the presence of myelomalacia is a deviation from the standard of care and poses a severe risk of permanent neurological deficit.
- Plan Criteria (Section 2.2(A)) was misapplied. Even under the plan's own terms, the PT requirement was met (5 weeks completed vs. 4 weeks required under the exception) and the interventional requirement was waived due to the documented pacemaker contraindication.

### VII. CONCLUSION AND DISPOSITION
The prior authorization denial for anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED. 

The procedure is authorized at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center, to be performed by Dr. Michael Torres, MD. This authorization is valid for 90 days from the date of this letter, expiring on January 28, 2025.
