Dear Ms. Osei and Dr. Torres,

The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C4-C5 level (CPT 22551) at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center. 

After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory mandates, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has determined that the initial denial issued on October 15, 2024, was in error. The denial is hereby OVERTURNED, and the requested surgical procedure is APPROVED.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The initial prior authorization request was denied by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, on October 15, 2024, citing failure to meet the conservative treatment requirements under Section 2.2 of HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024. Specifically, the denial noted that the member completed only 5 weeks of physical therapy (6 weeks required) and had no record of a cervical epidural steroid injection. 

Upon appeal review, this denial is overturned based on three independent, legally and clinically binding grounds:
1. Compliance with Plan Criteria: The member's primary diagnosis is progressive cervical myelopathy. Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2, Exception 2.2(A) ("Myelopathy Pathway"), the physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks (member completed 5 weeks), and the interventional treatment requirement is waived due to documented medical contraindications (permanent pacemaker).
2. California Regulatory Mandates: Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, plans are prohibited from mechanically enforcing conservative treatment or physical therapy requirements for patients with objective structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) and documented medical contraindications.
3. Generally Accepted Standards of Medical Practice: In accordance with the American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Joint Guidelines (2023), progressive cervical myelopathy with MRI-confirmed myelomalacia is a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) surgical emergency. Delaying surgery to complete conservative therapy is clinically contraindicated due to the high risk of irreversible spinal cord injury.

II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND RECORD REVIEW
The clinical record demonstrates that Ms. Patricia Osei is a 55-year-old female presenting with progressive cervical myelopathy at the C4-C5 level. 
- Clinical Symptoms & Neurological Exam (dated August 25, 2024): The neurological examination reveals severe upper motor neuron signs, including bilateral lower extremity spasticity, hyperreflexia, impaired fine motor skills in both hands, and a positive Lhermitte sign.
- Diagnostic Imaging (MRI dated August 20, 2024): A cervical spine MRI, performed using a pacemaker-compatible protocol, demonstrates severe central cord compression at C4-C5 with confirmed T2 signal change (myelomalacia), indicating progressive, active spinal cord injury.
- Comorbidities: The patient has a permanent pacemaker (implanted in 2019, Boston Scientific), Type 2 diabetes, and hypertension.
- Conservative Treatment History:
  - Physical Therapy: The patient completed 5 weeks of supervised physical therapy (15 sessions total). Therapy was discontinued due to pacemaker precautions limiting specific modalities and a clinical determination by the treating physician that further physical therapy was failing to address, and potentially exacerbating, her progressive neurological decline.
  - Pharmacotherapy: The patient trialed Baclofen 10mg TID for 3 months (resulting in only partial spasticity relief) and two separate 10-day courses of a Methylprednisolone taper (providing only temporary, transient improvement).
  - Interventional Treatment: No cervical epidural steroid injection was performed. An anesthesiology consultation by Dr. Rivera on September 10, 2024, concluded that a fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injection is strictly contraindicated due to the patient's permanent pacemaker. This written contraindication was documented in the patient's medical chart.

III. ANALYSIS OF HEALTHFIRST CLINICAL COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
The initial reviewer, Dr. Alan Foster, MD, applied the standard conservative treatment criteria under Section 2.2, which are designed for cervical radiculopathy, and failed to apply the plan's own explicit exceptions for cervical myelopathy.

1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1):
Ms. Osei meets Section 2.1(b) for Cervical Myelopathy. She has objective spinal cord compression confirmed on MRI (August 20, 2024) and classic myelopathic symptoms documented on neurological exam (August 25, 2024), including gait/spasticity issues, bilateral hand fine motor impairment, hyperreflexia, and a positive Lhermitte sign.

2. Conservative Treatment Requirement & Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway):
Because Ms. Osei's primary diagnosis is cervical myelopathy, the conservative treatment requirements are modified under Exception 2.2(A) as follows:
- Physical Therapy: The standard 6-week requirement under Section 2.2(a) is reduced to a 4-week minimum. Ms. Osei completed 5 weeks of physical therapy, which fully satisfies this modified requirement.
- Interventional Treatment: The standard requirement under Section 2.2(c) (minimum of 1 epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point series) is waived if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration or is otherwise contraindicated. Dr. Torres and Dr. Rivera (Anesthesiology) documented in writing that a cervical epidural steroid injection is medically contraindicated due to her permanent pacemaker. This satisfies the waiver criteria under Exception 2.2(A).
- Pharmacotherapy: The patient trialed Baclofen (3 months) and Methylprednisolone tapers (20 days total). While the plan's standard criteria under Section 2.2(b) require two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each, the clinical record demonstrates a robust trial of a muscle relaxant and oral corticosteroids. Any minor technical deficiency in the duration of the steroid taper is superseded by state regulatory overrides and clinical standards regarding neurological urgency (see Sections IV and V below).

Therefore, under a correct and non-mechanical application of HealthFirst's own CC-SPINE-2024 criteria, the conservative treatment requirements are met or waived, and the procedure should have been approved.

IV. ANALYSIS OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY MANDATES
Under California law, which governs this HealthFirst PPO Gold plan, the mechanical application of conservative treatment guidelines to deny necessary surgery for progressive neurological conditions is strictly prohibited.

1. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Prior Authorization for Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions):
APL 22-014, Section II.1, establishes that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a time-sensitive condition with high potential for irreversible harm. Under Section II.2, plans "SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization" when:
  (a) Objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (myelomalacia is explicitly cited as a qualifying finding);
  (b) The treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression; and
  (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines.
Ms. Osei's clinical record satisfies all three prongs of APL 22-014. The initial denial directly violates this DMHC mandate by enforcing a 6-week PT trial and an epidural injection on a patient with active myelomalacia and progressive cord injury.

2. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):
CIC § 10169.5(a) states that a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where:
  - (1) The treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member; or
  - (3) The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address.
Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(c) mandates that a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy "shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or whether the condition involves neurological urgency."
Ms. Osei's physical therapy was discontinued at 5 weeks because her physician determined that further therapy was not addressing her progressive neurological decline, and her MRI showed structural cord compromise (myelomalacia) that physical therapy cannot address. Denying coverage because she completed 5 weeks instead of 6 is a direct violation of CIC § 10169.5(c).

V. ANALYSIS OF CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND GENERALLY ACCEPTED STANDARDS
In accordance with California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, the ultimate standard for medical necessity determinations is whether the service is in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersedes restrictive internal plan criteria.

1. Surgical Indications for Cervical Myelopathy:
According to the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Clinical Guidelines (2020), surgical decompression is the gold standard and primary recommended treatment for moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy. Ms. Osei's clinical presentation—characterized by bilateral lower extremity spasticity, hyperreflexia, hand clumsiness, and a positive Lhermitte sign—places her in the moderate-to-severe category.

2. Clinical Significance of Myelomalacia:
The presence of myelomalacia (T2 signal change) on Ms. Osei's MRI indicates active, structural spinal cord damage. The AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021) state that there is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy or interventional injections prior to surgery in patients with moderate-to-severe myelopathy and myelomalacia. Delaying surgical decompression to complete arbitrary conservative treatment trials is associated with a significantly decreased likelihood of neurological recovery and a high risk of permanent, irreversible paraplegia or quadriplegia.

3. Contraindication of Interventional Treatment:
Performing a fluoroscopy-guided cervical epidural steroid injection in a patient with a permanent pacemaker and severe central cord compression poses significant clinical risks, as documented by the anesthesiology consultation. Forcing a patient to undergo a contraindicated interventional procedure to satisfy an administrative checklist violates the basic medical standard of "do no harm."

VI. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS AND FINAL DETERMINATION
Where a conflict exists between a health plan's internal clinical criteria, state regulatory mandates, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, the hierarchy of authority is clear: State Law and Regulatory Mandates (APL 22-014, CIC § 10169.5) and Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS Guidelines) supersede restrictive or mechanically applied plan criteria.

In this case, the initial denial was procedurally and clinically defective:
1. It failed to apply the plan's own Myelopathy Pathway Exception 2.2(A), under which the patient's 5 weeks of physical therapy was fully compliant, and the interventional requirement was waived.
2. It violated California Insurance Code § 10169.5 by mechanically enforcing a 6-week physical therapy requirement despite documented structural neurological compromise and physician-documented futility.
3. It violated DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 by requiring conservative treatment for a patient with progressive myelomalacia, a time-sensitive neurological condition.
4. It violated generally accepted standards of medical practice by delaying necessary, urgent surgical decompression for a Tier 1 surgical candidate, risking permanent neurological deficit.

Based on these findings, the prior authorization denial for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C4-C5 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED. The procedure is APPROVED as medically necessary.
