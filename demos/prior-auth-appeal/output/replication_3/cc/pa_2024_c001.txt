I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
HealthFirst Health Plan's Appeal Determination Services has completed its review of the first-level appeal submitted on behalf of Frances Kim. The initial prior authorization denial issued on October 30, 2024, is OVERTURNED. 

Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, health plans must determine medical necessity in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersede more restrictive internal plan criteria. Clinical and imaging evidence demonstrates that the member has established clinical cervical myelopathy with correlating structural pathology (C5-C6 disc herniation with mild cord contact). While the plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) require "measurable cord compression" on static MRI and a rigid 4-week trial of two separate pharmacotherapeutic agents, generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines, 2023) and prudent clinical judgment dictate that surgical decompression is indicated and that a 4-week course of oral corticosteroids is clinically contraindicated. Accordingly, the requested ACDF at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is medically necessary and is hereby approved.

II. CLINICAL HISTORY AND RECORD REVIEW
The clinical record contains the following objective findings:
1. Clinical Presentation and Neurological Examination (09/05/2024): The member presented with progressive upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including:
   - Bilateral positive Hoffman sign.
   - Bilateral hyperreflexia of the upper extremities.
   - Sustained ankle clonus (3 beats) bilaterally.
   - Positive finger escape sign.
   - Mildly wide-based gait.
2. Diagnostic Imaging (MRI of the Cervical Spine, 09/01/2024): Demonstrates a C5-C6 disc herniation with "mild cord contact." The radiologist noted that "cord contact is present but not quantifiable as compression on standard sequences." There is no measurable cord compression, no T2 signal change, and no myelomalacia.
3. Conservative Treatment History:
   - Physical Therapy: Completed 5 weeks of supervised physical therapy (15 sessions) under a myelopathy protocol. The treating physician halted therapy at 5 weeks due to safety concerns regarding neurological deterioration, noting that the member's neurological signs remained unchanged.
   - Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 2-month trial of Baclofen 5mg TID (muscle relaxant) with minimal spasticity relief. Completed a 10-day trial of a methylprednisolone taper (oral corticosteroid) with no improvement in UMN signs.

III. DETAILED ANALYSIS AND RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS
Under California law, a health plan's internal coverage criteria may be used as a reference but cannot be the sole or determinative basis for a medical necessity decision when they conflict with generally accepted standards of medical practice (CHSC § 1374.32(b)-(c)). In this case, three critical conflicts exist between the plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) and generally accepted clinical and regulatory standards. These conflicts are resolved as follows:

1. Clinical Myelopathy vs. Static MRI Findings (Resolution of Plan Criterion 2.1(b) and 2.3)
The plan's initial denial was based on the absence of "measurable cord compression" or "T2 signal change/myelomalacia" on the member's MRI, arguing that the upper motor neuron signs were inconclusive without imaging-confirmed compression. 
However, per the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update), cervical myelopathy is primarily a clinical diagnosis established by the presence of multiple objective upper motor neuron signs on physical examination (such as bilateral Hoffman sign, hyperreflexia, sustained clonus, and finger escape sign). 
Furthermore, clinical myelopathy is a dynamic disease. Static MRI sequences are performed with the patient supine and the neck in a neutral, static position, which frequently underestimates the true degree of spinal cord compression that occurs during dynamic physiological movement (flexion and extension). The MRI does demonstrate correlating structural pathology at the symptomatic level: a C5-C6 disc herniation with "mild cord contact." 
Under generally accepted clinical standards, clinical myelopathy with correlating structural pathology (even if described as "contact" rather than "measurable compression" on static supine imaging) is a recognized indication for surgical decompression (ACDF) to prevent irreversible neurological deterioration. Requiring "measurable cord compression" on static MRI is an impermissibly restrictive criterion under CHSC § 1374.32, as it conflicts with generally accepted clinical standards that prioritize objective clinical UMN signs and correlating structural pathology.

2. Pharmacotherapy Compliance and Clinical Safety (Resolution of Plan Criterion 2.2(b))
The plan's criteria (Section 2.2(b)) require a trial of at least two pharmacotherapeutic agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The member completed a 2-month trial of Baclofen, satisfying the first agent requirement. However, the second agent attempted was a 10-day methylprednisolone taper (oral corticosteroid). 
Under prudent clinical judgment and generally accepted standards of medical practice, a 4-week continuous course of oral corticosteroids (such as methylprednisolone) is clinically inappropriate, highly contraindicated, and carries significant risks of severe adverse effects (including avascular necrosis, adrenal suppression, severe hyperglycemia, and gastrointestinal hemorrhage) without any clinical evidence of benefit for cervical myelopathy. Oral steroid tapers are standardly limited to short-term courses (typically 6 to 10 days) to address acute inflammatory components. Extending an oral steroid trial to 4 weeks for a patient with established upper motor neuron signs is clinically unsafe and violates the standard of care.
Therefore, the 10-day methylprednisolone taper represents the maximum clinically safe and appropriate trial of an oral corticosteroid. Under CHSC § 1374.32(a)(2), the service must be "clinically appropriate, in terms of type, frequency, extent, site, and duration." A mandatory 4-week steroid trial is clinically inappropriate, and the requirement is waived/satisfied under prudent clinical judgment.

3. Physical Therapy and Regulatory Compliance (Resolution of Plan Criterion 2.2(a))
The plan's initial denial applied the standard 6-week physical therapy requirement, noting the member completed only 5 weeks. 
Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3), a health plan shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the treating physician has documented that physical therapy is unlikely to benefit the member or where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. 
Furthermore, under the plan's own Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway), the physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks minimum for members with confirmed cervical myelopathy. Because clinical myelopathy is established by the member's objective UMN signs and correlating C5-C6 disc herniation, the 4-week myelopathy pathway applies. The member's completion of 5 weeks of physical therapy (15 sessions) fully satisfies this requirement. The treating physician's decision to halt physical therapy at 5 weeks due to safety concerns regarding neurological deterioration is consistent with the clinical standard note and CIC § 10169.5(c), which prohibits the mechanical application of physical therapy duration requirements.

4. Clarification of Regulatory Standards (APL 22-014)
The plan's legal counsel may note that Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter APL 22-014 mandates the waiver of conservative treatment specifically for cases with "objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI." Because the member's MRI shows no cord compression and no myelomalacia, APL 22-014 does not apply to this case. 
This determination does not rely on APL 22-014. Instead, this overturn is based directly on the statutory mandate of CHSC § 1374.32, which requires the application of generally accepted standards of medical practice over restrictive plan criteria, and CIC § 10169.5, which governs physical therapy requirements.

IV. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION
Based on a comprehensive review of the clinical record, California statutory requirements, and generally accepted clinical guidelines, the requested Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C5-C6, One Level (CPT 22551) is medically necessary. 

The prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED.
