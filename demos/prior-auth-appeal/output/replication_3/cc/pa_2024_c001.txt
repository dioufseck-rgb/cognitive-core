Dear Frances Kim and Dr. Robert Adams,

The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a one-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory statutes, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has determined that the prior authorization denial issued on October 30, 2024, must be OVERTURNED. 

The requested surgical procedure is approved. Below is a detailed analysis of the clinical, regulatory, and guideline-based factors supporting this determination.

---

I. CLINICAL HISTORY AND PRESENTING PRESENTATION
The member is a 58-year-old female presenting with suspected cervical myelopathy and an imaging-negative upper motor neuron (UMN) syndrome. 
- Neurological Examination (dated September 5, 2024): Dr. Adams documented multiple objective, positive upper motor neuron signs, including:
  1. Bilateral Hoffman's sign.
  2. Bilateral hyperreflexia of the upper extremities.
  3. Sustained ankle clonus bilaterally (3 beats).
  4. Positive finger escape sign.
  5. Mildly wide-based gait.
- Imaging (MRI dated September 1, 2024): Demonstrated a C5-C6 disc herniation with mild cord contact. The radiologist noted that cord contact was present but not quantifiable as compression on standard sequences, with no measurable cord compression, no T2 signal change, and no myelomalacia.
- Conservative Treatment:
  - Physical Therapy: The member completed 5 weeks of supervised physical therapy (15 sessions) under a myelopathy protocol. The physical therapy was halted by the treating physician due to safety concerns regarding neurological deterioration (myelopathy concern), with neurological signs remaining unchanged.
  - Pharmacotherapy: The member trialed Baclofen 5mg TID for 2 months (with minimal spasticity relief) and a 10-day oral Methylprednisolone taper (with no improvement in UMN signs).
  - Interventional Treatment: None performed.

---

II. ANALYSIS OF PLAN CRITERIA VS. CLINICAL STANDARDS AND REGULATORY FRAMEWORK

1. Diagnosis of Cervical Myelopathy and Imaging Requirements
The initial denial by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, stated that the "Myelopathy exception does not apply" because the MRI showed "mild cord contact only — no cord compression, no cord signal change" and that "upper motor neuron signs on exam are not conclusive for myelopathy in the absence of imaging confirmation."

This rationale is inconsistent with both generally accepted standards of medical practice and California regulatory requirements:
- Clinical Standard: According to the AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine Guidelines for Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), a diagnosis of clinical cervical myelopathy is established primarily by the presence of upper motor neuron signs on physical examination (such as hyperreflexia, Hoffman's sign, clonus, and gait disturbance). Imaging-negative myelopathy is a well-recognized clinical entity where clinical signs precede gross MRI changes. The presence of multiple positive UMN signs in the absence of another explanation establishes clinical myelopathy.
- Regulatory Standard: Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(a), "medically necessary" services must be in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice. CHSC § 1374.32(b) explicitly mandates that the standard of review does not require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria if those criteria are more restrictive than generally accepted medical standards. Furthermore, Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 Section II.3 prohibits plans from applying internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice. 
- Resolution: The plan's internal requirement under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.1(b) (which requires quantifiable "spinal cord compression" on imaging to confirm myelopathy) is more restrictive than the clinical standard, which recognizes clinical myelopathy based on UMN signs and correlating structural pathology (the C5-C6 disc herniation with mild cord contact). Therefore, under CHSC § 1374.32 and DMHC APL 22-014, the clinical standard governs, and the diagnosis of cervical myelopathy is established.

2. Physical Therapy Requirement and Safety-Related Halt
The initial denial applied the standard 6-week physical therapy (PT) requirement, noting that the member had only completed 5 weeks.

This mechanical application of the PT requirement violates California law and the plan's own criteria:
- Plan Criteria: Under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway), the conservative treatment requirement is modified for members with confirmed cervical myelopathy. Specifically, the physical therapy requirement is reduced to a minimum of 4 weeks. Because the member has established clinical myelopathy, the Myelopathy Pathway applies. The member's completion of 5 weeks of PT (15 sessions) exceeds the 4-week minimum, fully satisfying Section 2.2(A).
- Regulatory Standard: California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(c) states that a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or whether the condition involves neurological urgency. Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(1), a plan cannot condition surgical approval on PT completion if the treating physician documents that PT is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member. Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3), PT cannot be mandated if the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address.
- Clinical Standard: The AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021) state that there is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with cervical myelopathy, and delaying surgery to complete a PT trial may result in neurological deterioration.
- Resolution: The treating physician appropriately halted physical therapy at 5 weeks due to safety concerns regarding myelopathy and neurological deterioration. Mechanically requiring an additional week of PT in the face of progressive upper motor neuron signs violates CIC § 10169.5 and contradicts the clinical safety standards outlined by AO Spine (2021). The 5-week trial is sufficient under both the plan's Myelopathy Pathway (Exception 2.2(A)) and California statutory protections.

3. Pharmacotherapy and Interventional Treatment Requirements
The initial denial cited the failure to meet standard conservative treatment requirements. Under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b), the plan requires a trial of at least two pharmacotherapy agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The member trialed Baclofen for 2 months (meeting the requirement for one agent) and a Methylprednisolone taper for 10 days (which is less than 4 weeks). No interventional treatments were performed.

- Plan Criteria: Under CC-SPINE-2024 Exception 2.2(A), the interventional treatment requirement (Section 2.2(c)) is waived if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration. Dr. Adams halted PT and avoided interventional procedures due to the risk of neurological compromise in the setting of active myelopathy, satisfying the waiver requirement.
- Clinical and Regulatory Standard: A 10-day oral steroid taper is the standard clinical duration for methylprednisolone; forcing a patient to continue oral corticosteroids for 4 weeks solely to satisfy an administrative plan requirement is clinically inappropriate and poses unnecessary medical risks. Under CHSC § 1374.32 and DMHC APL 22-014, the clinical standard of practice overrides the plan's rigid 4-week duration requirement for short-term steroid tapers. Furthermore, because cervical myelopathy is a progressive neurological condition with a risk of irreversible harm, requiring further conservative trials when the patient has already failed a muscle relaxant (Baclofen) and oral steroids is not supported by clinical evidence (NASS 2020, AO Spine 2021).
- Resolution: The conservative treatment requirements are satisfied. The interventional requirement is waived under Exception 2.2(A), and the pharmacotherapy trial is clinically sufficient under CHSC § 1374.32.

---

III. CONCLUSION AND ORDER OF APPROVAL

Based on a rigorous review of the clinical evidence and the governing California regulatory framework:
1. The member has a confirmed clinical diagnosis of cervical myelopathy at C5-C6, supported by multiple positive upper motor neuron signs (bilateral Hoffman's, hyperreflexia, ankle clonus, finger escape, and gait disturbance) and correlating MRI findings (C5-C6 disc herniation with mild cord contact), satisfying the clinical standard of medical practice (AANS/CNS 2023) which overrides the plan's restrictive criteria under CHSC § 1374.32 and DMHC APL 22-014.
2. The member's 5 weeks of completed physical therapy satisfies the 4-week minimum required under the plan's Myelopathy Pathway (Exception 2.2(A)).
3. The mechanical application of a 6-week PT requirement is rejected under CIC § 10169.5(c) due to the physician-documented safety halt for neurological concerns.
4. The requested one-level ACDF at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is medically necessary and clinically indicated.

Therefore, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED. The procedure is APPROVED.
