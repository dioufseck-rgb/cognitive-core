I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The prior authorization denial for a one-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED. This determination is based on two independent, reinforcing grounds: (1) a comprehensive clinical review demonstrating that the member fully satisfies the substantive medical necessity criteria for cervical radiculopathy under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 and California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5, and (2) the identification of severe procedural notice defects under California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a). 

While the procedural defects violate state law and must be documented and corrected by the plan, the substantive decision to OVERTURN is based on the clinical merits of the case.

II. PROCEDURAL NOTICE DEFECTS AND REGULATORY ANALYSIS
Under California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a), when a health plan issues a denial based on medical necessity, it is statutorily mandated to provide a written notice that includes the specific clinical criteria applied, the specific clinical reasons for the denial, and a notice of Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights. 

In this case, the plan's initial denial notice dated November 8, 2024, exhibited two critical procedural defects:
1. Missing Clinical Criteria: The denial text stated, "See attached criteria document," but no such document was attached or provided to the member or the requesting physician.
2. Missing IMR Notice: The mandatory notice of IMR rights was entirely omitted from the denial documentation.

Legal Effect under CHSC § 1374.31(b):
CHSC § 1374.31(b) explicitly states that a denial notice failing to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason is defective and "shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines." 

It is critical to clarify the precise legal remedy of this provision:
- This statutory defect tolls the member's appeal window, preventing the plan from asserting that any subsequent appeal is untimely.
- This defect does NOT serve as an independent, self-executing ground to substantively "overturn" the denial or mandate clinical approval of the surgery. 
- This notice defect must not be conflated with the "deemed approved" remedy under CHSC § 1367.01 and California Code of Regulations (CCR) Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1, which is strictly reserved for plan decision delays (failure to issue a decision within standard or expedited timeframes).

Therefore, while these procedural violations are documented as severe statutory infractions requiring corrective action by the plan, the substantive decision to overturn the denial and authorize the procedure is based solely on the clinical merits detailed below.

III. CLINICAL MERITS AND COMPLIANCE WITH PLAN CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
A review of the clinical record demonstrates that the member fully satisfies the medical necessity requirements set forth in HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024:

1. Section 2.1 — Diagnosis Requirement:
The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy C5-C6 with functional impairment, satisfying Section 2.1(a). This is supported by:
- Imaging: An MRI dated September 20, 2024 (within the 6-month window required by Section 2.1 and 2.3) demonstrating a large C5-C6 disc herniation with moderate left C6 nerve root compression.
- Objective Neurological Deficits: A neurological examination dated September 25, 2024, documenting left C6 dermatomal numbness, a diminished left bicep reflex, and objective left bicep weakness graded at 4/5.

2. Section 2.2 — Conservative Treatment Requirement:
The clinical record documents the completion of a comprehensive course of conservative treatment that meets and exceeds all plan requirements:
- Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): The member completed 7 weeks (21 sessions) of supervised physical therapy directed at the cervical spine. PT records document that the member achieved a "functional plateau" at week 6, with persistent, unresolved radicular symptoms.
- Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): The member completed trials of two qualifying neuropathic and anti-inflammatory agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each:
  * Gabapentin 600mg TID for 4 months (neuropathic agent, partial relief).
  * Naproxen 500mg BID for 3 months (NSAID, inadequate relief).
- Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): The member underwent a left C6 cervical epidural steroid injection on October 28, 2024, which provided only temporary (40%) relief before symptoms returned to baseline within 3 weeks.

3. Section 2.3 — Imaging Requirement:
The MRI dated September 20, 2024, was interpreted by a specialist and demonstrates structural pathology (large C5-C6 disc herniation with moderate left C6 nerve root compression) that directly correlates with the member's left-sided C6 radicular symptoms and objective neurological deficits.

IV. RESOLUTION OF SECTION 2.4 DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS
The plan's medical policy under Section 2.4 of CC-SPINE-2024 requires specific physician documentation, including an attestation of conservative treatment completion (Section 2.4(a)) and a statement of surgical risks and alternatives considered (Section 2.4(d)). The initial prior authorization request lacked a formal, separate attestation and risk statement.

Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5, a health insurer is prohibited from mechanically applying internal guidelines to deny coverage for administrative omissions or rigid timelines when a clinical plateau and clear clinical indications are documented. Specifically:
- CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) states that a plan shall not condition approval on physical therapy where the member has completed a course of therapy and the treating physician documents a functional plateau.
- CIC § 10169.5(c) prohibits the mechanical application of minimum physical therapy timelines without reference to the clinical reality of a functional plateau.

Dr. Sarah Kim's detailed clinical request and the comprehensive appeal records—which outline the presenting diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy, the clinical rationale for ACDF, the objective neurological deficits, and the documented failure of conservative treatments—fully satisfy the substantive intent of Section 2.4. Denying coverage mechanically based on the absence of a formal administrative checklist is clinically inappropriate and legally non-compliant under CIC § 10169.5. 

To resolve this administrative gap, the plan is directed to treat the comprehensive clinical records submitted with this appeal as satisfying the intent of Section 2.4, and may obtain the formal written statement of surgical risks and alternatives (Section 2.4(d)) post-service for administrative completion.

V. CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARD
This determination is further supported by generally accepted standards of medical practice. Under the AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Radiculopathy (2023) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Clinical Guidelines (2020), surgical decompression via ACDF (CPT 22551) is highly indicated (Tier 2 — Reasonable Indication) for patients with persistent cervical radiculopathy, correlating imaging evidence of nerve root compression, and objective neurological deficits (such as 4/5 motor weakness and diminished reflexes) who have failed to achieve adequate relief after 6 to 12 weeks of conservative management. Requiring further delay or denying coverage in the face of a documented functional plateau and progressive neurological weakness is contrary to these clinical standards and poses a risk of permanent neurological deficit.

VI. CONCLUSION
Because the member fully meets the clinical criteria under CC-SPINE-2024 and generally accepted medical standards, and because a mechanical denial for administrative omissions is prohibited under CIC § 10169.5, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED. The plan is directed to authorize the requested one-level ACDF at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) to be performed by Dr. Sarah Kim at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center.
