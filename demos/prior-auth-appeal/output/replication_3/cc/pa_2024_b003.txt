Dear Angela Morrison and Dr. Paul Nguyen,

The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C3-C4 level (CPT code 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial decision to deny coverage has been OVERTURNED. The requested procedure is approved.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The initial prior authorization request was denied on October 01, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, on the grounds that the member's magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) dated August 15, 2024, showed only "mild degenerative changes" at C3-C4, and therefore did not demonstrate sufficient pathology to support surgical intervention. While the initial reviewer conceded that the member met all conservative treatment requirements, the denial concluded that the procedure was not medically necessary.

Upon appeal review, this determination is overturned because the initial review failed to consider the definitive imaging study in the clinical record: a CT myelogram dated September 10, 2024. The member's prior C4-C5 ACDF (performed in 2019) created significant metallic hardware artifact on the August 15, 2024 MRI, rendering the MRI evaluation of the adjacent C3-C4 level unreliable and explicitly limiting its diagnostic value. The subsequent CT myelogram, performed specifically to overcome this artifact limitation, confirmed moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression and significant foraminal stenosis at C3-C4. This structural pathology directly correlates with the member's objective neurological deficits (left deltoid weakness of 3+/5 and diminished left bicep reflex). 

Because the clinical record demonstrates that the member meets all diagnostic, conservative treatment, and imaging requirements under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, and because California law and clinical evidence standards dictate that the CT myelogram supersedes the artifact-limited MRI, the denial is overturned.

II. CLINICAL HISTORY AND PRESENTING PRESENTATION
Mrs. Angela Morrison is a 67-year-old female with a history of a successful C4-C5 ACDF in 2019, from which she had an excellent clinical outcome for four years. In late 2023, she developed new, progressive neurological symptoms. A neurological examination on August 20, 2024, documented objective deficits in the left C4 dermatomal distribution, including:
1. Numbness in the left shoulder and lateral arm.
2. Left deltoid motor weakness graded at 3+/5.
3. A diminished left bicep reflex.

To address these symptoms, the member completed an extensive course of conservative management:
1. Physical Therapy: 10 weeks (30 sessions) of supervised physical therapy, which resulted in no improvement of her left C4 radicular symptoms.
2. Pharmacotherapy: A six-month trial of Pregabalin (300mg BID) yielding only partial relief, and a three-month trial of Duloxetine (60mg) yielding minimal benefit.
3. Interventional Treatment: A left C4 epidural steroid injection on September 25, 2024, which provided good initial relief (60%) but saw symptoms return to baseline by week three.

III. ANALYSIS OF PLAN COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2, cervical spinal surgery (CPT 22551) will be approved when all of the following criteria are satisfied:

1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): The member must have a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy, defined as nerve root compression with radicular symptoms (pain, numbness, weakness in a dermatomal distribution). This must be supported by an MRI or CT myelogram within 6 months demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms.
   - Evaluation: Met. The neurological exam on August 20, 2024, confirms objective left C4 radiculopathy (deltoid weakness 3+/5, numbness, diminished reflex). The CT myelogram on September 10, 2024 (within 6 months) confirms moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression and significant foraminal stenosis at C3-C4, which directly correlates with the left C4 dermatomal symptoms.

2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): The member must complete a course of conservative treatment consisting of:
   - (a) Physical Therapy: Minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. (Met: Member completed 10 weeks/30 sessions of PT).
   - (b) Pharmacotherapy: Trial of at least two listed agents (including neuropathic agents) for a minimum of 4 weeks each. (Met: Member trialed Pregabalin for 6 months and Duloxetine for 3 months).
   - (c) Interventional Treatment: At least one cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point series. (Met: Member received a left C4 epidural steroid injection on September 25, 2024).
   - Note: The initial denial on October 01, 2024, explicitly conceded that the "Member meets conservative treatment requirements."

3. Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement): Current imaging (within 6 months) must demonstrate structural pathology consistent with the member's symptoms and proposed surgical level.
   - Evaluation: Met. The CT myelogram dated September 10, 2024, is within 6 months of the appeal and demonstrates moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression and significant foraminal stenosis at C3-C4. This structural pathology is highly consistent with the member's left C4 radiculopathy and the proposed C3-C4 surgical level.
   - The initial reviewer's reliance on the MRI dated August 15, 2024, to deny coverage violates Section 2.3. The MRI report explicitly noted "SIGNIFICANT ARTIFACT from C4-C5 hardware limiting evaluation" and stated that it "cannot exclude moderate compression due to artifact." Section 2.1 explicitly permits the use of a "CT myelogram" as supporting documentation. Denying coverage based on an MRI that was acknowledged by the radiologist to be non-diagnostic due to hardware artifact, while ignoring a definitive CT myelogram, is a misapplication of Section 2.3.

4. Section 2.4 (Physician Documentation): The treating physician must provide clinical rationale, functional limitations, and risk discussions.
   - Evaluation: Met. Dr. Paul Nguyen provided documentation detailing adjacent segment disease at C3-C4, clinical examination findings, and the rationale for surgical intervention given the failure of conservative measures.

IV. ANALYSIS OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY REQUIREMENTS
Because the member is a California resident enrolled in a HealthFirst PPO plan, the review must conform to California statutory and regulatory standards, which supersede any conflicting or more restrictive plan criteria.

1. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31 (Defective Denial Notice):
   - Under CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2), a health plan must provide the specific clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reason for a denial. The initial denial notice dated October 01, 2024, cited only the August 15, 2024 MRI findings of "mild degenerative changes" and failed to address or acknowledge the September 10, 2024 CT myelogram, which was already part of the clinical record. By ignoring the definitive imaging study and failing to provide a clinical rationale for why the CT myelogram was disregarded, the initial denial notice is procedurally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b).

2. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 (Standard of Medical Necessity):
   - CHSC § 1374.32(a) defines medical necessity as services that a prudent physician would provide to treat an illness or injury in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice. Furthermore, CHSC § 1374.32(b) explicitly states that the medical necessity standard does not require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria, and that internal criteria cannot be the sole or determinative basis for a decision if the service meets generally accepted clinical standards.
   - In this case, denying a necessary surgical decompression for a patient with objective, progressive motor weakness (deltoid 3+/5) based on an artifact-obscured MRI—while a definitive CT myelogram confirms severe nerve compression—violates the statutory standard of medical necessity. Prudent clinical practice dictates that when metallic hardware limits MRI visualization, a CT myelogram is the governing diagnostic study.

3. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite):
   - Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), a plan cannot condition surgical approval on physical therapy where the member has completed a course of therapy and reached a functional plateau. The clinical record confirms that Mrs. Morrison completed 10 weeks of PT with "no improvement in left C4 radicular symptoms," establishing a clear functional plateau. Mechanically denying surgery based on a perceived lack of imaging findings while ignoring the clinical failure of PT and the presence of objective neurological compromise violates the intent of CIC § 10169.5.

V. ANALYSIS OF CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARDS
Generally accepted standards of medical practice, as compiled in the Clinical Reference Summary, strongly support surgical intervention in this case:

1. Diagnostic Validity of CT Myelogram:
   - According to the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy and Radiculopathy (2023), metallic hardware from prior spinal fusions creates significant magnetic susceptibility artifact on MRI, which frequently leads to the underestimation of adjacent segment pathology. In such cases, a CT myelogram is the established clinical gold standard to evaluate foraminal stenosis and nerve root compression. The initial reviewer's reliance on the MRI over the CT myelogram is clinically unsound and contrary to standard neurosurgical practice.

2. Surgical Indications for Cervical Radiculopathy:
   - Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), surgical decompression (ACDF) is strongly indicated (Tier 1/Tier 2) for patients with cervical radiculopathy who present with:
     - Objective neurological deficits (such as Mrs. Morrison's 3+/5 deltoid weakness and diminished bicep reflex).
     - Structural pathology on imaging (moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis confirmed on CT myelogram).
     - Failure of at least 6 to 12 weeks of conservative management (including physical therapy, neuropathic medications, and epidural steroid injections).
   - Mrs. Morrison meets all of these clinical indicators. Delaying surgical intervention in a patient with active motor weakness (3+/5 deltoid strength) poses a significant risk of permanent, irreversible neurological deficit, which is contrary to the clinical recommendations of the AO Spine International Guidelines (2021).

VI. RESOLUTION AND AUTHORIZATION
Based on a hierarchical review of the evidence, there is no conflict between the plan's clinical criteria and state law or clinical standards; rather, the initial denial resulted from a factual and procedural error in which the initial reviewer failed to evaluate the CT myelogram. 

1. The member's clinical presentation of left C4 radiculopathy is confirmed by objective neurological examination (August 20, 2024).
2. The structural pathology is definitively established by a CT myelogram (September 10, 2024) showing moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression, which supersedes the artifact-limited MRI (August 15, 2024).
3. The member has fully exhausted all conservative treatment requirements under CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2, including 10 weeks of physical therapy, two neuropathic medications, and a targeted epidural steroid injection, without lasting benefit.
4. The requested ACDF at C3-C4 (CPT 22551) is medically necessary under California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32 and is the standard of care under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023).

Therefore, the initial denial is OVERTURNED. Prior authorization for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C3-C4, one level) at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center, requested by Dr. Paul Nguyen, MD, is hereby APPROVED.

This authorization is valid for 90 days from the date of this letter, expiring on January 18, 2025, in accordance with CC-SPINE-2024, Section 3.5. If the procedure is not performed within this timeframe, a new prior authorization request must be submitted.
