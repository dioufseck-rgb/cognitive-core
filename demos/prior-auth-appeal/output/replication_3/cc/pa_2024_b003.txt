Dear Ms. Morrison and Dr. Nguyen,

The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its clinical and regulatory review of the first-level appeal submitted on October 15, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C3-C4 level (CPT code 22551).

After a comprehensive evaluation of the complete clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California statutory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards of care, HealthFirst has determined that the requested procedure is MEDICALLY NECESSARY. Accordingly, the initial denial issued on October 01, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED in its entirety, and the procedure is APPROVED.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & DISPOSITION
The initial prior authorization request was denied on October 01, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, citing that "imaging does not show sufficient pathology at C3-C4 to support surgical intervention" and that the "MRI shows mild degenerative changes." This determination was procedurally and clinically erroneous. The initial review relied exclusively on a cervical spine MRI dated August 15, 2024, which was explicitly documented by the radiologist as being severely compromised by hardware artifact from a prior 2019 C4-C5 fusion, thereby masking the true extent of the pathology.

To resolve this diagnostic limitation, a CT myelogram was performed on September 10, 2024. This study confirmed moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression and significant foraminal stenosis at C3-C4, which perfectly correlates with the member's objective neurological deficits (left deltoid weakness 3+/5, diminished bicep reflex, and left C4 dermatomal numbness). Because the member has also fully completed and failed an extensive course of conservative therapy (including 10 weeks of physical therapy, multiple neuropathic medications, and a targeted epidural steroid injection), all clinical, plan, and regulatory criteria for surgical intervention are met.

II. CLINICAL HISTORY AND PRESENTING PRESENTATION
Ms. Angela Morrison is a 67-year-old female with a history of a successful C4-C5 ACDF in 2019. She presented in 2024 with a new onset of progressive, severe left-sided cervical radicular symptoms since 2023, diagnosed as adjacent segment disease at C3-C4.
- Neurological Examination (August 20, 2024): Demonstrates objective neurological deficits in the left C4 dermatomal distribution, including shoulder and lateral arm numbness, left deltoid weakness graded at 3+/5, and a diminished left bicep reflex.
- Diagnostic Imaging:
  1. Cervical MRI (August 15, 2024): Revealed "SIGNIFICANT ARTIFACT from C4-C5 hardware limiting evaluation" at the C3-C4 region. The radiologist noted "mild degenerative signal changes visible" but explicitly stated they "cannot exclude moderate compression due to artifact."
  2. CT Myelogram (September 10, 2024): Performed specifically to overcome the MRI hardware artifact. The study confirmed "moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression" and "significant foraminal stenosis" at C3-C4, concluding that the prior MRI had significantly underestimated the pathology due to hardware artifact.

III. PLAN CLINICAL COVERAGE CRITERIA ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)
The requested ACDF at C3-C4 (CPT 22551) fully satisfies all requirements set forth in the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery — Cervical Procedures (Document: CC-SPINE-2024):

1. Section 2.1 — Diagnosis Requirement: MET. The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy, characterized by objective nerve root compression and corresponding radicular symptoms (left deltoid weakness, numbness, and reflex changes). Section 2.1 explicitly requires "MRI or CT myelogram within 6 months of request demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms." The CT myelogram dated September 10, 2024, is within the 6-month window and demonstrates moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression, satisfying this requirement.
2. Section 2.2 — Conservative Treatment Requirement: MET. The member completed all three required conservative modalities:
   - (a) Physical Therapy: Completed 10 weeks (30 sessions) of supervised physical therapy with documented functional outcomes ("no improvement in left C4 radicular symptoms"), exceeding the plan's 6-week minimum.
   - (b) Pharmacotherapy: Completed trials of two neuropathic agents for at least 4 weeks each: Pregabalin 300mg BID for 6 months (partial relief) and Duloxetine 60mg for 3 months (minimal benefit), satisfying the requirement.
   - (c) Interventional Treatment: Received a left C4 epidural steroid injection on September 25, 2024, which provided 60% initial relief but returned to baseline by week 3, satisfying the requirement for at least one interventional procedure.
3. Section 2.3 — Imaging Requirement: MET. The CT myelogram (September 10, 2024) serves as the current, valid imaging study within 6 months, demonstrating moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression and significant foraminal stenosis at the proposed surgical level (C3-C4), which is highly consistent with the member's left C4 radicular symptoms and objective weakness.
4. Section 2.4 — Physician Documentation: MET. Dr. Paul Nguyen provided the necessary clinical documentation, including the surgical rationale for adjacent segment disease, functional limitations, and discussion of risks and alternatives.

IV. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY COMPLIANCE AND LEGAL ANALYSIS
Under California law, the initial denial is legally unsustainable and must be overturned based on the following statutory provisions:

1. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 — Standard of Review: Under CHSC § 1374.32(a), "medically necessary" services are those that a prudent physician would provide in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice and that are clinically appropriate. Furthermore, CHSC § 1374.32(b) explicitly mandates that the medical necessity standard "does NOT require that the service be consistent with the plan's internal coverage criteria." Because a CT myelogram is the clinically accepted gold standard for evaluating cervical pathology in the presence of spinal hardware artifact, relying on a compromised MRI to deny care violates this statutory standard.
2. CHSC § 1374.31(b) — Defective Denial Notice: A denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the denial. The initial denial notice dated October 01, 2024, failed to acknowledge or address the CT myelogram dated September 10, 2024, which was part of the clinical record. By ignoring the definitive diagnostic study and relying solely on an MRI known to be non-diagnostic due to artifact, the denial notice is procedurally DEFECTIVE under California law.
3. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy as Prerequisite: Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), a plan cannot condition surgical approval on physical therapy where the member has completed a course of PT and reached a "functional plateau" without adequate resolution of symptoms. The physical therapy records confirm that after 10 weeks and 30 sessions, the member reached a functional plateau with "no improvement in left C4 radicular symptoms." Mechanically denying the surgery based on a perceived lack of imaging pathology while ignoring the clinical failure of PT violates this statute.

V. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND STANDARD OF CARE ANALYSIS
Generally accepted clinical standards of care strongly support surgical intervention in this case:
1. Diagnostic Modality Selection: According to the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), when a standard MRI is compromised by metallic hardware artifact from a prior fusion, a CT myelogram is the indicated and superior diagnostic modality to evaluate nerve root and spinal cord compression. The initial reviewer's reliance on the MRI findings of "mild degenerative changes" while ignoring the radiologist's explicit warning regarding "significant artifact" represents a severe clinical oversight.
2. Surgical Indications: Under the AANS/CNS Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine Guidelines for Management of Degenerative Cervical Disorders (2023 Update), the member meets Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) criteria for surgical decompression. She presents with cervical radiculopathy, objective motor weakness (deltoid 3+/5), and sensory deficits that have failed to respond to more than 6–12 weeks of comprehensive conservative management (including 10 weeks of PT, dual neuropathic pharmacotherapy, and an epidural injection). Delaying surgical decompression in the face of progressive motor weakness (deltoid 3+/5) poses a significant risk of permanent neurological deficit.

VI. CONCLUSION & AUTHORIZATION DETAILS
Based on the plan's clinical criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory standards (CHSC § 1374.32, CHSC § 1374.31, and CIC § 10169.5), and national neurosurgical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023), the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED.
