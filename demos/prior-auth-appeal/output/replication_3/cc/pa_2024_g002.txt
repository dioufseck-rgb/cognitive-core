Dear Ms. Nair,

The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the appeal filed on November 8, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has OVERTURNED the initial denial and APPROVED the requested surgical procedure.

1. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND DIAGNOSIS
The clinical record establishes a primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (ICD-10: G35.89). A neurological examination on September 5, 2024, documented objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia in the upper extremities, mildly impaired tandem gait, and bilateral hand clumsiness. A neurologist consultation on September 12, 2024, confirmed that these bilateral UMN signs are pathognomonic for cervical myelopathy.

A cervical spine MRI dated September 1, 2024, demonstrated moderate central cord compression at the C5-C6 level and bilateral foraminal stenosis. The MRI report explicitly noted "no T2 signal change" and "no myelomalacia."

2. INITIAL DENIAL AND PLAN CRITERIA EVALUATION
The initial utilization management denial on October 25, 2024, cited CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2, stating that you failed to complete the standard 6-week physical therapy (PT) requirement (having completed 5 weeks).

This initial review contained a pathway error. Because you have a confirmed primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy, the Myelopathy Pathway under Section 2.2A (Exception 2.2(A)) applies, which reduces the physical therapy requirement to a minimum of 4 weeks. You completed 5 weeks of supervised physical therapy, which exceeds this 4-week requirement.

However, a strict technical evaluation of the remaining criteria under Section 2.2A reveals two specific shortfalls:
a) Pharmacotherapy Shortfall: Section 2.2(b) requires a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. While you trialed Baclofen (10mg TID) for 3 months, your trial of the second agent, a Methylprednisolone dose pack, lasted only 3 weeks, falling short of the 4-week contractual minimum.
b) Interventional Waiver Wording: Section 2.2A waives the interventional treatment requirement if the treating physician documents that such treatment "poses risk of neurological deterioration." Dr. Chen's letter stated: "Interventional procedure not indicated — cord compression with myelopathy requires surgical evaluation, not injection." This letter does not use the exact contractual phrasing required by the plan.

3. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS UNDER CALIFORNIA REGULATORY LAW
Under California law, health plans cannot mechanically apply internal coverage criteria when they conflict with state regulations or generally accepted clinical standards of care. The conflicts regarding the conservative treatment shortfalls are resolved as follows:

First, under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, medical necessity determinations must align with generally accepted standards of medical practice. The plan's internal criteria cannot be the sole or determinative basis for a decision if they are more restrictive than clinical standards. According to the American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Joint Guidelines (2023), the North American Spine Society (NASS) Guidelines (2020), and AO Spine Guidelines (2021), surgical decompression is the primary recommended treatment for moderate cervical myelopathy. These guidelines state that there is no high-quality evidence supporting mandatory conservative trials (such as physical therapy, prolonged oral steroids, or injections) for moderate-to-severe myelopathy, and delaying surgery to complete such trials is clinically inappropriate and carries a risk of irreversible neurological decline.

Second, California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) prohibits conditioning the approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Your moderate central cord compression at C5-C6 with bilateral upper motor neuron signs represents objective structural neurological compromise. Therefore, the physical therapy and conservative treatment requirements cannot be mechanically applied to delay or deny necessary surgery.

Third, regarding the pharmacotherapy shortfall, a standard 3-week methylprednisolone dose pack represents a completed clinical course for oral corticosteroids. Forcing a patient with progressive structural cord compression to undergo an additional week of oral steroids solely to satisfy a mechanical plan criterion is clinically contraindicated and violates CHSC § 1374.32.

Fourth, regarding the interventional waiver, while Dr. Chen's letter does not use the exact phrase "poses risk of neurological deterioration," his clinical statement that "cord compression with myelopathy requires surgical evaluation, not injection" clinically implies that delaying surgery for an inappropriate invasive injection poses an unacceptable risk of neurological decline. Under CHSC § 1374.32, requiring an invasive cervical injection for a patient with moderate central cord compression and myelopathy is clinically inappropriate and inconsistent with accepted standards of medical practice.

4. FINAL DETERMINATION
Because generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS, NASS, and AO Spine guidelines) and California regulatory statutes (CHSC § 1374.32 and CIC § 10169.5) supersede the plan's internal conservative treatment requirements, the initial denial is OVERTURNED.

The requested Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is APPROVED as medically necessary.
