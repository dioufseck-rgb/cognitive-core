Dear Ms. Oduya and Dr. Walsh,

The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive, independent clinical review of the medical records, plan coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has determined that the prior authorization denial issued on October 15, 2024, must be UPHELD. 

The requested surgical procedure is not medically necessary at this time because the clinical record does not demonstrate that you have completed the required conservative treatment pathway, nor does it document objective neurological deficits or structural spinal cord compromise that would warrant immediate surgical intervention.

Below is a detailed analysis of the clinical findings, plan criteria, regulatory provisions, and clinical guidelines used to reach this determination.

1. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND RECORD REVIEW
The clinical records submitted for review establish the following clinical facts:
- Presenting Diagnosis: Right-sided C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy (Clinical Record).
- Diagnostic Imaging: A cervical spine MRI dated September 10, 2024, demonstrated a mild-to-moderate disc herniation at C5-C6 with right C6 nerve root contact but without measurable compression. The radiologist documented no cord contact, no cord compression, no T2 signal change, no myelomalacia, and mild right foraminal narrowing (Clinical Record - Imaging).
- Neurological Examination: A physical examination dated September 15, 2024, documented right shoulder and neck pain rated at 6/10, and mild right C6 dermatomal tingling. The examination revealed normal (2+ bilateral and symmetric) bicep reflexes, full (5/5 bilateral) grip strength, no motor weakness, and no upper motor neuron (UMN) signs. The functional assessment noted that the member is working full-time with no activity limitations (Clinical Record - Neurological Exam).
- Conservative Treatment History:
  - Physical Therapy: Completed 4 weeks (12 sessions) of supervised physical therapy. The physical therapy summary noted mild improvement in pain, no neurological deficits to track, and indicated that Dr. Walsh elected to halt physical therapy to seek surgical authorization (Clinical Record - Conservative Treatment).
  - Pharmacotherapy: Completed an 8-week trial of Naproxen 500mg BID, which provided moderate pain relief (Clinical Record - Conservative Treatment).
  - Interventional Treatment: No interventional treatments (such as epidural steroid injections, medial branch blocks, or trigger point injections) have been performed (Clinical Record - Conservative Treatment).

2. ANALYSIS OF PLAN CLINICAL COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2, prior authorization for cervical spinal surgery (CPT 22551) requires that ALL medical necessity criteria be satisfied. The clinical record fails to meet these criteria in the following areas:

- Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): The member does not meet the diagnostic criteria for surgical intervention. 
  - Section 2.1(a) requires a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy, defined as nerve root compression with radicular symptoms (pain, numbness, weakness in a dermatomal distribution). While the member has radicular symptoms (pain and mild tingling), the MRI dated September 10, 2024, shows nerve root contact "without measurable compression."
  - Section 2.1(b) (Cervical Myelopathy) is not met, as there is no spinal cord compression, no myelomalacia, and no myelopathic symptoms (such as gait disturbance, bilateral upper extremity weakness, hyperreflexia, or positive Hoffman sign).
  - Section 2.1(c) (Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit) is not met, as the neurological exam dated September 15, 2024, explicitly documents "no weakness," normal reflexes, and full grip strength.
- Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): The member has not completed the mandatory conservative treatment pathway:
  - Section 2.2(a) requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. The member completed only 4 weeks of physical therapy before it was halted by the treating surgeon (Clinical Record).
  - Section 2.2(b) requires a trial of at least two specified pharmacotherapy agents (NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, or oral corticosteroids) for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The member has only trialed one agent (Naproxen, an NSAID) for 8 weeks (Clinical Record).
  - Section 2.2(c) requires at least one interventional treatment (cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point injection series). The member has had no interventional treatments (Clinical Record).
- Section 2.2(A) (Exception - Myelopathy Pathway): This exception, which reduces the physical therapy requirement to 4 weeks and waives the interventional treatment requirement, does not apply. The exception is restricted to cases where cervical myelopathy is the primary diagnosis. The member's primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, and there is no clinical or imaging evidence of myelopathy (Clinical Record).

Consequently, under Section 3.2 of CC-SPINE-2024, authorization must be denied because the conservative treatment requirements have not been met. Additionally, under Section 3.3, authorization is excluded because the imaging does not demonstrate pathology (such as measurable nerve root or spinal cord compression) consistent with an urgent surgical presentation.

3. ANALYSIS OF CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK
HealthFirst has evaluated this appeal under applicable California statutes and regulations to determine if any regulatory provisions override the plan's internal criteria:

- California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite): Under this statute, a health plan cannot condition surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy under three specific exceptions. None of these exceptions are met:
  1. CIC § 10169.5(a)(1): The treating physician has not documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member.
  2. CIC § 10169.5(a)(2): The member has not reached a "functional plateau" without adequate resolution of symptoms. The physical therapy records note "mild improvement in pain" and indicate that therapy was halted prematurely by the physician, not due to a documented plateau (Clinical Record).
  3. CIC § 10169.5(a)(3): The condition does not involve objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. The MRI shows no measurable nerve root compression and no spinal cord compression, and the neurological exam shows no objective deficits (Clinical Record).
  Therefore, the plan's 6-week physical therapy requirement remains legally enforceable under California law.
- Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014: This guidance addresses prior authorization for time-sensitive neurological conditions and allows for the waiver of conservative treatment when there is objective imaging evidence of progressive pathology (such as cord signal change or myelomalacia) and the treating physician documents urgency. This guidance does not apply to the member's case because the MRI dated September 10, 2024, explicitly notes "no cord contact," "no cord compression," and "no myelomalacia" (Clinical Record).
- California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 (Standard of Review for Independent Medical Review): Under California law, "medically necessary" services must be in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice and clinically appropriate in terms of type, extent, and duration. As detailed below, the requested surgery does not meet generally accepted clinical standards for medical necessity at this time.

4. RESOLUTION OF PHYSICIAN DISPUTE AND CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARDS
Dr. Patrick Walsh, MD, has appealed the initial denial, arguing that his clinical judgment as the treating surgeon with 25 years of experience and direct patient contact should take precedence over a remote reviewer (Appeal Basis). Dr. Walsh asserts that the member has significant cervical pathology and will suffer "permanent disability" if the surgery is not authorized immediately (Clinical Record - Treating Surgeon Statement).

While HealthFirst respects Dr. Walsh's extensive clinical experience, California law (CHSC § 1374.32) and generally accepted standards of medical practice require that medical necessity determinations be based on objective clinical evidence rather than subjective assertions or professional authority. 

A conflict exists between Dr. Walsh's assertion of imminent permanent disability and the objective clinical record. This conflict is resolved in favor of the objective record and established clinical guidelines:
- Objective Imaging vs. Urgency Claim: Dr. Walsh claims the member has "significant cervical pathology" risking permanent disability. However, the objective MRI report dated September 10, 2024, shows only "mild to moderate disc herniation" with "no cord compression" and "no myelomalacia" (Clinical Record). Under the Joint Guidelines of the American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS) and the Congress of Neurological Surgeons (CNS) (2023), surgical urgency is indicated by Tier 1 criteria, which include moderate-to-severe myelopathy, progressive neurological deficits, or myelomalacia on MRI. The member's imaging and clinical presentation do not meet any Tier 1 criteria.
- Objective Exam vs. Disability Claim: Dr. Walsh's claim of imminent permanent disability is directly contradicted by his own neurological examination dated September 15, 2024, which documents that the member has normal reflexes, full (5/5) grip strength, no motor weakness, and is currently "working full-time, no activity limitations" (Clinical Record).
- Clinical Standards for Surgery: Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), surgical intervention for cervical radiculopathy is a Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) option only after a patient has persistent symptoms despite an adequate conservative treatment trial of 6 to 12 weeks. Because the member has only completed 4 weeks of physical therapy, has trialed only one pharmacotherapy agent, and has had no interventional treatments, the clinical presentation falls under Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated) due to an incomplete conservative trial. 

According to the AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021), there is no clinical evidence supporting surgical intervention for mild radiculopathy without a completed course of conservative management. Delaying surgery to complete conservative therapy in the absence of spinal cord compression or progressive motor deficit does not pose a risk of neurological deterioration.

Therefore, the objective clinical evidence and generally accepted medical standards support the initial reviewer's determination (Dr. Rivera, Board-certified neurosurgeon) that the request does not meet medical necessity criteria for surgical intervention at this time.

5. CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS
The prior authorization denial for ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is UPHELD. To establish medical necessity for future surgical consideration under CC-SPINE-2024 and generally accepted clinical standards, the following conservative measures are recommended:
1. Completion of an additional 2 weeks of supervised physical therapy (to satisfy the 6-week requirement under Section 2.2(a)).
2. Trial of a second class of pharmacotherapy (such as a muscle relaxant or neuropathic agent) for at least 4 weeks (to satisfy the two-agent requirement under Section 2.2(b)).
3. Evaluation for and completion of at least one interventional treatment, such as a cervical epidural steroid injection (to satisfy Section 2.2(c)).

If these conservative measures fail to provide adequate symptomatic relief, or if you develop new, objective neurological deficits (such as progressive motor weakness), a new prior authorization request with updated clinical documentation may be submitted.
