DETERMINATION: UPHOLD DENIAL

HealthFirst has completed its review of the appeal submitted on October 30, 2024, regarding the prior authorization denial for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, plan coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, the decision to deny prior authorization is UPHELD.

CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND HISTORY
The member is a 44-year-old female presenting with a diagnosis of right-sided cervical radiculopathy at C5-C6. 
- Imaging: A cervical spine MRI dated September 10, 2024, demonstrated a mild-to-moderate disc herniation at C5-C6 with right C6 nerve root contact but without measurable compression. There was no cord contact, no cord compression, no T2 signal change, and no myelomalacia. Mild right foraminal narrowing was noted.
- Neurological Examination: A clinical examination dated September 15, 2024, documented right shoulder and neck pain rated at 6/10, and mild right C6 dermatomal tingling. The examination revealed normal (2+) bilateral and symmetric bicep reflexes, full (5/5) bilateral grip strength, no motor weakness, and no upper motor neuron (UMN) signs. The functional assessment indicated that the member is working full-time with no activity limitations.
- Conservative Treatment: The clinical record indicates the member completed 4 weeks of supervised physical therapy (12 sessions) with mild improvement in pain and no neurological deficits to track, at which point the treating surgeon elected to halt therapy. Pharmacotherapy consisted of Naproxen 500mg BID for 8 weeks, which provided moderate pain relief. No interventional treatments (such as epidural steroid injections) have been performed.

PLAN CLINICAL COVERAGE CRITERIA ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2, prior authorization for cervical spinal surgery requires that all medical necessity criteria be satisfied:
1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1): The member has a diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy, but the supporting MRI does not demonstrate structural pathology consistent with severe or measurable nerve root compression (showing only nerve root contact without measurable compression, and no cord compression).
2. Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2): The member has not completed the standard conservative treatment pathway:
   - Section 2.2(a) requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. The member completed only 4 weeks before therapy was halted.
   - Section 2.2(b) requires a trial of at least two listed pharmacotherapy agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. The member has only trialed one agent (Naproxen).
   - Section 2.2(c) requires at least one interventional treatment (such as a cervical epidural steroid injection, medial branch block, or trigger point injection series). No interventional treatments have been attempted.
3. Myelopathy Pathway Exception (Section 2.2 Exception): This exception does not apply because the member's primary diagnosis is radiculopathy, and there is no clinical or imaging evidence of cervical myelopathy or myelomalacia.
Therefore, pursuant to Section 3.2, authorization must be denied because the conservative treatment requirements have not been met.

CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARD ANALYSIS
Generally accepted clinical standards, including the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), do not support surgical intervention for this clinical presentation:
- Under the AANS/CNS Guidelines (Section 3.1), the member's presentation is classified as Tier 3 (surgery not indicated) or fails to meet Tier 2 (reasonable indication) criteria. 
- Tier 2 surgical indications for radiculopathy require persistent symptoms after an adequate 6-to-12-week trial of conservative treatment or significant functional impairment. The member has full motor strength (5/5), normal reflexes, no activity limitations, is working full-time, and has only trialed 4 weeks of physical therapy and a single medication.
- There is no high-quality clinical evidence supporting surgical decompression in patients with mild radiculopathy and nerve root contact without compression who have not completed a standard conservative trial.

CALIFORNIA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK ANALYSIS
The plan's determination complies with all applicable California regulatory requirements:
1. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL 22-014): This guidance mandates the waiver of conservative treatment requirements only for time-sensitive neurological conditions, specifically cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI. Because the member's MRI explicitly notes "no cord compression" and "no myelomalacia," and the clinical exam shows no myelopathic symptoms, the myelopathy exception under APL 22-014 does not apply.
2. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5: Under this statute, a plan cannot condition surgical approval on physical therapy completion if physical therapy is contraindicated, if the patient reached a documented "functional plateau," or if there is objective structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. In this case:
   - There is no documentation that physical therapy is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member (CIC § 10169.5(a)(1)).
   - The physical therapy records do not document a functional plateau; rather, they note mild improvement in pain before the treating surgeon elected to halt therapy (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)).
   - The MRI and clinical exam show no objective structural neurological compromise (such as measurable nerve root compression or cord compression) that physical therapy cannot address (CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)).
Consequently, the plan's physical therapy and conservative treatment requirements remain legally enforceable under California law.

RESOLUTION OF PHYSICIAN CONFLICT
We have carefully considered the appeal arguments submitted by the treating surgeon, Dr. Patrick Walsh, MD, who asserts that his 25 years of spine surgery experience and direct patient examination should supersede the plan's remote review, and that the member faces permanent disability without immediate surgery.

While we respect Dr. Walsh's extensive clinical experience, under generally accepted standards of medical practice and California regulatory standards (CHSC § 1374.32), medical necessity determinations must be based on objective clinical evidence rather than subjective assertions of professional authority. 
- The objective clinical record contradicts the assertion of clinical urgency or risk of permanent disability: the MRI dated September 10, 2024, shows only mild-to-moderate herniation with nerve root contact and no compression, and the neurological exam dated September 15, 2024, confirms normal reflexes, full (5/5) motor strength, and no functional limitations.
- Professional experience and direct examination do not alter these documented objective findings. Therefore, the objective clinical evidence and established clinical guidelines must take precedence, and the conservative treatment pathway must be completed before surgical intervention can be considered medically necessary.
