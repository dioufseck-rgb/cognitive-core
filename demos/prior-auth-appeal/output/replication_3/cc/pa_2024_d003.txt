The California Division of Medicine and Regulation has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization denial for the requested Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's internal coverage criteria, and the applicable California statutory and regulatory framework, the initial denial issued by HealthFirst on October 15, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED. 

HealthFirst is ordered to immediately authorize and cover the requested surgical procedure. The legal and clinical bases for this determination are detailed below.

I. PROCEDURAL ANALYSIS & STATUTORY DEEMED APPROVAL
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a), health plans are mandated to provide written notice of any medical necessity denial. This notice must explicitly include the specific clinical criteria applied, the specific clinical reasons for the denial, and a notice of Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights. 

The initial denial notice issued by HealthFirst on October 15, 2024, stated that the request was denied based on a peer-to-peer discussion between the plan medical director (Dr. Alan Foster) and the requesting physician, and that the detailed criteria review was "provided verbally during peer-to-peer call." No written criteria, specific clinical reasons, or IMR notices were enclosed in the written denial.

Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is legally defective and "shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines."

Furthermore, under CHSC § 1367.01 and California Code of Regulations (CCR) Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1, a health plan must issue a prior authorization decision within five (5) business days of receiving all necessary information. Because HealthFirst's October 15, 2024 notice was legally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b), it is treated as if no denial was issued. Consequently, the plan failed to issue a timely, legally compliant decision within the statutory 5-business-day timeframe.

Under CHSC § 1367.01 and CCR Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1, if a plan fails to issue a timely decision, the request is "DEEMED APPROVED" for the period of the delay. Remanding this case to allow the plan to reissue a compliant denial notice would violate these statutory timelines and allow the plan to circumvent the consequences of its untimely processing. Therefore, the procedural defect legally nullifies the denial, triggering an immediate, non-appealable deemed approval under California law.

II. CLINICAL-LEGAL ANALYSIS & PHYSICAL THERAPY OVERRIDE
The plan's verbal denial was substantively based on the member's failure to meet the 6-week physical therapy requirement outlined in HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a). The clinical record indicates that the member completed 5 weeks of supervised physical therapy (15 sessions).

California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 directly regulates and overrides a health plan's ability to condition surgical approvals on the completion of physical therapy. Under CIC § 10169.5(a), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where:
1. The member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)); OR
2. The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address (CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)).

Both statutory exceptions are met in this case:
- Functional Plateau: The member completed 5 weeks (15 sessions) of physical therapy. In the appeal letter, the treating physician (Dr. Robert Chen) explicitly documented and declared that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms. Under CIC § 10169.5(b), a "functional plateau" is determined by the treating physician or physical therapist.
- Structural Neurological Compromise: The clinical record contains objective evidence of severe structural neurological compromise. The MRI dated September 1, 2024, demonstrates a C6-C7 disc herniation with right C7 nerve root compression and moderate foraminal stenosis. The neurological examination dated September 5, 2024, confirms correlating objective deficits, including right C7 dermatomal numbness and tingling, a diminished tricep reflex, and 4+/5 grip strength. Physical therapy cannot resolve this mechanical nerve root compression.

Under CIC § 10169.5(c), a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy "shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the member has achieved a functional plateau or whether the condition involves neurological urgency." Applying the hierarchy of state law > clinical standard > plan criteria, the statutory provisions of CIC § 10169.5 override HealthFirst's internal 6-week physical therapy requirement. The member's 5-week course of physical therapy is legally and clinically sufficient.

III. CLINICAL EVIDENCE & MEDICAL NECESSITY STANDARDS
Under CHSC § 1374.32, the standard of review for medical necessity is based on generally accepted standards of medical practice and clinical appropriateness, rather than strict adherence to a plan's internal, more restrictive criteria.

According to the American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS) and the Congress of Neurological Surgeons (CNS) Joint Guidelines (2023) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Clinical Guidelines (2020), surgical decompression via ACDF (CPT 22551) is a Tier 2 (reasonable and indicated) intervention for patients presenting with cervical radiculopathy, persistent symptoms, and objective neurological deficits (such as reflex changes and motor weakness) who have failed a reasonable course of conservative management.

The member has completed a robust conservative regimen, including:
1. Physical Therapy: 5 weeks (15 sessions) resulting in a documented functional plateau.
2. Pharmacotherapy: 3 months of Meloxicam 15mg daily (NSAID) and a 2-week Prednisone taper (oral corticosteroid), satisfying the requirement for two agents trialed for at least 4 weeks under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b).

While the plan's internal criteria (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(c)) require an invasive interventional treatment (such as an epidural steroid injection), generally accepted clinical standards do not mandate invasive injections prior to surgical decompression when there is documented, progressive structural neurological compromise (4+/5 grip strength and diminished reflex). Under CHSC § 1374.32(b) and DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, a plan's internal criteria cannot be more restrictive than generally accepted clinical standards. Forcing a patient with objective motor weakness and reflex deficits to undergo invasive spinal injections when conservative therapy has plateaued is clinically inappropriate and inconsistent with standard medical practice.

IV. ORDER OF THE DIVISION
Pursuant to California Health & Safety Code § 1374.31, § 1367.01, and California Insurance Code § 10169.5, the prior authorization denial for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is hereby OVERTURNED.

1. Immediately approve prior authorization for CPT 22551 at Stanford Medical Center, as requested by Dr. Robert Chen, MD.
