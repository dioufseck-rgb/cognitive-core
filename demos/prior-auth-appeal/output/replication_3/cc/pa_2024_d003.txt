DISPOSITION:
The prior authorization denial for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C6-C7 (CPT 22551) is hereby REMANDED to HealthFirst Health Plan. The plan is ordered to immediately vacate its initial denial and re-evaluate the request in accordance with California statutory requirements and the instructions detailed in this determination.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
On October 15, 2024, HealthFirst Health Plan issued an initial denial of prior authorization for a single-level ACDF at C6-C7 (CPT 22551) requested by Dr. Robert Chen on behalf of Jessica Turner. The plan's written denial notice cited "MED-003" and stated that the request was denied based on a peer-to-peer discussion between the plan's medical director, Dr. Alan Foster, and the requesting physician, during which verbal criteria were discussed. The member appealed on October 30, 2024, on the grounds that the plan failed to provide written clinical criteria in the denial notice, in violation of California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31.

A review of the clinical record reveals that the member does not fully satisfy the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) due to shortfalls in conservative therapy (5 weeks of physical therapy completed versus 6 weeks required, lack of interventional treatment, and a 2-week prednisone trial versus the 4-week requirement). However, the plan's denial notice is critically and fatally defective under California law because the clinical criteria were communicated verbally only, and the mandatory Independent Medical Review (IMR) notice was omitted. Furthermore, the plan mechanically applied its 6-week physical therapy requirement in violation of California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5, failing to evaluate the treating physician's written declaration of a functional plateau. 

Because a defective denial notice under CHSC § 1374.31(b) must be treated as if no denial was issued, and because statutory regulatory requirements supersede conflicting plan criteria, this case is REMANDED to the plan for immediate re-evaluation and reissuance of a legally compliant determination.

II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND RECORD REVIEW
The clinical record submitted for review contains the following objective findings:
1. Presenting Diagnosis: Cervical radiculopathy at C6-C7.
2. Diagnostic Imaging: An MRI of the cervical spine dated September 1, 2024, demonstrates a C6-C7 disc herniation, right C7 nerve root compression, and moderate foraminal stenosis.
3. Neurological Examination: A physical and neurological exam dated September 5, 2024, reveals objective neurological deficits, including right C7 dermatomal numbness and tingling, a diminished tricep reflex, and right hand grip strength of 4+/5.
4. Conservative Treatment History:
   - Physical Therapy: The member completed 5 weeks of supervised physical therapy (total of 15 sessions). While a formal plateau was not documented in the physical therapist's daily notes, the treating physician, Dr. Robert Chen, explicitly declared in the appeal letter that the member had reached a functional plateau.
   - Pharmacotherapy: The member completed a 3-month trial of Meloxicam (15mg daily), which provided only partial relief. The member also completed a 2-week oral prednisone taper, which yielded only temporary improvement.
   - Interventional Treatment: No cervical epidural steroid injections, medial branch blocks, or trigger point injections have been performed.

III. PLAN CRITERIA COMPLIANCE ANALYSIS
Under HealthFirst Health Plan's Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery (Document: CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2), authorization for CPT 22551 requires that all medical necessity criteria be satisfied:
1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1): The member meets Section 2.1(a) (Cervical radiculopathy with nerve root compression and radicular symptoms) and Section 2.1(c) (Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit), supported by the MRI dated September 1, 2024, and the neurological exam dated September 5, 2024.
2. Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2):
   - Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): The plan requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy. The member completed only 5 weeks, representing a 1-week shortfall.
   - Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): The plan requires a trial of at least two specified agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each. While the member's 3-month trial of Meloxicam (NSAID) satisfies the first agent requirement, the 2-week prednisone taper (oral corticosteroid) falls short of the 4-week minimum duration required for the second agent.
   - Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): The plan requires at least one interventional treatment (e.g., cervical epidural steroid injection). The member has had none.
3. Myelopathy Pathway Exception (Section 2.2 Exception): This exception is inapplicable because the member's primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy, and there is no documented diagnosis of cervical myelopathy or myelomalacia.

Consequently, under a strict, mechanical reading of the plan's internal criteria, the clinical record does not satisfy the conservative treatment requirements of Section 2.2, and the request would be subject to denial under Section 3.2.

IV. REGULATORY COMPLIANCE AND STATUTORY VIOLATIONS
Under California law, statutory and regulatory mandates govern prior authorization processes and supersede any conflicting internal plan guidelines. The plan's initial review and denial of this request involved multiple critical statutory violations:

1. Defective Denial Notice under CHSC § 1374.31(a) and (b)
California Health & Safety Code § 1374.31(a) mandates that when a plan issues a denial based on medical necessity, it "SHALL... [p]rovide written notice of the denial" which must "[i]nclude in the denial notice: the specific criteria applied, the specific clinical reason for the denial, and notice of IMR rights."
In this case, the plan's written denial notice dated October 15, 2024, failed to include any of these elements. Instead, the plan relied on a verbal peer-to-peer call between Dr. Alan Foster and Dr. Robert Chen to communicate the clinical criteria.
CHSC § 1374.31(b) explicitly states: "A denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria applied or the specific clinical reason for the denial is DEFECTIVE and shall be treated as if no denial was issued for purposes of appeal timelines." Verbal communication during a peer-to-peer call cannot substitute for a written denial notice. Because the plan failed to document the specific clinical criteria and reasons in writing, the denial notice is legally defective and invalid.

2. Omission of Mandatory Independent Medical Review (IMR) Notice
CHSC § 1374.31(a)(2) requires the plan to include a notice of the member's right to an Independent Medical Review (IMR) in every medical necessity denial. The plan's denial notice completely omitted this notice, representing a major procedural violation that deprives the member of their statutory rights.

3. Violation of Physical Therapy Conditioning under CIC § 10169.5
California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a) provides that a health insurer "shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on completion of physical therapy where... (2) [t]he member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms."
Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(c) prohibits plans from mechanically applying a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy without reference to whether a functional plateau has been reached.
Here, the member completed 5 weeks (15 sessions) of physical therapy, and the treating physician, Dr. Robert Chen, documented in writing in the appeal letter that the member had reached a functional plateau. By mechanically denying the request because the member completed 5 weeks instead of the plan's required 6 weeks, the plan violated CIC § 10169.5. The plan was legally required to evaluate the treating physician's declaration of a functional plateau rather than mechanically enforcing its internal 6-week rule.

V. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND GENERALLY ACCEPTED STANDARDS
Generally accepted clinical standards, including the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020) and the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), support conservative management as the initial treatment for cervical radiculopathy. However, these guidelines also recognize that when a patient exhibits objective neurological deficits (such as right C7 dermatomal numbness, a diminished tricep reflex, and a 4+/5 grip strength deficit) and has failed to improve after a substantial course of physical therapy and multi-agent pharmacotherapy, surgical decompression via ACDF is a standard and effective treatment option.
Furthermore, clinical standards dictate that forcing a patient who has already reached a functional plateau to undergo additional physical therapy is clinically inappropriate, as it delays necessary definitive care and exposes the patient to prolonged neurological compromise. This clinical consensus is codified in California law under CIC § 10169.5, which recognizes that a documented functional plateau supersedes arbitrary, mechanical duration requirements.

VI. RESOLUTION OF CROSS-SOURCE CONFLICTS
A conflict exists between the plan's internal clinical criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), which require 6 weeks of physical therapy, 4 weeks of two separate medications, and an interventional procedure, and California statutory law (CHSC § 1374.31 and CIC § 10169.5), which invalidates verbal-only denials and prohibits the mechanical application of physical therapy duration requirements when a functional plateau is documented.
Applying the established hierarchy of authority, California statutory law and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) regulations strictly supersede the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria. The plan cannot rely on clinical non-compliance with its internal criteria to sustain a denial that was issued through a legally defective process and in violation of statutory protections. Because the denial notice is legally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b), it must be treated as if no denial was issued, and the plan's mechanical enforcement of the 6-week physical therapy requirement is legally impermissible under CIC § 10169.5.

VII. ORDER OF REMAND AND INSTRUCTIONS TO THE PLAN
Based on the foregoing findings, HealthFirst Health Plan is hereby ORDERED to:
1. Vacate the defective initial denial dated October 15, 2024.
2. Re-evaluate the prior authorization request for a single-level ACDF C6-C7 (CPT 22551) for Jessica Turner.
3. In conducting this re-evaluation, the plan must:
   - Apply CIC § 10169.5 and evaluate the treating physician's written declaration of a functional plateau, rather than mechanically enforcing the 6-week physical therapy requirement of CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a).
   - Evaluate whether the member's objective neurological deficits (diminished tricep reflex, 4+/5 grip strength) and failed conservative trials (3 months of Meloxicam, 2 weeks of Prednisone, and 5 weeks of PT) establish medical necessity under generally accepted clinical standards.
4. Within five (5) business days of the date of this order, the plan must issue a new determination. If the plan determines that the request is approved, it must issue an authorization. If the plan determines that the request is denied, it must issue a fully compliant, written denial notice that:
   - Explicitly details the specific clinical criteria applied and the specific clinical reasons for the denial.
   - Includes the mandatory written notice of Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights as required by CHSC § 1374.31(a).
   - Is provided in writing to both the member and the treating physician, Dr. Robert Chen.

Failure to issue a compliant written decision within the statutory timeframe may result in the request being deemed approved pursuant to CHSC § 1367.01 and CCR Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1.
