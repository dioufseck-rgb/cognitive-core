- Surgical Rationale: Dr. Wilson recommended a two-level fusion to address the C5-C6 pathology and to prophylactically stabilize the C6-C7 level to prevent adjacent segment disease, noting a 25% 5-year incidence of adjacent segment disease after single-level ACDF.

I. ANALYSIS OF C5-C6 LEVEL (OVERTURNED / APPROVED)

A. Plan Criteria Compliance (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)
The clinical record fully satisfies all medical necessity criteria for the C5-C6 level under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria Document CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2:
1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1(a)): The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with nerve root compression and correlating radicular symptoms (right C6 numbness, tingling, diminished bicep reflex, and 4/5 grip strength) supported by an MRI within 6 months demonstrating structural pathology (large C5-C6 disc herniation with moderate-severe nerve root compression) consistent with symptoms.
2. Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2):
   - Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): The member completed 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy (exceeding the 6-week minimum) directed toward the cervical condition.
   - Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): The member completed trials of two covered agents for at least 4 weeks each (Gabapentin for 4 months and Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks).
   - Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): The member underwent a right C6 epidural steroid injection on October 1, 2024 (meeting the minimum of 1 interventional treatment).
3. Imaging Requirement (Section 2.3): The MRI dated September 1, 2024, is within 6 months and demonstrates structural pathology consistent with the C5-C6 level.
4. Physician Documentation (Section 2.4): Dr. Wilson provided the necessary clinical rationale and documentation of functional limitations.

B. Clinical Evidence Standards
Under generally accepted clinical standards, specifically the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Radiculopathy (2023) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020), surgical decompression and fusion (ACDF) is highly indicated for patients with persistent radiculopathy who have objective neurological deficits and correlating imaging findings that have failed to respond to an adequate course (6-12 weeks) of conservative management. The member meets Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication) under these guidelines.

C. California Regulatory Standards
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, "medically necessary" services include those that are in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice and are clinically appropriate in terms of site and duration. The C5-C6 ACDF meets this standard. Furthermore, under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2), because the member completed a course of physical therapy and reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms, conditioning surgical approval on further physical therapy is prohibited.

II. ANALYSIS OF C6-C7 LEVEL (UPHELD / DENIED)

A. Plan Criteria Non-Compliance (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)
The clinical record fails to satisfy the medical necessity criteria for the C6-C7 level under HealthFirst Document CC-SPINE-2024:
1. Imaging and Symptom Correlation (Section 2.3 & Section 3.3): Section 2.3 requires that current imaging demonstrate structural pathology consistent with the member's symptoms and the proposed surgical level. Section 3.3 explicitly mandates denial when imaging does not demonstrate pathology at the proposed surgical level consistent with the clinical presentation. 
2. Clinical Mismatch: The MRI shows only a mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing at C6-C7, with no nerve root compression or cord contact. Furthermore, there are no documented C7 radicular symptoms or neurological deficits (such as C7 dermatomal sensory loss, triceps weakness, or diminished triceps reflex) in the clinical record. Therefore, the C6-C7 level fails to meet Section 2.3 and is excluded under Section 3.3.

B. Clinical Evidence Standards
Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) and NASS Guidelines (2020), surgical fusion of an adjacent asymptomatic level solely for prophylactic stabilization to prevent adjacent segment disease does not meet the threshold of medical necessity. 
- Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated) of the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) specifically excludes surgical intervention for imaging findings without correlating symptoms. 
- While adjacent segment disease is a recognized long-term risk (noted by Dr. Wilson as a 25% 5-year incidence), there is no high-quality clinical evidence supporting prophylactic fusion of a level with only mild degenerative changes and no clinical symptoms. Doing so unnecessarily increases surgical time, blood loss, and the risk of pseudarthrosis without a proven therapeutic benefit.

C. California Regulatory Standards
Under the California Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard set forth in CHSC § 1374.32(a), a service must be clinically appropriate in terms of extent and site, and must be in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice. Prophylactic fusion of an asymptomatic cervical level with minimal imaging findings is not clinically appropriate and does not conform to generally accepted standards of medical practice. Therefore, the C6-C7 level is not medically necessary under California law.
