Dear Ms. Brooks and Dr. Park,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its first-level internal appeal review of the prior authorization denial for the requested anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C4-C5 (CPT 22551). 

After a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, and the California regulatory framework, we have determined that a genuine clinical conflict exists regarding the radiological interpretation of the patient's MRI. Because this conflict cannot be unilaterally resolved at the internal appeal level under the plan's approved criteria, the final determination is to REMAND this case for Independent Medical Review (IMR) under California Health and Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.30.

I. CLINICAL BACKGROUND AND ESTABLISHED FACTS
The clinical record establishes the following undisputed facts regarding the member's clinical presentation:
1. Clinical Symptoms: The member exhibits objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral hyperreflexia of the upper extremities, and a subtle balance impairment on tandem gait (documented in the Neurological Exam dated October 5, 2024).
2. Conservative Treatment: The member completed an 8-week course of supervised physical therapy (24 sessions) directed at her cervical spine, which resulted in no improvement in her UMN signs (documented in the Physical Therapy Summary).
3. Pharmacotherapy: The member completed trials of Gabapentin (400mg TID for 3 months with partial benefit) and Cyclobenzaprine (6 weeks with minimal benefit), satisfying the pharmacotherapy requirements.
4. Interventional Treatment Contraindication: The treating physician, Dr. James Park, MD, did not refer the patient for epidural steroid injections due to concerns regarding cord compression. Under clinical standards, epidural steroid injections are relatively contraindicated in patients with significant spinal cord compression due to the risk of acute neurological deterioration.

II. THE RADIOLOGICAL CONFLICT
The central dispute in this case concerns the interpretation of the cervical spine MRI dated October 1, 2024. Three qualified specialists have provided conflicting interpretations of the same imaging study:
1. Radiologist A (Original Read, October 1, 2024): Noted C4-C5 disc herniation with cord contact. Identified "possible early T2 signal change" but concluded this finding was "equivocal" and "may represent artifact," recommending clinical correlation.
2. Radiologist B (Treating Hospital Second Read, October 15, 2024): Interpreted the same MRI as demonstrating "C4-C5 cord compression with early myelomalacia." Radiologist B explicitly noted that the "T2 hyperintensity is reproducible on multiple sequences and represents early cord signal change, not artifact," concluding the findings are consistent with early myelopathy.
3. Plan Radiologist (Review, November 5, 2024): Reviewed the imaging and concluded that the "T2 signal change is below threshold for clinical significance," is "not consistent with myelomalacia," and "represents artifact or normal variant."

III. APPLICATION OF PLAN CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1(b), authorization for cervical spinal surgery under the myelopathy pathway requires a "confirmed diagnosis" of cervical myelopathy, defined as spinal cord compression with myelopathic symptoms. 

If cervical myelopathy is confirmed, the conservative treatment requirements are modified under Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway):
- The physical therapy requirement is reduced to a 4-week minimum (satisfied by the member's 8 weeks of PT).
- The interventional treatment requirement (Section 2.2(c)) is waived if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration (satisfied by Dr. Park's documentation of the relative contraindication of epidural injections in the presence of cord compression).
- The pharmacotherapy requirement remains in full (satisfied by the member's trials of Gabapentin and Cyclobenzaprine).

However, because of the directly conflicting interpretations of the MRI by qualified radiologists, the diagnosis of myelopathy/myelomalacia cannot be unilaterally "confirmed" under Section 2.1(b) at the internal appeal level. Unilaterally accepting Radiologist B's reading to overturn the denial would ignore the conflicting assessments of Radiologist A and the Plan Radiologist, establishing an unsustainable precedent where any disputed reading is automatically decided against the plan's board-certified reviewers. Conversely, relying solely on the Plan Radiologist's review to uphold the denial ignores the treating hospital's specialist reading and the patient's objective clinical UMN signs.

IV. REGULATORY AUTHORITY AND JUSTIFICATION FOR REMAND
To resolve this clinical impasse, we must apply the appropriate California regulatory framework:
1. Standard of Review at the Internal Appeal Level: At the internal first-level appeal, the plan is contractually and operationally bound to apply its approved clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024). The plan's internal reviewers do not have the unilateral authority to waive the "confirmed diagnosis" requirement of Section 2.1(b) when a genuine radiological conflict exists.
2. Correction of CHSC § 1374.32(b) Application: While clinical guidelines (such as the AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines) support surgical decompression for moderate-to-severe myelopathy with myelomalacia, the statutory standard of review under CHSC § 1374.32(b)—which mandates that medical necessity determinations be made in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice regardless of restrictive internal plan criteria—applies specifically to the Independent Medical Reviewer (an external third party designated by the DMHC). It does not govern the plan's internal first-level appeal.
3. Remand to Independent Medical Review (IMR): Under CHSC § 1374.30, any enrollee whose request for service has been denied or delayed on the basis of medical necessity has the right to apply for an Independent Medical Review. Because the conflicting qualified radiologist interpretations of the same MRI create genuine clinical uncertainty, this dispute must be resolved by an independent, external specialist. 

Therefore, HealthFirst is remanding this case to the Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) IMR process under CHSC § 1374.30. This administrative action ensures that an independent, board-certified neuroradiologist or neurosurgeon will review the disputed MRI sequences and clinical records to determine whether the requested ACDF is medically necessary under the objective standards of CHSC § 1374.32.

To facilitate an expedited resolution, HealthFirst has prepared the necessary documentation for submission to the DMHC. 
- The member or the authorized representative (Dr. Park) may submit the IMR application directly to the DMHC.

Under an expedited review, the DMHC will issue a binding decision within three (3) days of receipt of the completed application (per CHSC § 1374.30 / CCR Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1).
