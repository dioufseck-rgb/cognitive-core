Dear Helen Brooks,

The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the appeal filed on your behalf by Dr. James Park, MD, regarding the prior authorization denial for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C4-C5 level (CPT 22551) to be performed at UCSF Medical Center. 

After a comprehensive clinical and regulatory evaluation of your medical records, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California statutory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, HealthFirst has OVERTURNED the initial denial. The requested surgical procedure (CPT 22551) is APPROVED.

I. CLINICAL BACKGROUND AND ESTABLISHED FACTS
The clinical records demonstrate that you are a 49-year-old female presenting with early cervical myelopathy. The following clinical facts are established:
1. Neurological Examination (dated October 5, 2024): Documented objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs, including bilateral Hoffman's signs, bilateral hyperreflexia of the upper extremities, and subtle balance impairment on tandem gait.
2. Diagnostic Imaging (MRI dated October 1, 2024): The original MRI report noted a C4-C5 disc herniation with cord contact and possible early T2 signal change. A subsequent second read by Radiologist B at the treating hospital (dated October 15, 2024) confirmed C4-C5 cord compression with early myelomalacia, noting that the T2 hyperintensity is reproducible on multiple sequences and represents early cord signal change rather than an artifact.
3. Conservative Treatment: You have completed 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy (24 sessions) with documented failure to improve your UMN signs. Additionally, you completed a 3-month trial of Gabapentin (400mg TID) with only partial benefit and a 6-week trial of Cyclobenzaprine with minimal benefit.
4. Interventional Treatment: No interventional procedures (such as epidural steroid injections) were performed. Dr. James Park documented that an epidural steroid injection was intentionally not performed because it is relatively contraindicated in the presence of spinal cord compression and poses a risk of acute neurological deterioration.

II. PLAN CLINICAL CRITERIA ANALYSIS
The initial denial on November 20, 2024, asserted that the "Myelopathy Exception" did not apply because the plan's imaging reviewer (Dr. Alan Foster, MD, on November 5, 2024) interpreted the T2 signal change as an artifact or normal variant below the threshold for clinical significance. Consequently, the plan evaluated your request under the "Standard Pathway," denying authorization because you had not completed an interventional procedure. This application of the plan's criteria was incorrect under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024:

1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1): You have a confirmed diagnosis of cervical myelopathy under Section 2.1(b), supported by objective myelopathic symptoms (gait disturbance, hyperreflexia, and positive Hoffman signs) and MRI evidence of cord compression.
2. Myelopathy Pathway Exception (Section 2.2(A)): Under Section 2.2(A), when cervical myelopathy is the primary diagnosis, the conservative treatment requirements are modified as follows:
   - Physical Therapy: The minimum requirement is reduced from 6 weeks to 4 weeks. Your completion of 8 weeks of physical therapy (24 sessions) fully satisfies and exceeds this requirement.
   - Pharmacotherapy: The requirement for a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each (Section 2.2(b)) is fully met by your 3-month trial of Gabapentin and 6-week trial of Cyclobenzaprine.
   - Interventional Treatment: Section 2.2(A) explicitly states that the interventional treatment requirement (Section 2.2(c)) is WAIVED if the treating physician documents that interventional treatment poses a risk of neurological deterioration. Dr. Park provided clear documentation that an epidural steroid injection was contraindicated due to cord compression and the risk of neurological deterioration.
   
Therefore, you fully satisfy the plan's own Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A) Myelopathy Pathway Exception. The initial reviewer's insistence on the completion of an interventional procedure was a misapplication of the plan's guidelines.

III. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY COMPLIANCE ANALYSIS
The initial denial and any further delay of surgical authorization violate California regulatory standards:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3): Under California law, a health plan cannot condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where the member's condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Your cervical myelopathy with myelomalacia represents objective structural neurological compromise. Conditioning surgical approval on further conservative therapy or mechanical adherence to standard pathways is a direct violation of CIC § 10169.5(a)(3).
2. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:
   - Section II.1: The Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) designates cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI as an urgent condition where delay risks irreversible neurological harm.
   - Section II.2: Plans are strictly prohibited from requiring conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia or cord signal change) and the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed to arrest progression.
   - Section II.3: Plans are prohibited from applying internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice.
   - By denying authorization and delaying surgery for an active spinal cord injury based on a conflicting internal radiologist review, the plan violated APL 22-014.
3. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32: Under CHSC § 1374.32, medical necessity must be evaluated in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice. The plan cannot unilaterally rely on its own radiologist's review (dated November 5, 2024) to dismiss the reproducible myelomalacia findings documented by the treating hospital's radiologist (dated October 15, 2024) and the objective clinical findings of your treating neurosurgeon. Under CHSC § 1367.01, the plan must issue a final binary determination (Overturn or Uphold) within 30 days of an appeal; a "remand" or delay is not legally permissible and would trigger a "deemed approved" status under CHSC § 1367.01 and CCR Title 28 § 1300.67.2.1.

IV. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND GUIDELINE ALIGNMENT
The requested ACDF C4-C5 procedure is strongly supported by generally accepted clinical standards:
1. AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023 Update): Surgical decompression is designated as a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) recommendation for patients with cervical myelopathy, objective cord compression, and myelomalacia on MRI.
2. AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021): These guidelines state that there is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy or interventional injections prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in these patients to complete conservative trials is clinically contraindicated and associated with a high risk of irreversible neurological deterioration.
3. Cochrane Review (2021): Confirms that conservative treatment has limited to no efficacy in arresting the progression of moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy or addressing objective upper motor neuron deficits.

V. CONCLUSION
Because you meet the plan's Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(A) Myelopathy Pathway Exception, and because the initial denial and any further delay of surgical authorization violate California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(3), DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014, and generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023), the initial denial is OVERTURNED.

The request for Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C4-C5 (CPT 22551) is APPROVED. Dr. James Park and UCSF Medical Center may proceed with scheduling the procedure.
