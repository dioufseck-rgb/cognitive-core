DETERMINATION: UPHOLD DENIAL

Dear Mr. Flores and Dr. Davis,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). After a comprehensive review of the clinical documentation, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, the decision has been made to UPHOLD the initial denial of coverage. The requested surgical procedure is not considered medically necessary under the member's plan criteria or under generally accepted clinical standards.

I. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND HISTORY
Mr. Flores is a 54-year-old male employed as a construction worker specializing in heavy equipment operation. He presents with a primary complaint of axial neck pain, rated at 5/10. 
- Imaging: A cervical spine MRI dated September 15, 2024, demonstrates severe central canal stenosis at C5-C6 with a canal diameter of 6 mm (normal is greater than 13 mm). The MRI report notes spinal cord contact at this level but explicitly states there is no cord compression, no T2 signal change, and no myelomalacia.
- Neurological Examination: A comprehensive neurological examination performed on September 20, 2024, was completely normal. There was no evidence of cervical radiculopathy (no dermatomal pain, numbness, or weakness) and no signs of cervical myelopathy (no gait disturbance, no bilateral upper extremity weakness, no hyperreflexia, no positive Hoffman sign, and no upper motor neuron signs).
- Conservative Treatment: The clinical record documents that the member has completed 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy (24 sessions) for axial neck pain, resulting in modest pain improvement (from 7/10 to 5/10) but with no neurological symptoms to treat. Pharmacotherapy included Naproxen 500mg BID for 3 months (providing moderate pain relief) and Cyclobenzaprine for 6 weeks (providing sleep improvement only). Additionally, the member underwent a C5-C6 epidural steroid injection on October 10, 2024, which provided 60% pain relief lasting 6 weeks.
- Requesting Surgeon's Assessment: Dr. Davis requested prophylactic decompression, noting that the member's critically narrow canal diameter (6 mm) puts him at high risk for acute spinal cord injury from trauma, particularly given his high-risk occupation as a construction worker operating heavy equipment.

II. PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS (CC-SPINE-2024)
The prior authorization request was evaluated against HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery — Cervical Procedures (Document: CC-SPINE-2024).
1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1): Section 2.1 requires a confirmed diagnosis of either (a) cervical radiculopathy with nerve root compression and radicular symptoms, (b) cervical myelopathy with spinal cord compression and myelopathic symptoms, or (c) cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit. 
   - Evaluation: The clinical record explicitly documents a completely normal neurological examination on September 20, 2024, with no radiculopathy, no myelopathy, and no objective neurological deficits. Therefore, the member does not satisfy the diagnosis requirements of Section 2.1.
2. Exclusions (Section 3.1): Section 3.1 states that authorization will be denied when "surgery is requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy."
   - Evaluation: Because the member's only clinical symptom is axial neck pain (5/10) and there is no documented radiculopathy or myelopathy, the request is subject to this exclusion.
3. Conservative Treatment (Section 2.2): While the member has completed conservative treatment (8 weeks of physical therapy, multiple pharmacotherapy trials, and an epidural steroid injection), meeting these conservative requirements does not override the fundamental lack of a qualifying diagnosis under Section 2.1.

III. CLINICAL EVIDENCE STANDARD ANALYSIS
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32, the standard for medical necessity must align with generally accepted standards of medical practice. We evaluated this case against the clinical guidelines from the American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS) / Congress of Neurological Surgeons (CNS) Joint Section on Disorders of the Spine (2023 Update) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020).
1. Surgical Indications (AANS/CNS 2023):
   - Tier 1 (Strong Indication): Recommended for moderate to severe myelopathy with objective cord compression, progressive neurological deficits, or myelomalacia (cord signal change) on MRI. Mr. Flores has no myelopathy, no neurological deficits, and no myelomalacia.
   - Tier 2 (Reasonable Indication): Recommended for mild myelopathy or persistent radiculopathy failing conservative treatment. Mr. Flores has neither condition.
   - Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated): Specifically includes "axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy" (Section 3.1(a)) and "imaging findings without correlating symptoms" (Section 3.1(b)).
2. Prophylactic Decompression: Generally accepted clinical standards do not support prophylactic surgical decompression (ACDF) for asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic cervical stenosis (axial pain only) in the absence of objective neurological deficits or myelomalacia, even when severe canal stenosis is present on imaging. The risk of major surgical complications from an ACDF outweighs the theoretical, unquantified risk of catastrophic spinal cord injury from future trauma.

IV. REGULATORY FRAMEWORK ANALYSIS (CALIFORNIA LAW)
1. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 (Standard of Review): This statute defines medical necessity based on what a prudent physician would provide in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice. As established in Section III, generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS, NASS) do not support surgical intervention for axial neck pain without neurological compromise. Therefore, the denial is consistent with the statutory definition of medical necessity.
2. DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 (Time-Sensitive Neurological Conditions): APL 22-014 addresses cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI, classifying it as an urgent condition where conservative treatment requirements may be waived. 
   - Evaluation: Mr. Flores's MRI explicitly notes "no T2 signal change" and "no myelomalacia," and his clinical exam shows no myelopathy. Therefore, the protections and waivers under APL 22-014 do not apply to this case.
3. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy Prerequisites): This statute prevents plans from mechanically requiring physical therapy if it is contraindicated, if a functional plateau is reached, or if there is objective structural neurological compromise. While the member has completed physical therapy, this statute does not mandate the approval of an otherwise medically unnecessary surgical procedure.

V. RESOLUTION OF APPEAL ARGUMENTS
The appeal basis argues that:
1. The member has severe cervical stenosis with cord contact and is at risk of myelopathy from minor trauma.
2. Prophylactic surgery is indicated for severe stenosis even without current deficits.
3. The member's occupation as a construction worker operating heavy equipment increases his risk of trauma.

Response: While the clinical concern regarding the narrow canal diameter (6 mm) and the potential risk of trauma in a construction environment is acknowledged, clinical evidence does not support prophylactic ACDF for this presentation. Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), surgical decompression is not indicated for imaging findings (such as stenosis or cord contact) in the absence of correlating clinical symptoms or objective neurological deficits. The standard of care for patients with severe stenosis and axial pain without neurological deficit is conservative management, activity modification to minimize trauma risk, and close clinical monitoring for the development of any new neurological symptoms (such as radiculopathy or myelopathy).

VI. CONCLUSION AND DISPOSITION
The prior authorization denial for prophylactic ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is UPHELD. The requested procedure does not meet the medical necessity criteria under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, nor does it meet the standard of medical necessity under California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32 or generally accepted clinical guidelines.
