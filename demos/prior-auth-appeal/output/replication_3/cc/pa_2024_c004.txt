APPEAL DETERMINATION: UPHOLD DENIAL

Dear Mr. Flores,

HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its review of the appeal regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level. After a thorough review of your clinical records, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California regulatory standards, and generally accepted clinical guidelines, we have determined that the original decision to deny coverage for this procedure must be UPHELD. 

Below is a detailed explanation of the clinical, regulatory, and guideline-based reasons for this decision, as well as information on your right to an Independent Medical Review (IMR) through the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC).

1. CLINICAL PRESENTATION & EVIDENCE REVIEWED
We reviewed all clinical documentation submitted with your request and appeal, which includes:
- MRI of the Cervical Spine (Dated September 15, 2024): Demonstrates severe central canal stenosis at C5-C6 with a canal diameter of 6mm (normal is greater than 13mm). There is spinal cord contact without compression. Crucially, there is no T2 signal change and no myelomalacia (spinal cord damage). The radiologist noted that a single trauma could cause acute cord injury given the severely reduced canal diameter.
- Neurological Examination (Dated September 20, 2024): Documented as completely normal. There is no evidence of radiculopathy (nerve root compression causing weakness or numbness in the arms) and no signs of myelopathy (spinal cord compression causing gait disturbance, hand clumsiness, or abnormal reflexes). Your primary complaint is axial neck pain, rated at 5/10.
- Conservative Treatment History: You completed 8 weeks of physical therapy (24 sessions) for axial neck pain, resulting in modest pain improvement (from 7/10 to 5/10). You also trialed Naproxen (500mg BID for 3 months) and Cyclobenzaprine (6 weeks), and received a C5-C6 epidural steroid injection on October 10, 2024, which provided 60% pain relief lasting 6 weeks.

2. PLAN CLINICAL COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.1, surgical authorization for ACDF (CPT 22551) requires a confirmed diagnosis of either:
(a) Cervical radiculopathy with corresponding nerve root compression and symptoms;
(b) Cervical myelopathy with spinal cord compression and objective myelopathic symptoms; or
(c) Cervical disc herniation with an objective neurological deficit.

Furthermore, Section 3.1 explicitly excludes coverage when surgery is requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy. Because your neurological examination is completely normal and your primary symptom is axial neck pain, your clinical presentation does not meet the diagnostic requirements of Section 2.1 and falls under the exclusion of Section 3.1.

3. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY STANDARDS & MEDICAL NECESSITY (CHSC § 1374.32)
We acknowledge that under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(a), the statutory definition of "medically necessary" includes health care services provided for the purpose of "preventing, evaluating, diagnosing, or treating an illness, injury, disease, or its symptoms." We also acknowledge that under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard does not require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria, and the plan's criteria cannot be the sole or determinative basis for a denial if the service is medically necessary to prevent injury.

Therefore, we have evaluated your request under the broader statutory standard of medical necessity, which requires that the requested service be in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice and clinically appropriate for your condition.

4. CLINICAL CATEGORY ERROR RECTIFICATION & STRUCTURAL HAZARD ASSESSMENT
We explicitly acknowledge that your conservative treatments (physical therapy and epidural steroid injection) were directed at managing your axial neck pain. We recognize that while these treatments successfully managed your pain, they did not, and physically cannot, widen your 6mm spinal canal or protect your spinal cord from traumatic impact. 

We do not conflate symptomatic pain management with structural protection. The conservative treatment has failed to resolve the underlying structural hazard (the 6mm canal), and pain management does not mitigate the mechanical risk of spinal cord injury. However, as detailed below, the presence of this structural hazard alone, in the absence of clinical symptoms or spinal cord signal changes, does not establish medical necessity for prophylactic surgery under generally accepted standards of medical practice.

5. INDIVIDUALIZED RISK ASSESSMENT & OCCUPATIONAL FACTORS
We have conducted an individualized risk assessment that factors in your specific occupation as a heavy equipment operator in construction. We acknowledge that operating heavy machinery in a construction environment carries an elevated risk of cervical trauma. We also acknowledge that because your cervical canal is critically narrow (6mm) with cord contact, any significant cervical trauma carries an extremely high risk of acute, irreversible spinal cord injury (such as quadriplegia). The radiologist's and your surgeon's warnings regarding this risk are fully noted and accepted as clinically valid.

However, under generally accepted standards of medical practice, prophylactic surgical decompression (ACDF) is not indicated for asymptomatic severe stenosis, even in patients with high-risk occupations. 

According to the American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Joint Section Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (2023 Update) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020):
- Surgical intervention is strongly recommended (Tier 1) for patients with moderate-to-severe clinical myelopathy or progressive neurological deficits.
- Surgical intervention is not indicated (Tier 3) for patients with axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy, or for imaging findings in the absence of correlating clinical symptoms.
- Clinical evidence does not support prophylactic decompression for asymptomatic severe stenosis because the documented risks of ACDF (including adjacent segment disease, pseudarthrosis, nerve injury, and general surgical complications) outweigh the statistical risk of acute spinal cord injury from trauma in an asymptomatic patient.

The standard of care for managing your elevated risk of trauma is activity modification—such as transitioning to lower-risk duties or modifying your work environment to avoid high-impact trauma—and close clinical monitoring for any developing neurological symptoms (such as numbness, weakness, or gait changes), rather than undergoing a prophylactic major spinal fusion.

6. CONCLUSION
While we understand and validate the serious concerns raised by your surgeon, Dr. Davis, regarding your occupational risk and the narrowness of your spinal canal, we must uphold the denial. Prophylactic surgery in the absence of neurological deficits, radiculopathy, clinical myelopathy, or spinal cord signal changes (myelomalacia) does not meet the definition of medical necessity under HealthFirst criteria, California law, or national clinical guidelines.

7. NOTICE OF RIGHT TO INDEPENDENT MEDICAL REVIEW (IMR)
If you disagree with this decision, you have the right to appeal to the California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) for an Independent Medical Review (IMR). 

An IMR is an evaluation of your case by independent, board-certified physicians who are not associated with HealthFirst. Under California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32, the IMR reviewer is legally mandated to apply the statutory definition of medical necessity and is not bound by the plan's internal criteria.

You may apply for an IMR if:
- Your provider has recommended the service as medically necessary, and
- The service has been denied, modified, or delayed by the plan based on medical necessity.

To apply for an IMR, please contact the DMHC Help Center:
- Toll-Free Telephone: 1-888-466-2219
- TDD: 1-877-688-9891
- Website: www.dmhc.ca.gov

There is no charge to you for an IMR. The DMHC will review your application and, if it qualifies, will refer your case to an independent review organization. A decision is typically rendered within 30 days of the DMHC receiving all required documentation.
