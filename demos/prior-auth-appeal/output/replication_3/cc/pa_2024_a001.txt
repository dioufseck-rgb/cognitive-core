FINAL DETERMINATION: OVERTURN INITIAL DENIAL (Prior Authorization Approved)

Dear Ms. Torres and Dr. Kim,

The HealthFirst Health Plan Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the appeal submitted on November 15, 2024, regarding the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT 22551). 

After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan's clinical coverage criteria, California statutory and regulatory mandates, and generally accepted standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has determined that the initial prior authorization denial issued on November 1, 2024, must be OVERTURNED. The requested surgical procedure is approved.

Below is a detailed explanation of the clinical findings, regulatory overrides, and clinical evidence that support this determination.

1. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND HISTORY
The clinical record demonstrates that Ms. Diana Torres is a 61-year-old female with a progressive, severe neurological condition. 
- Presenting Diagnosis: Progressive cervical myelopathy C5-C6 with myelomalacia.
- Clinical History: Ms. Torres presented to Dr. James Kim on August 10, 2024, with a 4-month history of progressive bilateral hand clumsiness and gait instability. 
- Neurological Examination (August 10, 2024): Documented significant upper motor neuron signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia in both upper and lower extremities, positive bilateral Babinski signs, and gait ataxia.
- Objective Imaging (MRI dated August 15, 2024): Demonstrated severe central cord compression at C5-C6 with T2 hyperintensity within the spinal cord, confirming the presence of myelomalacia (active spinal cord injury).
- Conservative Treatment History:
  - Physical Therapy: Initiated at Valley PT on September 1, 2024. After 2 weeks (6 sessions), Dr. Kim issued a written order to discontinue therapy on September 14, 2024, because the physical therapy was causing severe symptom exacerbation. Dr. Kim documented that physical therapy was clinically contraindicated.
  - Pharmacotherapy: Ms. Torres completed a 2-week trial of a methylprednisolone taper, which provided only temporary, mild improvement with no sustained benefit.
  - Interventional Treatment: None performed, as Dr. Kim documented that interventional procedures posed an unacceptable risk of neurological deterioration.

2. INITIAL DENIAL AND PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS
The initial prior authorization request was denied on November 1, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, of HealthFirst Utilization Management, citing non-compliance with HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria Document CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement). Specifically, the denial noted:
- The member completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy (standard pathway requires 6 weeks).
- The member trialed only one pharmacotherapy agent (standard pathway requires two agents for 4 weeks each).
- The member did not meet the conservative treatment requirements under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2.

Under the plan's internal criteria, even when applying the "Exception 2.2(A) — Myelopathy Pathway" for confirmed cervical myelopathy, the requirements are modified as follows:
- Physical therapy is reduced to a minimum of 4 weeks.
- Interventional treatment (Section 2.2(c)) is waived if the physician documents a risk of neurological deterioration.
- Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)) remains fully applicable, requiring a trial of at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each.

Because Ms. Torres completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy and trialed only one pharmacotherapy agent (methylprednisolone taper) for 2 weeks, she did not strictly satisfy the plan's internal CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 criteria, even under the modified Myelopathy Pathway.

3. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY OVERRIDES AND LEGAL UNENFORCEABILITY
Under California law, which governs this plan product, the plan's internal conservative treatment requirements are legally overridden and cannot be applied to Ms. Torres's case.

A. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 — Physical Therapy Restrictions
CIC § 10169.5(a) explicitly prohibits a health insurer from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy under the following circumstances:
- CIC § 10169.5(a)(1): The member's treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or is unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition. Dr. Kim's written declaration dated October 15, 2024, and the clinical chart notes from September 14, 2024, explicitly state that physical therapy caused symptom exacerbation, was contraindicated, and posed a risk of irreversible spinal cord injury.
- CIC § 10169.5(a)(3): The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Ms. Torres's MRI on August 15, 2024, provides objective evidence of severe central cord compression and myelomalacia (structural spinal cord damage), which physical therapy cannot resolve.
Furthermore, CIC § 10169.5(c) mandates that a plan's internal requirement for a minimum number of weeks of physical therapy shall not be applied mechanically without reference to whether the condition involves neurological urgency under subdivision (a)(3).

B. Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter APL 22-014
APL 22-014 addresses prior authorization requirements for time-sensitive neurological conditions:
- Section II.1 (Myelopathy Classification): The DMHC has determined that cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI constitutes a condition with potential for irreversible harm if untreated, and plans must treat such cases as urgent.
- Section II.2 (Conservative Treatment Exceptions): Plans shall not require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when:
  (a) Objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as cord signal change or myelomalacia);
  (b) The treating physician provides documentation that conservative treatment has been attempted and failed to arrest progression (documented by the failure and exacerbation during the 2-week PT trial); and
  (c) Surgical intervention is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines.
- Section II.3 (Plan Criteria vs. Clinical Standard): Plans shall not apply internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for these conditions. Where a conflict exists, the clinical standard governs.

C. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) — Independent Medical Review Standard
CHSC § 1374.32(b) establishes that the standard for medical necessity does not require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If the requested service is clinically appropriate and in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice, it must be approved regardless of whether the plan's internal criteria are more restrictive.

4. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND GENERALLY ACCEPTED STANDARDS OF MEDICAL PRACTICE
Generally accepted standards of medical practice, as compiled from major neurosurgical and spine societies, strongly support immediate surgical intervention and contraindicate conservative treatment for Ms. Torres's clinical presentation.

A. Diagnostic Significance of Myelomalacia
According to the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023) and NASS Guidelines (2020), the presence of myelomalacia (T2 signal hyperintensity within the spinal cord) represents active, structural spinal cord injury. Delaying surgery in the presence of myelomalacia is associated with a high risk of permanent, irreversible neurological deficits and incomplete postoperative recovery.

B. Inappropriateness of Physical Therapy and Conservative Treatment for Myelopathy
- AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021) state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
- Cochrane Review (2021) indicates that physical therapy has no documented efficacy in arresting progression or improving functional outcomes in patients with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy.
- Applying conservative treatment guidelines derived from cervical radiculopathy (nerve root compression) to cervical myelopathy (spinal cord compression) is a clinical category error.

C. Surgical Indications
Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Section 3.1, Ms. Torres's clinical presentation meets Tier 1 (Strong Indication) for surgical decompression (ACDF CPT 22551) due to:
- Confirmed moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression on MRI.
- Documented progressive neurological deficits (hand clumsiness, gait ataxia, hyperreflexia).
- Myelomalacia (cord signal change) on MRI.
Immediate surgical decompression is the standard of care to prevent irreversible spinal cord damage.

5. RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS
In resolving the conflict between the plan's internal clinical criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) and the governing authorities, HealthFirst applies the established hierarchy of authority: California State Law and Regulatory Mandates > Generally Accepted Clinical Standards > Plan Clinical Criteria.

- Conflict 1 (Physical Therapy Duration): The plan's requirement for 4 to 6 weeks of physical therapy is legally unenforceable under CIC § 10169.5(a)(1) and (a)(3) because Dr. Kim documented that PT was contraindicated and caused symptom exacerbation, and the MRI showed objective structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia).
- Conflict 2 (Pharmacotherapy Trial): The plan's requirement for a second 4-week pharmacotherapy trial is overridden by DMHC APL 22-014, which prohibits requiring a full course of conservative treatment when myelomalacia is present and delay risks irreversible neurological harm.
- Conflict 3 (Clinical Standard vs. Plan Criteria): Under CHSC § 1374.32(b) and DMHC APL 22-014 Section II.3, the plan's internal criteria cannot be applied because they are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021), which recommend immediate surgery without conservative trials for moderate-to-severe myelopathy with myelomalacia.

6. CONCLUSION AND AUTHORIZATION
Based on California Insurance Code § 10169.5, DMHC All-Plan Letter APL 22-014, and generally accepted clinical guidelines, the prior authorization denial is hereby OVERTURNED. 

Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C5-C6, one level (CPT 22551) is APPROVED.
