FINAL DISPOSITION: OVERTURN

The initial prior authorization denial for CPT 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) is hereby OVERTURNED. The requested surgical procedure is APPROVED. HealthFirst will authorize coverage for the procedure at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center as requested by Dr. James Kim, MD.

I. BACKGROUND AND INITIAL DENIAL REASON
On November 1, 2024, HealthFirst Utilization Management (reviewed by Dr. Alan Foster, MD) denied prior authorization for CPT 22551 under Clinical Coverage Criteria Document CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2. The denial was based on the following findings:
1. The member completed only 2 weeks of physical therapy (Valley PT, 2024-09-01 to 2024-09-14), failing to meet the standard 6-week requirement under Section 2.2(a) or the modified 4-week requirement under the Myelopathy Pathway Exception 2.2(A).
2. The member trialed only one pharmacotherapy agent (a 2-week methylprednisolone taper), failing to meet the requirement of trialing at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each under Section 2.2(b).
3. No interventional treatments under Section 2.2(c) were documented.

II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND EVIDENCE REVIEWED
The clinical record submitted with the appeal establishes the following objective medical facts:
- Presenting Diagnosis: Progressive cervical myelopathy C5-C6 with myelomalacia.
- Clinical History: A 4-month history of progressive bilateral hand clumsiness and gait instability, first documented on August 10, 2024.
- Neurological Examination (2024-08-10): Documented upper motor neuron signs, including bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia in both upper and lower extremities, positive bilateral Babinski signs, and gait ataxia.
- Objective Imaging (MRI dated 2024-08-15): Demonstrates severe central cord compression at C5-C6 with T2 hyperintensity within the spinal cord, confirming active myelomalacia (structural spinal cord injury).
- Conservative Treatment Trial: The member attempted physical therapy at Valley PT starting September 1, 2024. After 2 weeks (6 sessions), the treating physician, Dr. Kim, issued a written order to discontinue PT because it caused severe symptom exacerbation. The member trialed a 2-week methylprednisolone taper, which provided only temporary, mild relief with no sustained benefit.
- Physician Declaration: In a formal letter dated October 15, 2024, Dr. Kim declared that physical therapy is strictly contraindicated for this patient. He noted that continuing PT in the presence of documented myelomalacia risks causing irreversible spinal cord injury and that urgent surgical decompression is clinically indicated.

III. REGULATORY AND LEGAL ANALYSIS (CALIFORNIA LAW)
Under California law, the plan's internal clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) are legally unenforceable as applied to this member's clinical presentation. The denial must be overturned based on the following controlling statutory and regulatory provisions:

1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a) — Physical Therapy Prerequisites:
   Under CIC § 10169.5(a), a health insurer shall not condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy under either of the following circumstances, both of which are met in this case:
   - Subdivision (a)(1): The treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member given the nature and severity of the condition. Dr. Kim's written declaration of October 15, 2024, and the clinical records documenting symptom exacerbation during PT satisfy this provision.
   - Subdivision (a)(3): The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. The MRI dated August 15, 2024, showing severe central cord compression and T2 hyperintensity (myelomalacia) constitutes definitive objective evidence of structural neurological compromise.
   Consequently, the plan's 6-week and 4-week physical therapy requirements are legally inapplicable.

2. California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:
   APL 22-014, Section II.1 and II.2, addresses prior authorization for time-sensitive neurological conditions. The Department explicitly classifies cervical myelopathy with objective evidence of cord compression and myelomalacia on MRI as a condition where delay risks irreversible harm. 
   - Section II.2 mandates that plans SHALL NOT require completion of a full course of conservative treatment as a prerequisite to surgical authorization when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia), the treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed or is contraindicated, and surgery is indicated under clinical guidelines.
   - Section II.3 prohibits plans from applying internal criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice for these conditions.

3. California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b) — Standard of Review:
   Under California's Independent Medical Review (IMR) standards, medical necessity determinations must be made in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice. The law explicitly states that the IMR standard does not require consistency with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If a conflict exists between plan criteria and generally accepted clinical standards, the clinical standards govern.

IV. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND GENERALLY ACCEPTED STANDARDS OF MEDICAL PRACTICE
Generally accepted clinical guidelines do not support requiring conservative treatment for patients presenting with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy and myelomalacia:

1. AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023 Update) & NASS Guidelines (2020):
   These guidelines classify cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and myelomalacia (cord signal change) as a Tier 1 (Strong Indication) for surgical decompression. Conservative treatment is not recommended as a primary treatment for moderate-to-severe myelopathy because it has limited efficacy and delaying surgery is associated with a decreased likelihood of neurological recovery.

2. AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guideline (2021):
   The guidelines state: "There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."

3. Cochrane Review (Nikolaidis et al., 2021):
   Confirms that physical therapy does not produce meaningful functional improvement in patients with moderate-to-severe myelopathy and that surgical decompression is the standard of care to arrest progressive neurological deficits.

V. CONCLUSION
The member has documented progressive cervical myelopathy with severe central cord compression, myelomalacia, and objective upper motor neuron deficits. Requiring the member to complete additional physical therapy or trial further pharmacotherapy is clinically contraindicated, poses a severe risk of irreversible spinal cord injury, and directly violates California Insurance Code § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014. 

Because the clinical presentation meets the clinical standard for immediate surgical decompression and satisfies the statutory exemptions from conservative treatment, the prior authorization denial is OVERTURNED.
