HealthFirst Health Plan has completed its clinical and regulatory review of the first-level appeal submitted on behalf of George Harris regarding the prior authorization request for a Revision Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at the C5-C6 level (CPT code 22551). 

After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, the initial prior authorization denial issued on October 30, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED. Prior authorization for the requested revision surgery is APPROVED.

I. BACKGROUND AND PRIOR DENIAL
On October 30, 2024, HealthFirst issued an initial denial (Reason Code: MED-003) for a Revision ACDF C5-C6. The denial, authored by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, stated: "Revision surgery not covered under standard ACDF criteria. Member had prior C5-C6 ACDF in 2021. Revision criteria require documentation of hardware failure, pseudarthrosis, or new pathology at previously treated level. Documentation submitted does not confirm hardware failure."

The member submitted an appeal on November 14, 2024, on the basis that:
1. A cervical CT scan dated September 15, 2024, confirms pseudarthrosis (failure of fusion) at C5-C6.
2. Right C6 radicular symptoms have returned and worsened since the 2021 fusion.
3. Revision criteria are fully met per the treating surgeon, Dr. Karen Williams, MD.

II. CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND EVIDENCE SUMMARY
Mr. Harris is a 48-year-old male with a history of a C5-C6 ACDF performed in 2021, which initially yielded a good clinical outcome. However, his symptoms recurred approximately 18 months postoperatively and have progressively worsened. 
- Presenting Diagnosis: Pseudarthrosis C5-C6 following prior ACDF 2021 — failed fusion.
- Neurological Examination (September 20, 2024): Demonstrates a return of right C6 radiculopathy, including 4/5 right bicep weakness, a diminished right bicep reflex, and C6 dermatomal numbness. These objective deficits are documented as worse than his preoperative baseline.
- Imaging Studies:
  1. Cervical MRI (September 1, 2024): Demonstrated hardware artifact that limited the assessment of the fusion. The radiologist noted a "possible non-union at C5-C6 but cannot confirm" and that "residual disc material or recurrent herniation cannot be excluded."
  2. Cervical CT Scan (September 15, 2024): Performed specifically to evaluate fusion status without hardware artifact. The CT scan revealed "incomplete bridging bone formation" and "lucency around hardware suggesting pseudarthrosis." The findings were interpreted as "consistent with failed fusion/pseudarthrosis," while the hardware itself appeared intact without fracture.
- Conservative Treatment:
  1. Physical Therapy: Completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy with no clinical improvement. The treating physician noted that physical therapy cannot address structural hardware or fusion failure.
  2. Pharmacotherapy: Completed a 4-month trial of Naproxen 500mg BID (NSAID) and a 3-month trial of Gabapentin 300mg TID (neuropathic agent), both yielding only partial, inadequate relief.
  3. Interventional Treatment: Received a cervical epidural steroid injection on October 10, 2024, which resulted in minimal improvement, as expected in the presence of structural fusion failure.

III. PLAN CRITERIA ANALYSIS (HEALTHFIRST CC-SPINE-2024)
The requested procedure (CPT 22551) satisfies all applicable requirements of the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria for Spinal Surgery — Cervical Procedures (Document: CC-SPINE-2024):

1. Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): Mr. Harris has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (nerve root compression with radicular symptoms including pain, numbness, and weakness in a dermatomal distribution). This is supported by the neurological exam of September 20, 2024, showing right C6 dermatomal numbness, 4/5 bicep weakness, and a diminished bicep reflex.
2. Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): Mr. Harris has completed a documented course of conservative treatment consisting of:
   - (a) Physical Therapy: 8 weeks of supervised physical therapy (exceeding the 6-week minimum).
   - (b) Pharmacotherapy: Trials of Naproxen (NSAID) for 4 months and Gabapentin (neuropathic agent) for 3 months (exceeding the requirement of two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each).
   - (c) Interventional Treatment: One cervical epidural steroid injection on October 10, 2024 (satisfying the requirement of at least one interventional treatment).
3. Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement): The cervical CT scan dated September 15, 2024 (within 6 months of the request) demonstrates structural pathology (incomplete bridging bone and lucency around hardware consistent with pseudarthrosis) at the proposed surgical level (C5-C6) that directly correlates with the member's right C6 radicular symptoms.
4. Section 2.4 (Physician Documentation): Dr. Karen Williams, MD, provided the necessary clinical documentation, including attestation of completed conservative treatment, clinical rationale for revision surgery due to failed fusion, description of functional limitations, and discussion of surgical risks.

Furthermore, the plan's denial letter cited "Revision criteria" requiring "documentation of hardware failure, pseudarthrosis, or new pathology at previously treated level." The plan's initial reviewer denied the request solely because the documentation did "not confirm hardware failure." This represents a clear misapplication of the plan's own criteria. The criteria are written disjunctively ("hardware failure, pseudarthrosis, OR new pathology"). By requiring hardware fracture/failure and ignoring the documented pseudarthrosis, the plan applied a standard more restrictive than its own written policy. The CT scan of September 15, 2024, explicitly and objectively confirmed pseudarthrosis, thereby fully satisfying the revision criteria.

IV. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY COMPLIANCE AND CONFLICT RESOLUTION
Under California law, the plan's initial denial is procedurally and substantively deficient, and the clinical evidence must override the plan's restrictive interpretation:

1. Procedural Defect under CHSC § 1374.31(b): California Health & Safety Code § 1374.31(a)(2) requires that a denial notice include the specific criteria applied and the specific clinical reason for the denial. Under § 1374.31(b), a denial notice that fails to specify the clinical criteria or the specific clinical reason is defective. The plan's denial was procedurally defective because it ignored the primary diagnostic modality submitted—the cervical CT scan of September 15, 2024—and instead relied on the limited MRI report to claim that hardware failure was not confirmed. By failing to address the objective CT findings of pseudarthrosis, the plan failed to provide a valid clinical reason for denial.
2. Physical Therapy Protections under CIC § 10169.5: California Insurance Code § 10169.5(a)(3) prohibits an insurer from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy when the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Mr. Harris has objective structural compromise (pseudarthrosis/failed fusion with hardware lucency) and progressive neurological deficits (4/5 bicep weakness). As documented by Dr. Williams, physical therapy cannot restore structural bone fusion. Mechanically applying conservative treatment barriers to a patient with documented structural non-union and progressive motor weakness violates CIC § 10169.5.
3. Standard of Review and Clinical Standards under CHSC § 1374.32: California Health & Safety Code § 1374.32(b) establishes that the Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard does not require a service to be consistent with the plan's internal coverage criteria. If a service is in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice and is clinically appropriate, it must be approved. Section 1374.32(c) mandates that internal criteria shall not be the sole or determinative basis for a decision. To the extent that HealthFirst's internal "Revision criteria" are interpreted to require hardware fracture/failure to the exclusion of pseudarthrosis, such criteria are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice and are overridden by California law.

V. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND STANDARD OF CARE
According to generally accepted standards of medical practice, including the North American Spine Society (NASS) Evidence-Based Clinical Guidelines (2020) and the American Association of Neurological Surgeons / Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Joint Section Guidelines (2023):
1. Diagnostic Modality for Pseudarthrosis: In patients with prior spinal fusion hardware, MRI assessments are frequently compromised by hardware-induced magnetic susceptibility artifacts. The AANS/CNS guidelines establish that a high-resolution CT scan is the appropriate and definitive imaging modality to evaluate bony fusion status and hardware lucency. The plan's reliance on the MRI's inability to confirm non-union, while ignoring the CT scan's definitive confirmation of pseudarthrosis, is clinically unsound.
2. Indications for Revision Surgery: The presence of symptomatic pseudarthrosis (failed fusion) accompanied by recurrent, progressive radiculopathy and objective neurological deficits (4/5 motor weakness) constitutes a Tier 1 strong indication for surgical intervention (AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines, 2023).
3. Inefficacy of Continued Conservative Care: Clinical evidence demonstrates that conservative measures such as physical therapy and epidural injections cannot address the underlying structural instability of a failed fusion (pseudarthrosis). Prolonging conservative trials in a patient with progressive motor weakness (right bicep 4/5) increases the risk of permanent neurological deficit (NASS Guidelines, 2020).

VI. CONCLUSION AND DISPOSITION
The clinical documentation demonstrates that Mr. Harris has symptomatic C5-C6 pseudarthrosis with objective right C6 radiculopathy and motor weakness. He has completed and failed an extensive course of conservative therapy. The cervical CT scan definitively confirms pseudarthrosis, satisfying both the plan's internal revision criteria and generally accepted clinical standards. The plan's initial denial was procedurally defective under California Health & Safety Code § 1374.31(b) and substantively violated California Insurance Code § 10169.5.

Accordingly, the prior authorization denial for Revision ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED, and the procedure is APPROVED.
