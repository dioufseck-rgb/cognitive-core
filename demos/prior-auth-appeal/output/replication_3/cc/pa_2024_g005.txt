Dear Mr. Obi and Dr. Diallo,

The HealthFirst Appeals and Grievances Department has completed its review of the prior authorization appeal submitted on November 22, 2024, regarding the requested single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C6-C7 (CPT code 22551). 

After a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), the California Regulatory Framework, and generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, HealthFirst has determined that the initial prior authorization denial issued on November 08, 2024, must be OVERTURNED. Full prior authorization is hereby GRANTED for CPT code 22551 to be performed by Dr. Amina Diallo at Stanford Medical Center.

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY OF DETERMINATION
The initial denial was based on two grounds: (1) an alleged physical therapy (PT) shortfall (claiming only 5 weeks of PT were completed out of 6 required), and (2) poorly controlled type 2 diabetes (HbA1c 8.2%) presenting perioperative risk.

This appeal determination overturns both grounds for the following reasons:
1. The PT shortfall ground is factually incorrect. The clinical record documents that the member completed 7 weeks of continuous physical therapy (from July 01, 2024, through August 16, 2024, totaling 21 sessions) with a documented functional plateau, which fully satisfies and exceeds the 6-week requirement under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a).
2. The diabetes control ground is not a valid prior authorization criterion. HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 does not condition ACDF authorization on HbA1c levels or metabolic control. Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), basing a denial on a non-criterion is procedurally defective. Perioperative diabetes management is a clinical risk-mitigation responsibility of the surgical team, not a prior authorization prerequisite.

Because the member satisfies all applicable clinical coverage criteria under CC-SPINE-2024, the requested procedure is medically necessary and approved.

II. DETAILED ANALYSIS OF DENIAL GROUNDS

GROUND 1: PHYSICAL THERAPY REQUIREMENT
- Plan Criteria: CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a) requires a minimum of 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy directed toward the cervical condition, with documented functional outcomes.
- Initial Denial Finding: The initial denial stated that the member completed only 5 weeks of physical therapy.
- Appeal Review & Clinical Findings: A review of the enclosed physical therapy records reveals that the member participated in continuous, supervised physical therapy from July 01, 2024, through August 16, 2024, representing a total of 7 weeks and 21 sessions. At discharge, the physical therapist documented a functional plateau, noting "no further functional improvement expected."
- Regulatory Alignment: Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2), a health plan cannot condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy if the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician or therapist documents that the member reached a functional plateau without adequate resolution of symptoms. Mechanical application of a minimum week requirement without reference to a documented functional plateau is prohibited under CIC § 10169.5(c).
- Conclusion: The member completed 7 weeks of PT, exceeding the plan's 6-week requirement, and reached a documented functional plateau. The initial denial's finding was factually incorrect, and the plan's mechanical application of the timeline violates CIC § 10169.5. This denial ground is overturned.

GROUND 2: DIABETES CONTROL AND PERIOPERATIVE RISK
- Plan Criteria: HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024 contains no provisions, exclusions, or prerequisites conditioning surgical authorization for cervical spine procedures on diabetes control, HbA1c thresholds, or metabolic optimization.
- Initial Denial Finding: The initial denial cited the member's poorly controlled type 2 diabetes (HbA1c 8.2%) and associated wound healing concerns as a basis for withholding surgical authorization.
- Appeal Review & Clinical Findings: The clinical record confirms the member has a 12-year history of type 2 diabetes with a recent HbA1c of 8.2% on October 15, 2024. While the endocrinologist (Dr. Patel) and the treating surgeon (Dr. Diallo) both acknowledge the need for preoperative optimization, Dr. Diallo explicitly noted on October 20, 2024, that "diabetes management will be optimized preoperatively... Diabetes control is a surgical planning consideration, not a PA criterion."
- Regulatory Alignment: Under CHSC § 1374.31(b), a denial notice must specify the clinical criteria applied. Denying coverage based on a clinical parameter (HbA1c level) that is not part of the plan's published clinical criteria is procedurally defective. Furthermore, under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the medical necessity standard for Independent Medical Review (IMR) is based on generally accepted standards of medical practice, which do not permit health plans to use perioperative risk management as a barrier to coverage when the underlying spinal pathology meets surgical indications.
- Clinical Evidence Standard: The AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Guidelines (2020) do not condition the medical necessity of cervical decompression on metabolic thresholds. Perioperative risk management, including glycemic control, is a standard clinical protocol managed by the surgical and anesthesia teams immediately prior to surgery, not a prior authorization criterion.
- Conclusion: Because diabetes control is not a criterion under CC-SPINE-2024, and because denying authorization on this basis violates CHSC § 1374.31(b), this denial ground is invalid and is disregarded.

III. EVALUATION OF REMAINING MEDICAL NECESSITY CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)

To ensure a complete and reproducible review, all other medical necessity criteria under CC-SPINE-2024 were evaluated against the clinical record:

1. Diagnosis Requirement (Section 2.1):
- Requirement: Confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy (nerve root compression with pain, numbness, or weakness in a dermatomal distribution) supported by an MRI within 6 months.
- Clinical Evidence: The member has a presenting diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy C6-C7 with functional impairment. The neurological exam dated September 25, 2024, documents objective neurological deficits: left C7 dermatomal numbness, diminished left tricep reflex, left tricep weakness (4/5), and left grip weakness (4+/5). The MRI dated September 20, 2024 (within 6 months of the request) demonstrates a large C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate left C7 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis. This satisfies Section 2.1(a) and 2.1(c).

2. Conservative Treatment Requirement (Section 2.2):
- Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)): Satisfied. 7 weeks completed (July 01, 2024 – August 16, 2024) with documented functional plateau.
- Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)): Satisfied. The clinical record documents a trial of two neuropathic agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each: Pregabalin 150mg BID for 5 months (partial relief) and Duloxetine 60mg for 3 months (moderate benefit).
- Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)): Satisfied. The member underwent a left C7 epidural steroid injection on October 10, 2024, which provided 45% temporary relief before symptoms returned to baseline within 4 weeks.

3. Imaging Requirement (Section 2.3):
- Requirement: Current imaging (within 6 months) demonstrating structural pathology consistent with symptoms and proposed level.
- Clinical Evidence: The MRI dated September 20, 2024, confirms a large C6-C7 disc herniation compressing the left C7 nerve root, which directly correlates with the member's left C7 dermatomal numbness and tricep weakness.

4. Physician Documentation (Section 2.4):
- Requirement: Attestation of conservative treatment, clinical rationale, functional limitations, and statement of risks.
- Clinical Evidence: Dr. Diallo's clinical notes and letter provide the necessary clinical rationale, document functional limitations (member is working reduced hours due to pain and hand weakness), and outline the surgical risks and standard perioperative protocols.
