HealthFirst Health Plan’s Appeal Determination Services has completed its review of the first-level clinical appeal for the requested single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). Based on a comprehensive review of the clinical record, the plan’s clinical coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024), California regulatory requirements, and generally accepted clinical standards, the initial prior authorization denial issued on November 10, 2024, is hereby OVERTURNED.

The requested procedure is approved. Below is the detailed clinical and legal justification for this determination.

1. TIMELINE RECONCILIATION AND CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT ANALYSIS
A critical issue identified during the initial review was the temporal and mathematical impossibility of conservative treatment completion as of the initial denial date.

* Initial Denial Context (November 10, 2024): The clinical record indicates that the member's symptoms and active treatment began on October 1, 2024. Between October 1, 2024, and the initial denial on November 10, 2024, exactly 40 days (5.7 weeks) had elapsed. Consequently, the clinical claims of completing a full 6-week course of physical therapy (PT) and a 6-week course of Methylprednisolone prior to the denial were mathematically impossible. Furthermore, the right C6 epidural steroid injection was performed on October 28, 2024; as of November 10, 2024, only 13 days had elapsed, meaning the standard 2-week post-epidural observation period was incomplete. Therefore, the initial denial issued by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, on November 10, 2024, was contractually and clinically correct at that specific point in time, as the conservative treatment requirements under CC-SPINE-2024 Sections 2.2(a) and 2.2(b) were premature.
* Appeal Review Context (November 25, 2024): As of the appeal review date, exactly 55 days (7.8 weeks) have elapsed since the initiation of treatment on October 1, 2024. This passage of time resolves the prior mathematical impossibility. The clinical record now documents the full, indisputable completion of all conservative treatment modalities:
  - Physical Therapy: The member has now completed the full 6-week course of intensive, supervised physical therapy (18 total sessions) directed at the cervical spine, satisfying CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(a).
  - Pharmacotherapy: The member has completed a 6-week course of Methylprednisolone (dose pack x3) and a 5-week course of Gabapentin 600mg TID, satisfying the requirement of trialing at least two agents for a minimum of 4 weeks each under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b).
  - Interventional Treatment: The right C6 epidural steroid injection performed on October 28, 2024, has now been observed for 4 weeks (well exceeding the 2-week post-procedure observation window). The clinical record documents that the injection provided only 25% temporary relief and symptoms returned to baseline within 2 weeks (by November 11, 2024), confirming the failure of interventional therapy under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(c).

2. EVALUATION AGAINST PLAN CLINICAL COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
As of the appeal review date, the member fully satisfies all medical necessity criteria outlined in CC-SPINE-2024:
* Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): Satisfied. The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with objective neurological deficits. The MRI dated October 1, 2024, demonstrates a C5-C6 large disc herniation with moderate right C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis. The neurological exam dated October 1, 2024, confirms correlating right C6 dermatomal numbness, a diminished right bicep reflex, and 4/5 right grip strength.
* Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): Satisfied. As detailed in the timeline reconciliation above, the 6-week PT course, two-agent pharmacotherapy trial, and epidural steroid injection have all been completed and have failed to resolve the member's symptoms.
* Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement): Satisfied. The MRI dated October 1, 2024, is within the required 6-month window and demonstrates structural pathology consistent with the right C6 radiculopathy.
* Section 2.4 (Physician Documentation): Satisfied. Dr. Sarah Martinez, MD, has provided the necessary clinical rationale, documented functional limitations, and stated that surgical risks and alternatives were considered.

3. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY COMPLIANCE
* California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5: At the time of the initial denial on November 10, 2024, the statutory protections of CIC § 10169.5 did not apply because a standard course of physical therapy had not yet been completed. However, as of the appeal review on November 25, 2024, the member has fully completed the 6-week course of physical therapy. The treating physical therapist and occupational therapist have documented that the member reached a "functional plateau" (defined under CIC § 10169.5(b) as a documented failure to achieve meaningful functional improvement) with no improvement in fine motor deficits or radicular symptoms. Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) and (c), the plan is prohibited from conditioning approval on further physical therapy once a functional plateau has been documented following a completed course.
* California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32: Under the Independent Medical Review (IMR) standard, medical necessity is determined based on generally accepted standards of medical practice rather than a mechanical application of restrictive internal plan criteria. The clinical evidence base supports surgical intervention in this clinical scenario.

4. CLINICAL EVIDENCE AND FUNCTIONAL IMPAIRMENT ANALYSIS
The clinical evidence base, including the AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines for the Management of Degenerative Cervical Conditions (2023), supports surgical decompression for cervical radiculopathy when conservative options have failed.
* Tier 2 Clinical Indication: Under the AANS/CNS 2023 Guidelines, Section 3.1, surgery is a reasonable and indicated option (Tier 2) for "significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care."
* Objective Measurement of Functional Impairment: The member's occupation as a professional violinist is utilized strictly as the objective clinical framework to measure and define "significant functional impairment," rather than as an extra-contractual or non-clinical exception. The neurological exam and occupational therapy evaluations dated October 1, 2024, document that the right C6 nerve root compression has caused a 4/5 grip strength deficit and fine motor impairment rendering the member "unable to perform violin fingering tasks." Because fine motor control of the right hand is the primary functional metric for this patient, this deficit represents a profound, objective, and documented functional impairment.
* Failure of Conservative Care: Despite completing 18 sessions of intensive physical therapy, a 6-week course of oral steroids, and a targeted epidural injection, these objective motor deficits and functional limitations have failed to improve. According to the clinical reference standards (AO Spine Guidelines 2021), neurological motor deficits (such as 4/5 grip strength and fine motor impairment) are highly unlikely to respond to continued conservative therapy, and delaying surgical decompression in the face of a documented functional plateau risks permanent neurological deficit.

CONCLUSION
While the initial prior authorization denial on November 10, 2024, was temporally correct at the time of issuance, the clinical record as of the appeal review date of November 25, 2024, demonstrates that all conservative treatment requirements have now been fully and mathematically completed, and have failed. The member exhibits objective, severe functional impairment (4/5 grip strength and fine motor deficits) that meets the clinical standards for surgical intervention under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Tier 2.

Accordingly, the prior authorization denial for a single-level ACDF at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED, and the procedure is APPROVED.
