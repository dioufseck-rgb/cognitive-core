HealthFirst Health Plan's Appeal Determination Services has completed its review of the first-level appeal regarding the prior authorization request for a single-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551). 1. CHRONOLOGICAL RECONCILIATION OF CONSERVATIVE TREATMENT TIMELINE
A critical issue in this case is the timeline of conservative treatment. The initial prior authorization denial on November 10, 2024, was based on the determination that the surgical request was "premature" because the member's symptoms and conservative treatment had not yet reached the required 6-week threshold.

An exact chronological analysis of the clinical record reveals the following timeline:
- October 1, 2024: Initial clinical evaluation and diagnosis of acute C5-C6 radiculopathy. MRI and neurological exams performed. Conservative treatment initiated, including intensive physical therapy (PT), oral corticosteroids (Methylprednisolone), and neuropathic agents (Gabapentin).
- October 28, 2024: Right C6 epidural steroid injection performed.
- November 10, 2024: Initial prior authorization denial issued by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD. At this exact date, exactly 40 days (5.7 weeks) had elapsed since the initial diagnosis and treatment initiation on October 1, 2024. Additionally, the 2-week post-epidural observation period (initiated on October 28, 2024) was scheduled to conclude on November 11, 2024.
- November 11, 2024: The member completed the full 6-week course of intensive physical therapy (18 sessions total), completed the 6-week course of Methylprednisolone, and completed the 2-week post-epidural observation period, which confirmed a return to baseline symptoms.
- November 25, 2024: Appeal review date. Exactly 55 days (7.8 weeks) have elapsed since the initial diagnosis and treatment initiation.

We acknowledge that at the time of the initial review on November 10, 2024, the 6-week conservative treatment course and the 2-week post-epidural observation period were technically incomplete by 1 to 2 days. Therefore, the initial denial was technically correct based on the incomplete documentation available at that specific moment. However, by the time of this appeal review on November 25, 2024, the full 6 weeks of physical therapy, 6 weeks of pharmacotherapy, and the 2-week post-injection observation period have been fully completed, documented, and submitted. Consequently, the "premature" argument is neutralized, and all duration-based criteria are fully satisfied.

2. INAPPLICABILITY OF MYELOPATHY EXCEPTIONS
We explicitly note that the clinical record and MRI dated October 1, 2024, state: "No cord contact. No myelomalacia," and the primary diagnosis is "Acute cervical radiculopathy C5-C6." Because there is no objective evidence of spinal cord compression or myelopathy, the myelopathy exceptions under Section 2.2 Exception A of the plan's criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) and the regulatory protections under California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 are strictly inapplicable. This appeal is evaluated solely under the standard radiculopathy pathway, requiring full compliance with the 6-week conservative treatment criteria.

3. COMPLIANCE WITH PLAN COVERAGE CRITERIA (CC-SPINE-2024)
Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2, prior authorization for cervical spinal surgery (CPT 22551) is approved when all of the following criteria are satisfied:

- Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement): Satisfied. The member has a confirmed diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy with nerve root compression and corresponding radicular symptoms. The MRI dated October 1, 2024, demonstrates a C5-C6 large disc herniation with moderate right C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis. The neurological exam on October 1, 2024, confirms right C6 dermatomal numbness, a diminished right bicep reflex, and 4/5 right grip strength.
- Section 2.2 (Conservative Treatment Requirement): Satisfied.
  - (a) Physical Therapy: The member completed 6 weeks of intensive supervised physical therapy (18 sessions total) directed at the cervical spine. The physical therapy and occupational therapy records document a functional plateau at 6 weeks, with no improvement in fine motor deficits or radicular symptoms, and confirm the member's inability to perform professional duties.
  - (b) Pharmacotherapy: The member completed trials of two required agents for at least 4 weeks each: (1) Methylprednisolone dose pack x3 for a total of 6 weeks (resulting in temporary partial relief), and (2) Gabapentin 600mg TID for 5 weeks (resulting in partial pain relief but no motor improvement).
  - (c) Interventional Treatment: The member underwent a right C6 epidural steroid injection on October 28, 2024, which provided 25% temporary relief but no improvement in motor deficits, with symptoms returning to baseline at the 2-week mark (completed November 11, 2024).
- Section 2.3 (Imaging Requirement): Satisfied. The MRI dated October 1, 2024 (within 6 months of the request) demonstrates structural pathology (C5-C6 large disc herniation with moderate right C6 nerve root compression) consistent with the member's right-sided C6 radicular symptoms and motor deficits.
- Section 2.4 (Physician Documentation): Satisfied. Dr. Sarah Martinez, MD, provided the required written attestation of conservative treatment completion, the clinical rationale for surgical intervention, a detailed description of functional limitations (the member is a professional violinist unable to perform violin fingering tasks due to right hand weakness and fine motor deficits), and a statement of surgical risks and alternatives considered.

4. ANALYSIS OF THE PLAN'S DENIAL BASIS
The initial denial letter dated November 10, 2024, cited reason code MED-003, stating that "Plan criteria require at least 6 weeks of symptoms before surgical consideration."
First, as of the appeal review date (November 25, 2024), the member's symptoms have been present for nearly 8 weeks (since October 1, 2024), which exceeds the plan's asserted 6-week symptom duration threshold.
Second, a strict review of the plan's written coverage criteria (CC-SPINE-2024) reveals that there is no explicit "symptom duration" requirement of "at least 6 weeks" before surgical consideration. The 6-week requirement in Section 2.2(a) applies strictly to the duration of supervised physical therapy, not to the duration of symptoms. Citing a non-existent clinical criterion to deny coverage is a procedural defect under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), which requires plans to specify the exact clinical criteria applied.

5. CALIFORNIA REGULATORY COMPLIANCE AND CLINICAL STANDARDS
- California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 (Physical Therapy as Prerequisite): Under CIC § 10169.5(a)(2), a health plan cannot condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of further physical therapy once the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician documents that the member has reached a "functional plateau" without adequate resolution of symptoms. The physical therapy and occupational therapy records explicitly document a functional plateau at 6 weeks, with persistent fine motor deficits and right hand weakness (4/5 grip strength). Conditioning surgical approval on further conservative therapy violates this statutory provision.
- California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32 (Standard of Review for IMR): Under CHSC § 1374.32, "medically necessary" services must be in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice.
- Generally Accepted Clinical Standards: Under the American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS) Joint Guidelines (2023 Update) and the North American Spine Society (NASS) Guidelines (2020), the member meets Tier 2 "Reasonable Indication" for surgical intervention. This is defined as cervical radiculopathy with persistent symptoms after 6 to 12 weeks of conservative treatment and significant functional impairment not responding to conservative care. The member's occupation as a professional violinist makes his right-hand fine motor deficit and 4/5 grip strength a career-ending functional impairment. Delaying surgical decompression in the face of a documented functional plateau and objective motor deficits is clinically inappropriate and increases the risk of permanent neurological deficit.

CONCLUSION
While the initial prior authorization denial on November 10, 2024, was technically correct because the 6-week conservative treatment and post-epidural observation periods were incomplete by 1 to 2 days, the complete clinical record submitted for this appeal on November 25, 2024, demonstrates 100% compliance with all plan criteria under CC-SPINE-2024, California Insurance Code § 10169.5, and generally accepted clinical standards.

Accordingly, the prior authorization denial for a single-level ACDF C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is OVERTURNED, and the procedure is APPROVED.
