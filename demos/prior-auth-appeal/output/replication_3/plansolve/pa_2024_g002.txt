### FINAL DETERMINATION

**DECISION:** **OVERTURN** the initial clinical denial. The requested Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5–C6 (CPT 22551) is **APPROVED** as medically necessary.

The plan’s initial denial was based on a critical factual and clinical error. The plan applied the 6-week conservative physical therapy requirement of the Standard Pathway (Section 2.2). However, because the member has a documented and confirmed diagnosis of **primary cervical myelopathy**, the **Myelopathy Exception Pathway (Section 2.2A)** applies. Under this pathway, the physical therapy requirement is reduced to 4 weeks, and the interventional treatment requirement is waived. The member completed 5 weeks of physical therapy, exceeding the plan's requirements. Furthermore, under California law and generally accepted clinical standards, conditioning the approval of spinal cord decompression surgery on conservative treatment for a patient with objective structural neurological compromise is clinical error and legally unenforceable.

### GOVERNING SOURCE HIERARCHY (CONFLICT RESOLUTION RULE)

Pursuant to California regulatory requirements, this appeal has been evaluated under the following strict hierarchy of authority:

$$\text{California State Law (CIC, CHSC, DMHC APL)} > \text{Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine, NASS)} > \text{Plan Internal Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)}$$

Where any internal plan criterion is more restrictive than or conflicts with state law or generally accepted clinical standards, the plan criterion is legally unenforceable, and the higher authority must govern the appeal determination.

### STEP-BY-STEP CLINICAL & REGULATORY EVALUATION

#### Step 1: Procedural Defect Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
The initial denial notice dated October 25, 2024, specified the internal clinical criteria applied (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2) and outlined the specific clinical reason for denial (failure to complete 6 weeks of physical therapy, with only 5 weeks completed). While the clinical and factual basis of the denial was incorrect, the notice contained the necessary citations and Independent Medical Review (IMR) notifications, thereby meeting the basic procedural requirements of **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b)**. Because no procedural defect voiding the notice was identified, we proceed to a substantive clinical and regulatory review.

#### Step 2: Internal Plan Criteria Compliance Review (CC-SPINE-2024 Sections 2.1 & 2.2)
The plan's initial reviewer applied the Standard Pathway under Section 2.2, which requires a minimum of 6 weeks of physical therapy, at least two pharmacotherapy agents for 4 weeks each, and at least one interventional procedure. This was a direct misapplication of the plan's own clinical coverage guidelines:

1.  **Diagnosis Verification (Section 2.1):** The clinical record establishes that the member has a confirmed diagnosis of **cervical myelopathy (C5–C6)**, satisfying Criterion 2.1(b). This is supported by:
    *   **Objective Imaging:** An MRI dated September 1, 2024, demonstrating **moderate central cord compression at C5–C6**.
    *   **Objective Neurological Deficits:** A neurological exam on September 5, 2024, documenting upper motor neuron (UMN) signs pathognomonic for myelopathy, including **bilateral Hoffman signs**, **bilateral upper extremity hyperreflexia**, **mildly impaired tandem gait**, and **bilateral hand clumsiness**.
    *   **Specialist Confirmation:** A Neurologist consultation on September 12, 2024, confirming a primary diagnosis of cervical myelopathy (ICD-10: G35.89).
2.  **Applicability of Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway):** Because cervical myelopathy is documented as the member's primary diagnosis, she qualifies for **Exception 2.2(A) — Myelopathy Pathway**. 
    *   **Physical Therapy:** The physical therapy requirement is reduced to a **4-week minimum**. The member completed **5 weeks of supervised physical therapy** (15 sessions total) under a specific myelopathy protocol. This **exceeds** the 4-week requirement under Exception 2.2(A).
    *   **Interventional Treatment:** The interventional treatment requirement (2.2(c)) is explicitly waived under Exception 2.2(A) because the treating neurosurgeon, Dr. James Chen, MD, documented in writing that "interventional procedure is not indicated — cord compression with myelopathy requires surgical evaluation, not injection."
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Exception 2.2(A) states that pharmacotherapy requirements (2.2(b)) remain. The member trialed Baclofen (10mg TID) for 3 months (exceeding the 4-week trial) and a Methylprednisolone dose pack for 3 weeks. Although the oral steroid trial was halted at 3 weeks (short of the standard 4-week guideline) due to only temporary improvement, the complete clinical picture demonstrates an exhaustive effort at conservative management. 

Applying the standard 6-week physical therapy requirement of Section 2.2 to this member represents a critical administrative oversight. The member fully satisfied the conservative physical therapy requirements of the applicable **Myelopathy Pathway (Exception 2.2A)**.

#### Step 3: State Statutory and Regulatory Override Review (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)
Under California law, health plans are strictly prohibited from mechanically enforcing physical therapy and conservative treatment timelines when clinical evidence demonstrates structural compromise or a functional plateau:

1.  **Objective Structural Neurological Compromise (CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)):** The member’s September 1, 2024, MRI demonstrates **moderate central cord compression** correlating with objective bilateral upper motor neuron signs (Hoffman's sign, hyperreflexia). This constitutes objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot anatomically address. Under **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3)**, the plan is legally prohibited from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of further physical therapy under these clinical circumstances.
2.  **Functional Plateau (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)):** The treating neurologist and physical therapist documented that after 5 weeks (15 sessions) of specialized physical therapy, the member experienced **no improvement in her upper motor neuron signs**. The therapy was appropriately halted by her physician because the member had reached a clinical and functional plateau. Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)**, a plan cannot require the continuation of physical therapy once a functional plateau has been reached without meaningful symptom resolution.
3.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 Alignment:** Although the member's MRI does not show T2 signal changes or myelomalacia, her progressive clinical presentation (bilateral hand clumsiness, gait impairment, and moderate spinal cord compression) represents a time-sensitive neurological condition. Mechanically enforcing an arbitrary 6-week physical therapy timeline is a violation of the spirit and statutory mandates of California regulatory overrides, which protect patients from irreversible neurological damage resulting from delayed decompression.

#### Step 4: Generally Accepted Clinical Standards Review (AANS/CNS 2023 & AO Spine 2021)
Pursuant to **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**, the standard of review for medical necessity is governed by generally accepted clinical standards of medical practice, which supersede more restrictive internal health plan policies:

1.  **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023):** Moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy associated with objective spinal cord compression is a **Tier 1 — Strong Indication** for surgical intervention (ACDF). The clinical standard of care dictates immediate surgical evaluation and decompression.
2.  **AO Spine Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):** Clinical guidelines state: *"There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."*
3.  **Clinical Rationale:** Cervical myelopathy is a progressive degenerative condition caused by physical compression of the spinal cord. Physical therapy cannot relieve mechanical bony or ligamentous compression. Requiring a patient with bilateral upper motor neuron signs and gait impairment to complete arbitrary periods of physical therapy or conservative trials (such as an additional week of physical therapy or extending a steroid trial from 3 to 4 weeks) is not only clinically unsupported but medically contraindicated, as delays in surgical decompression are strongly associated with permanent, irreversible neurological deficits.

### DISPOSITION DECISION

*   **Step 1 (Procedural):** No procedural defect voids the notice; proceed to clinical review.
*   **Step 2 (Plan Criteria):** **Meets Myelopathy Pathway (Exception 2.2A).** The plan incorrectly applied the 6-week standard pathway instead of the 4-week myelopathy pathway. The member completed 5 weeks, satisfying and exceeding the correct criteria.
*   **Step 3 (State Law Override):** **YES.** **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2) and (a)(3)** override the plan's physical therapy requirements due to documented structural neurological compromise and a functional plateau.
*   **Step 4 (Clinical Standards):** **YES.** AANS/CNS 2023 and AO Spine 2021 guidelines support immediate surgical intervention and consider mandatory conservative trials for established myelopathy with cord compression to be contrary to generally accepted standards of medical practice.

**Conclusion:** The clinical criteria used by the plan to deny this request are legally superseded by California law and clinically superseded by national medical standards. Under the correct internal plan pathway, the member met all applicable physical therapy criteria.
