### III. EVALUATION FINDINGS

#### Step 1: Procedural Adequacy and Denial Notice Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
The initial denial notice dated October 25, 2024, issued by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, cited clinical criteria "CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2" and stated the specific clinical reason for denial: *"Member does not meet conservative treatment requirements... Physical therapy: 5 weeks completed. Standard pathway requires 6 weeks minimum."* 

Because the denial notice specified the criteria and the clinical reason, it is procedurally adequate under **CHSC § 1374.31(b)**. This review proceeds to a clinical evaluation.

#### Step 2: Internal Plan Criteria Compliance Check (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 & 2.2A)
The plan's initial review denied coverage under the Standard Pathway (Section 2.2), which mandates 6 weeks of physical therapy (PT). This application of plan criteria represents a clinical and factual error:

1. **Primary Diagnosis Identification:** The clinical record establishes that the member’s primary diagnosis is **Cervical Myelopathy at C5-C6** (ICD-10: G35.89). This is confirmed by a neurologist consultation (September 12, 2024), demonstrating bilateral Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) signs (bilateral Hoffman signs, bilateral upper extremity hyperreflexia, and mildly impaired tandem gait), combined with an MRI (September 1, 2024) revealing moderate central cord compression at C5-C6.
2. **Pathway Misapplication:** Because cervical myelopathy is the primary diagnosis, **Exception 2.2(A) — Myelopathy Pathway** applies. 
3. **Physical Therapy Compliance:** Under Section 2.2(A), the physical therapy requirement is reduced to a **minimum of 4 weeks**. The member completed **5 weeks of supervised PT** (15 total sessions), which exceeds the applicable 4-week requirement. 
4. **Pharmacotherapy Compliance:** The member completed trials of two qualifying agents under Section 2.2(b): Baclofen 10mg TID (muscle relaxant) for 3 months, and a Methylprednisolone dose pack (oral corticosteroid) for 3 weeks.
5. **Interventional Treatment Waiver:** Section 2.2(A) waives the interventional treatment requirement (epidural steroid injections/blocks) if the physician documents that such treatments pose a risk of neurological deterioration. Dr. Chen documented this waiver on the record: *"Interventional procedure not indicated — cord compression with myelopathy requires surgical evaluation, not injection."*

**Conclusion under Step 2:** The member met all modified conservative treatment criteria under the plan's internal **Myelopathy Pathway (Section 2.2A)**. The plan’s application of the 6-week standard pathway was a misapplication of its own clinical coverage criteria.

#### Step 3: State Statutory and Regulatory Compliance Check (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)
Under California law, the plan's insistence on completing a rigid 6-week course of physical therapy is legally unenforceable:

1. **Objective Structural Neurological Compromise (CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)):** A health plan cannot condition the approval of surgery on the completion of physical therapy where the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address. The member's MRI dated September 1, 2024, reveals moderate central cord compression. This structural compression of the spinal cord is a mechanical compromise that physical therapy cannot remediate.
2. **Functional Plateau (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)):** The statute prohibits requiring further PT when a member has completed a course of therapy and the physician documents a functional plateau. The treating neurologist documented that PT was halted at 5 weeks due to a complete lack of improvement in upper motor neuron signs. 
3. **Standard of Review (CHSC § 1374.32(b)):** Under California Independent Medical Review (IMR) standards, the clinical necessity of a service is evaluated based on generally accepted standards of medical practice, which override restrictive internal plan criteria.

**Conclusion under Step 3:** California statutory law (**CIC § 10169.5**) explicitly overrides the plan’s physical therapy prerequisite, rendering the plan's denial legally non-compliant.

#### Step 4: Clinical Standard Alignment Check (AANS/CNS 2023 & AO Spine 2021)
The clinical record was evaluated against national, evidence-based neurosurgical guidelines:

1. **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023):** Cervical myelopathy with objective spinal cord compression and correlating upper motor neuron deficits (clumsiness, hyperreflexia, gait impairment) is classified as a **Tier 1 — Strong Surgical Indication**. Delaying surgical decompression to complete an arbitrary PT protocol is clinically contraindicated as it risks permanent, irreversible neurological deficit.
2. **AO Spine Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):** There is no high-quality clinical evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy for moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy. The guidelines warn that delaying surgery in myelopathic patients to complete physical therapy trials is not supported by evidence and represents a deviation from safe medical practice.

**Conclusion under Step 4:** Standard clinical guidelines confirm that immediate surgical decompression via ACDF is the medically necessary and standard-of-care intervention. Mandating further physical therapy is clinically inappropriate.

### IV. DISPOSITION SUMMARY
* **Plan Criteria Compliance:** **Met.** The member met the 4-week PT requirement for cervical myelopathy under CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2A.
* **State Law Compliance:** **Overridden.** Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)**, physical therapy cannot be mandated as a prerequisite due to objective structural neurological compromise (moderate central cord compression).
* **Clinical Standards Compliance:** **Met.** Surgical decompression is highly indicated as a Tier 1 recommendation under **AANS/CNS 2023** and **AO Spine 2021** guidelines.

### V. ORDER
The initial denial is **REVERSED** and **OVERTURNED**. HealthFirst shall authorize coverage for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center, as requested by Dr. James Chen, MD.
