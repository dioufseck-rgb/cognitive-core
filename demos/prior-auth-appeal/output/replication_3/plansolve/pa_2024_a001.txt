### I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Health Plan Appeals Division has conducted a comprehensive, independent review of the administrative and clinical record regarding the prior authorization appeal for CPT code 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) requested by Dr. James Kim on behalf of member Diana Torres. 

The initial coverage determination dated November 1, 2024, which denied the procedure under Reason Code MED-003 (failure to complete 6 weeks of physical therapy and failure to trial two pharmacotherapy agents under plan policy CC-SPINE-2024), has been **OVERTURNED IN FULL**. 

Applying the governing **Hierarchy of Authority** required for California enrollees:
$$\text{California State Law \& Regulations (CIC, CHSC, DMHC APL)} > \text{Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine)} > \text{Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)}$$

It is determined that the member possesses severe cervical myelopathy with objective spinal cord compression and myelomalacia (T2 hyperintensity). Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, the plan is legally prohibited from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of conservative physical therapy and pharmacotherapy when structural neurological compromise is present and therapy is contraindicated. Furthermore, generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023; AO Spine 2021) designate this clinical presentation as a Tier 1 strong surgical indication where delaying decompression poses a severe risk of permanent, irreversible spinal cord injury.

### III. CLINICAL & DIAGNOSTIC CLASSIFICATION

The initial reviewer's application of conservative therapy guidelines represents a fundamental **diagnostic classification error**. There is a critical clinical distinction between *cervical radiculopathy* (nerve root compression) and *cervical myelopathy* (spinal cord compression):

* **Radiculopathy** involves unilateral dermatomal symptoms and responds well to extended conservative management. Payer physical therapy requirements are medically appropriate for this pathology.
* **Myelopathy with Myelomalacia** involves direct compression and damage to the spinal cord itself. This is a progressive, structural disease characterized by upper motor neuron signs (Hoffman, Babinski, hyperreflexia, ataxia). Physical therapy cannot correct this mechanical compromise, and stretching/manipulation of a severely compressed, injured cord can lead to permanent quadriplegia or advanced neurological deficits.

The member's clinical presentation—characterized by gait ataxia, bilateral hand clumsiness, bilateral Hoffman signs, hyperreflexia, bilateral Babinski signs, and MRI-confirmed severe central cord compression with T2 cord signal change (myelomalacia)—unequivocally classifies her condition as **severe progressive cervical myelopathy**.

### IV. STATUTORY AND REGULATORY COMPLIANCE EVALUATION (CALIFORNIA LAW)

Because the member is a resident of California covered under a plan active in 2024, state statutory provisions supersede any restrictive internal plan criteria. The initial denial violates multiple California insurance laws and regulatory directives:

1. **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a):**
   This statute mandates that a health insurer *shall not* condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy under any of the following circumstances:
   * **§ 10169.5(a)(1):** The treating physician has documented in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit the member. *Met: Dr. Kim documented on October 15, 2024, that physical therapy was contraindicated and risked irreversible cord injury.*
   * **§ 10169.5(a)(3):** The condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. *Met: The member’s MRI demonstrates severe central cord compression with active myelomalacia.*
   
   Under CIC § 10169.5(c), mechanical application of a minimum physical therapy duration (such as the plan's 6-week or 4-week rule) without regard to these exceptions is unlawful.

2. **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:**
   The Department of Managed Health Care specifically addresses time-sensitive neurological conditions. APL 22-014 dictates that plans *shall not* require the completion of a full course of conservative treatment prior to surgical authorization when:
   * Objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (e.g., myelomalacia/cord signal change);
   * The treating physician documents that conservative treatment has failed or is clinically unsafe; and
   * Surgery is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines.
   
   The presence of myelomalacia is explicitly noted by the DMHC as an imaging finding that waives standard conservative trial mandates.

Therefore, the plan’s conservative treatment requirements (6 weeks of PT and trial of two drugs) are **statutorily waived and legally unenforceable** in this case.

### V. ALIGNMENT WITH GENERALLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL STANDARDS

Pursuant to California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), the standard of review for medical necessity must be based on generally accepted standards of medical practice, rather than the plan's internal medical necessity criteria.

1. **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update):**
   These guidelines state that patients exhibiting moderate-to-severe degenerative cervical myelopathy with objective spinal cord compression represent a **Tier 1 Strong Surgical Indication**. Conservative management is not recommended as a primary treatment. The presence of myelomalacia (T2 hyperintensity) indicates active, existing spinal cord injury and is the single strongest predictor of poor long-term functional recovery if decompression is delayed.
2. **AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):**
   AO Spine guidelines state: *"There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."*

Subjecting this member to 4 more weeks of physical therapy and an additional drug trial directly violates generally accepted clinical standards and exposes the member to unacceptable risks of permanent neurological decline.

### VI. PLAN CRITERIA RECONCILIATION & DISPOSITION

Under HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Criteria **CC-SPINE-2024**:
* The plan attempted to enforce Section 2.2, requiring 6 weeks of physical therapy and a 2-agent pharmacotherapy trial.
* The plan's internal "Myelopathy Pathway" (Exception 2.2(A)) reduces physical therapy to 4 weeks, but still mandates the 2-agent drug trial and is only triggered if myelopathy was classified as the primary diagnosis by the initial reviewer.
* The member only completed 2 weeks of physical therapy and trialed 1 drug (methylprednisolone), thus failing the literal terms of CC-SPINE-2024.

**Reconciliation via Hierarchy of Authority:**
While the member did not meet the plan's internal criteria, the plan's criteria are significantly more restrictive than both California State Law (CIC § 10169.5, DMHC APL 22-014) and Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine). 

Because the higher authorities in the hierarchy prohibit the enforcement of these conservative treatment mandates for patients with objective structural cord compromise and documented contraindications, **the plan's restrictive criteria are overridden**.

The medical record establishes that CPT code 22551 is medically necessary, clinically appropriate, urgent, and in complete alignment with national neurosurgical guidelines.
