### **FINAL DISPOSITION: OVERTURN THE DENIAL IN FULL**

The prior authorization denial issued by HealthFirst on November 18, 2024, is **OVERTURNED in full**. HealthFirst Health Plan is directed to **immediately authorize** CPT code 22551 (ACDF C5-C6) for member Robert Chang, to be performed by Dr. Elena Marchetti at UCSF Medical Center. 

Under the governing **California Hierarchy of Authority**, state statutory and regulatory mandates supersede restrictive internal health plan guidelines. Conditioning surgical approval on the completion of conservative physical therapy, pharmacotherapy, or interventional procedures for a patient presenting with acute, severe cervical myelopathy, objective cord compression, and extensive myelomalacia is a clinical category error, violates California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3), disregards Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, and directly contradicts generally accepted national clinical standards (AANS/CNS & AO Spine Guidelines).

### **I. CLINICAL SUMMARY & PRESENTING PATHOLOGY**

Mr. Robert Chang is a 38-year-old male with no prior cervical history who presented to the emergency department on November 10, 2024, with acute onset of progressive bilateral arm weakness, bilateral leg weakness, and significant difficulty walking developing over the preceding 72 hours. 

An urgent cervical spine MRI performed on November 10, 2024, demonstrated:
1. **Severe central cord compression** at the C5-C6 level.
2. **Extensive T2 hyperintensity** within the spinal cord, diagnostic of **extensive myelomalacia** (active, acute spinal cord injury/cellular distress).

A neurosurgical physical examination performed by Dr. Elena Marchetti revealed severe, progressive upper motor neuron (UMN) signs and objective neurological deficits consistent with an **incomplete spinal cord syndrome**:
* **Motor Strength:** Bilateral upper extremities (arms) graded at 2/5; bilateral lower extremities (legs) graded at 3/5.
* **Reflexes:** Hyperreflexia in all four extremities, bilateral positive Babinski signs, and new-onset urinary urgency.
* **Clinical Assessment:** Acute cervical myelopathy with rapidly progressive deficits representing an emergent surgical decompression situation.
* **Conservative Management Status:** Physical therapy (PT) is documented as 0 weeks and 0 sessions. Dr. Marchetti formally declared PT **absolutely contraindicated** as mechanical manipulation or neck extension during PT would pose an immediate risk of converting Mr. Chang's incomplete cord syndrome into a complete cord syndrome (permanent quadriplegia). Standard acute spinal cord injury protocol was initiated in the ER with a 24-hour inpatient course of intravenous (IV) methylprednisolone. No outpatient oral pharmacotherapy or interventional injections were performed.

### **II. EVALUATION PLAN STEP-BY-STEP ANALYSIS**

#### **Step 1: Procedural Adequacy and Disclosure Audit**
* **Finding:** The initial denial letter dated November 18, 2024, issued by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, cited internal medical policy CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 and detailed the lack of 6 weeks of PT, 2-agent pharmacotherapy, and interventional procedures. It also contained standard disclosures regarding Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(a). 
* **Conclusion:** The denial notice was procedurally adequate to avoid immediate invalidation under CHSC § 1374.31(b). The evaluation must proceed to the clinical and statutory analysis in Step 2.

#### **Step 2: Diagnostic Classification (Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy)**
* **Finding:** The medical record contains undeniable, high-level objective evidence of cervical spinal cord injury. The cervical MRI shows severe, central canal narrowing at C5-C6 with cord compression and extensive T2-weighted signal changes (myelomalacia). The physical exam demonstrates classic upper motor neuron signs (bilateral Babinski signs, generalized hyperreflexia), severe bilateral motor deficits (2/5 arms, 3/5 legs), and autonomic dysfunction (urinary urgency).
* **Conclusion:** Mr. Chang's primary diagnosis is **acute, severe cervical myelopathy** with incomplete spinal cord syndrome. He does not have uncomplicated cervical radiculopathy or axial neck pain. 

#### **Step 3: Application of Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2)**
* **Finding:** HealthFirst denied the request under standard criteria Section 2.2, citing "0 weeks" of PT, "no pharmacotherapy," and "no interventional procedures." 
* **Critique of Plan's Application:** 
  1. The plan reviewer mechanically applied standard radiculopathy conservative therapy criteria (6 weeks PT, multiple medications, injections) to a severe, acute spinal cord compression case. This constitutes a severe clinical category error, as radiculopathy and myelopathy are distinct pathological entities with completely different treatment pathways.
  2. The plan failed to apply its own **Exception 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway)**, which waives the interventional injection requirement if the treating physician documents a risk of neurological deterioration. 
  3. However, even under the modified Exception 2.2(A), the plan requires a minimum of 4 weeks of physical therapy and 8 total weeks of oral pharmacotherapy. Because Mr. Chang has 0 weeks of physical therapy and received only emergency IV methylprednisolone, he does not strictly meet the text of the plan's internal criteria.
* **Conclusion:** The request cannot be approved solely under the text of plan criteria CC-SPINE-2024. The review must transition to the higher authorities in the Hierarchy of Authority (State Law and National Clinical Standards) to determine if these plan-level requirements are legally and clinically superseded.

#### **Step 4: Statutory and Regulatory Override Review (California Law)**
Under California law, a health plan's internal clinical policies are subordinate to state statutory codes and regulatory directives. Two specific overrides are triggered by this case:

1. **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3) (Physical Therapy Override):**  
   The statute mandates that a health insurer **shall not** condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy where *"the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."*  
   * **Application:** Mr. Chang’s MRI demonstrates severe C5-C6 spinal cord compression and active myelomalacia. This represents profound structural neurological compromise. Physical therapy cannot mechanically relieve bony or disc-space compression of the spinal cord. Furthermore, the treating neurosurgeon documented that attempting PT is dangerous and contraindicated. The plan's PT requirement is therefore legally void.

2. **Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:**  
   The DMHC mandates that health plans **shall not** require a full course of conservative treatment prior to authorizing surgery for time-sensitive neurological conditions where:
   * (a) Objective imaging evidence demonstrates progressive pathology (specifically identifying **myelomalacia/cord signal change**); and
   * (b) The treating physician documents clinical urgency and that conservative care is inappropriate; and
   * (c) Surgery is indicated under generally accepted clinical guidelines.
   * **Application:** Mr. Chang has acute, rapidly progressive deficits (72-hour onset of walk difficulty and 2/5 bilateral arm weakness) with active, extensive myelomalacia on MRI. Dr. Marchetti documented extreme urgency (risk of complete permanent quadriplegia). Under APL 22-014, the plan’s requirements for prior physical therapy, conservative oral pharmacotherapy trials, and cervical injections are legally waived.

* **Conclusion:** California statutory and regulatory mandates under CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014 legally override and nullify all of HealthFirst’s conservative treatment prerequisites (PT, pharmacotherapy, and injections) for this member.

#### **Step 5: Clinical Standard Alignment (AANS/CNS & AO Spine Guidelines)**
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), medical necessity determinations must align with generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersede more restrictive internal plan criteria:

1. **AANS/CNS Guidelines (2023 Update):**  
   Moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy accompanied by objective cord compression and myelomalacia represents a **Tier 1 Strong Surgical Indication**. Immediate surgical decompression is recommended. There is no evidence supporting, and strong clinical consensus against, a trial of conservative therapy for this presentation.
2. **AO Spine Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):**  
   The guidelines state: *"There is no high-quality evidence supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical intervention for patients with moderate to severe cervical myelopathy. Delaying surgery in such patients to complete a physical therapy trial is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."*
3. **Clinical Risk of Delay:**  
   The presence of myelomalacia (T2 hyperintensity) indicates active, ongoing spinal cord injury. Delaying surgical decompression to complete an arbitrary 4-to-6-week physical therapy trial or a 4-to-8-week drug trial in a patient with 2/5 and 3/5 motor weakness constitutes clinical negligence. Continued compression of an already injured cord risks converting an incomplete spinal cord injury into permanent, irreversible quadriplegia and neurogenic bladder dysfunction.

* **Conclusion:** Generally accepted standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS and AO Spine) demand immediate surgical decompression. The plan's insistence on conservative trials is clinically contraindicated and is overridden under CHSC § 1374.32(b).

### **III. SUMMARY OF OVERRULED ARGUMENTS**

* **Plan's Argument:** The member completed 0 weeks of physical therapy, no outpatient pharmacotherapy, and no interventional injections under Section 2.2 of the CC-SPINE-2024 policy.
* **Overruling Fact:** Under California law (CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014), the health plan is legally prohibited from enforcing these conservative treatment requirements due to the objective presence of severe structural neurological compromise (spinal cord compression and myelomalacia) and documented neurological urgency. Furthermore, national clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS and AO Spine) establish that delaying surgery to perform physical therapy in a patient with severe, acute cervical myelopathy is clinically contraindicated and places the patient at imminent risk of permanent, complete paralysis.
