### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & DISPOSITION

As an expert health plan appeals reviewer for the State of California, I have evaluated the prior authorization appeal for Mr. Bernard Achebe. This review was conducted utilizing the mandated Hierarchy of Authority, governing conflicts in the following order: (1) California State Law & Regulatory Mandates, (2) Generally Accepted Clinical Standards, and (3) Plan Clinical Coverage Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024).

Based on a comprehensive review of the clinical record, objective imaging, and neurological examinations, the final disposition of this appeal is:

**DISPOSITION: UPHOLD (Confirm Denial of Prior Authorization for CPT 22551)**

**Summary of Clinical Reason for Upheld Decision:**  
While the treating neurologist asserts extreme surgical urgency, this subjective claim is directly contradicted by the patient’s objective clinical and radiographic evidence. Mr. Achebe presents with **mild, stable cervical myelopathy** (mJOA score of 15/18) with **no myelomalacia or T2 cord signal changes** on MRI. His neurological status has remained stable for six months, his tandem gait is intact, and his activities of daily living (ADLs) are fully independent. 

Under California law and generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, NASS 2020), immediate surgical intervention is not medically necessary for this mild, stable presentation. A structured course of conservative therapy remains safe, appropriate, and clinically indicated prior to surgical exposure. The patient has failed to complete either the plan’s standard conservative pathway or the modified myelopathy exception pathway (completing only 3 of 4 required weeks of physical therapy, and utilizing only 1 of 2 required pharmacotherapy agents). No statutory overrides are triggered because of the documented absence of structural cord damage (myelomalacia).

### DETAILED EVALUATION FINDINGS

#### STEP 1: Procedural Adequacy and Defect Review (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
* **Statutory Requirement:** Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), a medical necessity denial notice must specify the exact clinical coverage criteria applied, provide the specific clinical reasons for the denial, and include mandatory state Independent Medical Review (IMR) notifications. A failure to provide these elements renders the denial procedurally defective and legally void.
* **Evaluation:** The initial denial letter dated November 5, 2024, issued by Dr. Alan Foster, cited the standard conservative treatment pathway requirements, specifically identifying the incomplete elements (3 weeks of PT completed vs. 6 required; 1 pharmacotherapy agent completed vs. 2 required; and no interventional procedures). It also outlined standard appeal and state IMR rights.
* **Finding:** **Procedurally Adequate.** The denial notice met the statutory disclosure requirements of CHSC § 1374.31(b). The review must proceed to the clinical merits.

#### STEP 2: Diagnostic Classification and Severity Assessment
* **Clinical Standard:** Distinguishing between cervical radiculopathy (nerve root compression) and cervical myelopathy (spinal cord compression) is critical. Myelopathy requires objective spinal cord compression on MRI *and* matching upper motor neuron (UMN) signs (e.g., Hoffman sign, hyperreflexia). 
* **Clinical Record Analysis:**
  * **Imaging (MRI dated 10/01/2024):** C5-C6 moderate central cord compression with a canal diameter of 8mm and cord contact. Crucially, the radiologist notes: **"No T2 signal change. No myelomalacia."**
  * **Neurological Exam (dated 10/05/2024):** Positive bilateral Hoffman signs and mild hyperreflexia are present, confirming mild upper motor neuron involvement. However, his tandem gait is intact, hand grip strength is 4+/5 bilaterally (no frank weakness), and there are no dermatomal sensory deficits.
  * **Functional Assessment:** The member is fully independent in all ADLs. His modified Japanese Orthopaedic Association (mJOA) score is **15/18**, which is classified as **mild myelopathy** (scores 15–17).
  * **Temporal Course:** Symptoms have been documented as **stable for 6 months**.
* **Finding:** The member is correctly diagnosed with **mild, stable cervical myelopathy C5-C6 without myelomalacia**.

#### STEP 3: Plan-Level Criteria and Myelopathy Exception Analysis (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2A)
* **Plan Criteria:** HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2 requires 6 weeks of physical therapy, 2 pharmacotherapy agents for 4+ weeks, and 1 interventional treatment. However, under **Section 2.2A (Myelopathy Exception)**, the criteria are modified for confirmed myelopathy:
  * Physical therapy is reduced to a **4-week minimum**.
  * Pharmacotherapy remains at a **minimum of 2 agents** for 4+ weeks each.
  * Interventional treatment is waived if the physician documents that it poses a risk of neurological deterioration.
* **Comparison to Member's Clinical History:**
  * **Physical Therapy:** Completed only **3 weeks** (9 sessions). Dr. Osei halted PT, citing myelopathy concerns. This fails to meet even the reduced 4-week myelopathy exception minimum.
  * **Pharmacotherapy:** Completed only **1 agent** (Baclofen 5mg BID for 6 weeks, which yielded mild spasticity improvement). The plan requires a trial of at least two agents (e.g., combining Baclofen with an NSAID, oral corticosteroid, or gabapentinoid). 
  * **Interventional Treatment:** None completed. Dr. Osei's letter of urgency does not explicitly document why an interventional procedure would cause direct neurological risk, though he does declare general surgical urgency.
* **Finding:** **Negative Finding (Exception Not Met).** The member fails to meet the internal plan coverage criteria under CC-SPINE-2024. He fell short of the modified PT requirement (3 weeks completed vs. 4 required) and did not trial a second pharmacotherapy agent.

#### STEP 4: Application of California Statutory Overrides (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)
* **Statutory Requirement:** California law protects patients from arbitrary conservative therapy requirements in the presence of severe structural damage or progressive neurological decline.
  1. **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)** prohibits health plans from requiring PT if there is "objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address."
  2. **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014** mandates that plans waive conservative treatment if objective imaging shows "progressive pathology" (specifically citing *myelomalacia* or T2 cord signal change) AND the physician documents conservative failure.
* **Evaluation:**
  * The member's MRI explicitly notes **no T2 signal change and no myelomalacia**. This means there is no objective structural cord injury or tissue softening.
  * There is no serial imaging showing "progressive pathology." The patient’s clinical course has been documented as "stable" for 6 months, and his exam shows intact tandem gait and independent functional status.
  * Because there is no myelomalacia and the condition is mild and stable, the structural compromise is not of the severity that legally invalidates a conservative trial under CIC § 10169.5(a)(3).
* **Finding:** **Negative Finding (No Statutory Override).** The threshold of objective structural cord damage (myelomalacia) or clinical progression required to trigger a statutory override is not met.

#### STEP 5: Application of Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (CHSC § 1374.32(b))
* **Clinical Standard:** Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), the standard of medical necessity applied by independent reviewers is based on generally accepted standards of medical practice (AANS/CNS, NASS, AO Spine), which supersede more restrictive plan criteria.
  * **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) & AO Spine Guidelines (2021):** Immediate surgical decompression (Tier 1) is strongly indicated as a primary treatment for *moderate-to-severe* cervical myelopathy (mJOA < 15) or when *myelomalacia* is present, as delaying surgery in those cohorts poses a high risk of permanent, irreversible neurological deficit.
  * However, for **mild, stable cervical myelopathy** (mJOA 15–17, stable symptoms, intact functional status, no myelomalacia), clinical standards (such as NASS 2020 Guidelines) state that a supervised course of conservative management is safe, effective, and standard of care. 
  * **Analysis of Physician’s Assertion:** Dr. Osei’s letter of November 1, 2024, asserts that the patient requires "immediate surgical intervention" and that "further delay risks irreversible neurological deterioration." This subjective claim is clinically unsubstantiated. The objective evidence (MRI showing no myelomalacia, mJOA of 15, independent ADLs, intact tandem gait, and 6 months of stable symptoms) demonstrates that there is no imminent danger of rapid, irreversible deterioration. A 4-week physical therapy trial and appropriate multi-agent pharmacotherapy are not contraindicated for this specific clinical presentation.
* **Finding:** **Negative Finding (Clinical Necessity for Immediate Surgery Not Established).** Under generally accepted standards, immediate surgery is not indicated for mild, stable myelopathy without myelomalacia prior to a reasonable trial of conservative care. A conservative course of therapy is safe and appropriate.

### FINAL CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS

The initial medical necessity denial of CPT code 22551 for an ACDF C5-C6 is **UPHELD**. 

**Guidance for the Member and Provider:**
To meet medical necessity criteria for surgical approval, the member should:
1. **Complete the remaining conservative therapy requirements under the Myelopathy Exception (CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2A):**
   * Complete at least **one additional week** of supervised physical therapy (to reach the 4-week/12-session minimum). If physical therapy is fully tolerated, completing it is highly recommended.
   * Trial a **second pharmacotherapy agent** (e.g., adding a therapeutic trial of an NSAID, gabapentin, or pregabalin to the current Baclofen regimen) for at least 4 weeks.
2. **Document Clinical or Radiographic Progression:**
   * If the member experiences any objective clinical deterioration (e.g., worsening gait, declining hand grip strength, or a drop in the mJOA score below 15) or if follow-up imaging reveals new T2 cord signal changes (myelomalacia), the case should be immediately resubmitted for expedited review. Such findings would immediately trigger a clinical and statutory override, rendering surgery medically necessary.
