## I. FORMAL DETERMINATION

Pursuant to California regulatory requirements and evidence-based clinical standards, this independent clinical appeal review has evaluated the medical necessity of CPT 22551 for the member, Raymond Flores. 

The initial determination by the Health Plan to **DENY** coverage for the proposed cervical spine surgery is hereby **UPHELD**. 

Surgical intervention at C5-C6 is not medically necessary under the governing hierarchy of authority. The clinical record demonstrates that the member has pure axial neck pain without objective radiculopathy, cervical myelopathy, or radiographic structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia). Prophylactic spinal surgery for asymptomatic cervical stenosis, even in the context of high-trauma occupational risks, is not supported by California law, plan criteria, or national clinical standards.

---

## II. EVALUATION OF GOVERNING HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY

To resolve this appeal, this determination evaluates the clinical records against the established Hierarchy of Authority:

$$\text{California State Law (CIC / CHSC / DMHC)} > \text{Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS / AO Spine)} > \text{Plan Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)}$$

*   **California State Law:** Under California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 and Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014, health plans are prohibited from requiring conservative therapy (such as physical therapy) if there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise (such as myelomalacia) or progressive pathology.
*   **Clinical Standards:** The American Association of Neurological Surgeons and Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS, 2023) and AO Spine (2021) guidelines state that surgical decompression is a Tier 1 strong indication for moderate-to-severe myelopathy or objective myelomalacia. However, surgery is not indicated (Tier 3) for axial pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy, or for asymptomatic radiographic findings.
*   **Plan Criteria:** HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 3.1 explicitly excludes surgery requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy.

In this case, because there is no clinical or radiographic evidence of neurological compromise, radiculopathy, or myelopathy, **there is no conflict among the sources.** Plan criteria, clinical standards, and state law are in complete alignment: surgery is not indicated.

*   **Clinical Standards Compliance:** Under the AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023), surgical intervention for axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy, or for asymptomatic imaging findings, is classified as a **Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated)** recommendation. There is no high-quality clinical evidence demonstrating that prophylactic decompression of asymptomatic stenosis improves outcomes or prevents future injury in a manner that outweighs the immediate risks of cervical fusion.
*   **Statutory Exceptions Check:**

*   **Conclusion:** The medical record confirms axial neck pain with asymptomatic stenosis. Surgical intervention is prophylactic/experimental for this clinical presentation. **The denial must be upheld.**

## IV. RATIONALE AND RESPONSE TO APPELLANT ARGUMENTS

The appellant (Dr. Karen Davis, MD) submitted three primary arguments in support of the appeal:

1.  **Argument: Severe canal stenosis (6mm) with cord contact puts the patient at high risk of myelopathy from minor trauma.**
    *   *Clinical Response:* While a canal diameter of 6mm is radiographically narrow, the literature does not support prophylactic surgical decompression for asymptomatic stenosis. The AANS/CNS guidelines (2023) and NASS guidelines (2020) state that the natural history of asymptomatic cervical stenosis is relatively benign, and the rate of acute spinal cord injury from minor trauma is exceedingly low. Prophylactic ACDF carries a significant risk of severe, well-documented complications—such as adjacent segment degeneration, pseudoarthrosis, dysphagia, and nerve injury—which outweigh the theoretical, low-probability risk of a future traumatic spinal cord injury.
2.  **Argument: Prophylactic surgery is indicated for severe stenosis even without current deficits.**
    *   *Clinical Response:* This argument is inconsistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice. Under CHSC § 1374.32(b), medical necessity must align with generally accepted standards of medical practice. Major spine societies (AO Spine, NASS, AANS/CNS) have reached a consensus that prophylactic surgery is *not* indicated for asymptomatic spinal stenosis. Decompression is indicated only when there is clinical evidence of myelopathy, radiculopathy, or objective structural cord damage (myelomalacia on T2 MRI).
3.  **Argument: The patient’s occupation as a construction worker operating heavy equipment creates an elevated trauma risk.**
    *   *Clinical Response:* Occupational trauma risk is a relevant social and personal consideration, but it does not alter the physiological and evidence-based standards of medical necessity. In the absence of clinical or radiographic spinal cord injury (myelomalacia), prophylactic surgical fusion of the cervical spine is not considered clinically appropriate or effective for preventing injury. The standard of care for a patient with asymptomatic severe stenosis in a high-risk occupation is counseling regarding activity modification and close clinical surveillance, rather than elective spinal reconstruction.
