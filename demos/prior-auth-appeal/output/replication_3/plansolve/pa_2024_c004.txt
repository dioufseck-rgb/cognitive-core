### FINAL DETERMINATION
**DISPOSITION:** **UPHOLD THE DENIAL**

The initial denial of prior authorization for CPT code 22551 (Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion at C5-C6) is **UPHELD**. Clinical and diagnostic evidence confirms that the member exhibits axial neck pain only, with a completely normal neurological examination and no objective signs of clinical cervical myelopathy, radiculopathy, or spinal cord compression/myelomalacia. Under California regulatory standards, generally accepted spine surgery guidelines, and the plan's internal medical necessity criteria, surgical decompression is not indicated for prophylactic purposes or for the treatment of isolated axial neck pain in the absence of objective neurological deficit.

### II. EVALUATION & FINDINGS BY STEP

#### Step 1: Procedural Adequacy Audit (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
*   **Evaluation Methodology:** The initial denial notice issued on October 25, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, was audited against the statutory standards of California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b). Under California law, a medical necessity denial must explicitly specify the clinical criteria applied and the precise clinical reasons for the determination.
*   **Findings:** The denial notice dated October 25, 2024, cited the plan's specific clinical coverage criteria document (**CC-SPINE-2024**). It provided a detailed, patient-specific clinical rationale: noting that while cervical stenosis is documented, the member has a completely normal neurological exam, axial neck pain only, and no clinical or imaging evidence of myelopathy or radiculopathy. It further noted that under Section 3.1 of the criteria, surgery is excluded for axial pain without neurological deficits.
*   **Procedural Determination:** **Adequate.** The denial notice met all statutory notification requirements of CHSC § 1374.31(b). No procedural defects are present to warrant a procedural override.

#### Step 2: Clinical and Diagnostic Evidence Audit (Diagnostic Verification)
*   **Evaluation Methodology:** A comprehensive medical record review was performed, cross-referencing the member’s physical examination findings and imaging reports with clinical diagnostic classifications.
*   **Findings:**
    *   **Clinical Presentation:** The clinical records document that the member presents with axial neck pain (rated 5/10).
    *   **Neurological Examination (2024-09-20):** Dr. Karen Davis's physical examination findings indicate a **completely normal neurological examination**. There is a documented absence of upper motor neuron (UMN) signs (no hyperreflexia, no positive Hoffman sign, no clonus, no gait disturbance, and no bilateral hand weakness/clumsiness). Additionally, there is no evidence of dermatomal sensory loss, localized motor weakness, or reflex changes indicative of radiculopathy.
    *   **Cervical Spine MRI (2024-09-15):** The MRI of the cervical spine reveals severe central canal stenosis at C5-C6 with a canal diameter of 6mm (normal >13mm). However, the radiologist's interpretive report explicitly notes **"cord contact without compression"** and **"no T2 signal change, no myelomalacia."**
*   **Diagnostic Conclusion:** The member has severe anatomical cervical stenosis at C5-C6 with radiographic "cord contact." However, there is **no objective clinical cervical myelopathy** (which requires spinal cord compression on MRI *plus* matching clinical upper motor neuron signs) and **no radiculopathy** (which requires nerve root compression with correlating dermatomal signs). The clinical presentation is classified strictly as **axial neck pain without neurological deficit**.

#### Step 3: Statutory Override Evaluation (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)
*   **Evaluation Methodology:** The clinical record was assessed against California statutory provisions that restrict health plans from mechanically enforcing conservative treatment/physical therapy (PT) requirements.
    *   **CIC § 10169.5:** Prohibits plans from conditioning surgery on PT completion if there is objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that PT cannot address, or if a functional plateau is reached.
    *   **DMHC APL 22-014:** Mandates the waiver of standard conservative treatment trials for time-sensitive, progressive neurological conditions, specifically citing "myelomalacia on MRI" as a finding that supports waiver of conservative management.
*   **Findings:**
    *   **Conservative Treatment Completed:** The member has already completed a robust and exhaustive course of conservative therapy. This includes **8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised physical therapy** (which achieved a functional plateau with modest pain reduction from 7/10 to 5/10 but no neurological changes), **3 months of Naproxen**, **6 weeks of Cyclobenzaprine**, and a **C5-C6 epidural steroid injection** on October 10, 2024 (providing 60% pain relief for 6 weeks).
    *   **Neurological Compromise/Myelomalacia Assessment:** The member’s MRI explicitly confirms **no myelomalacia** and **no spinal cord compression** (only cord contact).
*   **Statutory Determination:** Because the member has already completed and failed a comprehensive course of conservative therapy, the plan's denial is *not* based on a failure to complete physical therapy (which would be subject to CIC § 10169.5). Rather, the denial is based on the **absence of a clinical surgical indication**. Because there is no progressive myelomalacia or active spinal cord compression, the emergency clinical carve-outs of DMHC APL 22-014 are not triggered. No statutory override is applicable to enforce surgical approval.

#### Step 4: Generally Accepted Clinical Standards Evaluation (CHSC § 1374.32)
*   **Evaluation Methodology:** The member's clinical presentation and the requesting surgeon’s rationale ("prophylactic decompression" due to high-risk construction occupation and a 6mm canal diameter) were evaluated against the California Independent Medical Review (IMR) clinical standard under CHSC § 1374.32(b). This standard mandates referencing generally accepted clinical guidelines (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021, and NASS 2020) over restrictive internal plan criteria.
*   **Findings & Clinical Literature Analysis:**
    *   **Surgical Indications for Cervical Stenosis:** Under the **AANS/CNS Joint Section 2023 Guidelines** and **AO Spine 2021 Clinical Guidelines**, surgical decompression (ACDF) is strongly indicated (Tier 1) for *moderate-to-severe clinical myelopathy* with objective spinal cord compression and/or *myelomalacia (T2 cord signal change)*. 
    *   **The Prophylactic Decompression Standard:** Dr. Karen Davis argues that surgery is indicated prophylactically to prevent catastrophic spinal cord injury because the member is a construction worker operating heavy machinery with a 6mm canal. However, **generally accepted medical literature does not support prophylactic cervical decompression for asymptomatic or purely axial-pain stenosis**, even in the presence of severe radiographic narrowing. 
    *   The **AO Spine 2021 Guidelines** explicitly state that the natural history of asymptomatic or non-myelopathic stenosis does not justify the significant surgical risks of an ACDF (such as adjacent segment disease, hardware failure, pseudarthrosis, or accidental cord injury) in the absence of active clinical myelopathy.
    *   The **AANS/CNS 2023 Standards** categorize this patient's clinical presentation under **Tier 3: Surgery Not Indicated** (defined as "axial neck pain without radiculopathy or myelopathy, and imaging findings without correlating clinical symptoms").
*   **Clinical Standards Determination:** Under California's clinical standard of review (CHSC § 1374.32), performing an invasive ACDF for prophylactic indications in a neurologically intact individual with axial-only neck pain is considered inconsistent with generally accepted standards of medical practice.

#### Step 5: Plan Criteria and Exception Compliance Audit (CC-SPINE-2024)
*   **Evaluation Methodology:** The request was evaluated against the contractual criteria outlined in HealthFirst Document CC-SPINE-2024.
*   **Findings:**
    *   **Section 2.1 (Diagnosis Requirement):** To qualify for cervical spine surgery, the member must have a confirmed diagnosis of either: (a) Cervical radiculopathy with matching radicular symptoms; (b) Cervical myelopathy with matching upper motor neuron signs; or (c) Cervical disc herniation with objective neurological deficit. The member satisfies none of these three categories.
    *   **Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Pathway Exception):** This exception reduces PT requirements and waives interventional requirements. However, it explicitly states: *"The myelopathy exception applies only when myelopathy is the PRIMARY diagnosis."* Because the member has no clinical myelopathy, this pathway cannot be invoked.
    *   **Section 3.1 (Exclusions):** This section explicitly mandates that authorization **shall be denied** when: *"Surgery is requested solely for axial neck pain without documented radiculopathy or myelopathy."* The member's presentation fits this exclusion precisely.
*   **Plan Criteria Determination:** The requested procedure does not satisfy the medical necessity criteria of CC-SPINE-2024 and is explicitly excluded under Section 3.1.

### III. APPEAL RESOLUTION SUMMARY

The appellant's primary arguments have been thoroughly reviewed but are medically insufficient to overturn the initial denial:
1.  **Risk of Myelopathy from Minor Trauma / 6mm Canal:** While a 6mm canal constitutes severe anatomical stenosis, the MRI explicitly confirms there is **no spinal cord compression** and **no myelomalacia (cord injury)**. Generally accepted medical guidelines (AANS/CNS, AO Spine) do not support prophylactic spinal fusion based purely on canal dimensions in an asymptomatic, neurologically intact patient.
2.  **Occupational Trauma Risk:** While the member's occupation as a construction worker involves physical labor, clinical guidelines do not establish occupational risk as an independent medical necessity criterion for prophylactic spinal surgery. The complications of an elective ACDF outweigh the statistical probability of a catastrophic event, and standard practice dictates occupational modifications rather than prophylactic spine fusion.
3.  **Conservative Treatment Failure:** While the member has completed and failed conservative treatment, this therapy was directed toward **axial neck pain**. Decompressive spinal surgery (ACDF) is an intervention designed to relieve neurological compression (radiculopathy/myelopathy), not to treat isolated degenerative axial neck pain. 

Because the initial denial notice was procedurally adequate (Step 1), the clinical examination is completely normal with no myelopathy or radiculopathy (Step 2), no statutory overrides are triggered (Step 3), generally accepted clinical standards classify the procedure as "not indicated" for this presentation (Step 4), and plan exclusions apply (Step 5), the plan's determination must be sustained.

**The initial denial of the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is UPHELD.**
