### FORMAL DISPOSITION
**PARTIAL OVERTURN / PARTIAL UPHOLD**  
*   **APPROVED:** Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF), Single-Level (C5-C6) — **CPT 22551**  
*   **DENIED:** Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF), Additional Level (C6-C7) — **CPT 22552**  

The initial determination by HealthFirst dated November 1, 2024, is **upheld in part and overturned in part**. Medical necessity has been established for surgical intervention at the C5-C6 level. However, medical necessity has **not** been established under plan criteria or generally accepted clinical standards for surgical intervention at the C6-C7 level. 

---

### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The member, a 60-year-old male, presents with symptoms and imaging findings consistent with severe right C6 cervical radiculopathy. He has completed a robust, 8-week conservative management trial, including supervised physical therapy, dual-agent pharmacotherapy, and a targeted epidural steroid injection, with persistent severe symptoms and matching motor weakness. 

The clinical record demonstrates severe structural pathology at C5-C6 (large disc herniation with moderate-to-severe C6 nerve root compression) that directly correlates with his clinical presentation. Conversely, the C6-C7 level demonstrates only age-appropriate, mild degenerative changes without objective spinal cord or nerve root compression, and lacks correlating clinical symptoms. 

While a single-level C5-C6 ACDF is medically necessary and fully supported, a two-level ACDF (extending to C6-C7) is unsupported by objective clinical evidence, plan policy, and generally accepted standards of medical practice.

In accordance with California regulatory requirements, this appeal has been evaluated under the following strict hierarchy of authority:
$$\text{California State Law (CIC, CHSC, DMHC APL)} > \text{Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine, NASS)} > \text{Plan Coverage Criteria (CC-SPINE-2024)}$$

Where the plan's internal coverage criteria are more restrictive than generally accepted clinical standards, state law (**CHSC § 1374.32(b)**) mandates that the clinical standards must override the plan's criteria.

### II. CLINICAL ANALYSIS & EVALUATION STEPS

#### Step 1: Procedural Adequacy and Regulatory Compliance Check
Pursuant to **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b)**, the initial denial notice issued on November 1, 2024, was evaluated for procedural adequacy. 
*   **Findings:** The denial notice clearly cited the specific plan clinical coverage criteria utilized (*HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024*), provided a clear and specific clinical reason for the determination (authorizing the C5-C6 pathology while denying the C6-C7 level due to mild changes only), and outlined the member's right to an Independent Medical Review (IMR).
*   **Conclusion:** The initial denial was **procedurally adequate** and compliant with California regulatory notification standards.

#### Step 2: Diagnostic and Clinical Classification (Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy)
To determine the correct clinical pathway and evaluate whether conservative treatment waivers apply, the member's primary pathology must be classified.
*   **Clinical Criteria for Cervical Myelopathy:** Requires objective spinal cord compression on MRI, upper motor neuron (UMN) signs (e.g., positive Hoffman sign, hyperreflexia, clonus), bilateral symptoms, and/or gait disturbance.
*   **Clinical Criteria for Cervical Radiculopathy:** Characterized by unilateral nerve root compression with corresponding dermatomal sensory, motor, or reflex deficits.
*   **Member Evaluation:** 
    *   *Symptomatology:* Unilateral right-sided C6 dermatomal numbness, tingling, and weakness. No bilateral upper extremity symptoms, no gait disturbance, and no bowel/bladder dysfunction.
    *   *Physical Exam (2024-09-20):* Significant right bicep weakness (3+/5), absent right bicep reflex, and right C6 dermatomal sensory loss. No upper motor neuron signs (no hyperreflexia, Hoffman sign, or clonus documented).
    *   *Imaging (2024-09-15):* MRI shows no evidence of spinal cord compression, cord contact, or myelomalacia (T2 cord signal change) at any level.
*   **Conclusion:** The member's primary clinical diagnosis is **Right C6 Cervical Radiculopathy**. He does **not** have cervical myelopathy. Consequently, standard conservative therapy rules apply, and the statutory myelopathy exceptions under **DMHC APL 22-014** and **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(A)** are not active.

#### Step 3: Evaluation of the C5-C6 Level Under State Law and Clinical Standards
The clinical necessity of surgical intervention at the C5-C6 level was reviewed against plan policy and California law.
*   **Clinical Findings:** The September 15, 2024 MRI shows a large disc herniation at C5-C6 causing moderate-to-severe right C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis. This directly correlates with the physical exam findings of 3+/5 right bicep weakness and an absent bicep reflex (C6 root).
*   **Conservative Treatment Trial:** 
    *   *Physical Therapy:* Completed 8 weeks (24 sessions) of supervised PT with persistent symptoms, constituting a documented "functional plateau" under **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2)**.
    *   *Pharmacotherapy:* Trialed on Pregabalin 150mg BID (4 months) and Naproxen 500mg BID (3 months) with incomplete relief, satisfying the dual-agent criteria of *CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2(b)*.
    *   *Interventional:* Completed a right C6 epidural steroid injection on October 15, 2024, with good temporary relief (70%) that fully recurred in 4 weeks, satisfying *Section 2.2(c)*.
*   **Conclusion:** The C5-C6 level fully satisfies **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.0** and meets **AANS/CNS (2023) Tier 2** surgical indications. The plan's initial decision to approve pathology at this level is confirmed.

#### Step 4: Evaluation of the C6-C7 Level Under State Law and Clinical Standards
The treating provider's request to perform a concurrent fusion at C6-C7 was evaluated against clinical and legal guidelines.
*   **Imaging Findings:** The September 15, 2024 MRI shows only a "small disc bulge" and "mild foraminal narrowing" at C6-C7. There is **no nerve root compression** and **no spinal cord contact** at this level.
*   **Clinical Findings:** The physical exam noted a "slightly diminished" right tricep reflex (C7), but "no definitive C7 dermatomal symptoms." The treating physician's notes show C7 symptoms were "possibly present but not clearly documented." Additionally, the physical therapy discharge summary at week 8 explicitly states that C7 symptoms were "minimal and possibly resolving."
*   **Analysis of Appeal Arguments:**
    1.  *Adjacent Segment Degeneration:* The appeal argues that adjacent levels should be addressed simultaneously to prevent accelerated degeneration. Under **AANS/CNS (2023)** and **AO Spine (2021) Guidelines**, prophylactic adjacent-level fusion in the absence of objective structural compromise, mechanical instability, or severe clinical symptoms is **not** medically indicated. Biomechanical studies indicate that extending a fusion unnecessarily actually increases stress on remaining adjacent segments, accelerating adjacent segment pathology.
    2.  *Clinical Standard (CHSC § 1374.32(b)):* A multi-level ACDF is only indicated when multiple levels demonstrate matching, severe radiographic compression and corresponding clinical deficits. C6-C7 demonstrates only mild, age-appropriate degenerative changes. Under **AANS/CNS Tier 3 Guidelines**, surgical decompression is not indicated for radiographic findings that lack matching, objective clinical symptoms or nerve root compression.
*   **Conclusion:** The clinical presentation and imaging do not support the necessity of a C6-C7 fusion. Performing a fusion at this level is inconsistent with generally accepted clinical standards and fails **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Sections 2.3 and 3.3**.

#### Step 5: Assessment of Conservative Treatment and Exceptions
*   **Findings:** The member successfully completed a comprehensive course of conservative treatment that failed to resolve his C6 radiculopathy. However, no C7-specific conservative treatments (such as a C7-selective nerve block) were attempted, nor was there any clinical need to do so given that C7 symptoms were minimal and resolving by week 8 of physical therapy.
*   **Applicability of Statutory Waivers:** Because there is no objective evidence of structural spinal cord compromise (myelomalacia) or progressive neurological deterioration at C6-C7, the conservative treatment waivers under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)** and **DMHC APL 22-014** do not apply to the C6-C7 level.

### IV. CONCLUSION & APPEALS INSTRUCTIONS
While the member is an excellent candidate for a single-level anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551), there is no clinical or radiographical justification for extending this procedure to a two-level fusion at C6-C7 (CPT 22552). 

A single-level C5-C6 ACDF is **APPROVED**. The addition of the C6-C7 level is **DENIED**.
