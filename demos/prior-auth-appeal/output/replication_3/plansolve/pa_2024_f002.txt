## I. FORMAL APPEAL DETERMINATION

**DISPOSITION: OVERTURN**

The initial decision to deny coverage for the requested Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is hereby **OVERTURNED**. Prior authorization is **APPROVED**. 

---

## II. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

On October 10, 2024, the plan issued a medical necessity denial (MED-003) for an ACDF at C5-C6, citing a discrepancy in physical therapy (PT) documentation. The initial reviewer noted that records from Riverside PT documented 6 weeks of therapy, while initial records from Coastal PT documented 4 weeks, creating what the plan defined as "conflicting" or "unverifiable" PT duration. 

Upon comprehensive appellate review, this denial is overturned based on two independent and compelling grounds:
1. **Procedural Defect (Step 1):** The initial denial notice violated California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b) by failing to cite the specific clinical coverage criteria applied or providing a clinical rationale for why the alleged PT discrepancy precluded surgical authorization for this clinically compromised patient. 
2. **Clinical and Statutory Compliance (Step 5):** The complete clinical record—including newly submitted, authenticated records from Coastal PT—establishes that the member has completed **12 weeks (36 sessions)** of supervised PT, achieved a documented functional plateau, exhausted appropriate pharmacotherapy, and failed interventional injection therapy. The clinical presentation of severe C5-C6 radiculopathy is fully supported by objective imaging (MRI) showing severe nerve root compression. Under both the plan’s internal clinical criteria (**HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2**) and California Insurance Code (**CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)**), medical necessity is fully established.

---

## III. PROCEDURAL ADEQUACY AND DENIAL NOTICE DEFECT CHECK (STEP 1)

Under California law, health plans must adhere to strict transparency standards when denying services on the basis of medical necessity. 

* **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b)** dictates that any medical necessity denial notice *shall* specify the exact clinical criteria applied, the specific clinical reasons for the denial, and include a clear explanation of Independent Medical Review (IMR) rights. A notice that fails to provide these details is legally **defective** and must be treated as if no denial was issued for the purpose of appeal timelines.
* **Evaluation of the 2024-10-10 Notice:** The initial denial letter relied strictly on administrative "uncertainty" regarding the duration of physical therapy, stating: *"Records conflict on duration... Plan requires verification of claimed PT duration before approval."* The plan failed to cite the active medical coverage criteria (**CC-SPINE-2024**) and provided no clinical analysis of the member’s actual neurological deficits or why a potential duration discrepancy of completed PT (which, even at the lowest estimate of 10 weeks, exceeded the plan's 6-week minimum) justified withholding surgical authorization.

**Finding:** The denial notice is procedurally **defective** under CHSC § 1374.31(b). This procedural failure alone supports an immediate **OVERTURN** or **REMAND**. To ensure clinical completeness, a full clinical and statutory evaluation of the medical record has been executed below.

## IV. DIAGNOSTIC CLASSIFICATION & STATUTORY WAIVER ANALYSIS (STEPS 2 & 3)

### 1. Radiculopathy vs. Myelopathy (Step 2)
The member's diagnostic profile must be categorized to apply the correct clinical and regulatory pathways:
* **Myelopathy** requires objective spinal cord compression on MRI accompanied by upper motor neuron (UMN) signs (e.g., hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman sign, clonus, gait disturbances, or bilateral upper extremity weakness).
* **Radiculopathy** is defined by nerve root compression with corresponding unilateral dermatomal symptoms and lower motor neuron findings (reflex loss, localized weakness, dermatomal numbness) without UMN signs.

**Clinical Record Evaluation:** 
* **MRI (2024-08-15):** Demonstrates a large C5-C6 disc herniation causing severe *right C6 nerve root compression* and foraminal stenosis. No spinal cord compression or myelomalacia (T2 cord signal change) is reported.
* **Neurological Examination (2024-08-20):** Reveals right C6 dermatomal numbness, right bicep weakness (4/5), an absent right bicep reflex, and a positive right Spurling sign. There are no documented UMN signs, gait disturbances, or bilateral deficits.

**Finding:** The member is diagnosed with severe, unilateral **C5-C6 Cervical Radiculopathy** with objective neurological deficits (motor and reflex loss). 

### 2. State Law Statutory Exemption Review (Step 3)
Because the primary diagnosis is cervical radiculopathy without objective spinal cord compression or myelomalacia, the statutory waivers of conservative therapy under **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3)** and **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014** (which apply to progressive structural compromise like myelomalacia) do not automatically exempt the member from a conservative treatment trial. The standard plan criteria under **Section 2.2** must be evaluated.

## V. CLINICAL STANDARDS AND INTERNAL PLAN CRITERIA RECONCILIATION (STEPS 4 & 5)

Under the governing **Hierarchy of Authority**, where plan criteria and clinical guidelines conflict, California State Law and Generally Accepted Clinical Standards supersede plan internal policies. In this case, the member successfully meets **both** the internal plan criteria and the statutory standards.

### 1. Physical Therapy Requirement (CC-SPINE-2024 § 2.2(a) & CIC § 10169.5)
* **Plan Requirement:** Minimum 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy with documented functional outcomes.
* **Clinical Records Submitted on Appeal:**
  1. **Riverside Physical Therapy (Complete):** Attended from 2024-04-01 to 2024-05-10. Completed **6 weeks (18 sessions)**. The treating physical therapist documented a clinical plateau; symptoms persisted without functional improvement.
  2. **Coastal Physical Therapy (Newly Submitted & Complete):** Attended from 2024-06-01 to 2024-07-12. Completed **6 weeks (18 sessions)** (the initial submission was partial, containing only weeks 1–4). The therapist documented a clear functional plateau, noting that maximum therapeutic benefit had been reached with no improvement in the member's neurological deficit.
* **Statutory Framework (CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)):** California law mandates that a health plan *shall not* condition approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of further physical therapy if the member has completed a course of physical therapy and the treating physician or therapist documents that the member reached a **functional plateau** without adequate resolution of symptoms.

**Finding:** The member completed **12 total weeks (36 sessions)** of supervised PT across two providers, with both documenting a functional plateau. This far exceeds the plan’s 6-week requirement. The initial denial based on "uncertainty" is fully resolved by the submitted complete records. Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)**, the plan is statutorily barred from requiring additional conservative PT.

### 2. Pharmacotherapy Requirement (CC-SPINE-2024 § 2.2(b))
* **Plan Requirement:** Trial of at least two covered agents (e.g., NSAIDs, neuropathic agents, muscle relaxants) for a minimum of 4 weeks each.
* **Member's Trial:**
  * *Gabapentin 600mg TID* for 5 months (Partial relief).
  * *Duloxetine 60mg daily* for 3 months (Partial neuropathic benefit).

**Finding:** The member successfully completed extended trials of two separate neuropathic agents, satisfying Section 2.2(b).

### 3. Interventional Treatment Requirement (CC-SPINE-2024 § 2.2(c))
* **Plan Requirement:** Minimum of one interventional treatment (e.g., epidural steroid injection).
* **Member's Trial:** Completed a right C6 epidural steroid injection on 2024-08-28. The procedure provided 60% temporary relief but symptoms returned to baseline within 3 weeks.

**Finding:** The member successfully completed the interventional trial, satisfying Section 2.2(c).

### 4. Alignment with Generally Accepted Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS & AO Spine)
Per the **AANS/CNS 2023 Joint Section Guidelines**, surgical decompression via ACDF (CPT 22551) is highly indicated (Tier 2/Reasonable Indication) for patients presenting with cervical radiculopathy who demonstrate:
1. Objective radiological evidence of nerve root compression (confirmed on 2024-08-15 MRI).
2. Corresponding objective neurological deficits (confirmed: right bicep weakness 4/5, absent bicep reflex, C6 dermatomal numbness).
3. Persistent, functionally impairing symptoms that have failed to respond to a comprehensive course of conservative management (>6–12 weeks of PT, pharmacotherapy, and epidural injections).

To delay surgical decompression in a patient with documented motor weakness (4/5 bicep strength) and sensory loss who has already completed 12 weeks of structured therapy is clinically contraindicated and increases the risk of permanent neurological deficit.

## VI. FINAL DISPOSITION AND AUTHORIZATION DETAILS

The clinical record demonstrates that Steven Clark is suffering from severe, symptomatic C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy. He has exhaustively trialed conservative therapy, including 12 weeks of supervised physical therapy resulting in a documented functional plateau, two long-term neuropathic medications, and a targeted epidural steroid injection. The clinical and imaging findings align perfectly with the medical necessity criteria outlined in **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2**, **generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023)**, and California statutory mandates (**CIC § 10169.5** and **CHSC § 1374.31**).

Accordingly, the prior authorization request for an Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion at C5-C6 is **APPROVED**.
