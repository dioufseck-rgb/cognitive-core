### **I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**
The member, Steven Clark, is a 46-year-old male with a diagnosis of symptomatic cervical radiculopathy at C5-C6 with correlating MRI pathology and objective neurological deficits (motor weakness and reflex loss). The plan's initial denial, dated October 10, 2024, was based on an alleged "documentation conflict" regarding the duration of conservative physical therapy (PT) completed by the member. 

Upon comprehensive review of the entire clinical record, the statutory requirements under California law, and generally accepted clinical standards, **the initial denial is OVERTURNED**. 

The clinical evidence demonstrates that the member has completed **12 weeks of physical therapy** (36 sessions total across two providers), with documented functional plateaus at both. Additionally, the member has completed more than 4 weeks of multi-agent pharmacotherapy and a targeted cervical epidural steroid injection, with only temporary relief. The plan's denial was both procedurally defective and clinically erroneous, as even the initially verified records (6 weeks of PT from Riverside PT) independently satisfied the plan’s standard conservative treatment requirements.

### **III. PROCEDURAL ADEQUACY AND DEFECT CHECK (CHSC § 1374.31(b))**
Under **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b)**, any medical necessity denial notice issued by a health plan must explicitly cite the specific clinical coverage criteria applied and provide a specific, individualized clinical reason linked to those criteria. 

*   **Finding:** The initial denial notice dated October 10, 2024, failed to cite the applicable clinical coverage guideline (**HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2**) and relied solely on a "documentation conflict" (discrepancy between Riverside PT and Coastal PT records) to assert that the PT duration was "uncertain." 
*   **Procedural Defect Identified:** Because the plan failed to link this administrative/documentation issue to the explicit clinical criteria or the statutory medical necessity rules, the notice is **legally defective under CHSC § 1374.31(b)**. 
*   **Resolution:** While a procedural defect can be grounds for immediate remand or overturn under **CHSC § 1367.01**, the clinical record is fully complete and unequivocally supports the medical necessity of the requested surgery. To prevent further clinical delay and potential neurological deterioration, this determination will proceed to a full clinical review on the merits to issue an binding Overturn.

### **VI. STATE LAW & CLINICAL STANDARDS ALIGNMENT**

#### **1. California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5**
Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)**, a health insurer is legally prohibited from conditioning approval of a surgical procedure on the completion of physical therapy if the member has completed a course of PT and the treating physician/therapist documents that the member has reached a **functional plateau** without adequate symptom resolution.
*   *Finding:* Both physical therapy providers explicitly documented a clinical and functional plateau. The therapist at Coastal PT documented a "maximum therapeutic benefit reached" with "no improvement in neurological deficit" (right-sided motor weakness and reflex loss). Under California law, forcing the patient to undergo or verify further conservative therapy is prohibited.

#### **2. Clinical Standards Evaluation (CHSC § 1374.32(b))**
Under **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**, independent review of medical necessity must align with generally accepted standards of medical practice, overriding more restrictive plan guidelines.
*   *Standards Applied:* American Association of Neurological Surgeons / Congress of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS, 2023) and North American Spine Society (NASS, 2020) guidelines.
*   *Clinical Consensus:* ACDF (CPT 22551) is highly indicated and represents the gold standard for patients with cervical radiculopathy who exhibit correlating, severe nerve root compression on MRI, objective motor deficits (4/5 bicep weakness, absent bicep reflex), and failure to respond to a comprehensive, multi-modal conservative trial (PT, neuropathic drugs, and epidural injections). Continuing conservative treatment in the face of progressive or static motor weakness (4/5) risks permanent neurological deficit.
