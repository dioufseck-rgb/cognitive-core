## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
An independent clinical and regulatory review has been conducted regarding the appeal filed by Dr. James Wilson on behalf of member Linda Park for a two-level Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5–C6 and C6–C7. 

The clinical evidence demonstrates that the member has severe, symptomatic, and conservative-treatment-refractory **right C6 radiculopathy** directly correlating with a large disc herniation and moderate-to-severe nerve root compression at the **C5–C6 level**. The member has fully exhausted all plan-mandated and clinically appropriate conservative treatments (including 8 weeks of physical therapy, multiple pharmacotherapies, and an epidural steroid injection) without lasting relief. Surgical intervention at the **C5–C6 level** is medically necessary and is hereby **APPROVED**.

However, clinical examination and objective imaging confirm that the adjacent **C6–C7 level** exhibits only mild degenerative changes without spinal cord contact, nerve root compression, or correlating clinical deficits. The requesting surgeon’s rationale for fusing this second level is purely prophylactic (to prevent future adjacent segment disease). Under generally accepted clinical standards and plan exclusions, prophylactic spinal fusion in the absence of active pathological compression and corresponding clinical symptoms is not medically necessary. Therefore, the denial of the **C6–C7 level** is **UPHELD**.

## CLINICAL AND REGULATORY EVALUATION

### I. GOVERNING SOURCE HIERARCHY & CONFLICT RESOLUTION
In accordance with California regulatory requirements, this appeal was reviewed under the following hierarchy of authority:
$$\text{California State Law (CIC, CHSC, DMHC APL)} > \text{Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine, NASS)} > \text{Plan Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)}$$
Per **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b)** and **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014**, any internal health plan criteria that are more restrictive than generally accepted standards of medical practice or California statutory mandates must be bypassed in favor of these higher authorities.

### II. STEP-BY-STEP EVALUATION

#### Step 1: Procedural Adequacy & Defect Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
The initial denial notice issued on October 28, 2024, by Dr. Alan Foster, MD, was evaluated for procedural adequacy. 
* **Findings:** The denial notice provided the specific clinical reasons for the decision, noting that imaging demonstrated pathology at C5–C6 only, while C6–C7 showed only mild degenerative changes without significant compression or corresponding neurological deficits. This clinical explanation satisfies the notification standards under **CHSC § 1374.31(b)**. The notice was procedurally adequate, allowing the appeal to proceed to a substantive clinical evaluation.

#### Step 2: Clinical Diagnostic Classification (Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy)
The clinical record and physical examination dated September 5, 2024, were analyzed to classify the member's condition.
* **Findings:** The neurological physical exam documents right C6 dermatomal numbness and tingling, a diminished right bicep reflex, and right grip strength of 4/5. Crucially, there are **no bilateral findings, no gait imbalances, and no upper motor neuron (UMN) signs** (such as hyperreflexia, clonus, or a positive Hoffman sign) documented. 
* **Conclusion:** The patient is diagnosed with **unilateral C5–C6 cervical radiculopathy** (specifically affecting the right C6 nerve root). There is no clinical evidence of cervical myelopathy. Consequently, the specialized myelopathy pathways (Plan Section 2.2(A)) and myelopathy-specific conservative treatment waivers are not applicable.

#### Step 3: Objective Imaging Analysis & Myelomalacia Assessment
The cervical spine MRI report dated September 1, 2024, was evaluated.
* **Findings:**
  * **C5–C6:** Large disc herniation with moderate-to-severe nerve root compression and moderate foraminal stenosis.
  * **C6–C7:** Mild disc bulge, minimal foraminal narrowing, no spinal cord contact, and no identifiable nerve root compression.
  * **Myelomalacia Check:** There is no evidence of spinal cord compression, spinal cord signal change, or myelomalacia at either level.
* **Conclusion:** Because there is no objective evidence of structural spinal cord injury (myelomalacia) or severe cord compression, the statutory waiver of conservative treatment for time-sensitive neurological conditions under **DMHC APL 22-014** and **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3)** is not triggered. The clinical necessity of surgical decompression must be evaluated using the standard radiculopathy conservative treatment pathway.

#### Step 4: Level-Specific Pathology Evaluation (C5–C6 vs. C6–C7)
An independent assessment of medical necessity was performed for each requested spinal level:

* **Sub-step 4a: C5–C6 Level (Approved)**
  * **Clinical Correlation:** The large disc herniation and moderate-to-severe nerve root compression identified on the MRI of September 1, 2024, directly correlate with the patient's right C6 radicular symptoms (numbness, tingling, diminished bicep reflex, and 4/5 grip strength). 
  * **Clinical Standards:** Under **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023) Tier 2**, surgical decompression is a highly reasonable and indicated option for patients with radiculopathy who demonstrate persistent, correlating symptoms after an appropriate conservative trial. Decompression at C5–C6 is clinically appropriate and medically necessary.

* **Sub-step 4b: C6–C7 Level (Denied)**
  * **Clinical Correlation:** The MRI of September 1, 2024, shows only mild degenerative changes (mild disc bulge, minimal narrowing) with no nerve root or cord compression at C6–C7. The neurological exam shows **no C7 dermatomal or reflex findings**. 
  * **Surgeon Rationale:** Dr. Wilson's note states that he recommends a two-level fusion to prophylactically stabilize C6–C7 to prevent adjacent segment disease (ASD), noting a 25% 5-year incidence.
  * **Clinical Standards:** Under **AANS/CNS Guidelines (2023) Tier 3** and **NASS Guidelines (2020)**, surgical intervention is explicitly *not indicated* for prophylactic purposes or for imaging findings without correlating clinical symptoms. While adjacent segment disease is a known phenomenon, there is no high-quality clinical evidence supporting prophylactic fusion of a healthy or minimally degenerated adjacent level. Fusing C6–C7 unnecessarily increases surgical time, blood loss, and the risk of pseudarthrosis, and actually accelerates adjacent segment strain at the next remaining mobile segment (C4–C5 or C7–T1). 
  * **Plan Exclusions:** HealthFirst Plan Criteria **Section 3.3** explicitly excludes coverage when imaging does not demonstrate pathology at the proposed surgical level consistent with the clinical presentation. Therefore, fusion at C6–C7 is not medically necessary.

#### Step 5: Conservative Treatment Evaluation (CIC § 10169.5)
Because conservative treatment is required for the symptomatic C5–C6 level, the member's compliance with **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2** and **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5** was evaluated:
1. **Physical Therapy:** The member completed **8 weeks (24 sessions)** of supervised physical therapy across two providers, exceeding the 6-week requirement under Plan Section 2.2(a) and satisfying **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)**. The clinical record notes that right C6 radicular symptoms persisted and a functional plateau was reached.
2. **Pharmacotherapy:** The member completed appropriate trials of two qualifying agents for more than 4 weeks each: **Gabapentin** (600mg TID for 4 months; neuropathic agent) and **Cyclobenzaprine** (6 weeks; muscle relaxant). This fully satisfies Plan Section 2.2(b).
3. **Interventional Treatment:** The member underwent a **right C6 epidural steroid injection** on October 1, 2024, which provided only temporary (50%) relief before symptoms fully returned by week 4. This satisfies Plan Section 2.2(c).
* **Conclusion:** The member has fully met and exhausted all plan-mandated and statutory conservative treatment requirements.

### III. CROSS-SOURCE CONFLICT RESOLUTION
The clinical standards established by the **American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS)**, the **Congress of Neurological Surgeons (CNS)**, and the **North American Spine Society (NASS)** align perfectly with the plan's medical necessity criteria in this case:
* Single-level ACDF is highly indicated for symptomatic, concordant radiculopathy that has failed comprehensive conservative management.
* Multi-level ACDF is *not* indicated for prophylactic stabilization of asymptomatic levels with mild degenerative changes. 

While the surgeon’s concern regarding adjacent segment disease is noted, extending a fusion to an asymptomatic, non-stenotic level to "prevent" future degeneration is contrary to generally accepted standards of medical practice. Under **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**, clinical standard-based evaluation dictates that we approve the necessary level (C5–C6) to treat the active pathology while upholding the denial of the unnecessary level (C6–C7) to protect the patient from unwarranted surgical risks.
