## FINAL DISPOSITION: UPHOLD DENIAL

The health plan’s initial decision to deny prior authorization for the requested Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) C5-C6 (CPT 22551) is **UPHELD**. 

The clinical documentation submitted does not demonstrate that the member has met the health plan’s medical necessity criteria for surgical intervention, nor does it meet the threshold for statutory overrides or clinical exceptions under California state law or generally accepted standards of medical practice. Additional conservative treatment remains a clinically safe and recommended pathway for this member's presentation.

## II. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY OF CLINICAL CASE AND APPEAL

### 1. Clinical Presentation
The member is a 44-year-old female presenting with a primary diagnosis of right-sided C5-C6 cervical radiculopathy. Her clinical symptoms consist of right shoulder and neck pain (rated 6/10) accompanied by mild tingling in the right C6 dermatomal distribution. She does not report any bilateral upper extremity symptoms, gait instability, or bowel/bladder dysfunction.

### 2. Diagnostic Imaging and Physical Exam
An MRI of the cervical spine dated September 10, 2024, reveals a mild-to-moderate C5-C6 disc herniation with right C6 nerve root contact. Critically, there is no measurable nerve root compression, no spinal cord contact, no spinal cord compression, and no T2-weighted signal changes (myelomalacia). 

A physical and neurological examination on September 15, 2024, demonstrated completely normal muscle strength (5/5 grip strength bilaterally), symmetric and normal deep tendon reflexes (bicep reflex 2+ bilaterally), and an absolute absence of upper motor neuron (UMN) signs (e.g., no Hoffman's sign, no clonus, no hyperreflexia). Functionally, the member continues to work full-time with no activity limitations.

### 3. Prior Treatments Completed
To date, the member has completed the following conservative management:
*   **Physical Therapy:** Four (4) weeks of supervised physical therapy (total of 12 sessions), which produced mild improvement in her pain. Therapy was halted prematurely by the treating surgeon to seek surgical authorization.
*   **Pharmacotherapy:** An eight-week course of Naproxen 500mg BID (NSAID), which provided moderate pain relief. No other pharmacological classes have been trialed.
*   **Interventional Treatments:** None.

### 4. Appeal Rationale and Clinical Dispute
The requesting surgeon, Dr. Patrick Walsh, MD, formally appeals the initial denial dated October 15, 2024. Dr. Walsh contends that:
1. His clinical judgment as the examining, treating surgeon with 25 years of experience and over 2,000 performed ACDFs should take precedence over a remote reviewer.
2. The denial is "inadequate and wrong."
3. The member will suffer "permanent disability" if the surgical authorization is not immediately granted.

### Step 3: Standard Plan Criteria Compliance Review (CC-SPINE-2024)
Because the member has cervical radiculopathy without myelopathy, her request must be evaluated against the standard medical necessity criteria outlined in HealthFirst Clinical Coverage Guideline **CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2**:

1.  **Physical Therapy (Section 2.2(a)):** Requires a minimum of **six (6) weeks** of supervised PT. 
    *   *Evaluation:* The member completed only **four (4) weeks** (12 sessions). Dr. Walsh elected to halt PT prematurely. This requirement is **unmet**.
2.  **Pharmacotherapy (Section 2.2(b)):** Requires a trial of at least **two (2) different classes** of pharmacological agents for a minimum of four (4) weeks each. Covered classes include NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, neuropathic agents, and oral corticosteroids.
    *   *Evaluation:* The member trialed only **one (1) agent** (Naproxen, an NSAID) for eight weeks. No second agent (such as cyclobenzaprine or gabapentin) was trialed. This requirement is **unmet**.
3.  **Interventional Treatment (Section 2.2(c)):** Requires at least **one (1) interventional procedure** (such as a cervical epidural steroid injection).
    *   *Evaluation:* The member has had **zero (0)** interventional procedures. This requirement is **unmet**.

**Exception 2.2(A) Applicability:** The plan's "Myelopathy Exception" (which reduces PT to 4 weeks and waives the interventional requirement) applies only when cervical myelopathy is the *primary* diagnosis. Because the member does not have cervical myelopathy, she does not qualify for this exception. Furthermore, even if the exception were applied, she would still fail due to completing only one pharmacological trial instead of two.

**Finding:** The requested procedure fails to satisfy any of the conservative treatment requirements under HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2. *Action: Proceed to Step 4.*

### Step 4: Statutory Physical Therapy & Conservative Treatment Exemption Analysis
We must evaluate whether California statutory overrides supersede the health plan’s physical therapy and conservative management requirements.

1.  **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a) Evaluation:**
    *   *Subdivision (a)(1) [Contraindication]:* Dr. Walsh has *not* documented that physical therapy is clinically contraindicated or would cause harm. In fact, physical therapy notes indicate that the member experienced "mild improvement in pain" without any adverse events.
    *   *Subdivision (a)(2) [Functional Plateau]:* The member did not complete her course of physical therapy, and there is no documentation from the treating physical therapist or physician establishing that she reached a "functional plateau" (defined as <10% improvement over three sessions or reaching maximum therapeutic benefit). She was improving when therapy was halted.
    *   *Subdivision (a)(3) [Structural Neurological Compromise]:* This statutory waiver applies when objective evidence reveals structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address. Examples include progressive spinal cord compression or myelomalacia. The member’s MRI explicitly notes *no* cord contact, *no* cord compression, and *no* myelomalacia. She has a normal neurological examination with 5/5 motor strength. No structural compromise exists that would legally waive the PT requirement.
2.  **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 Evaluation:**
    *   APL 22-014 mandates that plans waive conservative treatment requirements for "time-sensitive neurological conditions" where delay risks irreversible harm. This is specifically defined as cases with objective MRI evidence of progressive pathology (such as myelomalacia or cord signal changes) where the physician documents conservative treatment failure.
    *   The member has no cord compression, no myelomalacia, and no progressive clinical deficits. Her radicular pain is stable and has shown positive response to Naproxen and initial PT. Therefore, APL 22-014 does not apply.

**Finding:** There are no applicable California statutory overrides (CIC § 10169.5 or DMHC APL 22-014) to waive the conservative treatment requirements. *Action: Proceed to Step 5.*

---

### Step 5: Generally Accepted Clinical Standards Review
Pursuant to CHSC § 1374.32(b), medical necessity must be evaluated against generally accepted standards of medical practice (which supersede a plan’s more restrictive internal criteria if a conflict occurs). 

1.  **AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023 Update):**
    *   *Tier 1 (Strong Surgical Indication):* Reserved for moderate-to-severe myelopathy, objective cord compression, myelomalacia, or progressive motor deficits. The member meets none of these criteria.
    *   *Tier 2 (Reasonable Surgical Indication):* Surgical decompression is indicated for cervical radiculopathy *only* after persistent symptoms fail to resolve following an adequate **6-to-12 week trial** of conservative treatment.
2.  **AO Spine Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021) & NASS Guidelines (2020):**
    *   Both guidelines establish that for stable cervical radiculopathy without motor deficit, a minimum of 6 weeks of active conservative therapy (including supervised PT and pharmacotherapy) is the standard of care. Because radiculopathy has a high rate of spontaneous resolution (70–80%), immediate surgery without completing a standard conservative trial is inconsistent with evidence-based standards.

**Finding:** Generally accepted clinical standards do not support surgical intervention for stable cervical radiculopathy at this stage. Standard of care dictates completing a full 6-to-12 week conservative trial.

## IV. DIRECT REFUTATION OF APPELLANT’S ARGUMENTS

The appeal arguments submitted by Dr. Walsh are addressed below using objective clinical evidence and established standards of medical practice:

1.  **Argument:** *The treating surgeon's clinical judgment and 25 years of experience should take precedence over a remote plan reviewer.*
    *   **Refutation:** Under California law (CHSC § 1374.32), medical necessity determinations must be based on objective clinical evidence and "generally accepted standards of medical practice," rather than the subjective preference or experience of any individual physician. While Dr. Walsh's extensive surgical experience is respected, his request for immediate surgery for stable, non-deficit radiculopathy directly contradicts the consensus guidelines of the American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS), the Congress of Neurological Surgeons (CNS), and the North American Spine Society (NASS).
2.  **Argument:** *The member will suffer "permanent disability" if surgical authorization is withheld.*
    *   **Refutation:** This claim is clinically unsubstantiated by the medical record. The member's neurological exam reveals normal (5/5) bilateral grip and upper extremity strength, normal and symmetric reflexes, and no UMN signs. Furthermore, she is working full-time with "no activity limitations." Her MRI shows only mild-to-moderate disc herniation with nerve root contact, but *no* cord compression and *no* myelomalacia. Because there is no spinal cord injury or active motor nerve degradation, there is no clinical basis to suggest that completing a standard 6-week conservative pathway poses a risk of permanent disability.

## V. CLINICAL RECOMMENDATIONS FOR THE MEMBER

To assist the member in obtaining appropriate, clinically sound care and to establish medical necessity for future surgical authorization if conservative measures ultimately fail, the following clinical pathway is recommended:

1.  **Complete Physical Therapy:** Resume and complete the remaining two (2) weeks of the standard six-week supervised cervical physical therapy course.
2.  **Optimize Pharmacotherapy:** Trial a second class of conservative medication for at least four weeks (e.g., a muscle relaxant such as cyclobenzaprine, or a neuropathic agent such as gabapentin) to complement her use of Naproxen.
3.  **Consider an Interventional Procedure:** Consult with a pain management specialist for a cervical epidural steroid injection (TFESI) at the C5-C6 level. This may provide significant therapeutic relief and is a standard component of the conservative pathway.
4.  **Clinical Re-evaluation:** If she completes these steps and continues to suffer from severe, functionally limiting radicular pain, or if she develops any new, objective neurological deficits (such as upper extremity weakness or reflex changes), a repeat prior authorization request should be submitted with updated clinical documentation. Under those circumstances, surgical decompression would meet standard medical necessity criteria.
