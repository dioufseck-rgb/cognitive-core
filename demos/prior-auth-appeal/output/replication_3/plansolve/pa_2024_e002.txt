### **SECTION I: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

This administrative and clinical appeal review evaluates the medical necessity of a requested three-level ACDF (C3–C4, C4–C5, C5–C6) for a 53-year-old female member with multi-level cervical spondylosis, myelopathy, and radiculopathy. 

The health plan’s initial reviewer approved surgical intervention at the C4–C5 level only, denying the adjacent C3–C4 and C5–C6 levels on the basis that they did not demonstrate "pathology of sufficient severity to warrant surgical intervention." 

Following a rigorous evaluation under the governing hierarchy of California State Law, generally accepted clinical standards, and the plan’s internal criteria, this review **partially overturns** that decision:
1.  **C4–C5** represents a severe, time-sensitive cervical myelopathy with active spinal cord damage (myelomalacia), which must be decompressed immediately.
2.  **C5–C6** represents an active, clinically correlating left C6 radiculopathy with objective motor weakness (4-/5 left bicep strength) and diminished reflex that has completely failed a comprehensive 9-week conservative trial (physical therapy, multi-agent pharmacotherapy, and epidural steroid injection), meeting all clinical and regulatory criteria for surgery.
3.  **C3–C4** represents mild, non-specific degenerative changes with no correlating objective neurological deficits or radicular patterns. Clinical and physical therapy records confirm its contribution to the member's symptoms is "unclear." Decompressing this level is clinically contraindicated and fails to meet clinical standards.

### **SECTION II: CLINICAL AND REGULATORY ANALYSIS BY STEP**

#### **Step 1: Procedural Adequacy Review (CHSC § 1374.31(b))**
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b), a health plan's medical necessity denial notice is legally defective if it fails to specify the exact clinical criteria applied or the precise clinical reasons for a partial or full denial. 
*   **Review Findings:** The initial denial notice dated November 25, 2024, did not explicitly cite the plan's internal guideline ID (**HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024**), which constitutes a technical procedural deficiency under California law. 
*   **Resolution:** While a pure procedural defect can trigger an administrative overturn, the member exhibits active myelomalacia (spinal cord injury) on imaging. Delaying surgical authorization to remand the decision back to the plan for clerical correction would run contrary to California health safety laws and clinical safety. Therefore, this review proceeds directly to a binding, substantive evaluation of the clinical merits under California’s Independent Medical Review (IMR) standards (**CHSC § 1374.32(b)**), which prioritize generally accepted clinical standards over plan-specific rules.

#### **Step 2: Diagnosis and Clinical Classification (Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy)**
A critical error in utilization management is the mechanical application of radiculopathy guidelines (which emphasize prolonged physical therapy) to myelopathy cases (where delay risks permanent paralysis). This review separates the member's multi-level presentation into its distinct pathophysiological processes:
1.  **Cervical Myelopathy (C4–C5):** The neurological exam dated October 5, 2024, reveals classic Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) signs, including **bilateral hyperreflexia, bilateral positive Hoffman signs, and a wide-based gait**. The MRI dated October 1, 2024, confirms a matching structural cause: **severe spinal cord compression and T2 signal changes (myelomalacia) at C4–C5**. This represents an active, moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy.
2.  **Cervical Radiculopathy (C5–C6):** The neurological exam identifies a clear Lower Motor Neuron (LMN) deficit: **left bicep weakness (4-/5) and a diminished left bicep reflex**. The MRI confirms a matching structural cause: a **moderate disc herniation causing moderate left C6 nerve root compression and foraminal stenosis**. This represents a clinically correlated left C6 radiculopathy.
3.  **Non-Specific Axial Neck Pain (C3–C4):** The clinical exam notes local neck pain upon C3–C4 palpation, but **no specific radicular or dermatomal pattern**. The physical therapist's summary explicitly notes that any **"C3–C4 contribution [is] unclear."** The MRI confirms only a **mild disc bulge and minimal foraminal narrowing** without spinal cord compression or significant nerve root impingement.

#### **Step 3: Clinical Coverage Criteria & State Law Override Analysis**

##### **A. Statutory Overrides (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)**
California law severely limits a plan's authority to mandate conservative treatment (such as physical therapy) in the presence of severe structural pathology.
*   **The Law:** Under **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3)** and **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014**, plans *shall not* require the completion of physical therapy or conservative trials when there is objective imaging evidence of progressive structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot structurally repair.
*   **Application to C4–C5:** The member’s MRI demonstrates **T2 cord signal change (myelomalacia)** at C4–C5. This indicates active, irreversible ischemic or mechanical damage to the spinal cord. Physical therapy cannot relieve this physical cord compression. Consequently, all health plan conservative treatment requirements (including the 6-week PT rule) are **legally waived and overridden by California law** for the C4–C5 level.
*   **Application to C5–C6:** While the statutory waiver for severe cord compromise is centered on the C4–C5 myelomalacia, the adjacent C5–C6 level fully satisfies standard conservative criteria:
    *   **Physical Therapy:** The member completed **9 weeks (27 sessions)** of supervised physical therapy, far exceeding the plan's standard 6-week requirement (Section 2.2(a)). The therapist documented a **functional plateau** on the C5–C6 radicular symptoms ("C5–C6 radicular symptoms persist"), satisfying **CIC § 10169.5(a)(2)**.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** The member completed a multi-agent trial exceeding the plan's 4-week requirement (Section 2.2(b)), trialing **Baclofen 10mg TID for 3 months** and **Pregabalin 300mg BID for 4 months** with only partial pain relief and minimal spasticity control.
    *   **Interventional Treatment:** The member underwent a **cervical epidural steroid injection on October 20, 2024**, which yielded only modest, temporary improvement in radicular symptoms and failed to resolve the objective bicep weakness. This satisfies Section 2.2(c).
*   **Application to C3–C4:** Because there is no severe cord compression, myelomalacia, or correlating radicular deficit at C3–C4, no state law overrides apply to this level. The member has not completed, nor is there a clinical rationale for, conservative treatment targeted at C3–C4.

#### **Step 4: Multi-Level Pathology and Clinical Standards Review**
Pursuant to **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**, the clinical standards established by the **American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS/CNS Guidelines 2023)** and the **AO Spine International Guidelines (2021)** govern this review and override any more restrictive plan criteria.

*   **C4–C5 Level (APPROVED):** Meets **AANS/CNS 2023 Tier 1 (Strong Indication)**. Decompression and fusion are urgently indicated due to moderate-to-severe myelopathy with objective spinal cord compression and active myelomalacia (T2 cord signal change). AO Spine 2021 guidelines state that delaying surgery in patients with moderate-to-severe myelopathy to complete conservative trials is clinically unsupported and risks irreversible neurological decline.
*   **C5–C6 Level (APPROVED / OVERTURNED):** Meets **AANS/CNS 2023 Tier 2 (Reasonable/Indicated)**. The member has an objective, correlating neurological deficit (left bicep motor weakness of 4-/5 and a diminished bicep reflex) corresponding to moderate left C6 nerve root compression from a moderate disc herniation. This motor weakness has persisted despite a robust 9-week course of physical therapy, 4 months of pregabalin, and a targeted epidural steroid injection. Under generally accepted neurosurgical standards, decompressive surgery is highly indicated to prevent permanent LMN motor deficit (loss of bicep function). 
*   **C3–C4 Level (DENIED / UPHELD):** Falls under **AANS/CNS 2023 Tier 3 (Surgery Not Indicated)**. The MRI demonstrates only a "mild disc bulge" and "minimal foraminal narrowing" without cord compression or nerve root compromise. The clinical examination reveals only non-specific neck pain upon palpation, without any radicular or myelopathic findings attributable to this level. The physical therapist and treating surgeon both document that the C3–C4 contribution is "unclear." Decompressing or fusing this level would represent surgery for axial neck pain or asymptomatic imaging findings, which is explicitly contraindicated by the **North American Spine Society (NASS) 2020 Guidelines** and is excluded under **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 3.1**.

### **SECTION IV: FORMAL DETERMINATION**

Pursuant to the authority granted under California Health & Safety Code § 1374.30 et seq., and based upon a comprehensive review of the clinical records, imaging reports, physical therapy documentation, and governing state laws:

1.  The health plan's denial of **Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C5–C6 is hereby OVERTURNED**. Surgical decompression and fusion at this level are medically necessary due to persistent, objective radicular motor weakness that has failed exhaustive conservative therapy.
2.  The health plan's denial of **Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C3–C4 is hereby UPHELD**. Surgical intervention at this level is not medically necessary, as there is no objective correlating pathology or neurological deficit.
3.  The health plan's prior approval of **Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) at C4–C5 is AFFIRMED**.

The medical necessity of a **two-level ACDF spanning C4–C5 and C5–C6** is established. The health plan shall immediately authorize the modified two-level procedure.
