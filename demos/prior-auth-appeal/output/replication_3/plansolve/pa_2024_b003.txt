### I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & DISPOSITION

This is an independent appeal review of a prior authorization denial issued by HealthFirst on October 1, 2024, for a single-level anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C3-C4 (CPT 22551). The initial denial (Reason Code: MED-003) cited "insufficient pathology" at C3-C4 based on a cervical spine MRI. 

An exhaustive evaluation of the clinical record, conducted in accordance with California statutory requirements, clinical practice guidelines, and plan criteria, reveals that **the initial denial was both procedurally defective and clinically erroneous**. The initial reviewer failed to account for a massive hardware artifact on the MRI that obscured the C3-C4 level and ignored the subsequent diagnostic CT myelogram, which explicitly confirmed moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression. 

Because the clinical record demonstrates that the member has severe, symptomatic adjacent segment disease at C3-C4 with objective clinical deficits (3+/5 deltoid weakness) and has exhausted all conservative treatments—completely satisfying the criteria under **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2**—the initial denial is **OVERTURNED IN FULL**.

---

### II. STATUTORY AND REGULATORY EVALUATION (HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY)

In accordance with California regulatory mandates, where conflicts arise, the clinical review hierarchy of authority is established as follows: **California State Law & Regulations (CIC, CHSC, DMHC APL)** *supersedes* **Clinical Standards (AANS/CNS, AO Spine, NASS)** *supersedes* **Plan Clinical Criteria (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)**.

#### 1. Procedural Defect Audit (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
Under **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b)**, a health plan's medical necessity denial notice must specify the exact clinical coverage criteria applied and provide the precise clinical reason for the denial. 
*   **Audit Finding:** The initial denial notice dated October 1, 2024, cited "MED-003" and stated that "imaging does not show sufficient pathology... MRI shows mild degenerative changes." The plan failed to cite the applicable clinical policy document (**HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024**) or its specific subsections. 
*   **Procedural Ruling:** The denial notice is **procedurally defective** under **CHSC § 1374.31(b)** and is treated as if no compliant decision was issued. While a procedural defect can trigger a remand, the clinical evidence is fully mature and overwhelmingly demonstrates medical necessity. Therefore, this review proceeds to a substantive clinical evaluation to resolve the matter directly in favor of the member.

#### 2. Statutory Override of Conservative Care Requirements (CIC § 10169.5)
The initial review asserted that the member's imaging was insufficient but acknowledged that conservative treatment was met. To establish a firm statutory baseline, **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(2)** prohibits plans from mechanically enforcing or extending physical therapy requirements when a treating physician documents that a member has completed physical therapy and achieved a "functional plateau" without symptom resolution. 
*   **Finding:** The member completed 10 weeks (30 sessions) of supervised physical therapy with a documented "no improvement" in her radicular symptoms. This clinical presentation constitutes a **functional plateau** under **CIC § 10169.5(b)**. Any insistence by the plan on further conservative management is legally barred.

### III. CLINICAL CLASSIFICATION ANALYSIS

#### 1. Clinical Classification: Radiculopathy vs. Myelopathy
A critical clinical distinction must be established to determine the correct evaluation pathway:
*   **Cervical Myelopathy** requires spinal cord compression accompanied by upper motor neuron (UMN) signs (e.g., hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman's sign, bilateral symptoms, and gait/balance disturbances).
*   **Cervical Radiculopathy** is characterized by unilateral, dermatomal nerve root compression with corresponding lower motor neuron signs (e.g., sensory changes, localized weakness, and diminished reflexes).

**Member's Clinical Presentation (Neurological Exam dated August 20, 2024):**
*   **Symptoms:** Left C4 dermatomal distribution numbness (shoulder and lateral arm).
*   **Motor Strength:** Left deltoid weakness graded at **3+/5** (new onset).
*   **Reflexes:** Diminished left bicep reflex.
*   **Gait/UMN Signs:** No bilateral symptoms, no hyperreflexia, no positive Hoffman's sign, and no gait disturbances documented.

**Conclusion:** The member's primary presentation is **severe, progressive left C4 Cervical Radiculopathy** secondary to adjacent segment disease (following her 2019 C4-C5 fusion). There is no clinical evidence of cervical myelopathy. Therefore, standard criteria under **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2** apply.

### IV. RADIOGRAPHIC CORRELATION AUDIT

The sole basis of the plan's initial denial was Dr. Alan Foster's clinical finding that the C3-C4 imaging showed only "mild degenerative changes" and "insufficient pathology." A rigorous audit of the radiographic evidence reveals this finding to be factually and clinically incorrect.

*   **Cervical Spine MRI (August 15, 2024):** The radiologist noted "SIGNIFICANT ARTIFACT from C4-C5 hardware limiting evaluation." While the scan showed "mild degenerative signal changes," the report explicitly stated that the reading clinician **"cannot exclude moderate compression due to artifact."** 
*   **CT Myelogram (September 10, 2024):** Because of the severe metal artifact on the MRI, a CT myelogram (the gold standard for evaluating stenosis in the presence of spinal hardware) was appropriately performed. The findings were definitive: **"C3-C4: moderate-severe left C4 nerve root compression confirmed. Significant foraminal stenosis. MRI underestimated pathology due to hardware artifact from prior fusion."**

**Clinical Correlation:** The CT myelogram provides objective, high-resolution radiographic evidence of severe, localized structural pathology (moderate-to-severe left C4 nerve root compression) that correlates perfectly with the member's clinical deficits (3+/5 left deltoid weakness and left shoulder/arm numbness). The initial reviewer’s reliance on the compromised MRI while ignoring the CT myelogram constitutes a critical diagnostic error.

### VI. CLINICAL STANDARDS CONFORMANCE (AANS/CNS & AO SPINE)

According to the **AANS/CNS Joint Section Guidelines (2023 Update)** and the **AO Spine Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021)**:
1.  **Surgical Indication:** A single-level ACDF is a **Tier 2/Strong Surgical Indication** for patients presenting with cervical radiculopathy associated with objective, progressive neurological deficits (such as 3+/5 deltoid weakness) who have failed to improve after 6 to 12 weeks of comprehensive conservative management.
2.  **Diagnostic Quality:** When evaluating surgical pathology in a patient with prior spinal instrumentation, clinical standards dictate that standard MRI is frequently insufficient due to susceptibility artifacts. A CT myelogram must be utilized to accurately assess the nerve roots. Relying solely on a compromised MRI to deny surgical intervention deviates from the accepted standards of modern neurosurgical care.

### VII. FINAL DECISION AND ORDER

Based on the hierarchy of authority and a comprehensive clinical review:
1.  The initial denial of authorization was **procedurally defective** under **CHSC § 1374.31(b)**.
2.  The initial clinical review erred by relying on an MRI scan that was explicitly documented as compromised by hardware artifact, while ignoring a definitive, diagnostic **CT myelogram** which confirmed moderate-to-severe C3-C4 nerve root compression.
3.  The member has documented left C4 radiculopathy with severe, objective motor weakness (**3+/5 deltoid strength**) that correlates directly with the confirmed structural compression.
4.  The member has exhausted all plan-defined conservative care, including **10 weeks of physical therapy**, **two trials of neuropathic medications**, and a **cervical epidural steroid injection**, satisfying **HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2** and achieving a functional plateau under **CIC § 10169.5**.

Therefore, the initial decision of Dr. Alan Foster is **OVERTURNED**.
