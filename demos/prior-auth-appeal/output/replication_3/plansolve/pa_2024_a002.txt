### **I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

This case concerns a 47-year-old male with clinically severe, MRI-documented cervical spondylotic myelopathy at C6-C7, characterized by objective upper motor neuron (UMN) signs (bilateral Hoffman sign, hyperreflexia, gait instability) and spinal cord compression with early **myelomalacia** (T2-weighted cord signal changes). 

The plan initially denied coverage under clinical coverage policy *CC-SPINE-2024 (MED-003)* because the member completed only 4 weeks of physical therapy instead of the standard 6-week trial. The plan refused to apply its internal "myelopathy exception" (which permits a 4-week trial) because the administrative billing code utilized by the physician on the initial referral was "cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy" (ICD-10 M47.812).

This appeal is **OVERTURNED** because:
1. **California statutory mandates (CIC § 10169.5(a)(3) and DMHC APL 22-014)** strictly prohibit health plans from requiring conservative physical therapy trials in cases involving structural neurological compromise, such as spinal cord compression with myelomalacia.
2. **Generally accepted clinical standards (AANS/CNS 2023, AO Spine 2021)** classify moderate-to-severe myelopathy with myelomalacia as a Tier 1 strong surgical indication where delaying surgery for physical therapy is clinically contraindicated and risks permanent, irreversible neurological deficit.
3. **The plan's "primary diagnosis" restriction** is an administrative artifact that violates the clinical reality documented throughout the medical record, constituting a clinical category error. Under **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**, clinical standards and state laws supersede restrictive, non-evidence-based internal plan criteria.

### **II. EVALUATION FINDINGS & STEP-BY-STEP ANALYSIS**

#### **Step 1: Procedural Adequacy and Defect Review**
The initial denial notice issued on November 5, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, was evaluated for compliance with **California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.31(b)**. 
*   **Finding:** The denial notice specified the criteria applied (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024), provided the specific clinical rationale (failure to complete 6 weeks of physical therapy and failure to meet the "primary diagnosis" billing requirement for the myelopathy exception), and included standard Independent Medical Review (IMR) disclosures. 
*   **Result:** The denial notice is determined to be procedurally adequate. The review therefore proceeds to the substantive clinical and statutory evaluations.

#### **Step 2: Clinical Classification and Diagnostic Verification (Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy)**
A clinical distinction must be drawn between radiculopathy (nerve root compression) and myelopathy (spinal cord compression), as they involve fundamentally different clinical courses and treatment algorithms.
*   **Clinical Record Review:** 
    *   **Neurological Examination (July 1, 2024):** Documents bilateral grip weakness (3/5), bilateral positive Hoffman sign, bilateral hyperreflexia of the upper extremities, and impaired tandem gait. These are objective, classic upper motor neuron (UMN) signs indicative of cervical myelopathy.
    *   **Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) (July 10, 2024):** Confirms C6-C7 disc herniation with moderate spinal cord compression and, critically, **T2-weighted signal changes within the spinal cord consistent with early myelomalacia**.
*   **Result:** While the billing code used on the initial referral was *M47.812 (cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy)*, the clinical narrative and objective physical findings confirm a severe, active diagnosis of **cervical spondylotic myelopathy with myelomalacia**. The administrative billing label is a clinical artifact; the member's true pathology is structural spinal cord compromise.

#### **Step 3: Statutory Physical Therapy Exemption Assessment (California Law)**
Under the strict governing hierarchy of authority, California State Law overrides plan-specific clinical criteria. The plan's 6-week physical therapy requirement was evaluated against state statutes:
*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5(a)(3):** Prohibits a health plan from conditioning surgical approval on the completion of physical therapy if "the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." The member's MRI demonstrates moderate cord compression and myelomalacia (actual cord injury), which physical therapy cannot structurally resolve.
*   **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1):** Prohibits mandatory physical therapy trials when the treating physician documents in writing that physical therapy is contraindicated or unlikely to benefit. Dr. Sarah Chen, MD, explicitly halted the physical therapy after 4 weeks and documented that further physical therapy was contraindicated due to the risk of neurological decline in the setting of myelomalacia.
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014:** Explicitly mandates that health plans **shall not** require the completion of a full course of conservative treatment (including physical therapy) when objective imaging demonstrates progressive or severe pathology—specifically identifying **myelomalacia** on MRI as a finding that waives standard conservative timelines.
*   **Result:** The plan's physical therapy requirement is **legally waived and unenforceable** for this member under California law. 

#### **Step 4: Clinical Standard and Medical Necessity Review (National Guidelines)**
Pursuant to **CHSC § 1374.32(b)**, the standard of review for medical necessity must align with generally accepted standards of medical practice, overriding restrictive internal plan criteria.
*   **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update):** Classify moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective cord compression and/or MRI-documented myelomalacia as a **Tier 1 — Strong Surgical Indication**. Immediate surgical decompression is recommended to prevent permanent neurological deficits.
*   **AO Spine Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):** State that there is **no clinical evidence** supporting a mandatory course of physical therapy prior to surgical decompression in patients with moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy. The guidelines warn that delaying surgery to complete a physical therapy trial "is not supported by the evidence and may result in neurological deterioration."
*   **Result:** Medical necessity is established under national clinical guidelines. Forcing a patient with active myelomalacia to complete an arbitrary 6-week physical therapy trial violates the clinical standard of care and places the patient at risk of irreversible quadriplegia or permanent motor deficits.

#### **Step 5: Plan Criteria Compliance Review (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024)**
The plan's internal criteria were reviewed to assess whether the initial reviewer correctly applied policy CC-SPINE-2024:
*   **Plan Policy CC-SPINE-2024, Section 2.2(A) (Myelopathy Exception):** Modifies conservative treatment by reducing physical therapy to a 4-week minimum and waiving interventional procedures if the treating physician documents a risk of neurological deterioration. 
*   **Member's Conservative Treatment Status:**
    *   **Physical Therapy:** Completed 4 weeks (12 sessions) before being halted due to MRI findings of myelomalacia.
    *   **Pharmacotherapy:** Completed 3 months of Gabapentin 300mg TID (neuropathic agent) and 2 months of Naproxen 500mg BID (NSAID). This satisfies the plan's requirement of two agents for 4+ weeks.
    *   **Interventional Treatment:** A cervical epidural steroid injection was performed on September 15, 2024, yielding no improvement.
*   **Discrepancy:** The plan denied the exception solely because the "primary diagnosis" listed in the billing field was radiculopathy, rather than myelopathy. However, the plan's clinical records and PT referral notes clearly show that myelopathy was documented as a co-existing, dominant clinical finding. Denying a clinical exception based purely on an administrative billing code when the clinical notes establish the exception's physical criteria is a clinical category error.
*   **Result:** The member met the clinical spirit of **Exception 2.2(A)**. Regardless, even if the plan's internal criteria strictly exclude the member based on the billing code, this restriction is entirely superseded by California statutory law (Step 3) and generally accepted clinical guidelines (Step 4).

### **III. CONCLUSION & ORDER**

Marcus Webb presents with a critical, time-sensitive neurological condition: cervical spondylotic myelopathy with spinal cord compression and early myelomalacia at C6-C7. 

The initial denial, which insisted upon an additional 2 weeks of physical therapy, is clinically dangerous and legally invalid:
1. Under **CIC § 10169.5** and **DMHC APL 22-014**, the presence of MRI-confirmed cord compression and myelomalacia legally exempts this member from the plan’s physical therapy requirements.
2. Under **CHSC § 1374.32(b)** and **AANS/CNS / AO Spine Guidelines**, surgical decompression (ACDF) is the primary, Tier 1 indicated treatment, rendering the plan's 6-week physical therapy requirement inapplicable.

Therefore, the initial decision is **OVERTURNED**.
