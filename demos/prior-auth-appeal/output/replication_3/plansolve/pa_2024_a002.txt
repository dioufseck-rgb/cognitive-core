### I. GOVERNING HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY
In accordance with California insurance law and standard utilization review protocols, this appeal has been evaluated under the following strict hierarchy of authority:
$$\text{California State Law \& Regulations} > \text{Generally Accepted Clinical Standards} > \text{HealthFirst Plan Criteria}$$
Where the health plan’s internal clinical criteria are more restrictive than California statutory mandates or generally accepted professional medical standards, the plan’s criteria are superseded, and the higher authority must govern the coverage determination.

---

### II. CLINICAL ANALYSIS & DETERMINATION BY STEP

#### Step 1: Procedural Adequacy and Regulatory Disclosure Check (CHSC § 1374.31(b))
The initial denial notice issued on November 5, 2024, by Dr. Patricia Huang, MD, cited reason code MED-003, stating that the member failed to complete the standard 6-week physical therapy (PT) requirement and failed to qualify for the myelopathy exception because the primary billing diagnosis listed on the treating physician's note was "cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy" (ICD-10 M47.812) rather than primary myelopathy.

*   **Finding:** The denial notice technically cited the plan's 6-week PT requirement, detailed the rejection of the myelopathy exception based on the primary billing code, and provided the standard California Independent Medical Review (IMR) disclosures. However, because the plan applied these criteria to deny a clinically urgent, progressive neurological condition based on administrative billing artifacts, the substantive application of the criteria was legally and clinically deficient. We proceed to **Step 2** to evaluate the true clinical presentation of the patient.

#### Step 2: Diagnostic Classification and Clinical Presentation Verification (Myelopathy vs. Radiculopathy)
A comprehensive review of the clinical record was conducted to determine whether the member's condition represents cervical radiculopathy, cervical myelopathy, or a combination of both.

*   **Clinical Findings (Exam Date: 2024-07-01):**
    *   **Bilateral Symptoms:** The patient presents with bilateral hand weakness and severe coordination problems.
    *   **Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) Signs:** Physical examination reveals **bilateral 3/5 grip weakness**, a **positive bilateral Hoffman sign**, and **bilateral hyperreflexia** of the upper extremities.
    *   **Gait Disturbances:** The neurological exam documents **impaired tandem gait**, demonstrating functional spinal cord compromise.
*   **Radiographic Findings (MRI Date: 2024-07-10):**
    *   The cervical spine MRI reveals a **C6-C7 disc herniation causing moderate spinal cord compression**.
    *   Crucially, the MRI demonstrates **T2 signal change within the cord consistent with early myelomalacia** (structural spinal cord injury).
*   **Conclusion:** The clinical presentation and imaging findings establish a definitive, severe clinical diagnosis of **cervical spondylotic myelopathy with active myelomalacia**. Radiculopathy is present but is secondary to the dominant, progressive spinal cord compression. The diagnostic threshold for clinical myelopathy is fully satisfied. We proceed to **Step 3**.

#### Step 3: Plan Criteria and "Primary Diagnosis" Technical Application (HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2A)
The plan denied the "Myelopathy Exception" (Section 2.2A) because the treating physician utilized the ICD-10 billing code **M47.812 (cervical spondylosis with radiculopathy)** on PT referrals and listed it on the primary diagnosis line of the administrative billing record.

*   **Evaluation of CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2A:** The plan’s criteria state that the myelopathy exception (which reduces the PT requirement to 4 weeks and waives interventional therapy) "applies only when myelopathy is the PRIMARY diagnosis."
*   **Clinical Reality vs. Administrative Artifact:** The treating physician's clinical narrative notes consistently and unambiguously describe cervical myelopathy, upper motor neuron deficits, and radiographic myelomalacia. Dr. Chen documented that the ICD-10 billing code utilized was an administrative artifact.
*   **PT Completion:** The member successfully completed **4 weeks of supervised physical therapy (12 sessions)** before the treating physician halted the therapy due to safety concerns.
*   **Conclusion:** The clinical record demonstrates that myelopathy is the clinically dominant driver of the surgical recommendation. The plan's decision to deny the exception based strictly on a billing/ICD-10 line item, while ignoring the clinical narrative and the objective 3/5 motor weakness and gait impairment, represents a hyper-technical and clinically unsound misapplication of its own clinical criteria. Under a proper clinical reading of Section 2.2A, the member met the modified myelopathy criteria by completing 4 weeks of PT. We proceed to **Step 4** (Statutory Override).

#### Step 4: Statutory Override Evaluation (CIC § 10169.5 & DMHC APL 22-014)
California law strictly limits a health plan's ability to mandate conservative therapy when doing so poses a risk of irreversible neurological injury.

*   **California Insurance Code (CIC) § 10169.5 Compliance:**
    *   Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(3)**, a health insurer *shall not* condition surgical approval on physical therapy completion when "the condition involves objective evidence of structural neurological compromise that physical therapy cannot address." Moderate spinal cord compression with **myelomalacia** (T2 cord signal change) represents severe structural neurological compromise that cannot be corrected, stabilized, or addressed by physical therapy.
    *   Under **CIC § 10169.5(a)(1)**, the PT mandate is waived because the treating physician documented in writing that continued physical therapy is **contraindicated** and likely to cause neurological deterioration. Dr. Chen halted PT immediately upon receiving the MRI report confirming myelomalacia.
*   **DMHC All-Plan Letter (APL) 22-014 Compliance:**
    *   APL 22-014 mandates that plans *shall not* require completion of a full course of conservative treatment when objective imaging demonstrates progressive pathology (specifically identifying **myelomalacia** as a qualifying finding), the treating physician documents conservative failure/contraindication, and surgery is indicated under generally accepted guidelines.
*   **Conclusion:** The presence of moderate cord compression, myelomalacia, and UMN signs triggers a **complete statutory waiver of the plan's physical therapy requirements** under both CIC § 10169.5 and DMHC APL 22-014. The plan's insistence on a 6-week PT trial is legally prohibited. We proceed to **Step 5** to verify clinical standards.

#### Step 5: Generally Accepted Clinical Standards Evaluation (CHSC § 1374.32(b))
Under California Health & Safety Code (CHSC) § 1374.32(b), Independent Medical Review and appeal determinations must be based on generally accepted standards of medical practice, which supersede restrictive health plan rules.

*   **AANS/CNS Joint Guidelines (2023 Update):** Establish that moderate-to-severe cervical myelopathy with objective spinal cord compression and myelomalacia constitutes a **Tier 1 — Strong Indication** for prompt surgical decompression (ACDF). Extended conservative therapy is contraindicated because myelomalacia represents active spinal cord cellular injury; delaying surgery is associated with irreversible motor deficits and permanent gait impairment.
*   **AO Spine International Clinical Practice Guidelines (2021):** Confirm there is **no high-quality clinical evidence** supporting a mandatory trial of physical therapy prior to surgical decompression in patients presenting with myelopathy and myelomalacia. The 6-week PT trial requirement is derived from radiculopathy evidence and represents a clinical category error when applied to myelopathy.
*   **Conservative Care Failures in this Case:** The member has already failed a comprehensive 3-month trial of Gabapentin (300mg TID), a 2-month trial of Naproxen (500mg BID), a cervical epidural steroid injection (September 15, 2024) which yielded no change in motor deficits, and 4 weeks of physical therapy.
*   **Conclusion:** Generally accepted clinical standards strongly mandate surgical intervention (ACDF C6-C7) for this clinical presentation. Requiring additional physical therapy is medically inappropriate and poses an immediate risk of irreversible spinal cord injury.

---

### III. DETERMINATION SUMMARY

The plan's denial was based on a hyper-technical reading of administrative billing codes to bypass its own clinical myelopathy exceptions, and a mechanical application of a 6-week physical therapy requirement. 

1.  **Clinical Reality:** The member has objective cervical spondylotic myelopathy with active spinal cord injury (**myelomalacia**), marked by bilateral 3/5 grip weakness, hyperreflexia, and gait impairment.
2.  **Plan Criteria Misapplication:** The member completed 4 weeks of PT, which clinically satisfied the modified myelopathy exception under HealthFirst CC-SPINE-2024 Section 2.2A.
3.  **California Statutory Override:** Under **CIC § 10169.5** and **DMHC APL 22-014**, health plans are legally prohibited from conditioning surgical approval on physical therapy when objective structural neurological compromise (myelomalacia) is present and the treating physician has documented that physical therapy is contraindicated.
4.  **National Clinical Guidelines:** Both the **AANS/CNS (2023)** and **AO Spine (2021)** guidelines class this presentation as a Tier 1 surgical indication, warning that mandatory conservative care delays cause irreversible neurological harm.

Based on the governing hierarchy of authority, California statutory protections and generally accepted clinical standards override the plan's restrictive criteria. 

**The denial of CPT code 22551 for Marcus Webb is OVERTURNED. Authorization must be issued immediately.**
